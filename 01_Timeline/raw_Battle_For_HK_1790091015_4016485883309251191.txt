Title: Receiving ships in Hong Kong Harbour during the 19th Century
Source: http://battleforhongkong.blogspot.com/2025/05/receiving-ships-in-hong-kong-harbour.html
Feed: Battle_For_HK

Content:
<p style="text-align: justify;">The receiving ships were hulked by the removal of their upper masts and rigging and by the removal of some or all of their guns. They were moored in mid-harbour. They typically acted as hospital ships, store ships, accommodation ships and as headquarters of the Royal Navy in Hong Kong. They are depicted in early photos or artists renditions with canvas covers stretched above their upper decks providing some relief from the summer heat. Some of the receiving ships also called harbour ships overlapped each other in Hong Kong. At the end of their useful life they were mostly sold in Hong Kong for breaking up.&nbsp;</p><p style="text-align: justify;"><b>HMS <i>Minden</i></b>&nbsp;was a 74-gun third-rate ship of the line. A battleship from the days of the sailing navy. She was launched in 1810 in Bombay, India. The <i>Minden</i> was hulked in 1842 and served as a hospital ship anchored in Victoria Harbour. She replaced the shore-based Royal Naval Hospital which was destroyed by a typhoon in July 1841. The first Royal Naval Hospital was a temporary mat-shed structure located near the site of Wellington Barracks. <i>Minden</i>&nbsp;was replaced as a hospital ship by HMS <i>Alligator </i>in 1846 and <i>Minden</i> became a store ship and receiving ship. <i>Minden </i>was sold for breaking up in 1861. Two streets in Kowloon, Minden Row and Minden Avenue, are named after this ship commemorating its early association with Hong Kong.&nbsp;</p><p style="text-align: justify;"><br /></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjeWW6ONzX398u4BvTUe-FkUOfb53Vil3JsW4KEX1RbO5RQedkjGcbjL2aaq9WUqABQDQybNtk9CCFB0aqFCh9Vv0ey6E8Lq63inikr_uXWtnS867LBcKHtqGwJad-C_r4usQBtdv5x8W5TdbtjT7FH8sZ6gBexj6Fasr-g4V9E0-Dlc1XylRtQHvGG/s602/HMS_Minden_at_the_Bombardment_of_Algiers_1816_by_Chambers_(cropped).jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" height="640" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjeWW6ONzX398u4BvTUe-FkUOfb53Vil3JsW4KEX1RbO5RQedkjGcbjL2aaq9WUqABQDQybNtk9CCFB0aqFCh9Vv0ey6E8Lq63inikr_uXWtnS867LBcKHtqGwJad-C_r4usQBtdv5x8W5TdbtjT7FH8sZ6gBexj6Fasr-g4V9E0-Dlc1XylRtQHvGG/w350-h640/HMS_Minden_at_the_Bombardment_of_Algiers_1816_by_Chambers_(cropped).jpg" width="350" /></a></div><p></p><p style="text-align: center;">HMS <i>Minden </i>in action at the bombardment of Algiers in 1816 (Source: Wikipedia)</p><p style="text-align: center;"><br /></p><p style="text-align: justify;">HMS <i>Alligator </i>was a smaller vessel than the <i>Minden</i>&nbsp;but also constructed in India as a 28-gun sixth rate corvette., also described as a frigate. She had seen action during the First Opium War. In 1842 she was converted to act as a troop ship before being hulked in 1846 and serving as a naval hospital. She was sold for breaking up in October 1865 in Hong Kong</p><p style="text-align: justify;"><br /></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjTLWH0gLQSx8wX6MrAQiMWItww93-D35LjRi2723Ds3kHtF4CIRqonKzDnvduz1gsyl_HY3ghy2hAXFHHPUg6NEfPTpAqxB4IwwxXdmRvPW-cbV-_6dTLWUOOjbFW26ly8jds3yoSemfekMT-SFnGDk0yrdv5hcIK9Y3fwvzpo4wQAAQ5OibM_1i9c/s339/download.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" height="282" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjTLWH0gLQSx8wX6MrAQiMWItww93-D35LjRi2723Ds3kHtF4CIRqonKzDnvduz1gsyl_HY3ghy2hAXFHHPUg6NEfPTpAqxB4IwwxXdmRvPW-cbV-_6dTLWUOOjbFW26ly8jds3yoSemfekMT-SFnGDk0yrdv5hcIK9Y3fwvzpo4wQAAQ5OibM_1i9c/w640-h282/download.jpg" width="640" /></a></div><div style="text-align: center;">The Corvette HMS <i>Alligator</i>&nbsp;served as a hospital ship in HK Harbour (Source: Wikipedia)</div><p style="text-align: justify;"><b><br /></b></p><p style="text-align: justify;"><b>HMS <i>Hercules</i>&nbsp;</b>was launched at Chatham in 1815. She was a 74-gun third rate ship of the line.&nbsp; In 1854 she arrived in Hong Kong to serve as Receiving Ship. She was sold in 1865 for breaking up.&nbsp;</p><p style="text-align: justify;"><b>HMS <i>Melville </i></b>built at Bombay and launched in 1817. She was a former 74-gun third rate ship of the line.&nbsp; She became a hospital ship in 1857. She was sold for scrap in 1873 and the funds received were used to finance a new Royal Naval Hospital which was built on the raised ground now occupied by Ruttonjee Hospital .</p><p style="text-align: justify;"><b>HMS <i>Prince Charlotte</i>:&nbsp; </b>Built at Portsmouth and launched in 1825. She was a first rate ship of the line carrying 104 guns. She was named after the daughter of the Prince Regent (later George IV).&nbsp; In 1837 she was flagship of the Mediterranean fleet. She was sent to Hong Kong in 1858 during the Second Opium War (1857 - 1859).</p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiokoesG3uMZ76B-kL6UWxvk_G1ClFG3beOj22b8blt1iX8gaOLL1i3NaI0IWVBi9po09S6TB7-SL8-SX7wqPD-ZusYmKZFkgfHEyS42Q0prMwC6pOJCajyDrPoqVMABgN9YqXGUBAOk4ypBHhP7OCpV_Gte0u1bRWUSS57UtII8xkWr_XFNu7RAhcN/s289/download-1.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" height="243" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiokoesG3uMZ76B-kL6UWxvk_G1ClFG3beOj22b8blt1iX8gaOLL1i3NaI0IWVBi9po09S6TB7-SL8-SX7wqPD-ZusYmKZFkgfHEyS42Q0prMwC6pOJCajyDrPoqVMABgN9YqXGUBAOk4ypBHhP7OCpV_Gte0u1bRWUSS57UtII8xkWr_XFNu7RAhcN/w400-h243/download-1.jpg" width="400" /></a></div><p style="text-align: center;">HMS Princess Charlotte (Source: www world naval ships).</p><p style="text-align: center;"><br /></p><p style="text-align: justify;"><b>HMS <i>Meeanee</i></b><i> </i>was a two-decker 80-gun second rate ship of the line. She was built in Bombay and launched in 1821.&nbsp; Her name strikes one as rather strange but she was in fact&nbsp; named after the Battle of Meeanee. The English rendition is sometimes referred to as Miani.&nbsp; The battle n British India took place in 1843. In 1857 Meeanee was fitted with engines and screw propoulsion.&nbsp; In 1867 (70) she was hulked and served as a hospital ship in Victoria Harbour catering for Army personnel. She survived until 1906 when she was sold to the breaker's yard.</p><p style="text-align: justify;"><b>HMS <i>Victor Emanuel</i> </b>was launched at Pembroke Dockyard in 1855. She had originally been named HMS <i>Repulse. </i>The ship was remamed after King Victor Emanuel II who in 1861 became King of a united Italy. She carried 80 to 90 guns and was categorized as a second rate ship of the line with steam power (single screw) and full sailing rig. It was a time when the sailing navy was giving way to steam propulsion. She arrived in Hong Kong to act as receiving ship in 1874 replacing HMS <i>Princess Charlotte.</i><b>&nbsp;</b>She was the receiving ship for 21 years. The senior naval officer had his HQ on board this vessel.&nbsp;</p><p style="text-align: justify;"><b><br /></b></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjrjdR6vWPpT9085pgBGpVDW08tjpjbO59ghRm8U-8f_-g0AAWHqS4d0UArTzMOaIXB-PTHxcIivEZ3wYQCZKmicwme7YKvMT4LXuptfQahagk8kzsUxZTlUYvB3PB2zCuYcDywBjWcbfDoEsJ2pU5FAXWLwz1Ykn3vlhwcF2WkxYb6aQiyB2LEnebB/s600/article-32165158.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" height="482" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjrjdR6vWPpT9085pgBGpVDW08tjpjbO59ghRm8U-8f_-g0AAWHqS4d0UArTzMOaIXB-PTHxcIivEZ3wYQCZKmicwme7YKvMT4LXuptfQahagk8kzsUxZTlUYvB3PB2zCuYcDywBjWcbfDoEsJ2pU5FAXWLwz1Ykn3vlhwcF2WkxYb6aQiyB2LEnebB/w640-h482/article-32165158.jpg" width="640" /></a></div><div style="text-align: center;">HMS <i>Victor Emanuel </i>hulked and acting as the Receiving Ship in Hong Kong Harbour (Source: Web site HK Museum of War Resistanc and Coastal Defence)</div><p style="text-align: center;"><br /></p><p style="text-align: justify;"><b>HMS <i>Tamar </i></b>was launched at London Docks in 1863. She was a hybrid similar to <i>Victor Emanuel</i> possessing sail, mast and full rigging combined with a steam engine which powered powering a single screw and originally originally with two funnels later reduced to one. This was dismantled when she was hulked. She was built from iron rather than wood. She served for many years as a trooper and becoming receiving ship in Hong Kong in 1897 and replacing <i>Victor Emanuel.</i> She, like other receiving ships, acted as a hospital ship, accommodation ship and RN HQ. She was hulked in the harbour until 1913 when she was moved and moored alongside the West Wall of the Royal Naval Basin. During the Second World War she was towed out to the harbour in December 1941 following the Japanese inasion of Hong Kong. She was scuttled off the Wanchai water front. Parts of what is believed to be her metal hull bottom were discovered during reclamation work along with artefacts that suggest these may have been&nbsp; from that venerable old Victorian warship. When she was scuttled at her mooring it was reported that a large number of rats made their way up from the lower decks as the ship started to submerge. She sank slowly with part of her superstructure remaining above water.</p><p style="text-align: justify;"><br /></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEh_zvWasYbYB4Xkk8sHM18GFe1R1oGnWcaHCzOlAnZkbfZJrV_uMtx2Bm7BMNDCr5x52jBGDz4_jjeF5IcvL857M-ZNN1xBNg7qcfmmcOE-WFIDzwO3oOnFKxFrBxl7vbbaBhFVadjRXZPcshB-CG_QmzPqFp_yPB7YJOmRp5GGusiJHB8UYwrVM1Og/s1042/HK_HMS_Tamar_anchored_off_the-%20Dockyard_%201905.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em;
