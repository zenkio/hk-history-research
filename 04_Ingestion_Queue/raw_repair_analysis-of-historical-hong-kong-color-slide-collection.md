Primary tabs View What links here Submitted by gw on Wed, 08/26/2026 - 08:12 This week's newsletter is a guest post by regular contributor GW: Introduction My Mum recently rediscovered a set of slides she’d bought in Hong Kong in about 1986. They’ve been digitized and are posted below in order of the reference codes printed on their frames with their titles. The accompanying comments aim to establish each slide’s “where” and “when”. Many of the “where’s” are obvious, but the “when’s” less so. Dates without a quoted source come from elsewhere on Gwulo. The Slides 1. XB3, Rickshaws In The Street. XB3, Rickshaws In The Street, by gw I guess tourists were the main buyers of slides like these, so a set would have to include a pic of that favourite Hong Kong stereotype, the rickshaw, even though they’d ceased being used as a form of transport well before the 1980’s. If I remember correctly, the only ones still around in the mid-80’s were parked outside the Star Ferry, Hong Kong side and, for a fee, were available for a short ride around the concourse area and a photo. This pic was taken on Connaught Road, Central. In the background, from right to left, are the 2nd GPO Building (1911-1976), Union/Swire House (completed 1962) and St. George’s Building (demolished 1966). That gives a preliminary date range for the photo of 1962 to 1966. The hoardings on the left are where Queen’s Building stood until replaced by the Mandarin Hotel. The Mandarins’ soft opening was on 1 September 1963, but it obviously wasn’t near completion when this photo was taken, meaning it was probably taken in 1962 or early 1963. 2. XB6, Des Voeux Road, Central. XB6, Des Voeux Road Central, by gw It’s the junction of Des Voeux Road with Pedder Street left, Chater Road right, and was probably taken from the enclosed footbridge over Des Voeux Road. Helpfully, the names of two of the nearest buildings are visible - The Landmark and Wheelock House. On the right, the dark building with the MTR logo is World Wide House. The Landmark and World Wide House both opened in 1980 and had Wheelock House as a neighbour until its 1982 demolition, meaning this photo was taken between 1980 and 1982. 3. XB 7, The Hong Kong University. XB7, The Hong Kong University, by gw HKU’s old Main Building is the focus of this photo but doesn’t help date it. Directly behind it are the more modern Students Union Building and University Library, with the huge Knowles Building on the left. Infront is the old Administration Building with its dome. All these were present by 1973. To the right of the Admin Building is a white building that curves around to follow Bonham Road. It’s the Pao Siu Loong Building and was still under construction in June 1973 (1), meaning the photo was taken after that date. The undeveloped lower slopes of Lung Fu Shan, behind the Uni, were the obvious place to go when the Uni needed to expand. After this photo was taken, the next major project was the construction of the Haking Wong Building and others on the wooded slopes behind and to the right of the existing buildings. By April 1979 construction was underway (2), but there’s no sign of it in the photo, meaning it was taken before April 1979. Therefore, the slide pic was taken at sometime between 1973 and 1979. (1). HK Maps website. Map ref. 1973. The site is labelled as, “CIP June 1973”, meaning “construction in progress June 1973”. (2). HK Maps website. Map ref. 1979. The site is labelled as, “Construction In Progress April 1979”. 4. XB 10, Typhoon Shelter. XB 10, Typhoon Shelter, by gw The camera is looking west over the Causeway Bay Typhoon Shelter and the largely vacant Wanchai reclamation. The presence and absence of buildings on the left side of the photo helps date it. The Excelsior Hotel (completed 1973) is the most prominent building. Just visible behind and above it is the World Trade Centre (completed November 1975). A bit further down Gloucester Road towards Central, the British-American Tobacco Factory or its replacement, Elizabeth House, should be visible, but neither is seen. That’s probably because the Tobacco Factory had been demolished, but construction of Elizabeth House hadn’t reached a height visible from this angle. The Tobacco Factory was under demolition in February 1974 (1), the site was cleared but undeveloped in 1976 (2), and Elizabeth House was completed on 27 November 1978. Assuming a huge project such as Elizabeth House would have been visible at least one year before completion, the photo must have been taken in 1977 at the latest. Therefore, the date range for this slide is between 1975 and 1977. 1. HKMaps website. Map ref. 1974.4 labels the Tobacco Factory site, “Demolition in progress Feb 1974”. 2. HKMaps website. Aerial photo ref. 1976.3 shows the Tobacco Factory site cleared and vacant. 5. XB 15, MTR. XB 15, MTR, by gw It says “First Train” on the front of the train, so you might think this is a pic of the first journey on the opening day of the MTR - 1 st October 1979. But it’s not. Here’s a GRS photo taken at the start of the first journey. The MTR's First Journey, by gw “First Train” is written on the front of the trains in both pics, but the information displayed above the front windows is different. The train in the slide pic was the “05” for “Kwun Tong”, whereas it was the “01” and “Special” in the first journey pic. A possible explanation for the slide pic is that it shows the train that made the first ever MTR journey, but sometime later. The slide image must have been taken on or after 1 October 1979, but I can’t be more specific than that. 6. XB 17, Squatter Areas. XB 17, Squatter Areas, by gw I’ve no idea of the exact location shown here or the date taken. 7. XC 2, Night View Of Kowloon. XC 2, Night View Of Kowloon, by gw Although the title says it’s a view of Kowloon, the most prominent building is Connaught Centre (completed 1973) in Central. It’d already reached its full height when the photo was taken, but under zoom the upper floors are unlit and have an unfinished look. Also, there’s something clinging to both sides that I can’t identify. It doesn’t appear in photos of the completed building, so may have been used during construction and then removed. The nearly completed building suggests a date of 1972 or 1973. Partial corroboration of this date range comes at the right edge of the photo – the vacant space to the right of the Furama Hotel. It would shortly be filled by Hutchison House (completed 1974) and the Bank Of America Tower (completed 1975). As there’s no sign of either in the photo, and assuming huge tower blocks such as these would take over a year to build, the photo must have been taken in 1973 at the latest. Over in Kowloon, the Tsim Sha Tsui East Reclamation appears to be complete, but without buildings. The newly built Sheraton Hotel (completed 1973) can be seen at the bottom of Nathan Road, but its upper floors are largely in darkness. Being a popular hotel, and at night, I’d expect many of the harbour facing guest rooms to have their curtains open and lights on. Possibly the Hotel wasn’t fully open when the photo was taken. Based on all the above, the photo was taken in 1972 or 1973. 8. XC 5, Cross Harbour Tunnel, Kowloon Entrance. XC 5, Cross Harbour Tunnel, by gw Nobody’d heard of “health and safety” when this photo was taken. A group of guys are riding on the open back of a truck, two people on a motorbike aren’t wearing helmets, and a pedestrian is casually sauntering out of the tunnel! The Cross Harbour Tunnel opened on 2 August 1972. Rising above the tunnel portal, but on the other side of the harbour, is the Excelsior Hotel.
