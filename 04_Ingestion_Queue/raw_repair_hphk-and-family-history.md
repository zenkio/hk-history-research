HPHK and Family History | Historical Photographs of Hong Kong Skip to content Historical Photographs of Hong Kong Home About HPHK Introducing Historical Photographs of Hong Kong Guest blog: Hong Kong’s animal outliers: Chinese ‘wonks’ in a British colonial city HPHK and Family History Posted on September 10, 2026 by Administrator This post comes from Vivian Kong, a Co-Director of Hong Kong History Centre and Senior Lecturer in Modern Chinese History at the University of Bristol. Besides promoting family history research and preservation amongst the global Hong Kong diaspora, Vivian is also working on a new book project about a woman with family roots in Hong Kong, Cornwall, and Singapore. Have you ever wondered how your grandparents met? What your parents were like as kids? Where they killed time in their youth? As I work on a new initiative to support those with family roots to Hong Kong to research and preserve their family history, I came to meet with a group of family historians who are keen to learn more about their family’s past in the city. They tried asking their family questions, but any of us who have tried getting our family to talk about the past know, this can be challenging. Sometimes it is too painful to think about the past, and they would rather say nothing. This we should respect. But more often than not, our family is willing to talk, but time did its trick and they just need a bit more help unlocking old memories. This is when historical photographs come in handy. You might be thinking of your own family photo albums. Capturing birthdays, first days of school, graduations, cycling trips, fun days on the beach, weddings, or first cars, family albums can of course be very useful and give us all sorts of clues to our family history. They can also help evoke memories: in a family history workshop I organized earlier this year, a participant shared how he used family photographs to get family elders to recollect their ties to Sha Tin during oral history interviews. But as our team has been developing the Historical Photographs of Hong Kong (HPHK) platform, I began wondering if the collections on our platform can also help with family history research at all? Here are some ideas I have on how you might use our platform to pursue your family history. Use our photos of different topics to get them to talk! To build on the brilliant idea of my workshop participant, I’d suggest identifying photographs on our platform around a theme that you’d like your family to discuss. The ‘ topics ’ function on our platform, which sorted all our photographs into 42 different topics of HK history, might be helpful here. Festivals bring family together like no other occasions, so let’s start with our ‘ Festival ’ topic! Let’s use the mid-autumn celebration photos as an example, especially as we have it coming up real soon. You can show your family these photos featuring celebration in the Kowloon Park in 1983. Fig 1: Families playing with lanterns and lighting candles at night during Mid-Autumn Festival, 1983. HPHK Nigel Spry Collection, DM3263/1/6/24 Fig 2: Families playing with lanterns and lighting candles at night during Mid-Autumn Festival, 1983. HPHK Nigel Spry Collection, DM3263/1/6/22 You can first ask them if they think these lanterns from the 1980s looked like the ones they had growing up. You can ask if they played with the candles like the kids here: Fig 3: Children lighting candles during Mid-Autumn Festival at night, 1983: HPHK Nigel Spry Collection, DM3263/1/6/11 You can then follow up and ask them how would they celebrate the festival when they were little, where they would go, with whom, at what time of the night, and what would they do in the park. I tried asking my family these questions before myself – and learnt this properly behaved serious grownup (or so I’d thought my whole life!) was such a rascal! Another useful ‘topic’ for family history discussions is, of course, the photographs labelled ‘ Family Life’ ! These are particularly useful if you’re interested in finding out what their childhood and/or their upbringing are like, as many of them feature children in traditional baby carriers on the back of vendors at work and busy shoppers. Fig 4: A street sellers and a baby in Shek O, 1983. HPHK, Nigel Spry Collection, DM3263-1-1-1-001 Fig 5: A vendor with a baby selling fish at Sheung Shui Market, 1984. HPHK, Nigel Spry Collection, DM3263/1/1/106 Fig 6: Woman with a baby and a street seller in an outdoor wet market in Hong Kong, 1982. HPHK, Nigel Spry Collection, DM3263/1/1/67 You can start with the traditional baby carriers, and ask if you family had ever seen those traditional baby carriers, and whether they’d seen one used in the family before. You can then ask them more about who would use it, whether this was the person that took care of them when they were little, and whether, like the women featured in the photos, their carer would multi-task as they had the children on their back. You can invite them to talk about the other task(s) that their carer would do, which might encourage your family to elaborate further on stories they might otherwise think too trivial to mention but could enrich your understanding of your family’s past. You can also do this with any question(s) you might have with the photos: it really is about using the photos as prompt to encourage them to tell you more about their past. You can also browse our platform by ‘ time periods’ : this is a very useful function for you to identify photographs from a period most relevant to any part of your family history that you hope to get them to talk about. Another good way to spark memorie is to show them photographs of the area(s) that you know they might have frequented. Currently we have some wonderful photographs of different districts in Hong Kong, which you can use to invite your family to identify the location(s) and ask if they used to go there (and if so, for what, with whom, on what occasions, etc.) Fig 7: Dragon dance at Castle Peak Road Yuen Long, 1960s. HPHK, Nigel Spry Collection, DM3263/1/1/31 Fig 8: The Catholic Centre Hong Kong in Central, 1983. HPHK, Nigel Spry Collection, DM3263/1/16/ Fig 8: Couple and families in front of the City Hall High Block, 1982. HPHK, Nigel Spry Collection, DM3263/1/5/2 Besides getting your family to talk, browsing through the photographs on our platform will help you develop a stronger understanding of Hong Kong’s history! This is essential for family history projects about Hong Kong, as it will help you understand how wider historical contexts informed your family’s lives. Do give this a try, and let us know how it goes via this short survey : we would love to hear your experience with the platform! Last but not least, if you have any family photographs from 19 th and 20 th century Hong Kong that you think would be useful for researchers and other family historians like you, do get in touch with us. We would love to have them in our Hong Kong Collections too!
