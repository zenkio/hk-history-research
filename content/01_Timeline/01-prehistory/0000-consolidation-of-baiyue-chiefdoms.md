## Background
Prior to the mid-first millennium BCE, the coastal regions of modern-day Hong Kong and the Pearl River Delta were inhabited by Neolithic and Bronze Age maritime communities. Archaeological findings, such as the geometric-stamped pottery found at **Sham Wan (深灣)** and **Sha Ha (沙下)**, suggest these groups maintained complex seafaring networks. During the **Spring and Autumn (春秋)** and **Warring States (戰國)** periods, external pressures from expanding northern states in the Central Plains began to influence the social organization of the southern coastal peoples.

## What happened
Between approximately **500 BCE** and **250 BCE**, scholars observe a transition from localized tribal villages to larger, more cohesive political units. These groupings are collectively referred to in historical records as the **Baiyue (百越)**, or 'Hundred Yue.' The consolidation process is widely interpreted as a defensive and organizational response to resource competition and the encroaching influence of powerful northern states, such as the **Chu (楚)** and **Yue (越)** kingdoms. While direct records from this era in the Hong Kong region are absent, the emergence of more sophisticated bronze weaponry and fortified settlements suggests the formation of hierarchical chiefdoms capable of mobilizing labor and defense.

## Significance
This era serves as the critical transition between purely archaeological, non-literate societies and the more formalized administrative history that followed the **Qin (秦)** conquest in **214 BCE**. The consolidation of the Baiyue set the cultural and demographic foundation for the region. Whether these groups functioned as a unified state or a loose confederation remains a subject of ongoing historical debate. The period marks the final stage of indigenous autonomy before the integration of the Lingnan region into the broader imperial Chinese sphere.

## Perspectives

- **Classical Chinese Historiography**: Traditional records view the Baiyue as a decentralized, 'barbarian' cultural group whose eventual integration into the Qin administration was an inevitable process of civilizing and unification under imperial rule.
- **Modern Archaeological Scholarship**: Contemporary researchers emphasize the agency and regional complexity of the Baiyue, noting that their social structures were sophisticated responses to maritime trade and local environmental conditions rather than mere remnants of primitive tribes.
