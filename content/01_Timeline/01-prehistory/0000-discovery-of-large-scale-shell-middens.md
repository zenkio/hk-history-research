## Background
During the mid-Holocene, rising sea levels and the stabilization of the coastline created an environment rich in marine life across the archipelago now known as Hong Kong (香港). Archaeological investigations at sites such as **Sham Wan (深灣)** on **Lamma Island (南丫島)** have revealed extensive cultural layers that predate the establishment of formal administrative structures. These early societies transitioned from nomadic hunter-gatherer lifestyles to more sedentary coastal occupation patterns, utilizing the abundance of mollusks and other shellfish available in the intertidal zones.

## What happened
Between **5000 and 3000 BCE**, prehistoric inhabitants gathered vast quantities of marine mollusks, primarily oysters and various gastropods. As these resources were processed and consumed, discarded shells accumulated in layers, forming massive 'shell middens' (貝丘). These deposits often reach several meters in depth and contain not only shell remains but also stone tools, cord-marked pottery shards, and animal bone fragments. The physical scale of these middens implies that groups of people stayed in these coastal locations for extended, or perhaps seasonal, periods to exploit the local marine environment systematically.

## Significance
The accumulation of these middens serves as a critical indicator of a profound dietary shift. The reliance on marine mollusks suggests a sophisticated understanding of local ecology and the development of specialized tools for harvesting and processing resources. While these sites do not provide evidence of writing or state-level bureaucracy, they demonstrate that early coastal societies were well-adapted to the maritime environment. The middens function as 'archaeological archives,' preserving data on the ancient climate, available species, and the technological capabilities of the inhabitants during the Neolithic period.

## Perspectives

- **Archaeological Scholarship**: Contemporary archaeologists view these middens as essential data points for reconstructing the paleo-environment and tracking the migration of Neolithic cultures along the South China coast. There is active debate regarding whether the creation of these middens was a purely localized adaptation or part of a broader cultural movement originating from the Pearl River Delta.
- **Local New Territories Communities**: For some local communities, these prehistoric sites represent a deep ancestral connection to the land and sea, predating colonial narratives. However, perspectives vary on whether these early populations represent direct ancestors or distinct, vanished groups whose cultural practices did not survive into the historical era.
