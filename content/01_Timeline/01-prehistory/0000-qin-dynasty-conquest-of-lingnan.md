## Background
Prior to the late third century BCE, the region encompassing modern Hong Kong and the broader Lingnan area was inhabited by diverse indigenous groups collectively referred to in ancient Chinese texts as the Baiyue (百越) peoples. Archaeological evidence from islands such as Lamma and Cheung Chau indicates complex maritime networks, bronze metallurgy, and neolithic settlements that had developed independently of the northern Central Plains.

## What happened
Following the unification of China, Emperor **Qin Shi Huang** (秦始皇) turned his ambitions southward to secure control over the wealth, trade routes, and strategic depth of the Lingnan region. Around **214 BCE**, after overcoming fierce resistance from local populations, Qin forces successfully subdued the territory. The imperial court established three commanderies to govern the newly annexed southern lands: Guilin (桂林), Xiang (象郡), and Nanhai (南海). Modern Hong Kong fell under the administrative jurisdiction of **Nanhai Commandery** (南海郡), specifically administered through Panyu County (番禺). This military campaign brought an end to the region's prehistoric era by integrating it into the administrative framework of the early Chinese imperial state.

## Significance
The Qin conquest marked a profound turning point in the historical trajectory of the Hong Kong region. It introduced centralized imperial administration, facilitated the initial wave of migration from the north, and integrated the southern coastal fringe into broader Chinese economic and cultural networks. However, the exact nature, depth, and permanence of Qin administrative control over the coastal islands and immediate vicinity of modern Hong Kong remain subjects of ongoing scholarly debate, given the scarcity of direct contemporary textual and material records.

## Perspectives

- **Traditional Chinese Historiography**: Views the conquest as a civilizing and unifying milestone that successfully brought the culturally diverse southern frontier into the fold of the Chinese empire, laying the administrative foundations for all subsequent dynasties.
- **Modern Archaeological and Local Perspective**: Emphasizes that despite formal imperial annexation, indigenous Baiyue populations maintained their distinct maritime cultures and local ways of life with minimal direct interference from northern imperial authorities for centuries.
