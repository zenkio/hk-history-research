## Background

Before the **Han Dynasty** (漢代) expanded its direct control southward, the region encompassing modern-day Hong Kong and the surrounding Pearl River Delta was inhabited by various indigenous communities collectively referred to as the **Baiyue** (百越) or Southern Yue. Following the Qin dynasty's initial conquests, the region formed part of the independent kingdom of **Nanyue** (南越), established by **Zhao Tuo** (趙佗). 

## What happened

In **111 BCE**, during the reign of **Emperor Wu of Han** (漢武帝), imperial forces defeated the Nanyue kingdom. To secure the newly acquired southern frontier and pacify potential rebellions, Han authorities instituted administrative controls over the commanderies established in the region, such as **Nanhai** (南海) and **Hepu** (合浦). Historical records suggest that imperial policy involved both military presence and the forced or incentivized resettlement of portions of the indigenous coastal Yue populations deeper inland or to different controlled areas. While the exact scale and mechanisms of these population movements within the immediate Hong Kong territory remain difficult to quantify from sparse surviving texts, this era saw the establishment of basic administrative infrastructure, evidenced by archaeological finds such as salt-pan operations and early Han-style burials in places like **Tuen Mun** (屯門) and **Lei Cheng Uk** (李鄭屋).

## Significance

The resettlement and administrative integration initiated around **-110** (approximate date) accelerated the incorporation of the coastal frontier into the broader Chinese imperial system. It marked the beginning of centuries of demographic and cultural interaction between incoming Han administrators and settlers and the indigenous Yue inhabitants, laying the groundwork for the region's long-term development.

## Perspectives

- **Traditional Historiography (Qing/Beijing)**: Views the Han dynasty's southward expansion and subsequent population management as a civilizing mission that successfully unified the southern frontier under a benevolent administrative system, bringing Confucian order and economic development to the Yue people.
- **Modern and Critical Scholarship**: Interprets the resettlement policies as an exercise of colonial-style imperial subjugation that forcibly disrupted indigenous societies, dismantled local political structures, and subordinated native populations to the economic and military interests of the northern central government.
