## Background
Prior to the formal integration of the Lingnan region into the Qin Empire in **214 BCE**, the coastal areas of present-day Hong Kong and the Pearl River Delta were inhabited by various Yue (越) peoples. Archaeological evidence, including stone tools and early pottery shards found at sites like Sham Wan (深灣), suggests a society deeply intertwined with the marine environment. These coastal communities relied on the South China Sea for sustenance, fostering a culture where mastery of the water was a fundamental survival skill.

## What happened
Between roughly **300 BCE and 214 BCE**, local Yue shipbuilders demonstrated sophisticated engineering capabilities. Historical accounts, though limited, describe the use of 'bird-beak' prows and high-speed, shallow-draft vessels capable of navigating complex coastal waters. Unlike the bulky, deep-sea vessels favored by northern powers of the time, these Yue craft were designed for maneuverability in marshes, estuaries, and along the rocky coastlines of the Hong Kong archipelago. These developments allowed the Yue to command coastal trade routes, effectively controlling the movement of goods and people long before the construction of fixed terrestrial infrastructure.

## Significance
This era of maritime innovation established the Pearl River Delta as a significant economic and strategic zone. The mobility afforded by these vessels enabled the Yue to maintain a distinct, decentralized social structure that resisted total assimilation by the expanding northern dynasties. For the region's development, these techniques laid the foundation for the area’s subsequent role as a gateway for maritime trade. While the Qin conquest introduced formal administration, the underlying maritime culture established by the early Yue shipbuilders persisted, shaping the region's long-term reliance on the sea.

## Perspectives

- **Colonial-era Historiography**: Traditional British-era accounts often portrayed these early inhabitants as primitive, focusing primarily on their eventual suppression by the Qin forces as the beginning of 'civilized' history in the region.
- **Contemporary Academic/Localist**: Modern scholars and local historians frequently argue that the Yue possessed a highly developed, indigenous maritime civilization that existed independently of northern dynasties, emphasizing technological self-reliance and cultural continuity.
