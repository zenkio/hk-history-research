## Background
Between **800 BCE** and **300 BCE**, the coastal regions of the Pearl River Delta, including the territory now known as Hong Kong, experienced a transformation in ceramic production. Archaeologists identify this period by the emergence of pottery decorated with stamped geometric motifs—such as chevrons, circles, and net patterns—which superseded earlier corded-ware traditions. This stylistic shift is generally associated with the expansion of the **Baiyue** (百越) peoples throughout southern China.

## What happened
The spread of geometric pottery was not an isolated technological event but a regional phenomenon. Excavations at sites like **Sham Wan** (深灣) and **Sha Ha** (沙下) have revealed large quantities of these stamped sherds, indicating that the technology was pervasive across local settlements. The production process involved the use of carved paddles to imprint patterns onto the clay before firing. While the exact migration or diffusion patterns remain a subject of active research, the consistency in design suggests a shared cultural milieu that connected the inhabitants of Hong Kong to the broader riverine societies of the Lingnan region. Trade connectivity likely facilitated the movement of these cultural markers along the complex estuarine waterways of the Pearl River.

## Significance
The proliferation of geometric pottery is widely interpreted by scholars as evidence of increasing social complexity and regional integration. The transition reflects a move toward more settled lifestyles capable of supporting specialized craft production. However, it is uncertain whether this transition occurred through the rapid migration of new ethnic groups or through the gradual acculturation of existing populations. The geometric styles serve as a vital marker for archaeologists attempting to reconstruct the socio-economic ties between prehistoric Hong Kong and the mainland during the **Zhou Dynasty** (周朝) period. By examining the chemical composition of these ceramics, researchers hope to confirm the extent of local production versus external trade, though data remains incomplete.

## Perspectives

- **Mainland Academic Perspective**: Many scholars associated with the Beijing historical school view the geometric pottery culture as a clear indicator of the expansion of the Huaxia cultural sphere into the southern frontiers. They emphasize the integration of these regional groups into the wider administrative and cultural orbit of the central Chinese states.
- **Local Hong Kong Archaeological Perspective**: Local researchers often place greater emphasis on the indigenous maritime adaptations of the Pearl River people, arguing that the pottery reflects a unique, distinct coastal development rather than purely a derivative of northern cultural expansion.
