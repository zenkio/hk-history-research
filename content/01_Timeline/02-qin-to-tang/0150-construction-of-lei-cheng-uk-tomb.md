## Background
During the Qin (秦) and Han (漢) dynasties, the territory comprising modern Hong Kong was integrated into the imperial administration, notably under the commandery of Nanhai (南海郡) and specifically Panyu County (番禺縣). While written texts from this early period are sparse regarding the coastal frontiers, archaeological findings indicate that the region experienced a steady influx of settlers from the Central Plains, alongside indigenous populations.

## What happened
Around **150** CE, during the Eastern Han (東漢) dynasty, a tomb was constructed at the site of modern-day Lei Cheng Uk (李鄭屋). Built using hollow and wedge-shaped stamped bricks—some bearing inscriptions such as "Ji Bu Zuo" (吉不咎)—the structure features a distinctive multi-chambered layout, typically comprising a passage, an antechamber, and side chambers. The tomb contained a variety of grave goods, including bronze mirrors, bells, bowls, and pottery models of houses and grain silos, suggesting the occupant was a person of notable wealth and local status.

## Significance
The discovery of the **Lei Cheng Uk Han Tomb** (李鄭屋漢墓) in **1955** during leveling work for a resettlement estate provided tangible, physical evidence of Han administrative presence and wealthy settlers in the region. It remains one of the most significant archaeological discoveries in Hong Kong, illuminating the material culture and burial practices of the southern frontier during the early imperial era.

## Perspectives

- **Later scholarship**: Modern archaeologists and historians generally view the tomb as evidence of the southward expansion of Han culture and administrative reach, noting the strong stylistic similarities between the tomb's architecture and those found in Guangdong and other parts of southern China.
- **Local Hong Kong perspective**: For local heritage advocates and the public, the tomb serves as a vital physical anchor for Hong Kong's deep historical roots, demonstrating that the territory was an integrated part of Chinese civilization nearly two millennia ago.
