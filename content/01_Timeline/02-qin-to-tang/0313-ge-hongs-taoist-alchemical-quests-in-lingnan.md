## Background
During the Jin dynasty, the southern frontier region of Lingnan was viewed by the central imperial court as a remote, malaria-prone periphery inhabited by diverse indigenous populations. Despite its reputation, the region offered isolation and a wealth of rare flora and minerals that attracted scholars of esoteric traditions. **Ge Hong** (葛洪), a prominent Taoist thinker, physician, and alchemist, sought out this environment to escape political instability in the north and to further his research into longevity and elixirs.

## What happened
Around **313**, historical tradition places **Ge Hong** in the Lingnan area, where he composed significant portions of his seminal work, the *Baopuzi* (抱朴子). According to local lore and later gazetteers, he spent time residing and practicing alchemy in the hills of the region, including areas corresponding to modern-day Hong Kong and the New Territories. He reportedly climbed local peaks to gather medicinal herbs, commune with nature, and set up furnaces for alchemical experimentation. His presence transformed certain hills and caves into sites of spiritual significance, attracting local disciples and hermits seeking medical knowledge and spiritual retreat.

## Significance
**Ge Hong**'s activities cemented the association of Hong Kong's topography with Taoist asceticism and traditional Chinese medicine. While direct archaeological evidence linking specific artifacts to **Ge Hong** himself remains scarce, his legacy permeated the region's cultural landscape. Later generations of Tao practitioners and hermits were drawn to the territory's hills, establishing temples and shrines—such as those on **Lantau Island** (大嶼山)—that tied their lineage back to early southern Taoist traditions. His synthesis of Confucian statecraft, Taoist mysticism, and pharmacology helped integrate the Lingnan frontier into the broader intellectual history of imperial China.

## Perspectives

- **Local Hong Kong and New Territories Communities**: Local oral traditions and temple histories proudly claim Ge Hong's direct presence, linking specific geographical features like Taoist caves and peaks to his physical quest for immortality. These communities view him as a foundational cultural figure who brought high Chinese civilization and esoteric knowledge to the early southern frontier.
- **Modern Scholarship**: Historians and textual scholars approach the accounts of Ge Hong's exact itinerary in Hong Kong with caution, noting that much of the local folklore was compiled centuries later during the Ming and Qing dynasties. While his general residency in Lingnan is well-documented, the specific localization of his activities in modern-day Hong Kong is seen as part of a later tradition of place-branding and the localization of Taoist hagiography.
