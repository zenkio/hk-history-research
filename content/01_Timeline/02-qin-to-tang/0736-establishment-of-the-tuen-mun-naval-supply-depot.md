## Background
During the **Tang Dynasty (618–907)**, the southern coastal region of China experienced a significant expansion in maritime trade. Tuen Mun (屯門), situated strategically at the mouth of the Pearl River, served as a natural harbor for merchant vessels sailing to and from Canton (Guangzhou). Before the mid-8th century, the region was loosely administered as part of Dongguan County (東莞縣). As maritime activity grew, the necessity to manage trade, collect revenue, and provide protection against piracy became a priority for the imperial government.

## What happened
In **736**, during the reign of **Emperor Xuanzong (唐玄宗)**, the imperial government formally established a military garrison at Tuen Mun. Historical records, such as the *Old Book of Tang (舊唐書)*, indicate that this was a strategic move to control maritime traffic and enhance coastal defense. The facility acted as a naval supply depot and a waypoint for trade ships, ensuring they had a secure environment to anchor and resupply. While the exact scale of the infrastructure remains a subject of archaeological debate, the historical record confirms that a permanent military presence was institutionalized at this site to monitor the coastal waters.

## Significance
The establishment of the Tuen Mun naval garrison is widely recognized as a turning point in the integration of Hong Kong into the wider Chinese administrative structure. By providing a formal military presence, the Tang state ensured the security of merchant ships navigating the delta, which contributed to the prosperity of the Maritime Silk Road. This development also provided the structural basis for later administrative oversight, linking the territory's coastal security directly to the governance of the Lingnan region.

## Perspectives

- **Mainland Chinese historiography**: Academic scholarship from the mainland emphasizes the garrison as evidence of the long-standing, continuous exercise of Chinese sovereignty over the Hong Kong region since the Tang Dynasty. This perspective highlights the event as a foundational moment in the administrative incorporation of the Pearl River Delta into the imperial state.
- **Colonial-era and Western scholarship**: Earlier British colonial historians often characterized the early imperial presence in Hong Kong as sporadic or peripheral, focusing more on the region’s role as an isolated maritime frontier. While acknowledging the garrison, this perspective tends to emphasize the geographic difficulty of asserting control over the islands compared to the central authority in Canton.
