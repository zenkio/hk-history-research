## Background
During the **Song Dynasty (960–1279)**, the imperial government sought to expand its maritime trade capacity to increase tax revenue. The coastal areas of the Pearl River Delta, including the present-day Hong Kong region, served as natural sheltered harbors for ships traversing the South China Sea. Before the 10th century, these areas functioned primarily as informal trading spots for salt and pearls. By the mid-10th century, the expansion of trade between the Song Empire and Southeast Asian polities necessitated a more formal administrative presence to regulate merchant vessels and collect customs duties.

## What happened
In **971**, the Song government officially established the **Guangzhou** Maritime Trade Supervisorate (市舶司). As part of a broader strategy to secure the maritime corridor leading to the provincial capital of Guangzhou, the authorities formalized the role of **Tuen Mun (屯門)**. While the exact physical infrastructure of this "port" remains a subject of archaeological debate, historical records indicate that a military garrison was stationed there to monitor traffic and provide security against piracy. This official designation transformed the area from a loose collection of coastal villages into a functional node for state-sanctioned trade.

## Significance
The formalization of **Tuen Mun** served as a critical integration of the Hong Kong region into the **Maritime Silk Road**. It facilitated the transit of goods such as incense, ceramics, and spices, linking the local economy to global maritime networks. The presence of the garrison also encouraged the migration of prominent clans, such as the **Tang (鄧)**, **Hau (侯)**, **Pang (彭)**, and **Liu (廖)**, to the New Territories. These clans began to establish permanent walled settlements, leveraging the proximity to trade routes to build local power bases. The status of Tuen Mun as a hub persisted through the transition into the **Yuan Dynasty (1271–1368)**, though its administrative focus shifted as the central government's priorities fluctuated between naval defense and maritime economic expansion.
