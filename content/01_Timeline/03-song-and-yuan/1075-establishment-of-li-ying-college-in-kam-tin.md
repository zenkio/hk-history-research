## Background
During the Northern Song dynasty (**960–1127**), the imperial court actively promoted Confucian education and expanded the imperial examination system to peripheral regions of the empire. As migration from the Central Plains brought prominent clans southward into the Pearl River Delta, local lineages sought to integrate themselves into the broader imperial scholastic and bureaucratic networks. Establishing formal study halls became a crucial method for these clans to cultivate literacy, social status, and political influence.

## What happened
Around **1075**, members of the **Tang** (鄧) clan, who had settled in the fertile lands of Kam Tin, are traditionally credited with founding **Li-Ying College** (力瀛書院). This institution served as the region's first formal study hall. The college provided instruction in Confucian classics, history, and poetry, preparing young lineage members for the imperial civil service examinations. While physical archaeological remains of the original **1075** structure are virtually nonexistent, oral traditions and later lineage genealogies mark this establishment as a foundational moment for formal scholarship in the territory.

## Significance
The founding of Li-Ying College represented a pivotal shift in the cultural landscape of the region, transitioning it from a peripheral frontier into a participant in imperial Chinese literati culture. By institutionalizing Confucian education, the college laid the groundwork for local clans to achieve scholarly degrees and bureaucratic office, which in turn reinforced their socioeconomic dominance in the New Territories for centuries to come. It established a precedent for lineage-sponsored education that would be replicated by other prominent clans across the region during the late imperial era.

## Perspectives

- **Local New Territories Lineage Communities**: Descendants and local historians view the founding of Li-Ying College as a definitive proof of their ancestors' early cultural sophistication and deep integration into mainstream Chinese civilization during the Northern Song dynasty.
- **Modern Historiography**: Academic scholars note that while lineage genealogies trace the college's origin to the eleventh century, exact contemporary documentation is scarce, suggesting that some details may reflect later Ming and Qing dynasty efforts by the Tang clan to aggrandize their lineage history.
