## Background
During the Northern Song dynasty (**960–1127**), the imperial court encouraged the migration of Han Chinese families to the southern frontier regions to develop agriculture, secure borders, and integrate local populations into the empire's administrative and economic fabric. The coastal areas and fertile river valleys of present-day Hong Kong gradually attracted mainland families seeking new opportunities away from northern conflicts.

## What happened
Around **1075**, **Tang Fu-hip** (鄧符協), a scholar-official and descendant of the prominent northern Tang lineage, crossed into the region and chose to settle in Kam Tin (錦田), historically known as Shum Tin (岑田). Oral tradition and clan genealogies suggest he acquired land, established agricultural settlements, and founded private academies to promote Confucian education. This initial foothold formed the epicenter of the Tang clan's territorial expansion throughout the New Territories over subsequent centuries.

## Significance
The settlement of the Tang clan marked a crucial turning point in the demographic and socio-political history of the region. By combining ancestral halls, ritual networks, fortified walled villages, and academic institutions, the Tangs laid the foundation for the lineage system that defined rural society. Their success in clearing land, managing water resources, and dominating local trade established the framework for the **Five Great Clans** (五大氏族) of Hong Kong, shaping land tenure patterns that persisted well into the British colonial era.

## Perspectives

- **Local New Territories Communities**: Genealogical records and oral histories preserved by the Tang clan emphasize ancestral pioneering, scholarly pedigree, and continuous ancestral stewardship of the land for nearly a millennium, reinforcing their traditional leadership role in regional society.
- **Later Scholarship**: Modern historians analyze the migration through the lens of socioeconomic adaptation, noting how northern lineages leveraged imperial exam success, marriage alliances, and control over fertile alluvial plains to build enduring local hegemony.
