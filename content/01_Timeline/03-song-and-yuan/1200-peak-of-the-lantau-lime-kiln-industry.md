## Background
During the **Song** dynasty (**宋朝**), the economy of the Pearl River Delta experienced significant growth, driving high demand for building materials. Shells and limestone found along the coast of **Lantau Island** (**大嶼山**) served as the primary raw materials for producing lime, which was vital for various industries including construction, agriculture, and maritime trade.

## What happened
Around **1200**, coastal kilns operating across several sites on Lantau Island reached their peak production capacity. Local artisans gathered oyster shells and coral, burning them in stone-lined kilns to produce calcium oxide, commonly known as quicklime. This product was packed and shipped via maritime routes to markets throughout the delta to support growing urban and rural settlements.

## Significance
The lime-burning industry highlights the early commercial integration of the Hong Kong region into the broader economic networks of the Song empire. The widespread distribution of these kilns demonstrates that the coastal areas were not merely peripheral fishing communities, but active participants in regional manufacturing and trade.
