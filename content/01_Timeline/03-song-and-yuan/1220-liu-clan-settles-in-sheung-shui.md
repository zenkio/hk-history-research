## Background
During the Song dynasty (**960–1279**), the population of the region experienced gradual growth driven by migration from the Central Plains of China to the southern frontier. Northern Hong Kong, including areas like Sheung Shui (上水), began to see more permanent settlement as families sought arable land and security away from northern conflicts.

## What happened
Around **c. 1220**, members of the Liu lineage (**廖氏**), originating from Jiangxi province via Fujian and Guangdong, are traditionally understood to have settled in the Sheung Shui area. Over time, the clan cleared land, established agricultural systems, and constructed permanent settlements. This migration contributed to the broader pattern of powerful clans establishing localized networks of power, ancestral halls, and fortified villages across the New Territories.

## Significance
The settlement of the Liu clan is a key example of how major lineages shaped the cultural, economic, and social landscape of early Hong Kong. By transforming marshlands and lower-yielding areas into productive agricultural fields, the Liu family laid the groundwork for a long-standing local community that would influence regional governance, land tenure, and ancestral traditions for centuries.

## Perspectives

- **Local New Territories Communities**: For indigenous villagers and the Liu lineage itself, the settlement is remembered through ancestral genealogies and clan history as a foundational act of establishing ancestral roots and rightful stewardship over the land.
