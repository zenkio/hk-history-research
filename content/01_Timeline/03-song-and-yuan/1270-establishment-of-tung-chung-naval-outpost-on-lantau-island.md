## Background
During the Southern Song dynasty (**960–1279**), the maritime trade of the Pearl River estuary grew substantially, connecting the region to broader Southeast Asian and Indian Ocean trade networks. However, this economic prosperity also attracted significant maritime violence. By the mid-to-late **13th century**, piracy and armed smuggling had become rampant along the southern coast of Guangdong, threatening the lucrative salt trade and merchant shipping vital to the imperial exchequer.

## What happened
Between **1270 and 1275**, as the Mongol threat pressed heavily from the north and coastal disorder grew, the Song administration moved to fortify its southern maritime periphery. Historical and archaeological indications suggest that a military and naval outpost was established at Tung Chung (東涌) on Lantau Island (**大嶼山**). This installation was designed to project imperial naval power, protect commercial vessels navigating the Pearl River approaches, and maintain order over the salt-producing communities operating in the surrounding waters and coastal flats.

## Significance
The establishment of the Tung Chung outpost marks an important administrative and military integration of the outlying islands into the imperial defense network of the Song dynasty. While the outpost could not ultimately save the dynasty from its final collapse during the Mongol conquest, it highlights the strategic maritime importance that the Hong Kong region held centuries before it emerged as a modern entrepôt.

## Perspectives

- **Local New Territories Communities**: Local lineage traditions and oral histories in the region often connect the early settlement and defense of Lantau Island to the turbulent final decades of the Song dynasty, viewing the era as a foundational period of migration and localized clan survival.
- **Imperial Chinese Historiography**: Traditional dynastic records and later Qing (清) period gazetteers frame the establishment of coastal outposts as necessary measures of dynastic governance to suppress coastal rebellion, curb illegal salt-panning, and restore imperial order.
