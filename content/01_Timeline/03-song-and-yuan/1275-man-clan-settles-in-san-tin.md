## Background
During the late Southern Song dynasty (960–1279), the southward migration of major clans from the Central Plains of China fundamentally shaped the demographics of the Pearl River Delta. Amidst the encroaching Mongol forces of the Yuan dynasty, lineage groups sought refuge in coastal and island regions of what is now Hong Kong. Among these was the **Man clan** (文氏), whose members traced their ancestry to northern China before settling in Guangdong and subsequently moving into the San Tin area.

## What happened
Around **c. 1275**, branches of the Man clan established permanent villages in San Tin, situated in the northwestern part of the New Territories. This migration was closely tied to the broader loyalist movement led by **Wen Tianxiang** (文天祥), a celebrated statesman and general who resisted the Mongol conquest. While Wen Tianxiang famously fought to preserve the Song dynasty, his relatives and lineage members consolidated their presence in the region, acquiring land, establishing agricultural foundations, and building ancestral halls that would anchor their community for centuries.

## Significance
The arrival and permanent settlement of the Man clan in San Tin completed a crucial phase in local history: the establishment of the **Five Great Indigenous Clans** (新界五大氏族) of the New Territories, which also included the Tang, Hau, Pang, and Liu lineages. These clans developed sophisticated walled villages, practiced ancestral worship, and maintained localized autonomy through changing dynastic landscapes. The legacy of the Man clan endures in San Tin through heritage structures such as the **Tai Fu Tai** (大夫第) mansion, offering physical testimony to the clan's later imperial bureaucracy success and regional prominence.
