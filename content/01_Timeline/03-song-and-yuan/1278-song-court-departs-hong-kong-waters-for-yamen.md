## Background
By the late 1270s, the invading Mongol forces of the Yuan dynasty had pushed the remnants of the Southern Song (南宋) court deep into the southern coastal regions. Following the fall of the capital Lin'an (臨安), loyalist officials escorted the young boy-emperors southward. After moving through various coastal outposts, the imperial entourage sought refuge in the territory encompassing modern-day Hong Kong, temporarily establishing bases in areas such as Kowloon and the New Territories.

## What happened
In **June 1278**, facing relentless naval pressure and the rapid advance of Yuan forces under General **Bayandur** (伯顏都兒 / 拜延篤 - note: specific Mongol commanders' movements in the immediate local theater are subject to textual nuances), the Song loyalist leaders, notably **Wen Tianxiang** (文天祥) and **Lu Xiufu** (陸秀夫), alongside the imperial princes, realized that the local waters no longer offered secure defense. The court and its remaining fleet evacuated their temporary anchorages in Hong Kong waters, embarking on a westward voyage toward Xinhui (新會) in Guangdong province. This movement set the stage for the catastrophic naval battle at Yamen (崖門) the following year, which marked the definitive end of the Song dynasty.

## Significance
The departure from Hong Kong waters marked the final chapter of the Song court's physical presence in the region, transforming local geography into enduring symbols of loyalty and tragic resistance. The voyage underscored the strategic importance of the Pearl River Delta's complex coastline and islands as temporary refuges for a collapsing maritime empire.
