## Background

Pearl harvesting in the waters of southern China, particularly in Tai Po Hoi (大埔海) or Tolo Harbour, had a long history dating back centuries before the Mongol conquest. During the Song and Yuan dynasties, pearls gathered from these southern commanderies were prized as luxury goods for the imperial court and elite consumers. The local population, including indigenous groups and coastal villagers, were often conscripted or coerced into diving for these freshwater and saltwater pearls under dangerous and grueling conditions.

## What happened

By **1295**, the unrelenting demand for pearls had taken a catastrophic toll on the local diving population. Contemporary records and later historical compilations indicate that the physical hazards of deep-water diving—combined with exhaustion, cold, and pressure-related illnesses—led to extremely high mortality rates among the divers. Facing severe depopulation, local distress, and the practical collapse of the harvesting labor force, the Yuan (元朝) authorities issued a temporary suspension of official pearl harvesting activities in Tai Po Hoi. This intervention marked a rare instance where imperial resource extraction was reined in due to unsustainable human loss.

## Significance

The suspension of the Tai Po pearl harvest provides a vital window into the socioeconomic pressures experienced by the coastal regions of modern-day Hong Kong during the Mongol period. It illustrates the tense relationship between peripheral coastal communities and the centralized imperial state. While the ban was intended to offer relief, the pearl industry experienced fluctuating fortunes in subsequent centuries, leaving a lasting legacy in local folklore and the economic history of the New Territories.

## Perspectives

- **Imperial Yuan Administration**: From the perspective of the central government, the suspension was viewed as a pragmatic administrative adjustment to preserve the local labor base and maintain social stability after unsustainable losses among producers of a valuable state commodity.
- **Local Divers and Indigenous Communities**: For the coastal inhabitants and divers, the harvesting operations were an oppressive imposition that endangered lives, making the eventual suspension a necessary reprieve from a deadly economic burden.
