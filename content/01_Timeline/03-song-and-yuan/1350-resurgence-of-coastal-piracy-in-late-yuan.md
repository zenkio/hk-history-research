## Background
During the later years of the Yuan dynasty (元朝) around **c. 1350**, central authority experienced a significant decline, weakening administrative and military control over the southern coastal peripheries. The waterways and jagged coastline surrounding the modern Hong Kong region—including Tuen Mun (屯門) and the Pearl River Delta—offered numerous hidden bays and islands that were ideal for maritime activities and shelter. Historical records suggest that economic pressures, political instability, and the breakdown of official maritime monopolies contributed to an environment where unauthorized seafaring and armed maritime groups could flourish.

## What happened
Armed maritime groups and pirate fleets expanded their operations throughout the waters of Guangdong (廣東), leveraging the weakened state of Yuan naval patrols. These groups targeted merchant shipping, coastal salt-producing communities, and vulnerable villages, severely disrupting the regional economy and maritime trade routes connecting southern China to Southeast Asia. While comprehensive tactical details remain scarce due to the fragmentary nature of surviving local records, contemporary accounts indicate that local populations faced mounting insecurity, prompting some communities to organize their own localized defenses or seek refuge further inland.

## Significance
This mid-14th-century surge in piracy illustrates the fragility of late Yuan imperial control at the local level and highlights the historical importance of Hong Kong's geography as a maritime frontier. The disruption caused during this period paved the way for subsequent Ming dynasty (明朝) maritime policies, including early attempts at naval suppression and later coastal evacuation edicts aimed at curbing illicit maritime networks in the region.

## Perspectives

- **Qing and dynastic historiography**: Imperial records often frame the late Yuan and early Ming piracy waves through the lens of social disorder and rebellion, emphasizing the restoration of imperial order and the suppression of lawless elements along the southern frontier.
- **Local New Territories lineages and communities**: Oral traditions and local genealogies often remember this era of instability as a critical period that forced ancestral clans to strengthen internal village fortifications, establish mutual-aid alliances, and adapt their settlement patterns for better security.
