## Background
During the **Song** and **Yuan** dynasties (**宋、元朝**), the coastal regions of southern China experienced a significant expansion in maritime commerce. As maritime technology improved and government policies fluctuated between restriction and encouragement of overseas trade, the waters around the Pearl River Delta became critical arteries for merchants. Located strategically at the estuary, **Tuen Mun** (**屯門**) emerged as a key geographical node for ships navigating between the South China Sea and Guangzhou (**廣州**).

## What happened
Between **1350** and **1360**, **Tuen Mun** developed into a vibrant port of call for international merchant vessels. Historical indicators suggest that the location served as a vital anchorage where traders could secure fresh water, provision their ships, and wait for favorable monsoon winds. While specific records detailing the daily volume of commerce during this exact decade remain scarce, archaeological finds and general trade patterns from the late Yuan period point to active exchange involving ceramics, salt, and other regional commodities. The area also attracted naval patrols and administrative attention from the imperial government, which sought to regulate maritime traffic and counter piracy.

## Significance

This period laid the foundations for subsequent centuries of coastal development, maritime defense operations, and cross-border interactions in the Pearl River Delta.
