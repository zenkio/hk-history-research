## Background
During the mid-to-late Ming dynasty, the coastal regions of southern China faced persistent security challenges from armed maritime groups operating outside imperial control. These groups engaged in trade, smuggling, and raiding along the estuaries and islands of Guangdong. In **1572**, the prominent pirate commander **Lin Daoqian** (林道乾), pressured by imperial forces in Fujian and eastern Guangdong, shifted his operations further down the coast toward the Pearl River Delta.

## What happened
**Lin Daoqian** (林道乾) selected **Lantau Island** (大嶼山), specifically areas such as **Tai O** (大澳), as a temporary stronghold and operational base. From this island redoubt, his fleet launched coordinated raids against coastal settlements, salt pans, and passing merchant vessels. The local inhabitants, primarily fisherfolk and farmers living far from the administrative centers of Xin'an County (新安縣), had little defense against the well-armed raiding parties. Ming authorities mobilized regional naval patrols to confront the incursion, leading to armed engagements around the Pearl River estuary as the imperial forces attempted to clear the maritime approaches.

## Significance
The events of 1572 highlighted the severe vulnerability of the delta's coastal and island communities to maritime violence. While the Ming court ultimately pressured **Lin Daoqian** (林道乾) to move his fleet further south toward Southeast Asia, the raids demonstrated the urgent need for enhanced coastal defense infrastructure. This episode contributed to a growing administrative awareness that eventually led to increased military oversight and fortified outposts in the region during the late sixteenth century.
