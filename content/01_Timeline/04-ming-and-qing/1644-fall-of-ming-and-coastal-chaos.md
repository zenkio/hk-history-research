## Background

By the early **17th century**, the coastal areas of present-day Hong Kong were already plagued by piracy and weak imperial administration. When the Ming dynasty (大明) fell to the Manchu-led Qing dynasty (大清) in **1644**, the Pearl River Delta became a contested zone. While northern and central China quickly fell under Qing control, the southern coastal regions offered natural geographical advantages for resistance movements.

## What happened

Following the fall of Beijing, various **Southern Ming** (南明) loyalist regimes attempted to rally support across Guangdong province. Local strongmen, naval commanders, and pirate confederations capitalized on the power vacuum. Maritime leaders such as **Zheng Chenggong** (鄭成功) and other regional commanders utilized the intricate network of islands, deep-water bays, and sheltered inlets around Hong Kong—including modern-day **Tuen Mun** (屯門) and **Lantau Island** (大嶼山)—as operational bases, supply routes, and refuges. 

Clashes between Qing imperial forces and loyalist-pirate coalitions resulted in severe disruptions to local trade and agriculture. Because the coastal population often provided logistical support, intelligence, or direct manpower to the anti-Qing resistance, the nascent Qing court viewed the inhabitants of the Hong Kong region with deep suspicion. This prolonged instability set the stage for even more draconian imperial interventions later in the century, notably the forced depopulation policies of the Great Clearance.

## Significance

The tumultuous transition from Ming to Qing exposed the extreme vulnerability of Hong Kong's coastal communities to shifts in continental politics. It demonstrated how local geography could be leveraged by maritime rebels against continental empires, while also illustrating the devastating human cost borne by local villagers caught in the crossfire of dynastic warfare.

## Perspectives

- **Qing Imperial Administration**: Viewed the coastal populations of Guangdong and the Hong Kong region as lawless, untrustworthy, and deeply sympathetic to rebels, justifying harsh military pacification and border controls.
- **Local New Territories Communities**: Experienced the transition primarily as a period of extreme existential peril, where survival required navigating the competing demands of imperial tax collectors, loyalist navies, and marauding pirates.
- **Southern Ming Loyalists**: Regarded the islands and waters of the Pearl River Delta as a vital strategic frontier and legitimate staging ground to restore native Chinese rule against Manchu conquest.
