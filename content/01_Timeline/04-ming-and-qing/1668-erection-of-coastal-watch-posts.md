## Background

During the early **Qing dynasty** (大清), the newly established Man-chu regime faced persistent threats from loyalist forces under **Zheng Chenggong** (鄭成功), who operated from Taiwan and along the southeastern coast of China. To cut off local supplies and intelligence to these maritime resistance forces, the imperial court issued the drastic **Great Clearance** (遷界令) beginning in **1661**. This policy forced coastal populations in Guangdong and other southern provinces to evacuate inland by tens of li. By **1668**, after years of severe depopulation and administrative strain, the Qing government adjusted its strategy to secure the newly established frontier, leading to the formal construction of military observation posts along the restricted perimeter near modern Sha Tau Kok and Shenzhen.

## What happened

In **1668**, following a memorial by Guangdong governor **Wang Lai** (王来) and other officials requesting the relaxation and better defense of the border, the Qing military authorities erected a network of watch posts (dun-tai 墩台), signal towers, and defensive passes. These installations were strategically placed along the demarcation line of the evacuation boundary to monitor maritime traffic, prevent unauthorized return of the displaced villagers, and intercept anti-Qing elements. Troops were deployed to these outposts to maintain a permanent state of readiness, using smoke and fire signals by day and night to communicate across the rugged terrain of the Xin'an (新安) county coastline.

## Significance

The erection of these coastal watch posts marked a transition from total abandonment to militarized border control during the early Qing period. While the watch posts helped stabilize the region for the eventual repatriation of local villagers following the official rescission of the evacuation order in **1669**, they also cemented state control over a previously peripheral coastal zone. The physical infrastructure altered the local landscape and laid early foundations for administrative and military grid systems in the borderlands between Xin'an county and Mirs Bay.

## Perspectives

- **Qing Imperial Administration**: From the perspective of Beijing and provincial authorities, the watch posts were essential defensive necessities required to suppress sedition, secure maritime borders, and enforce imperial sovereignty against external loyalist threats.
- **Local New Territories Communities**: For the indigenous lineages and displaced villagers, the watch posts and the broader clearance policies represented immense suffering, loss of ancestral lands, and heavy state intrusion that disrupted centuries of maritime and agricultural life.
