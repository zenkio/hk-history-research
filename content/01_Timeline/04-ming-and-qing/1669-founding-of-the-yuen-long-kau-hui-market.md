## Background
In the early years of the Qing dynasty, the coastal regions of Guangdong experienced severe disruption due to the Great Clearance (遷界令) enforced between **1661** and **1669**. This draconian imperial policy forced residents along the coastline to evacuate inland to counter anti-Qing resistance led by Zheng Chenggong (鄭成功). Once the coastal ban was lifted in **1669**, the imperial government encouraged repopulation and the restoration of agriculture and trade. In the northwestern New Territories, the Tang lineage (鄧氏), one of the prominent early clans of the region, sought to re-establish their local economic dominance and support the returning agrarian population.

## What happened
To facilitate the exchange of local farm produce, fish, and imported goods, the Tang clan founded the Yuen Long Kau Hui market (元朗舊墟), historically known simply as Yuen Long Hui (元朗墟), around **1669**. Situated near the Shan Pui River (山貝河), the market benefited from water transport links that connected local villages to wider regional trade networks. Over subsequent decades, the market grew into a vital commercial hub featuring structured streets, ancestral halls, and specialized shops, serving as the economic center for the surrounding inhabitants of Kam Tin (錦田) and other settlements.

## Significance
The establishment of the Yuen Long Kau Hui illustrates how local elite lineages organized and revived regional economies in the wake of state-mandated displacement during the early Qing period. While later superseded by the development of the modern Yuen Long New Town and the newer market areas established in the 20th century, the old market remains a significant landmark of traditional New Territories settlement history, customary land use, and indigenous lineage governance.

## Perspectives

- **Qing Imperial Administration**: From the perspective of the imperial government, the relaxation of the coastal clearance and the subsequent authorization of local markets were strategic measures to restore agricultural output, secure tax revenues, and pacify the newly re-established frontier populations.
- **Local New Territories Lineages**: For powerful clans such as the Tangs, the founding of the market was crucial for reasserting territorial control, consolidating economic power, and providing essential commercial infrastructure for returning kin and tenant farmers after years of displacement.
