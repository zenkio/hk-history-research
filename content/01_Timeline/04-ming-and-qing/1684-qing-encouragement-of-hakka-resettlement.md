## Background

Following the establishment of the Qing dynasty (大清), the imperial court faced persistent loyalist resistance led by **Koxinga** (鄭成功) operating from Taiwan. To sever local supply lines and maritime communications, the Kangxi Emperor (康熙帝) instituted the Great Clearance (遷界令) in **1661**. This draconian policy forced the total evacuation of coastal populations along the Guangdong (廣東) littoral, including the area comprising modern-day Hong Kong, and burned villages and farmlands. By **1669**, with the Ming loyalist threat neutralized, the Qing court permitted residents to return. However, due to the immense suffering and displacement, few original inhabitants came back, leaving vast tracts of fertile land abandoned and uncultivated.

## What happened

To restore agricultural productivity and tax revenues in the newly secured territories, the Qing government issued relocation decrees in **1684** offering incentives for inland settlers to move southward. Among those who responded were numerous Hakka (客家) families originating from interior provinces such as Fujian (福建), Jiangxi (江西), and inland Guangdong. These migrants settled into the abandoned valleys and uplands of the Sun On County (新安縣) region. Unlike the earlier Punti (本地) inhabitants who typically occupied fertile lowland river plains and coastal flatlands, the newly arrived Hakka communities cultivated the higher, less accessible hillside terrain, introducing terrace farming and expanding the overall agricultural footprint of the territory.

## Significance

The resettlement initiative of **1684** permanently altered the ethnic and cultural composition of Hong Kong. It established a dual-lineage societal structure characterized by a persistent division and occasional friction between the older Punti settlers and the newly arrived Hakka communities, which lasted well into the British colonial period. The resilient Hakka clan networks, fortified villages, and distinct dialect preserved through this migration laid deep cultural foundations across the New Territories that remain visible in local heritage today.

## Perspectives

- **Qing / Beijing Official Perspective**: From the perspective of the imperial court, the resettlement was a pragmatic and successful administrative measure to restore social order, productive land use, and tax collection in a previously depopulated frontier zone.
- **Local New Territories Communities Perspective**: For indigenous lineage groups, the influx of Hakka settlers represented both a necessary economic revitalization and a source of prolonged land-use competition, which historically manifested in territorial disputes between Punti and Hakka villages.
- **Later Scholarship**: Modern historians view the 1684 decrees as a watershed moment that transformed Hong Kong's demographics from a homogenous coastal fishing and farming society into a complex, multi-ethnic regional economy.
