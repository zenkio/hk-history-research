## Background
Following the **Great Clearance** (遷界令) of the early Qing Dynasty, which aimed to curb coastal support for anti-Qing resistance, the subsequent return of settlers to the Pearl River Delta left the region with a fragile maritime security infrastructure. By the **1790s**, the weakening of Qing naval oversight and local economic hardships drove many sailors, fishermen, and marginalized laborers toward maritime banditry. These groups initially operated as independent, loosely affiliated units often referred to as 'water bandits' (水賊).

## What happened
Between **1790 and 1800**, the pirate landscape underwent a structural transformation. **Zheng Yi** (鄭一), a prominent maritime leader, successfully implemented a policy of centralizing command among the fragmented fleets. Through intimidation, political marriage, and the establishment of a hierarchical code of conduct—known as the 'pirate laws'—**Zheng Yi** created the foundation of what would become the Red Flag Fleet (紅旗幫). By the end of the decade, this coalition exerted unprecedented control over the shipping lanes connecting Guangzhou (廣州) to the South China Sea, effectively extracting 'protection money' from merchant vessels and challenging the local Qing administration's monopoly on violence.

## Significance
The centralization of these fleets forced the Qing imperial government to shift from a policy of sporadic containment to a massive, systematic coastal military buildup. This period marked a transition where piracy evolved from small-scale smuggling into a formidable maritime state-within-a-state. The scale of the coalition forced the Qing authorities to eventually seek aid from foreign naval powers, a development that had long-term implications for the subsequent colonial presence in the region. The organization established by **Zheng Yi** and later expanded by his wife **Zheng Yi Sao** (鄭一嫂) fundamentally altered the security dynamics of the Pearl River Delta for the next two decades.

## Perspectives

- **Qing Imperial Perspective**: The Qing authorities viewed the coalition as a persistent 'rebellion' (賊寇) and a direct threat to the legitimacy and tax-collecting power of the throne. They emphasized the necessity of harsh military crackdowns to restore order and protect the maritime trade routes essential to the Canton System.
- **Local Coastal Community Perspective**: For many local communities, the pirate fleets were an ambiguous reality; they were often seen as both a source of terror and an alternative power structure that provided services like protection and dispute resolution where the distant Qing bureaucracy failed.
- **Later Historical Scholarship**: Modern historians analyze the coalition not just as criminality, but as a sophisticated socio-economic organization. Research focuses on how the coalition managed logistics, labor, and maritime trade, suggesting it functioned as a proto-state entity.
