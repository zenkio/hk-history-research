## Background
During the early **19th century**, foreign trade in southern China was strictly regulated through the Canton System (廣州貿易體系). Western merchants, primarily British and American, conducted all legal commerce through a designated guild of Chinese monopoly merchants known as the Cohong (公行). Tensions frequently arose over high tariffs, restrictive trade practices, and the lack of direct communication with Qing imperial officials. Foreign traders increasingly felt constrained by these institutional barriers, setting the stage for organized resistance.

## What happened
In **1823**, foreign merchants in Canton (廣州) took a decisive step by establishing a General Chamber of Commerce. This formal coalition allowed Western traders to pool their resources, coordinate lobbying efforts, and present a unified front against the Cohong and the imperial customs administration, known locally as the Hoppo (粵海關監督). Rather than relying on sporadic, individual petitions, the chamber provided a structured mechanism to protest commercial restrictions and advocate for free trade principles.

## Significance
The formation of the chamber represented a crucial evolution in foreign commercial tactics leading up to the First Opium War. By organizing collectively, Western traders laid the groundwork for institutionalized pressure against Qing trade monopolies. Modern scholars view this development as a key precursor to the breakdown of the Canton System, reflecting how rising Western economic assertiveness began to challenge the traditional tributary and regulatory framework of the Qing Empire.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial & Western Merchant Perspective**: Foreign traders viewed the chamber as a necessary and progressive step to combat monopolistic extortion, arbitrary taxation, and restricted market access imposed by the Qing administration.
- **Qing / Beijing Official Perspective**: Imperial authorities regarded foreign merchant associations with suspicion, viewing unauthorized organized lobbying as a direct challenge to state sovereignty and established tributary regulations.
- **Later Historiography**: Modern historians analyze the chamber as an early manifestation of Western corporate imperialism, demonstrating how economic coordination directly preceded and facilitated military conflict.
