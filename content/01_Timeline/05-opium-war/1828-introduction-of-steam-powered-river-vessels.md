## Background

By the late **1820s**, the Pearl River delta was a bustling yet tightly regulated hub of maritime trade centered on the Canton System (十三行). Transport between the trading factories of Canton (廣州), the Portuguese enclave of Macau (澳門), and the outer maritime anchorages relied traditionally on wind-powered junk vessels and oared rowboats. These traditional craft were heavily dependent on seasonal monsoons and tidal currents, creating unpredictable transit times that complicated the coordination of foreign commerce and communications.

## What happened

Around **1828**, early steam-powered vessels began to make tentative appearances on the waters of the Pearl River. While large ocean-going steamers were still decades away from dominating the China trade, smaller steam-assisted craft and river tenders started to navigate the delta waterways. These initial introductions were often met with curiosity and caution by local authorities and boatmen, as coal-fired propulsion challenged long-established river transport monopolies and customary navigational practices.

## Significance

The advent of steam power marked the beginning of a technological shift in South China's waterways, gradually reducing travel times between Canton and coastal outposts. This improvement in communication reliability laid the groundwork for the more aggressive integration of Western industrial technology into the region during the treaty port era that followed the First Opium War.

## Perspectives

- **British and Western Merchant Perspective**: Foreign traders viewed the introduction of steam vessels as a long-overdue modernization necessary to overcome inefficient Qing administrative controls and unpredictable river currents, enhancing the speed of commercial intelligence.
- **Qing Official Perspective**: Imperial authorities often regarded foreign-introduced steam craft with suspicion, viewing them as potential instruments of smuggling or naval reconnaissance that threatened traditional maritime security and domestic riverine regulations.
