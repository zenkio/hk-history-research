## Background

By **1840**, escalating tensions between the British Empire and the Qing dynasty over trade, diplomacy, and the opium trade culminated in the outbreak of the First Opium War. Seeking a secure naval and military base near the Chinese mainland to project power northward along China's eastern coast, British authorities and military commanders targeted the sheltered waters around the Pearl River Delta and the island of Hong Kong (香港島) as a strategic staging ground.

## What happened

Beginning around **June 21, 1840**, a large British expeditionary fleet consisting of warships, troop transports, and supply vessels began gathering in the waters of Hong Kong and the surrounding archipelago. Commanded by naval and military officers, the armada used the area as a staging post, logistical hub, and anchorage. From this forward operating base, the British forces prepared to launch subsequent naval blockades and military offensives against Qing positions further north along the Chinese coast, marking a pivotal escalation in the conflict.

## Significance

The gathering of the fleet in Hong Kong waters is viewed by historians as a critical watershed moment in the transition of Hong Kong from a collection of rural fishing and agricultural communities into a vital British imperial outpost. The use of the region as a military springboard directly preceded the formal acquisition of Hong Kong Island under the Convention of Chuenpee and the subsequent Treaty of Nanking. It established the deep-water harbor's enduring role as a major maritime and geopolitical hub in East Asia.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Perspective**: Contemporary British accounts and later colonial histories framed the gathering of the fleet as a necessary and brilliant strategic maneuver to protect British commercial interests, secure redress for diplomatic insults, and enforce free trade principles against a resistant Qing administration.
- **Qing and Mainland Chinese Perspective**: Qing official records and modern mainland scholarship view the assemblage of foreign warships as an act of imperialist aggression and an illegal invasion of sovereign Chinese territory, initiating a century of national humiliation through gunboat diplomacy.
- **Local Communities Perspective**: For the indigenous villagers, fishermen, and coastal communities of the Pearl River Delta and Hong Kong region, the sudden arrival of a foreign armada meant severe disruptions to local livelihoods, forced provisioning, and the unsettling realization that their home waters had become a theater of international war.
