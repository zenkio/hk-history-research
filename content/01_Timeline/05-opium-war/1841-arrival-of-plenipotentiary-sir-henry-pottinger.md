## Background

By mid-1841, the First Opium War had entered a complex diplomatic and military phase. Captain **Charles Elliot** (義律) had negotiated the Convention of Chuenpi in early 1841, which secured the initial cession of Hong Kong Island but satisfied neither the British government nor the Qing court. Foreign Secretary Lord Palmerston dismissed Elliot for failing to extract more concessions and appointed **Sir Henry Pottinger** (砵甸乍) with instructions to expand the scope of military operations and secure a permanent, formal territorial acquisition.

## What happened

On **August 10, 1841**, **Sir Henry Pottinger** arrived in Macao and subsequently proceeded to Hong Kong to assume his duties as sole Plenipotentiary and Chief Superintendent of British Trade in China. Upon taking office, Pottinger immediately criticized Elliot's localized approach. Rather than lingering in the Pearl River Delta, Pottinger prioritized a decisive escalation of the war. Within weeks, he directed the British expeditionary force northward along the Chinese coast, capturing Amoy (Xiamen), Chusan (Dinghai), and Ningpo, which ultimately forced the Qing empire to sue for peace.

## Significance

Pottinger's arrival marked a crucial turning point in Hong Kong's transition from a temporary military anchorage into a permanent colonial possession. While **Charles Elliot** had governed with provisional arrangements, **Sir Henry Pottinger** laid the groundwork for formal administrative structures ashore, appointing magistrates and establishing a land registry. His leadership directly led to the Treaty of Nanking in 1842, which formally ceded Hong Kong Island in perpetuity and fundamentally altered the trajectory of modern East Asian history.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Perspective**: Pottinger is viewed as a decisive statesman who replaced hesitant diplomacy with resolute action, laying the firm administrative and legal foundations for the successful establishment of the colony.
- **Qing/Beijing Perspective**: Pottinger's appointment and subsequent military escalation are seen as the intensification of unprovoked imperialist aggression, violating China's sovereignty through gunboat diplomacy.
- **Local Hong Kong and New Territories Perspective**: Indigenous villagers and local inhabitants experienced the transition as an abrupt imposition of foreign rule that disregarded traditional land rights and local governance structures.
