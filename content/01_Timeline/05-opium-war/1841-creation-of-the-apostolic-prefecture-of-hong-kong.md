## Background

Before the British occupation of Hong Kong Island in **1841**, Catholic pastoral care for the region was nominally overseen by the ancient Diocese of Macau (天主教澳門教區), which had been established in the 16th century. As the First Opium War unfolded and British forces secured the island, European missionaries recognized the strategic opportunity to expand spiritual and charitable infrastructure under British protection. The shifting geopolitical landscape necessitated a distinct ecclesiastical jurisdiction independent of Portuguese ecclesiastical oversight in Macau.

## What happened

On **April 22, 1841**, **Pope Gregory XVI** issued a decree elevating Hong Kong to an independent Apostolic Prefecture. **Father Theodor Joset** was appointed as the first Prefect Apostolic. Arriving shortly after the British flag was raised, **Theodor Joset** faced immense challenges, including a lack of physical infrastructure, tropical diseases, and a rapidly growing, transient population of laborers, sailors, and military personnel. Under his guidance, the nascent Catholic mission began organizing social welfare, education, and medical services alongside religious worship, laying the groundwork for enduring community institutions.

## Significance

The creation of the Apostolic Prefecture marked a pivotal moment in the institutional history of Hong Kong. It represented the decoupling of local religious administration from Portuguese Macau and mirrored the British colonial administration's efforts to formalize governance on the newly acquired territory. By establishing structured missions, orphanages, and schools, the Catholic Church became one of the earliest Western institutional pillars on the island, deeply intertwining religious work with the social welfare development of early colonial Hong Kong.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Perspective**: British authorities generally welcomed the establishment of Western religious institutions, viewing them as vital partners in providing social welfare, education, and moral stabilization for a volatile frontier population.
- **Qing/Beijing Perspective**: Imperial authorities in Beijing largely viewed the expansion of Western missionary activities through the lens of encroaching foreign imperialism, often associating ecclesiastical jurisdictions with unequal treaties and the erosion of Chinese sovereignty.
- **Later Scholarship**: Modern historians analyze the prefecture's founding as a key moment in the globalization of Catholic missions, noting how missionaries navigated the friction between British colonial ambitions and Portuguese ecclesiastical traditions.
