## Background

Following the outbreak of the First Opium War and the initial British occupation of Hong Kong Island in **1841**, foreign mercantile houses sought secure bases for their expanding trade operations, particularly the lucrative and controversial opium trade. Among these firms, **Jardine, Matheson & Co.** (怡和洋行), led by **William Jardine** (威廉·渣甸) and **James Matheson** (詹姆士·馬地臣), held a dominant position in British commercial networks operating in South China.

## What happened

In **November 1841**, **Jardine, Matheson & Co.** completed the construction of the first substantial European stone godowns (warehouses) and wharves at **East Point** (東角). This location, situated on the northern shore of Hong Kong Island, was acquired through the first government land sales held by the early colonial administration. The new settlement provided the firm with secure storage facilities for goods, repair capabilities, and a fortified operational base that bypassed the older mercantile hubs of Canton and Macau.

## Significance

The establishment of the **East Point** settlement marked a crucial moment in the spatial organization of early colonial Hong Kong, illustrating how private British corporate power directly shaped the physical infrastructure of the island. While the colonial government ostensibly directed urban planning, powerful merchant houses like Jardine's wielded immense influence over land allocation and economic development, setting a precedent for corporate-state relations in the territory.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial perspective**: Colonial administrators and British merchants viewed the rapid development of East Point as a triumph of enterprise and civilization, necessary for transforming an alleged barren island into a thriving international entrepôt.
- **Qing and Beijing perspective**: Imperial Chinese officials regarded the establishment of foreign corporate settlements on Hong Kong Island as an illegal occupation born of unequal gunboat diplomacy and the forced imposition of the opium trade.
- **Local Hong Kong and New Territories communities' perspective**: Indigenous coastal and farming communities witnessed their traditional waters and surrounding lands co-opted by foreign corporate entities, marginalizing local livelihoods in favor of British imperial commercial expansion.
