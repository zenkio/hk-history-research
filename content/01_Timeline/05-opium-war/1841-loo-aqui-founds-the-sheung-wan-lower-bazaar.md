## Background

Following the British occupation of Hong Kong Island in **January 1841**, the colonial administration sought to establish order and attract Chinese merchants and laborers to support the fledgling port. Early British officials, operating under **Captain Charles Elliot**, recognized the need for local provisioning and commercial infrastructure. Before formal land auctions and town planning took definitive shape, Chinese entrepreneurs saw economic opportunities in the new territory, which was declared a free port.

## What happened

In **August 1841**, the prominent Chinese provisioner and contractor **Loo Aqui** (盧亞貴), alongside other local merchants, established permanent commercial shops and residential housing in the Sheung Wan area. This development became known as the Sheung Wan Lower Bazaar (下街市集). It represented the creation of Hong Kong's first organized Chinese mercantile quarter, moving beyond temporary squatter structures to more permanent masonry and timber buildings tailored to Chinese trade and community needs.

## Significance

The founding of the Lower Bazaar marked a crucial early step in the integration of Chinese commercial enterprise into the colonial urban landscape. It laid the spatial foundations for the Chinese mercantile network that would drive Hong Kong's economic growth throughout the nineteenth century. The event highlights the agency of early Chinese merchants in shaping the colony's development, operating alongside or in tension with British colonial land policies and urban segregation practices.
