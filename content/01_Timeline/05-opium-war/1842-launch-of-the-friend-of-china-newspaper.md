## Background

In the early 1840s, as the First Opium War unfolded and British forces occupied Hong Kong Island, the burgeoning settlement transitioned from a military base into a nascent commercial entrepôt. British and foreign merchants operating in the region required a dedicated public medium for commercial intelligence, shipping news, and political commentary. Prior to this, print media in South China was largely limited to publications based in Canton (Guangzhou) or Macao (澳門), such as the *Canton Register*.

## What happened

On **March 17, 1842**, *The Friend of China* (中國之友) printed its inaugural issue in Hong Kong. Founded and edited by **George Wyndham Cooke** (郭富), the newspaper quickly established itself as a fixture of the colonial landscape. It provided vital intelligence regarding the progress of military operations, opium trade regulations, real property auctions, and maritime shipping schedules. The publication operated amidst the shifting environment of early colonial administration under administrators such as **Sir Henry Pottinger** (璞鼎查).

## Significance

The launch of the newspaper marked a critical milestone in the development of Hong Kong's public sphere. For the British mercantile community, it served as an influential organ for lobbying the Foreign Office and criticizing official policies that hampered free trade. For later historians, the newspaper provides an invaluable, albeit partisan, primary record of the social, economic, and administrative evolution of Hong Kong during its transition to a crown colony.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration and merchants**: Viewed the newspaper as a crucial instrument for fostering a free-market commercial hub, championing British legal norms, and providing necessary channels for merchant grievances against government oversight.
- **Qing dynasty officials**: Regarded foreign-language newspapers published on occupied Chinese territory as unauthorized and disruptive foreign propaganda that frequently justified imperialist aggression and illegal trade.
