## Background

During the First Opium War, British plenipotentiary **Charles Elliot** (義律) sought a permanent trading base and deep-water anchorage near Canton (Guangzhou). Negotiations with Qing dynasty imperial commissioner **Qishan** (琦善) led to the abortive Convention of Chuenpee in January 1841. Though never officially ratified by either governments, the preliminary agreement set the stage for a formal military takeover of Hong Kong Island.

## What happened

On **January 26, 1841**, Commodore **James Bremer** led naval forces ashore at a promontory on the northern coast of Hong Kong Island. The landing party hoisted the Union Jack, fired a Royal Salute from the warships, and formally claimed the island for **Queen Victoria**. The site was named Possession Point. Initial infrastructure development focused on establishing a naval depot, administrative tents, and temporary barracks to secure the newly claimed foothold.

## Significance

This event marked the foundational moment of British colonial rule in Hong Kong, preceding the formal ceding of the island under the Treaty of Nanking in 1842. Possession Point transformed from a small fishing and farming hamlet into the administrative and military nucleus of the early colony, setting the trajectory for Hong Kong's development as a major global entrepôt.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial**: Viewed the landing as a triumphant and lawful assertion of sovereignty to protect expanding mercantile interests and secure a strategic base in East Asia following diplomatic breakdowns.
- **Qing Imperial**: Regarded the landing and subsequent occupation as an illegal act of foreign aggression and military encroachment on sovereign Chinese territory, carried out during ongoing negotiations.
- **Local Communities**: Indigenous Tanka boat people and Punti villagers viewed the sudden arrival of foreign warships and troops with cautious uncertainty, as their traditional livelihoods and local governance structures were abruptly incorporated into a foreign colonial framework.
