## Background
Following the formal cession of Hong Kong Island under the Treaty of Nanking in 1842, the British colonial authorities faced the urgent task of transforming the undeveloped coastal terrain into a functional administrative and mercantile hub. The settlement of **Victoria City** (維多利亞城) required substantial building materials to construct colonial offices, warehouses, and residential quarters for the growing European and local merchant populations. The island’s topography featured significant granite deposits, which colonial surveyors identified as readily accessible resources for construction.

## What happened
In **1843**, the colonial government authorized the establishment of a quarry at **Morrison Hill** (摩理臣山), named after the educator and missionary **Robert Morrison** (馬禮遜). The site was chosen for its proximity to the burgeoning waterfront of the **Central District** (中環). Laborers, primarily from the local Chinese community, were recruited to excavate the granite. The stone extracted was transported to construction sites, forming the bedrock and masonry for many of the earliest permanent structures in the colony. The quarrying operations significantly altered the physical geography of the area, gradually leveling what was once a prominent hill.

## Significance
The exploitation of the **Morrison Hill Quarry** represents a pivotal moment in the physical imposition of colonial architecture on the landscape. By sourcing building materials locally, the administration reduced logistical dependencies on imports, accelerating the urbanization of the waterfront. However, the site was also a locus of labor, reflecting the reliance of colonial infrastructure on the local workforce. As the city expanded, the quarry was eventually phased out, and the land was reclaimed and redeveloped for residential and commercial use in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, leaving little trace of the original topographical feature.

## Perspectives

- **Colonial Administration**: Viewed the quarry as a practical and necessary industrial initiative to facilitate the rapid development and economic viability of the newly established trade port.
- **Local Chinese Laborers**: For the workers, the quarry provided a source of employment during a period of economic instability, though they faced harsh working conditions and the physical displacement of historical terrain.
- **Qing Dynasty Officialdom**: Authorities in the Qing Empire generally regarded the rapid building projects as part of the formalization of foreign occupation, often noting the changing geography as evidence of the colonial footprint.
