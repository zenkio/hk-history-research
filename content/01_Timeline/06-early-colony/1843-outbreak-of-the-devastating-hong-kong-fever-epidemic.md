## Background

Following the **Treaty of Nanking** in **1842**, the British established a colony on Hong Kong Island (香港島). The initial settlement focused heavily on the unhealthy waterfront areas of **Victoria City** (維多利亞城). Insanitary conditions, lack of infrastructure, and extensive soil disruption from early construction created prime breeding grounds for disease vectors.

## What happened

Beginning around **May 1843**, a devastating wave of illness swept through the military garrison and the civilian inhabitants. Contemporary observers referred to the affliction simply as **'Hong Kong Fever'**. Symptoms typically included high fevers, chills, and debilitating physical exhaustion. The disease struck down British soldiers, administrators, and local laborers alike at alarming rates. Military units stationed on the island suffered catastrophic mortality and morbidity rates, overwhelming the primitive medical facilities available at the time.

## Significance

The epidemic cast severe doubt on the viability of the entire colonial enterprise. The high death toll among British troops and officials prompted intense debates within the British government regarding whether the colony should be retained or abandoned. Ultimately, decisions were made to relocate military personnel to higher elevations, such as **Peak** areas and **Coolie Heights**, and to invest in better sanitation and drainage, ensuring the colony's survival despite the heavy human cost.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Early colonial officials viewed the epidemic as a tragic environmental obstacle and a severe administrative crisis. The staggering loss of life among troops nearly convinced London to abandon Hong Kong entirely, leading to urgent calls for infrastructural and sanitary reforms.
- **Later Medical and Historical Scholarship**: Modern historians and epidemiologists generally identify the 'Hong Kong Fever' as severe malaria, exacerbated by the disturbance of malarial swamps during early British construction. Scholars emphasize how ecological ignorance and poor urban planning directly contributed to the high mortality rates.
