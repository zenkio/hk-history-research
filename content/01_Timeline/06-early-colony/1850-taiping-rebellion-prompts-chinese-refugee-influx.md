## Background
In **1850**, the Taiping Rebellion (太平天國之亂) erupted in southern China as a massive civil war against the ruling Qing dynasty (清朝), led by the millenarian visionary **Hong Xiuquan** (洪秀全). As the conflict devastated vast regions of the mainland, millions of people were displaced, creating a desperate need for safety beyond the war zones.

## What happened
Hong Kong (香港), established as a British colony a few years prior in **1841**, quickly became a primary destination for those fleeing the chaos. The influx included not only impoverished refugees but also wealthy Chinese merchants (華商) and property owners who brought capital, business networks, and skills with them. The colonial population swelled rapidly, straining existing infrastructure but providing an immediate and potent stimulus to the local economy, property markets, and trade.

## Significance
This large-scale migration fundamentally reshaped Hong Kong's identity from a small trading outpost into a vibrant, cosmopolitan entrepôt with a predominantly Chinese population. The presence of wealthy merchants laid the groundwork for a powerful local Chinese elite who would play a crucial role in the social and economic governance of the colony for decades to come, altering the power dynamics between the British administration and the local populace.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Colonial authorities initially viewed the massive influx with anxiety, fearing overpopulation, increased crime, and potential political subversion, but soon recognized the economic benefits of the incoming capital and labor.
- **Qing Government**: Imperial authorities generally regarded the refugees and emigrants in the foreign-controlled territory with suspicion, viewing Hong Kong as a haven for anti-dynastic rebels and criminals.
- **Local Chinese Merchants and Refugees**: For the migrants, Hong Kong offered a relatively secure sanctuary under British rule where they could preserve their wealth, conduct commerce, and escape the devastation of the mainland civil war.
