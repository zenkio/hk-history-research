## Background

Following the British occupation of Hong Kong Island in **1841** and the formal cession under the Treaty of Nanking in **1842**, the newly established Victoria City (維多利亞城) faced severe geographical constraints. The urban area was hemmed in by steep hillsides rising directly from Victoria Harbour (維多利亞港), leaving very little flat land for commercial godowns, offices, and thoroughfares. As maritime trade grew rapidly, merchants and colonial administrators increasingly recognized that the natural shoreline was inadequate to support a bustling entrepôt.

## What happened

In **1851**, following a disastrous fire that destroyed large portions of the waterfront district, proposals to reshape the coastline gained urgent momentum. The **Praya Reclamation Scheme** was formally initiated to carve out usable land along the central waterfront. The project involved depositing granite rubble, excavated earth from nearby hills, and construction waste into the shallow waters of the harbor to push the shoreline outward. This monumental engineering effort resulted in the creation of a wide new thoroughfare, known as Praya Central (中環海旁), along with newly minted waterfront lots sold to commercial enterprises.

## Significance

Laying the groundwork for decades of future urban expansion, the reclamation permanently altered the physical geography of Hong Kong Island. It dramatically increased the commercial capacity of Victoria City by allowing direct access for cargo handling and shipping warehouses. However, it also initiated a long-term environmental and spatial relationship with Victoria Harbour, setting a precedent of altering the natural coastline to meet economic demands that continues to shape Hong Kong's urban landscape.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration**: Viewed the reclamation as an absolute necessity for modernizing infrastructure, improving sanitation, and boosting the economic efficiency of the entrepôt.
- **Local merchants and traders**: Generally welcomed the creation of accessible waterfront lots and wider roads, which directly facilitated cargo handling and commercial growth.
- **Later urban historians**: Analyze the project as the genesis of Hong Kong's long-standing reliance on land reclamation, noting both its economic benefits and its early ecological impact on Victoria Harbour.
