## Background

Following the Treaty of Nanking in **1842**, Hong Kong Island was ceded to Great Britain as a colonial entrepôt. However, British officials soon identified strategic vulnerabilities. The southern shore of Victoria Harbour, controlled by the Qing Empire (大清), remained outside British jurisdiction, posing potential security risks to shipping and military installations on the island.

Simultaneously, the urban center of Victoria (維多利亞城) experienced rapid population growth and acute overcrowding. Refugees and migrants fleeing the turmoil of the Taiping Rebellion (太平天國) on the Chinese mainland flooded into the colony, intensifying housing shortages and prompting colonial authorities to look northward across the harbor for expansion space.

## What happened

Amidst the hostilities of the Second Opang War, British and French forces occupied parts of northern China and threatened Beijing. **Harry Parkes** (巴夏禮), serving as the lead British negotiator, utilized the military leverage of the allied forces to press territorial demands in the south.

On **March 20, 1860**, Parkes and Qing imperial commissioner **Yue-he-tong** (generally referenced in British accounts through broader diplomatic channels, though final ratification occurred via the Convention of Peking) negotiated the transfer. Under the arrangement, the area south of Boundary Street—known as Kowloon (九龍)—was initially leased in perpetuity to Great Britain, though the text would later be interpreted by the British as an outright cession in the subsequent Convention of Peking (北京條約) ratified in **October 1860**.

## Significance

The acquisition fundamentally transformed the geography and defense strategy of British Hong Kong. It secured the northern littoral of Victoria Harbour, allowing the Royal Navy and colonial administrators to regulate all maritime traffic entering and leaving the port.

By integrating the Kowloon Peninsula into the colony, the British established a permanent foothold on the mainland. This geographical enlargement set a direct precedent for the later **1898** Second Convention of Peking, which leased the New Territories for 99 years and shaped the final territorial configuration of modern Hong Kong.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial perspective**: Administrators viewed the acquisition as a vital and pragmatic necessity for sanitary reform, urban relief, and the military defense of Victoria Harbour against regional rivals and pirates.
- **Qing dynasty perspective**: Imperial authorities regarded the lease as an enforced concession extracted under military duress during a national crisis, marking another unequal treaty eroding Chinese territorial sovereignty.
- **Local communities and later scholarship**: Historians and local residents note that the expansion divided indigenous communities across the newly drawn boundary lines, initiating a complex era of colonial governance over rural mainland populations.
