## Background
In the early years of the British colonial settlement of Victoria (域多利亞城), illumination relied primarily on oil lamps, which provided dim and unreliable lighting. As the urban center expanded and the population grew, the colonial administration and business community sought more efficient and modern infrastructure to support commerce and maintain public order. The establishment of the **Hong Kong and China Gas Company** (香港中華煤氣有限公司), incorporated in **1862**, laid the foundation for the introduction of modern gas utility services in the region.

## What happened
On **December 3, 1864**, the company successfully lit the first gas street lamps in the Victoria district. A newly constructed gasworks plant, situated in the Sheung Wan (上環) area, produced the coal gas required to power the network. This technological shift replaced the older oil-based system, casting a brighter and more consistent glow across the principal thoroughfares of the colonial settlement.

## Significance
The transition to gas lighting significantly transformed nighttime urban life in Hong Kong. From the colonial perspective, the brighter streets improved public safety, reduced opportunities for criminal activity under the cover of darkness, and symbolized the technological progress of the British Empire in the Far East. For local Chinese residents and merchants, the reliable illumination facilitated extended business hours and altered the nocturnal rhythms of the bustling port city, even as Western municipal technologies increasingly reshaped the traditional landscape.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Viewed the introduction of gas lighting as a triumph of Victorian engineering and municipal governance, enhancing law and order while projecting civilizational progress in a strategic Asian port.
- **Local Chinese Community**: Observed the modernization of urban infrastructure with a mixture of practical adaptation to extended commercial opportunities and a recognition of the expanding reach of colonial authority over daily life.
