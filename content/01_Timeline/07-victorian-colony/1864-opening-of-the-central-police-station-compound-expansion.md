## Background
Following the British occupation of Hong Kong Island and the subsequent Convention of Peking in **1860**, which ceded the Kowloon Peninsula, the colony experienced a rapid influx of residents and commercial activity. This demographic pressure, coupled with rising concerns over urban crime and internal security, strained the existing colonial administrative and policing apparatus. To project authority and maintain public order in the burgeoning Victorian settlement, colonial authorities recognized the necessity of enlarging the primary law enforcement facilities.

## What happened
In **1864**, expansion works at the Central Police Station Compound (中區警署建築群) were completed, enlarging the physical footprint and operational capacity of the colonial police force in the central district. The expanded compound integrated additional cells, administrative offices, and quarters designed to accommodate the growing number of local and expatriate police personnel. While architectural records detail the physical enlargement of the site, precise operational protocols and the immediate day-to-day impacts on the surrounding urban environment remain partially obscured by incomplete surviving documentation from the period.

## Significance
Historians view the **1864** expansion as a physical manifestation of the consolidation of British colonial governance in early Hong Kong. The enhanced infrastructure underscored the administration's commitment to establishing a permanent, institutionalized apparatus of state control. However, the exact motivations and immediate efficacy of these expanded policing measures continue to be subject to varying historical interpretations regarding the balance between public safety, social control, and the subjugation of the local population.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Viewed the expansion as a vital and progressive measure to secure life and property, establish the rule of law, and manage the logistical challenges of a rapidly expanding commercial port.
- **Local Chinese Community**: Often experienced the growing police presence through a lens of suspicion and increased colonial surveillance, viewing expanded infrastructure as an instrument of foreign control.
