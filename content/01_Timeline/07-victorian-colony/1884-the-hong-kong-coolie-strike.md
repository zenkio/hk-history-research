## Background
In **September 1884**, tensions escalated in the British colony of Hong Kong against the backdrop of the Sino-French War (中法戰爭). Local Chinese laborers, including cargo handlers, stevedores, and boatmen, harbored deep resentment toward French aggression in Vietnam and perceived complicity or unfair treatment by the British colonial administration. The immediate flashpoint occurred when local coolies refused to handle French vessels or provide services to French naval and merchant ships.

## What happened
The labor action quickly transformed from a localized refusal to service French ships into a broad general strike involving thousands of Chinese workers. Transport, shipping, and urban logistics were severely disrupted. The colonial government, fearing the loss of Hong Kong's commercial viability and potential public unrest, attempted to break the strike using police force and by importing alternative labor. Clashes broke out between striking workers and colonial authorities, leading to arrests and heightened anxiety among British officials regarding the control and loyalty of the colony's vast Chinese majority.

## Significance
The **Hong Kong Coolie Strike** is widely viewed by modern historians as an early and powerful demonstration of Chinese working-class political solidarity and nationalism in the colony. It revealed the capacity of organized labor to disrupt the colonial economy and challenge British authority. However, historians and contemporary observers interpret the motivations behind the strike through different lenses, debating the degree to which the action was driven by spontaneous patriotic fervor for the Qing empire, economic grievances against local employers, or orchestration by secret societies and trade guilds.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Colonial officials viewed the strike primarily as a threat to public order and commerce, suspecting coercion by anti-foreign secret societies rather than genuine, widespread political sentiment among workers.
- **Later Scholarship**: Modern historians emphasize the strike as a foundational moment of anti-imperialist working-class solidarity, demonstrating how local labor could intertwine economic demands with broader Chinese nationalism.
