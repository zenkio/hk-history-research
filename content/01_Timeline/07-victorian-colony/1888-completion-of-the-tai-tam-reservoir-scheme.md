## Background
By the 1880s, the rapidly growing population of Victoria City faced severe water shortages. The existing Pok Fu Lam Reservoir, completed in **1863**, was no longer sufficient to meet the demands of the colonial administration, commercial enterprises, and local residents. Reliable freshwater was essential for preventing epidemics and sustaining the colony's economic ambitions.

## What happened
Engineers initiated the Tai Tam Reservoir Scheme (大潭水塘計劃) to harness water from the southern side of Hong Kong Island. The project involved constructing a major dam, aqueducts, and a tunnel through the mountains to channel water to the northern urban areas. Work progressed through the 1880s, culminating in the official completion of the initial phase in **1888**. This engineering feat required substantial manual labor, utilizing local workforce and technical oversight by colonial officials.

## Significance
Securing a stable long-term water supply transformed the physical and demographic landscape of Victoria. It enabled the colonial government to plan for rapid urban expansion into the late 19th century. While contemporary British authorities lauded the engineering achievement as a triumph of modern infrastructure, the development also underscored the colonial state's growing infrastructural control over the island's natural resources.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration**: Viewed the reservoir as a vital technological triumph that ensured the survival, sanitation, and continued commercial prosperity of the Victorian colony.
- **Local Hong Kong communities**: Experienced the infrastructural transformation as part of a tightening colonial administration that reshaped local access to land and water resources.
