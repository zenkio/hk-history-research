## Background

Following the Convention of Peking in **1860**, the British colonial government formally annexed the Kowloon Peninsula (九龍半島), expanding the footprint of the Hong Kong colony. Throughout the 1860s and 1870s, cross-harbor transportation between Kowloon and Hong Kong Island (香港島) relied primarily on fragmented services operated by local junk and sampan owners. As commercial and residential activity in Tsim Sha Tsui (尖沙咀) and Central (中環) gradually increased, the need for a reliable, scheduled, and weather-resistant transit method became increasingly apparent to both the colonial administration and merchants.

## What happened

In **May 1888**, Parsi entrepreneur **Dorabjee Naorojee** (爹核士·娜律哲) formally launched a regularized steam ferry service across Victoria Harbour. Utilizing a small fleet of wooden-hulled steam vessels, his enterprise provided scheduled crossings that reduced uncertainty for daily commuters and cargo transporters alike. While informal predecessors had operated previously, Naorojee's venture introduced fixed timetables and a more structured corporate approach to harbor-crossing transit. The enterprise proved commercially viable, eventually leading to the formation of the **Star Ferry Company** (天星小輪有限公司) in **1898**, which formalized and expanded upon these early routes.

## Significance

The establishment of Naorojee's regular steam ferry service marked a pivotal turning point in the integration of the Kowloon Peninsula with the urban core of Hong Kong Island. By stabilizing cross-harbor transit, the service facilitated the early suburbanization of Kowloon and supported the expanding colonial economy. Later transport scholarship often views this initiative as the institutional genesis of one of Hong Kong's most enduring public symbols, transforming the harbor from a geographical barrier into a connected urban waterway.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Colonial authorities welcomed the private initiative as a vital infrastructure improvement that fostered commerce and connected the newly acquired Kowloon territory without requiring direct public expenditure or subsidy.
- **Local Water Communities**: Traditional junk and sampan operators likely viewed the introduction of mechanized, scheduled steam ferries with economic apprehension, as corporate transit threatened their traditional livelihoods in harbor transport.
