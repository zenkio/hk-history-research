## Background
By the late **19th century**, the growing British colony of Hong Kong faced severe topographical constraints. The narrow strip of flat land between Victoria Peak and Victoria Harbour restricted urban expansion, particularly for the burgeoning commercial houses and financial institutions based in Central. Existing docking and warehouse facilities struggled to keep pace with the massive growth in maritime trade between Europe, China, and the wider British Empire.

## What happened
In **1889**, merchant and municipal leader **Sir Paul Chater** (遮打), working alongside legislative councillor **James Johnstone Keswick** (堅忌威), formally proposed and advanced the massive Praya Reclamation Scheme. The project aimed to reclaim approximately **65 acres** of land along the Central waterfront, stretching from Sheung Wan towards the eastern edges of Central. Approved by the colonial government, the engineering feat involved dredging, depositing millions of tons of fill material—much of it excavated from local hillsides such as the Mid-Levels—and constructing new seawalls, docks, and thoroughfares, notably Des Voeux Road (德輔道).

## Significance
The reclamation drastically altered the geography and economic geography of Victoria Harbour. It pushed the shoreline significantly further north, creating vital new commercial real estate that attracted foreign banks, trading hong compounds, and corporate headquarters. While it provided much-needed modern infrastructure for the colonial economy, it also permanently severed the older waterfront communities from the harbor and sparked enduring debates over urban planning priorities, property values, and the environmental impact on the harbor.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration and Business Elite**: Viewed the reclamation as a visionary and necessary triumph of modern engineering that secured Hong Kong's status as a premier global entrepôt by easing critical land shortages and boosting commercial capacity.
- **Later Urban and Environmental Historians**: Argue that while the project fueled economic growth, it established a precedent of prioritizing commercial real estate over ecological preservation, permanently altering the natural ecosystem of Victoria Harbour.
