## Background
By the late 19th century, the British colony of Hong Kong relied primarily on gas lighting provided by the Hong Kong and China Gas Company, established in 1862, alongside traditional oil lamps. As Victoria City grew as a bustling commercial port, the colonial administration and business leaders sought more efficient and modern infrastructure to match developments in European metropoles. In **1889**, the Hongkong Electric Company (香港電燈有限公司) was founded by **Hoon-chung Chater** and partners, obtaining the colony's first electricity licence to supply power for public and private use.

## What happened
On **December 1, 1890**, the company's newly constructed power station on Chatham Path in Wan Chai (灣仔) commenced operations. Initially, the plant utilized coal-fired steam generators to provide electricity for public street lighting, specifically targeting select thoroughfares in central Victoria City. While domestic and commercial adoption grew gradually due to high initial costs, the launching of the Wan Chai plant marked the physical introduction of electrical grids to the urban landscape.

## Significance
The commencement of electrical power generation laid the groundwork for modern urban infrastructure in Hong Kong, eventually transforming manufacturing, transportation, and daily life. It signaled a shift from mid-Victorian gas technology toward late-Victorian electrical engineering, setting a precedent for subsequent utility developments across the territory, including the later electrification of the Peak Tram and the Star Ferry services.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration and business elite**: Viewed electrification as a vital civilizational advancement that brought the Victorian colony up to date with modern European capitals, enhancing commercial efficiency and urban safety.
- **Local Chinese urban residents**: Experienced the introduction of electricity primarily as an elite urban phenomenon, with gas and oil lighting remaining dominant in Chinese-majority tenement districts for years to come.
