## Background
By the late 19th century, the British colony of Hong Kong, comprising Hong Kong Island and the Kowloon Peninsula south of Boundary Street, faced growing security and defense vulnerabilities. British officials argued that effective defense of the harbor required control of the surrounding hills and waters. Following China's defeat in the First Sino-Japanese War and the subsequent scramble for concessions by European powers, Britain pressed the weakened Qing court for a territorial expansion.

## What happened
On **June 9, 1898**, the Second Convention of Peking (展拓香港界址專條) was signed in Beijing by **Sir Claude MacDonald** for Britain and **Li Hongzhang** (李鴻章) and **Prince Qing** (慶親王) for the Qing dynasty. The treaty granted Britain a 99-year rent-free lease over the area north of Boundary Street up to the Sham Chun River, along with 235 outlying islands and Mirs Bay and Deep Bay. The total area increased the colony by roughly twelvefold. British administration formally began on **July 1, 1898**.

## Significance
The acquisition transformed Hong Kong's geography, economy, and demographic makeup, absorbing vast rural populations and agricultural land into the British colonial framework. The 99-year duration of the lease became the legal and chronological anchor for the eventual transfer of sovereignty of the entirety of Hong Kong to the People's Republic of China in **July 1997**.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial**: Viewed the expansion as a vital and pragmatic security measure necessary to protect the burgeoning trade port of Hong Kong from potential rival European powers in the region.
- **Qing/Beijing**: Regarded the lease as part of the unequal treaties forced upon a weakened China during a period of national humiliation and foreign encroachment.
- **Local New Territories communities**: Initially offered armed resistance during the Six-Day War of 1899, fearing the disruption of traditional land rights and taxation systems, before negotiating a pragmatic coexistence with colonial authorities.
