## Background
By the late **19th century**, Hong Kong's rapidly growing population faced severe public health challenges. Fresh milk was scarce, expensive, and frequently contaminated, contributing to high rates of waterborne diseases and infant mortality. European residents and affluent locals desired a reliable supply of safe dairy products, but the humid subtropical climate made traditional European cattle farming and milk preservation nearly impossible without advanced infrastructure.

## What happened
In **1886**, **Patrick Manson** (倫遜) and a group of prominent businessmen founded **The Dairy Farm Company** (牛奶公司) in **Pok Fu Lam** (薄扶林) on the western coast of **Hong Kong Island** (香港島). Manson imported disease-resistant dairy cattle and established an integrated operation that combined cattle-breeding, milk production, and modern cold-storage facilities. The company utilized cutting-edge refrigeration technology to store and transport milk safely across the colony, eventually opening retail depots in central areas like **Central** (中環).

## Significance
The establishment of the Pok Fu Lam Farm marked a pivotal turning point in colonial public health and agricultural commerce. By successfully adapting dairy production to a tropical environment and pioneering early commercial refrigeration in Asia, the company transformed fresh milk from an expensive luxury for the colonial elite into an accessible commodity for a broader segment of the population, laying the foundations for one of Hong Kong's most enduring corporate enterprises.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Colonial authorities viewed the enterprise as a triumph of British sanitary science and entrepreneurial ingenuity, successfully addressing public health crises and improving the living standards of the European community.
- **Local Hong Kong Residents and Farmers**: While urban consumers welcomed access to affordable and safe milk, traditional agricultural communities sometimes viewed large-scale corporate ventures like Dairy Farm as disruptive to traditional land use and local farming economies.
