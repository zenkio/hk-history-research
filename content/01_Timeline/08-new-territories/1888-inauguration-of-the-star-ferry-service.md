## Background
Prior to the late 19th century, transport across Victoria Harbour relied primarily on small wooden sailboats and unlicensed junk operations, which were often unreliable and vulnerable to unpredictable weather conditions. As the commercial importance of both Victoria City on Hong Kong Island and the growing military and transport hub on the Kowloon Peninsula increased, the need for a dependable, scheduled mechanical transport system became apparent. 

## What happened
In **1888**, **Dorabjee Naorojee Mithaiwala** (德庇治·拿羅哲·米泰華拿) founded the Kowloon Ferry Company, introducing a regular steam-powered ferry service across Victoria Harbour. The initial fleet consisted of small steam vessels, such as the *Morning Star*, running between Central and Tsim Sha Tsui (尖沙咀). The venture proved immediately popular with commuters, merchants, and travelers, eventually leading to the expansion of the fleet and the formal incorporation of the Star Ferry Company in **1898** under the management of **Sir Catchick Paul Chater** (遮打).

## Significance
The inauguration of the ferry service fundamentally transformed the daily integration of the twin urban cores of Hong Kong. By drastically reducing travel time and increasing the reliability of cross-harbour transit, the service stimulated economic development, real estate expansion in Kowloon, and closer administrative links across the water, ultimately shaping the spatial geography of the modern city.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Viewed the ferry service as a vital modernization milestone that facilitated efficient economic integration, imperial administration, and commercial expansion across the colonial territory.
- **Local Hong Kong and Kowloon Communities**: Welcomed the reliable steam transport as a vast improvement over hazardous sampans, though many traditional boat operators faced economic disruption and displacement from their livelihoods.
- **Later Scholarship**: Historians often analyze the enterprise as a critical catalyst in early urbanization, highlighting how private transport ventures laid the physical groundwork for the modern metropolis long before cross-harbour tunnels were built.
