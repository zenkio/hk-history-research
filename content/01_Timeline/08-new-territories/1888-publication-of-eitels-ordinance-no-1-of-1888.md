## Background
During the late nineteenth century, the colonial administration of Hong Kong (香港) faced rapid population growth and increasing pressure on urban housing. European residents, particularly elite merchants and officials, sought to distance themselves from the densely populated, plague-prone Chinese quarters. Housing and sanitary concerns became intertwined with racial attitudes among the colonial governing class.

## What happened
In **1888**, the colonial government published Ordinance No. 1, an administrative and legislative measure heavily influenced by **Ernest John Eitel** (歐德). The ordinance established formal reservation policies, empowering the government to restrict Chinese residency in designated upscale neighborhoods, most notably the Peak (太平山) district. While framed by officials as a sanitary and public health measure aimed at preventing disease outbreaks, the practical effect was the implementation of de jure spatial segregation.

## Significance
This legislation marked a turning point in the urban development and social stratification of colonial Hong Kong. By legally codifying residential division along racial and class lines, Ordinance No. 1 set a precedent for future urban planning and housing policies. It reinforced the socio-political hierarchy of the colony, institutionalizing a physical divide between the European ruling elite and the local Chinese population that persisted well into the twentieth century.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Colonial officials viewed the ordinance as a necessary public health and sanitary measure to protect residents from infectious diseases and to manage urban overcrowding in a rapidly expanding port city.
- **Local Chinese Community**: Chinese merchants and residents perceived the policy as discriminatory and humiliating, restricting their freedom of movement and property ownership based purely on race despite their growing economic contributions to the colony.
- **Modern Scholarship**: Later historians emphasize that the ordinance utilized public health pretexts to mask deliberate racial segregation, institutionalizing white privilege and spatial control within the British Empire.
