## Background
In the late 19th century, Hong Kong faced significant challenges regarding the safety of its food supply, particularly concerning milk and perishable goods. High mortality rates among the European population and the elite class were often attributed to poor sanitation and the consumption of contaminated imported dairy. Before the intervention of modern industrial methods, the colony relied on limited local production and shipments from overseas that often suffered from spoilage in the tropical climate.

## What happened
The **Dairy Farm Company** (牛奶公司), co-founded by **Sir Patrick Manson** and **Sir Paul Chater** in 1886, significantly scaled its operations at the turn of the century. By **1901**, the company had solidified its presence in **Pok Fu Lam** (薄扶林). This period saw the implementation of systematic pasteurization and the development of large-scale cold storage facilities. These infrastructure investments allowed the company to preserve meat and dairy products, fundamentally changing the logistical capabilities of the colony. The company established a network of depots to distribute these goods, effectively creating an early cold chain in Hong Kong.

## Significance
The introduction of pasteurized milk and consistent refrigeration is widely credited with improving public health outcomes for those with access to these products. By mitigating the risks of bovine tuberculosis and other foodborne pathogens, the **Dairy Farm Company** helped stabilize the diet of the colonial administration and the emerging middle class. However, the influence of the company went beyond health; it served as a model for corporate modernization and industrial agriculture within the **New Territories** (新界) and the **Hong Kong Island** (香港島) urban areas. Its operations played a role in the broader colonial effort to sanitize the city and align its infrastructure with British standards of the early 20th century.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: The administration viewed the company as a vital success in public health, essential for maintaining the colony's viability and making the tropical environment more habitable for European civil servants and business interests.
- **Local Hong Kong and New Territories Communities**: For the local population, the rise of the Dairy Farm often represented a transition toward a westernized diet, though these products remained largely inaccessible to the laboring classes due to price, creating a distinct social divide in dietary standards.
