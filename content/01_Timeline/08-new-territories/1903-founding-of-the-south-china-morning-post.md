## Background

At the turn of the 20th century, Hong Kong's press landscape was expanding rapidly alongside its status as a major global trading port. British colonial administration dominated the governance of the territory, while the recently signed Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory (1898) brought the New Territories under British lease. Amidst growing political ferment in the region—including reformist and revolutionary movements directed against the declining Qing dynasty in mainland China—there was an increasing demand for English-language journalism that could cater to both the British colonial establishment and the literate local elite.

## What happened

On **November 6, 1903**, the *South China Morning Post* published its first edition. The newspaper was co-founded by **Tse Tsan-tai** (謝纘泰), a prominent local Chinese-Australian businessman and reformer, and **Alfred Cunningham** (康寧), an experienced British journalist. The publication aimed to provide rigorous coverage of local commerce, shipping intelligence, and political commentary. While structured as a Western-style newspaper, its founding partnership reflected a unique bridge between the British colonial administration and the aspirations of educated local residents.

## Significance

The launch of the *South China Morning Post* established what would become one of the most influential English-language newspapers in East Asia. Over the subsequent decades, the publication served as a primary organ for colonial commerce, legal notices, and public debate. Its pages documented the rapid modernization of Hong Kong, the impact of the 1911 Xinhai Revolution on the colony, and the shifting social dynamics between European expatriates and the local Chinese population. It remains a vital historical archive for understanding the economic and social trajectory of early 20th-century Hong Kong.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration**: Viewed the new publication as a welcome addition to the colony's free press, providing a reliable English-language medium for commercial intelligence, maritime updates, and official announcements.
- **Local reformers and Chinese community**: Saw the involvement of Tse Tsan-tai as an important platform for local voices and reformist ideas, bridging the gap between the British ruling class and the aspirations of the broader Chinese populace.
