## Background

By the early 20th century, the medical landscape of Hong Kong was undergoing substantial transformation under British colonial rule. The London Missionary Society, alongside prominent local figures, had previously established institutions like the Alice Memorial Hospital (雅麗氏紀念醫院) to provide Western medicine to the local Chinese population. However, maternal care remained severely constrained by traditional practices, high infant and maternal mortality rates, and cultural hesitations regarding male Western practitioners attending local women during childbirth. 

## What happened

On **June 3, 1904**, the **Alice Memorial Maternity Hospital** (雅麗氏紀念產科醫院) officially opened its doors as the first dedicated maternity hospital in the territory. Operating in conjunction with the existing medical missions, the facility was established to address the critical lack of specialized obstetric care. It aimed to provide a safe environment for childbirth while simultaneously introducing formal training programs for local Chinese women to become qualified midwives, bridging Western clinical standards with local community needs.

## Significance

The establishment of the hospital marked a pivotal moment in the modernization of Hong Kong's public health infrastructure. By institutionalizing Western obstetrics and focusing on the education of local midwives, the hospital gradually altered traditional midwifery practices across the colony. Later scholarship often highlights this institution as a key site where colonial health policy, Christian mission work, and local Chinese gender roles intersected, laying foundational frameworks for modern nursing and maternal care in Hong Kong.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Viewed the hospital as a triumph of civilizing mission and public health reform, demonstrating the benevolence and efficacy of Western medical science in the newly expanded colony.
- **Local Chinese Communities**: Navigated the hospital with a mixture of traditional practices and pragmatic acceptance, utilizing the facility for difficult deliveries while slowly integrating Western hygienic standards into local customs.
