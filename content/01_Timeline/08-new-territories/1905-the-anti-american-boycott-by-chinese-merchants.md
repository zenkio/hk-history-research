## Background

By the early 20th century, the United States had implemented strict anti-Chinese legislation, notably the **Chinese Exclusion Act**, which severely restricted Chinese immigration and subjected laborers already in the U.S. to harsh treatment. In response to these discriminatory policies, nationalist sentiment surged across mainland China and within the British colony of Hong Kong (香港). Local Chinese merchant guilds, which held significant sway over the colony's entrepôt trade, sought a way to express their outrage and pressure Washington into reforming its immigration laws.

## What happened

Beginning in **1905**, Chinese merchants in Hong Kong, alongside counterparts in cities like Guangzhou (廣州) and Shanghai (上海), initiated a comprehensive boycott of American imports. Merchants refused to handle, buy, or sell U.S. goods, severely disrupting trade routes across the Pearl River Delta. While the British colonial administration in Hong Kong officially maintained neutrality under pressure from London and Washington, the government quietly worried about public order and the disruption to commerce. Despite colonial anxieties and attempts to suppress overt political agitation, the merchant guilds maintained the boycott for months, utilizing traditional networks of solidarity and trade organization.

## Significance

The **1905** anti-American boycott marked a watershed moment in the political awakening of Hong Kong's Chinese merchant class. It proved that local economic elites could mobilize collective action on an international scale to defend the dignity of overseas and domestic Chinese populations. The event highlighted the complex triangular relationship between the British colonial authorities, the declining Qing (清) dynasty, and an increasingly assertive local Chinese populace, foreshadowing the anti-foreign movements of the later Republican era.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Authorities**: Colonial officials viewed the boycott with considerable alarm, fearing that anti-foreign agitation would spiral out of control and damage Hong Kong's status as a stable, neutral free port while straining diplomatic relations with the United States.
- **Chinese Merchant Guilds**: For the local merchant elite, the boycott was a matter of patriotic duty and commercial self-respect, demonstrating that Chinese economic power could be leveraged to challenge Western racial discrimination.
