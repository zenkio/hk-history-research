## Background

Following the Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory in 1898, the British colonial government sought to integrate the newly acquired New Territories (新界) with the urban core of Hong Kong Island and Kowloon (九龍). Plans soon emerged for a railway connecting Kowloon to Guangzhou (廣州), facilitating trade and travel across the border with the fading Qing Empire (清朝). Construction of the British section of the Kowloon-Canton Railway (九廣鐵路英段) commenced in 1906, presenting formidable engineering challenges across difficult terrain, notably the excavation of the Beacon Hill Tunnel (煙墩山隧道).

## What happened

On **October 1, 1910**, the British section of the railway officially opened to traffic, initially operating between Kowloon and Tai Po (大埔). The completion of the entire line connecting to Guangzhou would follow later in the decade. The railway utilized a standard gauge and quickly became a major artery for both passenger transit and freight, altering the daily rhythms of commute and commerce across the region.

## Significance

The opening of the railway modernized regional transport and physically bound the rural New Territories closer to the urban governance of the British colony. While designed as a strategic commercial conduit to mainland China, it also catalyzed local development along its route, shaping settlement patterns and economic activity for decades to come.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Viewed the railway as a triumph of modern engineering and imperial infrastructure that would secure Hong Kong's economic dominance and facilitate efficient administrative control over the New Territories.
- **Local New Territories Communities**: Experienced mixed impacts; while the railway improved regional mobility, its construction involved land acquisitions and disruptions to traditional agrarian livelihoods and fung shui beliefs.
- **Later Scholarship**: Analyzes the railway project as part of broader imperial railway politics in late Qing China, noting how colonial infrastructure simultaneously integrated territory and reinforced regional inequalities.
