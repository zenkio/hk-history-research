## Background
By the early 20th century, Hong Kong had established itself as a vital commercial hub under British colonial rule. In **May 1911**, the colonial administration undertook a comprehensive population census to better understand the demographic shifts occurring across the territory, which now encompassed both the ceded island and peninsula territories, alongside the newly leased New Territories (新界).

## What happened
The **1911 Population Census** recorded a dramatic surge in the total population, crossing the 450,000 threshold. This sudden and massive influx was widely observed to coincide with the chaotic political climate on the Chinese mainland, specifically the widespread uprisings and instability leading up to the fall of the Qing Dynasty (清朝). Urban areas in Victoria City (維多利亞城) and parts of Kowloon (九龍) absorbed a significant portion of these new arrivals, comprising merchants, political exiles, and displaced peasants alike.

## Significance
The census data provided colonial authorities with critical metrics regarding housing, public health, and social control during a volatile period in regional history. Modern scholarship often utilizes these records to map the immediate human cost and migration patterns associated with the Xinhai Revolution (辛亥革命). However, historians continue to debate the exact precision of these figures, given the transient nature of the refugee population and the methodological limitations of early colonial record-keeping.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Colonial officials viewed the census as a vital administrative tool necessary for maintaining public order, managing strained infrastructure, and monitoring potential security threats posed by the influx of political agitators and refugees from the mainland.
- **Qing Loyalists and Mainland Refugees**: For many refugees and political exiles fleeing the collapse of the Qing Dynasty, Hong Kong served as a crucial sanctuary from violence, though many anticipated a eventual return once stability was restored on the mainland.
