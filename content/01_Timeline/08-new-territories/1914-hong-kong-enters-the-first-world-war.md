## Background

By **August 4, 1914**, the intensification of European rivalries culminated in Britain's declaration of war against Germany. As a strategic British crown colony in East Asia, Hong Kong was immediately integrated into the British Empire's global defense and economic strategies. The colony's proximity to German-leased territories in China, particularly Qingdao (青島), heightened anxieties regarding regional security and colonial stability.

## What happened

Upon receiving news of the war, Governor **Sir Francis Henry May** (梅含理) enacted emergency measures to secure the colony. The Hong Kong Volunteer Defence Corps (香港義勇防衛軍) was mobilized to patrol critical infrastructure and maintain internal security. Concurrently, the colonial administration moved swiftly against the local German community. German and Austro-Hungarian nationals deemed of military age were interned, while valuable German commercial enterprises and shipping assets within the harbor were confiscated by the government. Censorship was imposed on telegraphic and postal communications to prevent intelligence leaks to enemy forces.

## Significance

The outbreak of the First World War marked a significant turning point in Hong Kong's twentieth-century trajectory, demonstrating the profound impact of European geopolitical conflicts on distant Asian territories. It underscored the colony's dual identity as both a thriving commercial entrepôt and a vital imperial military outpost. The emergency powers invoked during this period also expanded the scope of colonial governance and surveillance, setting precedents for state control during subsequent crises.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Viewed the mobilization and internment measures as essential, patriotic duties necessary to protect imperial interests, secure vital trade routes, and neutralize potential fifth-column threats within the colony.
- **Local German Community**: Experienced sudden disruption, economic ruin, and the loss of civil liberties as long-standing business networks and community institutions were dismantled overnight by colonial authorities.
- **Chinese Local Community**: Observed the European conflict with a mixture of detachment and economic anxiety, as wartime shipping disruptions and trade shifts impacted local livelihoods and markets.
