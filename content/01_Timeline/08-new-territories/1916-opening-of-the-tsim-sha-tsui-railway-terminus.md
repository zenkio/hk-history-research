## Background

The **Kowloon-Canton Railway (九廣鐵路)**, British Section, was conceived to connect the bustling port of Hong Kong with mainland China, competing with other imperial rail networks of the era. Construction of the southern section began in 1906, facing severe engineering challenges through the rugged terrain of the New Territories. The terminus at **Tsim Sha Tsui (尖沙咀)** was designed to reclaim land from Victoria Harbour, creating a grand terminal station that reflected the architectural ambitions of the British colonial administration.

## What happened

On **March 28, 1916**, the Tsim Sha Tsui Railway Terminus was formally opened to the public. While construction of the iconic red-brick and granite station building and its famous Clock Tower (鐘樓) continued in phases, the opening marked the operational centralization of railway traffic on the Kowloon peninsula. The terminus served as the primary nexus where passengers transferred between local steamers and long-distance trains heading north toward Guangzhou (廣州).

## Significance

The completion of the terminus anchored Kowloon as the colony's primary international passenger and rail gateway, physically linking British-administered Hong Kong directly to the Chinese mainland. Architecturally, the station and its surviving Clock Tower became enduring landmarks of the urban landscape. Strategically, the rail link intensified economic and social integration across the border, though this connectivity would frequently be tested by the volatile political upheavals in early 20th-century China.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration**: Colonial authorities viewed the railway and its grand terminus as a triumph of modern engineering and a vital commercial link that would secure Hong Kong's economic primacy as the entrepôt of South China.
- **Local and New Territories communities**: For many local residents and villagers whose lands were surveyed or resumed for the railway's construction, the project represented heavy-handed colonial intrusion, though it later offered new avenues for regional trade and mobility.
- **Qing and early Republican perspectives**: Chinese officials and nationalists observed the British-run railway with mixed feelings of technological admiration and sovereignty concerns, as foreign imperial powers carved out transportation monopolies on Chinese soil.
