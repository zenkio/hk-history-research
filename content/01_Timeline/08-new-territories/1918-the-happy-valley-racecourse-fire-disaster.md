## Background

By the early 20th century, the annual Hong Kong Derby held at the Happy Valley Racecourse (跑馬地馬場) had become a major social and sporting event for the colony. On **February 26, 1918**, the second day of the annual race meeting, massive crowds gathered to watch the races. To accommodate the surging attendance, temporary bamboo and wooden grandstands were constructed around the field. Due to the high demand for viewing space, these structures were heavily congested with spectators from diverse backgrounds.

## What happened

On the afternoon of the race, disaster struck when one of the temporary bamboo and wooden grandstands collapsed under the immense weight of the crowd. The sudden structural failure caused panic, and shortly afterward, a fire broke out, rapidly consuming the highly flammable building materials. Because the stands were densely packed and exit routes were inadequate, many spectators were trapped. Contemporary estimates suggest that over 600 people lost their lives, making it the deadliest disaster in Hong Kong's colonial history.

## Significance

The sheer scale of the tragedy shocked the colonial administration and the local populace alike, leading to immediate investigations into venue construction and crowd management. In the aftermath, the government instituted sweeping reforms in public venue safety standards, building regulations, and fire prevention policies. The disaster also profoundly impacted the management of the Royal Hong Kong Jockey Club (香港賽馬會), prompting a permanent transition to more robust, non-combustible grandstand structures and stricter capacity limits for public gatherings.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration**: Viewed the disaster primarily as a severe regulatory failure regarding temporary structures, prompting an overhaul of municipal building codes and public safety oversight to prevent future recurrence.
- **Local Hong Kong communities**: Experienced the tragedy as an immense communal grief that devastated families across the colony, leading to coordinated charitable relief efforts and collective mourning for the hundreds of victims.
