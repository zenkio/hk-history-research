## Background
Founded in **1886** by **Sir Patrick Manson** (曼遜) and a group of local businessmen, the Dairy Farm, Ice and Cold Storage Company initially operated a small dairy in Pok Fu Lam to provide a hygienic milk supply to the local population. By the early **1920s**, rapid urbanization and population growth in Hong Kong increased the demand for safe, reliable dairy products, prompting the company to seek expansion beyond its original footprint.

## What happened
In **1923**, Dairy Farm substantially consolidated and expanded its facilities in Pok Fu Lam (薄扶林). The enterprise incorporated modern agricultural technologies of the era, including improved refrigeration, mechanized milking processes, and veterinary oversight to manage the imported cattle herds. This expansion transformed the pastoral landscape of the Pok Fu Lam valley into an industrial-scale dairy operation, supported by a network of local transport and urban distribution depots.

## Significance
The establishment and scaling of the Pok Fu Lam Farm marked a crucial turning point in the sanitary modernization of Hong Kong's food supply. From the colonial administration's perspective, the venture was praised for mitigating public health risks associated with unpasteurized milk and traditional dairy practices. Concurrently, the availability of commercially produced dairy products gradually reshaped the dietary habits and consumption patterns of the urban population, integrating Western-style dairy consumption into daily life for middle- and upper-class residents.
