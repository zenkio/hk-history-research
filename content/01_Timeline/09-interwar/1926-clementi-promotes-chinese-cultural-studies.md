## Background

During the 1920s, Hong Kong experienced severe political unrest, most notably the Canton-Hong Kong strike of **1925**–**1926**. Influenced by the rise of radical nationalism and anti-imperialist sentiment from the Chinese mainland, the colonial government sought effective strategies to stabilize the colony. Governor **Sir Cecil Clementi** (金文泰), who assumed office in **1925**, was an accomplished scholar of Chinese language and literature. Unlike some of his predecessors, Clementi believed that engaging directly with traditional Chinese elites and promoting classical scholarship could foster a more moderate, stable leadership within the Chinese community.

## What happened

In **1926**, Governor Clementi successfully established the Department of Chinese at the University of Hong Kong (香港大學). To lend the new department prestige and authority, prominent traditional scholars, such as **Lai Chi-yuk** (賴際熙) and **Au Tai-tin** (區大典), were appointed to teach classical Chinese literature, history, and philosophy. The curriculum emphasized traditional Confucian texts and historical studies rather than modern, revolutionary political thought. Clementi also supported the establishment of schools that taught through the medium of classical studies and maintained close ties with conservative scholars who had fled the political turmoil of the Republican era.

## Significance

The creation of the Chinese Department marked a significant turning point in the educational and cultural policy of British Hong Kong. It elevated the status of Chinese studies within a predominantly English-medium institution and institutionalized Hong Kong's role as a sanctuary for traditional Chinese scholarship away from the turbulent political movements of Republican China. The long-term impact of Clementi's cultural policies remains a subject of analysis among historians studying colonial governance, identity formation, and educational development in the colony.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration**: Viewed the promotion of traditional Chinese culture as a stabilizing measure to cultivate moderate, loyal local elites and counter the radical, anti-British nationalism emanating from the mainland.
- **Conservative Chinese scholars and elites**: Welcomed the initiative as a vital patronage of classical learning and a sanctuary for preserving traditional Chinese civilization amidst the iconoclasm of the Republican era.
- **Nationalist and modernizing perspectives**: Sometimes criticized these policies as a deliberate attempt by the colonial power to cultivate political conservatism and divert the local population away from modern patriotic and revolutionary movements.
