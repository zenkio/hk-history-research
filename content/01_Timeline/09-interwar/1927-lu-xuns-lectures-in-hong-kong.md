## Background
By **1927**, the prominent writer and intellectual **Lu Xun** (魯迅) had established himself as a leading figure of the New Culture Movement in China. Amidst political turmoil on the mainland, including the fracturing of the First United Front, **Lu Xun** traveled south, eventually arriving in British Hong Kong on **February 18, 1927**. His visit occurred against the backdrop of a growing Chinese-language press and intellectual community in the colony, which had been invigorated by recent events such as the **1925-1926 Canton-Hong Kong strike**. Local educators and student organizations eagerly anticipated his arrival to hear his thoughts on literature and modern society.

## What happened
During his brief stay in Hong Kong, **Lu Xun** delivered two major public lectures. The first, titled 'The Silent China' (無聲的中國), was given on **February 18, 1927**, at the YMCA Hall on Bridges Street. A few days later, on **February 22, 1927**, he delivered his second lecture, titled 'Literature of a Revolutionary Period' (革命時代的文學), at the Hong Kong City Hall. Both events drew large crowds consisting of students, teachers, and local intellectuals. In his talks, he addressed the power of the written word, the state of Chinese literature, and the responsibilities of writers during times of profound social change.

## Significance
The lectures served as a major catalyst for local literary discourse and modern cultural debates in Hong Kong. While colonial authorities monitored the activities of prominent mainland intellectuals closely, the event highlighted Hong Kong's role as a vital refuge and intellectual crossroads for Chinese thinkers. Later scholarship notes that **Lu Xun**'s visit helped foster a more politically engaged Chinese-language literary scene in the colony, bridging mainland revolutionary currents with local intellectual life.
