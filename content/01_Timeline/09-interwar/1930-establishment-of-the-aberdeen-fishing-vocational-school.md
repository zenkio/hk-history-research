## Background
During the **1930s**, Hong Kong's maritime population, particularly the boat-dwelling Tanka community, largely existed on the margins of mainstream colonial society. Traditional floating households relied entirely on generational fishing knowledge, facing severe economic vulnerability due to changing market conditions, piracy, and modernization pressures. Educational access for children in these fishing communities was virtually non-existent, as land-based schools were geographically and culturally distant from their daily realities.
