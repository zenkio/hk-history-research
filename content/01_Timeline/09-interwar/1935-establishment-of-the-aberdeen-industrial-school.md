## Background
During the interwar period, particularly in the early **1930s**, Hong Kong experienced a significant influx of refugees and migrants fleeing economic instability and political turmoil in mainland China. This rapid population growth severely strained urban infrastructure and exacerbated issues of urban poverty, homelessness, and juvenile delinquency. In response to these pressing social challenges, both colonial authorities and religious organizations sought ways to expand welfare provisions and social safety nets for vulnerable populations, particularly young males.

## What happened
In **1935**, the **Salesians of Don Bosco (慈幼會)**, a Catholic religious order with a long history of educational and youth work, officially established the **Aberdeen Industrial School (香港仔工業學校)** in the southern district of Hong Kong Island. Situated in Aberdeen (香港仔), the institution was designed to offer practical vocational training alongside basic education. Students were instructed in various trades—such as carpentry, shoemaking, and mechanics—intended to equip them with marketable skills for employment in Hong Kong's developing industrial economy. The facility also provided residential care, food, and moral guidance for orphans and impoverished boys.

## Significance
The founding of the school marked a notable development in the history of Hong Kong's social welfare and educational systems. While the colonial government of the era largely adhered to a policy of non-intervention in many social spheres, it increasingly relied on and subsidized religious and charitable organizations to manage welfare and education. The institution addressed the immediate crisis of juvenile vagrancy while supplying a disciplined, semi-skilled workforce that contributed to the colony's light manufacturing sector in the years leading up to the Second World War.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration**: Colonial officials viewed the establishment of religious-run industrial schools as a cost-effective method to mitigate urban crime and juvenile delinquency without significantly expanding direct government expenditure on social welfare.
- **Salesian missionaries**: The organizers and religious leaders perceived their mission primarily as a moral and humanitarian duty to rescue destitute youth from poverty, providing them with both spiritual salvation and practical tools for self-reliance.
