## Background

Following the Marco Polo Bridge Incident on **July 7, 1937** (七七事變), the Second Sino-Japanese War erupted in full scale. As Imperial Japanese forces advanced rapidly through northern and coastal China, civilian populations faced intense violence, bombing, and displacement. Hong Kong (香港), operating as a British colony, initially remained isolated from the direct mainland frontlines, presenting a precarious safe haven for the surrounding Guangdong (廣東) region.

## What happened

Between 1937 and 1941, an estimated hundreds of thousands of refugees crossed the border from mainland China into Hong Kong. This sudden surge drastically swelled the colony's population, overwhelming existing housing, food supplies, and public health infrastructure. Makeshift squatter settlements proliferated across urban areas like Victoria City (維多利亞城) and parts of the New Territories (新界). Local authorities and charitable organizations struggled to provide adequate relief, shelter, and sanitation, leading to severe overcrowding and heightened vulnerability to disease.

## Significance

This unprecedented demographic shock fundamentally transformed Hong Kong's social and economic fabric. While the influx provided a large pool of cheap labor that aided certain manufacturing sectors, it also created acute humanitarian and administrative crises for the colonial government. The experience of the 1937 influx prefigured the much larger refugee waves that would follow the Chinese Civil War a decade later, permanently altering the territory's identity as a place shaped by migration.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Colonial officials viewed the influx primarily as an administrative and security challenge that severely strained public resources, threatened sanitation, and risked drawing Japanese retaliation or infiltration.
- **Local Communities and Charities**: Chinese residents and charitable networks in Hong Kong saw the refugees as compatriots in urgent need of humanitarian aid, mobilizing extensive relief efforts despite the overwhelming scale of the crisis.
