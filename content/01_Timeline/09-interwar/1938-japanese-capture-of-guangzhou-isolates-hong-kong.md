## Background
Leading up to **October 21, 1938**, the Second Sino-Japanese War had increasingly destabilized southern China. Guangzhou (廣州) served as the economic and logistical anchor for Hong Kong's trade with the mainland, connected by the Kowloon-Canton Railway (九廣鐵路). As the Imperial Japanese Army advanced through Guangdong province, the safety and economic viability of the British crown colony grew precarious, forcing colonial authorities to prepare for potential encirclement.

## What happened
On **October 21, 1938**, Japanese forces successfully occupied Guangzhou after a rapid campaign following a surprise landing at Daya Bay (大亞灣). The fall of the city was swifter than many British military planners had anticipated. With Guangzhou under Japanese control, the northern section of the Kowloon-Canton Railway was severed, halting the direct flow of passengers and cargo between Hong Kong and mainland China. Hostile forces were now stationed directly at the border of the New Territories (新界), dramatically altering the strategic landscape.

## Significance
The capture of Guangzhou fundamentally isolated Hong Kong economically and strategically, turning the colony into a vulnerable, surrounded enclave. The disruption of food and raw material supplies from the mainland exacerbated economic pressures within the territory. While the colony remained nominally neutral for the time being, the event foreshadowed the eventual Pacific War and the Japanese occupation of Hong Kong that began in December **1941**.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Colonial officials viewed the fall of Guangzhou with mounting alarm, recognizing that the defense of Hong Kong had become a nearly impossible strategic liability, yet they remained committed to maintaining neutrality and preserving trade where possible.
- **Local Hong Kong and Mainland Chinese Residents**: Local communities and mainland refugees perceived the event as a closing ring of tragedy, as Guangzhou's fall cut off escape routes and dramatically increased the influx of refugees seeking safety in the crowded urban areas of Hong Kong.
