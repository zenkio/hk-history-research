## Background
By the late 1930s, the urban landscape of Victoria City (維多利亞城) required modernization to address growing population densities and public health concerns. The previous market facilities had become inadequate for managing food safety and retail organization. In response, the colonial administration and the Public Works Department planned a state-of-the-art municipal building to centralize and regulate meat and produce vendors.

## What happened
On **May 1, 1939**, the new Central Market Building (中央街市) officially opened its doors to the public. Designed in the distinctive Streamline Moderne and Bauhaus styles, the structure featured reinforced concrete, functional symmetrical layouts, improved ventilation, and advanced sanitation standards that set a new benchmark for municipal buildings in the region.

## Significance
The opening of the Central Market represented a major shift in colonial urban management, emphasizing hygiene, municipal control, and European modernist architectural aesthetics in the heart of Hong Kong Island.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Viewed the project as a triumph of modern urban planning and public hygiene, demonstrating the government's commitment to improving colonial infrastructure and public health standards during the interwar period.
- **Local Vendors and Residents**: Experienced the transition as both an improvement in sanitary facilities and a tightening of municipal regulation over traditional daily commerce and wet market operations.
