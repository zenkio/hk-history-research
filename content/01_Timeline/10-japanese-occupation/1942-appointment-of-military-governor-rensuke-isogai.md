## Background
Following the defeat of British forces and the formal surrender of Hong Kong on Christmas Day, December 25, 1941, the territory entered a period of transition under temporary military administration. The Japanese Imperial Army sought to consolidate control over the strategic port city, integrating it into the broader Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere. On **February 20, 1942**, Lieutenant General **Rensuke Isogai** (**磯谷廉介**) was officially appointed as the first Japanese Military Governor of Hong Kong, replacing the initial provisional military administration.

## What happened
Upon taking office, **Rensuke Isogai** established the Military Governor's Office, which centralized all executive, legislative, and judicial power under the control of the Japanese Imperial Army. The administration immediately began restructuring local governance to align with imperial objectives. This included renaming streets and buildings with Japanese designations, implementing harsh economic controls, and issuing military yen to replace the Hong Kong dollar. Isogai's administration also initiated policies aimed at reducing the civilian population through forced repatriation of residents to mainland China, driven by severe food shortages and resource scarcity.

## Significance
The appointment of **Rensuke Isogai** marked the definitive beginning of the three-and-a-half-year period known locally as the "Three Years and Eight Months" (三年零八個月). It institutionalized military rule and set the tone for a regime characterized by severe oppression, food rationing, and cultural assimilation policies. The administrative structures established during Isogai's tenure effectively dismantled the pre-war British colonial framework and severely impacted the local populace, leaving deep historical scars that influenced Hong Kong's collective memory of the Pacific War.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial perspective**: Viewed the administration under Rensuke Isogai as an illegal occupation that temporarily interrupted legitimate British sovereignty and governance, characterized by the brutal subjugation of the local population and the destruction of established institutions.
- **Local Hong Kong community perspective**: Remembered the period under Isogai's military government with deep trauma, marked by extreme economic hardship, starvation, forced deportations, and constant fear under arbitrary military law.
- **Japanese imperial perspective**: Framed the military governorship as a necessary administrative step to liberate the territory from Western colonial powers and integrate it into the defense and economic framework of the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere.
