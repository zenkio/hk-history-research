## Background
Prior to the outbreak of the Pacific War in **December 1941**, Hong Kong relied on a mix of imported foodstuffs and local agricultural production. **The Dairy Farm, Ice and Cold Storage Company** (香港牛奶公司), established in **1886** by **Sir Patrick Manson**, operated major dairy herds in Pok Fu Lam (薄扶林) and supplied fresh milk to the urban population. Alongside smaller pig and poultry farms across the New Territories (新界), these enterprises formed the backbone of the colony's fresh protein supply.

## What happened
Following the British surrender on **Christmas Day 1941** (香港淪陷), the Japanese military administration imposed severe controls over all commercial operations, fuel, and transport. As imports of imported cattle feed and grain ceased due to Allied blockades and maritime warfare, local livestock operations quickly ran out of sustenance. Dairy herds could no longer be maintained on imported feed, and the Japanese authorities increasingly requisitioned land, facilities, and livestock for military needs or redirected agricultural output toward the occupation forces. Consequently, **The Dairy Farm** was forced to suspend its normal operations, leading to the near-total collapse of the commercial livestock industry in Hong Kong during **1942**.

## Significance
The shutdown of major agricultural producers like Dairy Farm had immediate and devastating consequences for the civilian population. Without a reliable supply of fresh milk and protein, rates of malnutrition, deficiency diseases such as beriberi, and mortality soared, particularly among infants and the elderly. The collapse of the livestock sector also accelerated the rural-urban demographic shifts, as displaced agricultural workers and farmers abandoned their land or fled to mainland China to escape worsening starvation and urban overcrowding.

## Perspectives

- **Japanese Occupation Authorities**: Military administration reports framed the reallocation of agricultural resources and the closure of private commercial enterprises as necessary measures to sustain the garrison and streamline wartime economic mobilization.
- **Local Hong Kong Residents**: For civilians enduring the 'Three Years and Eight Months' (三年零八個月) of occupation, the termination of fresh milk and meat supplies represented a profound humanitarian disaster that directly contributed to widespread starvation and disease.
- **Later Historians**: Modern scholarship emphasizes that the collapse of enterprises like Dairy Farm illustrates the total unsustainability of Hong Kong's densely populated urban economy under strict wartime blockades and predatory colonial extraction.
