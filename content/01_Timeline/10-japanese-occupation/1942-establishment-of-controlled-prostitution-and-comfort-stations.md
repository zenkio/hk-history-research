## Background
Following the fall of Hong Kong on **December 25, 1941**, the Japanese military administration sought to regulate prostitution and manage venereal diseases among troops. Pre-existing regulated prostitution under the former British colonial administration was dismantled and replaced by a military-run apparatus. The Imperial Japanese Army and Navy established a network of designated brothels, commonly referred to as comfort stations (慰安所), across various districts of the territory.

## What happened
Between **1942 and 1945**, thousands of women and girls from Korea, China, Japan, Taiwan, and local Hong Kong communities were coerced, deceived, or forced into sexual servitude within these military brothels. Facilities such as those in Wan Chai (灣仔) and other urban centers operated under strict military surveillance. While exact figures remain a subject of ongoing historical debate due to the destruction of wartime records, contemporary documentation and survivor testimonies indicate systemic deprivation, physical abuse, and severe psychological trauma. The operation of these stations persisted until the surrender of Japan in **August 1945**.

## Significance
This institutionalized system represents one of the darkest chapters of the Japanese occupation of Hong Kong. It underscores the broader wartime atrocities committed against civilian populations across the Asia-Pacific region. The legacy of the comfort stations remains a critical issue in modern historical memory, international human rights discourse, and discussions regarding wartime reparations and accountability.

## Perspectives

- **Later Historical Scholarship**: Extensive academic research over recent decades utilizes surviving military documents and oral testimonies to frame the comfort stations as a system of institutionalized military sexual slavery orchestrated by the state.
- **Post-War and Regional Perspectives**: Interpretations vary regarding the degree of coercion versus voluntary participation, though an overwhelming body of evidence highlights widespread deception and force used against vulnerable populations.
