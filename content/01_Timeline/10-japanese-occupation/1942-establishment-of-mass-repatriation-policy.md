## Background

Following the surrender of the British colonial administration on **December 25, 1941**, Hong Kong fell under the Japanese military occupation (香港日佔時期). The urban center faced an immediate and acute food crisis. Pre-war supply lines from mainland China and Southeast Asia were severed, and the local population had swelled during the early years of the Second Sino-Japanese War as refugees fled into the territory. To conserve dwindling food supplies and reduce the risk of civil unrest or resistance, the Japanese authorities, led by Governor **Rensuke Isogai** (磯谷廉介), sought drastic measures to depopulate the city.

## What happened

On **January 5, 1942**, the Japanese military administration formally established the mass repatriation policy. Notices were issued requiring hundreds of thousands of residents to leave the urban areas and return to their ancestral homes or provinces in Guangdong and other parts of mainland China. The policy was executed in phases throughout 1942 and subsequent years. While initially targeted at recent refugees and the unemployed, the forced deportations eventually encompassed broader segments of the population. People were crammed onto overcrowded junks or forced to walk across the border under harsh conditions, leading to widespread suffering, disease, and mortality along the routes.

## Significance

This policy drastically reduced Hong Kong's population from an estimated pre-war peak of over **1.6 million** down to approximately **600,000** by 1945. It fundamentally altered the demographic and social fabric of the territory during the war years. While the immediate logistical goal of lowering urban food consumption was achieved by the occupiers, the human cost was immense. The policy remains a stark example of the brutal administrative methods employed during the occupation, leaving a lasting scar in local historical memory.

## Perspectives

- **Japanese Military Administration**: Viewed the repatriation as an unavoidable logistical and economic necessity to stabilize the occupied territory, prevent starvation, and manage dwindling urban resources under wartime conditions.
- **Local Residents and Later Scholarship**: Characterize the policy as a ruthless and inhumane act of forced displacement that demonstrated total disregard for civilian lives, causing immense hardship and loss.
