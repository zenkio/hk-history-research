## Background

Following the surrender of the British garrison in **December 1941**, the Japanese military administration faced an acute food crisis in urban Hong Kong. Disruption of wartime trade routes, combined with the collapse of local agricultural supply chains and a massive influx of wartime refugees, left the territory unable to sustain its population. To preserve military rations and maintain control over the remaining urban populace, the Japanese authorities enacted a policy of population reduction known as the **Repatriation Scheme**.

## What happened

Beginning in **1942** and continuing through **1943**, the Japanese military and the **Hong Kong Overseas Chinese Association** (香港華民代表會 and 香港華民各界協議會) systematically rounded up unemployed, destitute, and urban residents deemed non-essential. These individuals were forcibly loaded onto junks or compelled to march on foot across the border into **Guangdong** (廣東) province. Contemporary records and later historical studies indicate that the deportees faced extreme hardship, including starvation, disease, and banditry along the perilous journey into unoccupied or Japanese-occupied territories in mainland China. While exact figures remain a subject of historical debate, estimates suggest that the population of Hong Kong plummeted from over one million before the war to roughly **600,000** by the mid-1940s.

## Significance

The forced evacuation stands as one of the most severe humanitarian disasters of the occupation period, reflecting the brutal calculus of Japanese wartime governance. The policy dramatically altered the demographic composition of the territory and left deep psychological scars on the local population. Post-war historiography and local memory frequently cite these forced deportations as a primary example of the suffering endured by ordinary residents under colonial and occupation regimes alike.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial / Post-War Perspective**: British authorities and subsequent colonial-era accounts viewed the forced deportations as a grave war crime and an act of cruel demographic manipulation that demonstrated the brutal nature of the Japanese military administration.
- **Local Hong Kong Community Perspective**: Local residents and survivors remembered the event through oral histories emphasizing unimaginable survival struggles, family separations, and the perilous nature of crossing the wartime border.
- **Japanese Administration Perspective**: Military administration records framed the population reduction as an unavoidable logistical and economic necessity required to prevent total starvation in a besieged and resource-starved territory.
