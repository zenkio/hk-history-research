## Background
Following the British surrender on **December 25, 1941** (**香港保衛戰**), the Japanese military administration sought to establish firm control over Hong Kong's dense urban population. To maintain public order, curb espionage, and address severe logistical and security challenges, the occupation government looked to replicate control mechanisms already deployed in mainland China and the Japanese home islands. The foundation was laid for a comprehensive grassroots surveillance network designed to mobilize citizens and root out dissent.

## What happened
Beginning in **April 1942**, the Japanese administration instituted the neighborhood association system, known locally by its Japanese name **Tonarigumi** (**鄰組**). Under this system, urban households were organized into small neighborhood units, typically consisting of around ten neighboring families. Each unit was required to elect a leader who acted as an intermediary between the occupation authorities and the residents. 

The system functioned as an instrument of total mobilization and social control. Tonarigumi units were held collectively responsible for the behavior of all members. They were tasked with monitoring local residents, reporting suspicious activities, enforcing rationing, organizing air-raid precautions, and assisting in census-taking. Failure to report anti-Japanese sentiment or infractions by a neighbor could result in collective punishment for the entire unit. This grassroots network deeply penetrated daily life, drastically restricting freedom of movement and assembly while fostering an atmosphere of mutual suspicion among residents.

## Significance
The establishment of the **Tonarigumi** marked a critical phase in the institutionalization of the Japanese military administration in Hong Kong (**香港日佔時期**). It demonstrated the occupiers' reliance on Japanese-style administrative frameworks to govern a reluctant colonial populace with limited military and police manpower. The system severely disrupted traditional community networks and left a profound psychological impact on survivors, highlighting the intrusive and coercive nature of wartime occupation governance.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial and later Western scholarship**: View the Tonarigumi as an oppressive mechanism of totalitarian control that suppressed fundamental human rights, weaponized neighbors against one another, and demonstrated the brutal nature of the Japanese military administration.
- **Local Hong Kong community memory**: Remember the system primarily through oral histories emphasizing fear, daily anxiety, and the constant threat of collective punishment, which forced ordinary residents to navigate extreme survival constraints under severe surveillance.
