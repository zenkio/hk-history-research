## Background
Following the British surrender of Hong Kong on **December 25, 1941**, the Japanese military administration, led by Governor **Isojiro矶谷廉介** (Isojiro Isogai) and later others, initiated a policy of cultural and political assimilation known as Japanization (Koka). To eradicate British colonial influence and integrate the local population into the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere, the authorities determined that linguistic conformity was essential. Previous European dominance in administration, commerce, and education needed to be systematically replaced.

## What happened
In **July 1942**, official efforts culminated in the systematic organization and opening of Japanese language institutions, notably the Japanese Language School (日本語學校). Attendance was made compulsory for specific segments of the population, including local civil servants, teachers, and school-aged children. Curriculums were redesigned to prioritize proficiency in the Japanese language alongside ideological indoctrination. Those who failed to comply or show adequate progress faced professional penalties, reflecting the coercive nature of the occupation regime's cultural policies.

## Significance
The enforcement of language education serves as a primary example of wartime cultural engineering in occupied territories. While intended by the administration to foster loyalty and eliminate Western symbols, the policy encountered passive resistance and logistical hurdles. Textbooks and qualified instructors were frequently in short supply, limiting the long-term effectiveness of the linguistic campaign before the occupation ended in **August 1945**.

## Perspectives

- **Japanese Military Administration**: Authorities viewed the language schools as a benevolent and necessary educational reform to liberate Asians from Western colonial dependency and unite them culturally under the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere.
- **Local Hong Kong Residents**: For the local population, mandatory Japanese classes were generally perceived as an unwelcome and oppressive imposition designed to erase local identity and enforce compliance through fear and professional coercion.
