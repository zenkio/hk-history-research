## Background

Following the fall of Hong Kong on **25 December 1941** (Christmas Day), the Japanese military administration sought to control the flow of information and reshape the local media landscape. The pre-war English-language press, such as the *South China Morning Post* and the *Hong Kong Telegraph*, was suspended. To replace these outlets and reach both the remaining foreign residents and the English-reading local elite, the occupation authorities launched a new daily newspaper, the *Hong Kong News* (香港新聞), which began publication in **May 1942**.

## What happened

The *Hong Kong News* functioned primarily as an instrument for psychological warfare, military propaganda, and enforced cultural assimilation under Japanese occupation. The newspaper was used to disseminate Imperial Japanese Army communiqués, broadcast anti-Allied rhetoric, and promote the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere. It also published mandatory announcements from the Japanese military administration, detailing food rationing, population registration, and curfew regulations. Alongside political content, the paper incorporated Japanese language lessons and cultural features designed to foster a pan-Asian identity aligned with Tokyo's imperial objectives.

## Significance

The newspaper remains a critical primary source for documenting daily life, administrative policies, and official narratives during the **1941-1945** occupation period, commonly referred to as the "Three Years and Eight Months" (三年零八個月). While modern historians rely on its archives to track wartime ordinances and economic shifts, scholars note that the content is heavily skewed by military censorship and does not reflect public sentiment or the severe hardships faced by the local population.

## Perspectives

- **Japanese Military Administration**: Viewed the newspaper as a vital educational and informational tool necessary for successfully integrating Hong Kong into the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere and maintaining public order.
- **Local Hong Kong Residents and Internees**: Regarded the publication with deep suspicion and skepticism, viewing its pages as little more than a vehicle for invader propaganda and forced assimilation while hiding the reality of wartime defeats.
