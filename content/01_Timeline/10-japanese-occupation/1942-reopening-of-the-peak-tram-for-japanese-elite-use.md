## Background

Following the British surrender of Hong Kong on **25 December 1941**, the Japanese military administration established control over the territory. Victoria Peak (太平山), previously an exclusive enclave reserved primarily for the Western colonial elite under British rule, was designated for the use of senior Japanese military and civilian officials. The mansions and properties abandoned or confiscated from British and wealthy residents were repurposed to house the new occupants.

## What happened

In **June 1942**, the Peak Tram (山頂纜車), which had sustained damage during the **Battle of Hong Kong** and ceased operations, resumed limited service. Unlike its pre-war function as public transport for residents of various backgrounds living on the Peak, the reopened tramway was operated strictly for the convenience of the Japanese elite. Ordinary citizens and the majority of the local population were barred from using the service, reflecting the rigid racial and administrative hierarchy imposed by the Japanese military administration.

## Significance

The resumption of the Peak Tram symbolizes the physical and social restructuring of Hong Kong during the early phase of the Japanese occupation. By transforming the Peak into a segregated administrative and residential zone for conquerors, the occupation authorities mirrored and intensified the spatial segregation previously practiced by the British colonial government, while redirecting the territory's infrastructure to serve the Japanese war and governance machine.

## Perspectives

- **Japanese Military Administration**: Viewed the requisition of the Peak and the reopening of the tram as a necessary logistical measure to ensure the efficient administration and security of senior officials governing the occupied territory.
- **Local Hong Kong Residents**: Experienced the exclusive reopening of the Peak infrastructure as a continuation and intensification of colonial-style spatial exclusion, reinforcing their disenfranchisement under the new regime.
