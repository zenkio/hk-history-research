## Background

Before the **Battle of Hong Kong** in **December 1941**, the University of Hong Kong (香港大學) served as the premier institution of higher learning in the British colony. As hostilities escalated and the Imperial Japanese Army invaded from Shenzhen, the campus and its premises were pressed into emergency service. Following the British surrender on **25 December 1941** (**Christmas Day**), the Japanese military authorities sought to reorganize the infrastructure of the captured territory to support their ongoing military operations in the region.

## What happened

In **1942**, the occupying forces commandeered major buildings of the university, notably the Main Building (本部大樓) and surrounding facilities. The university was repurposed to function as a military hospital for the Imperial Japanese Army. Academic programs were suspended, and faculty members, students, and staff were dispersed. Some staff members managed to flee to Free China, while others were interned or forced to navigate the hardships of occupied Hong Kong. The architectural spaces underwent significant alterations to accommodate medical wards, surgical units, and administrative offices necessary for military healthcare.

## Significance

The seizure of the university buildings symbolized the abrupt termination of British colonial educational institutions and the total mobilization of Hong Kong's infrastructure for the Japanese war effort. It marked a dark period for academic continuity in the territory. The conversion not only disrupted the education of an entire generation of students but also left a physical imprint on the university's historic structures, which would require extensive rehabilitation after the war ended in **1945**.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial / Institutional**: From the perspective of the university administration and British colonial authorities, the seizure was viewed as a blatant disruption of academic freedom and institutional continuity, resulting in the tragic halt of education and the displacement of scholars.
- **Japanese Military**: The Imperial Japanese Army viewed the requisition of large, Western-style institutional buildings as a military necessity to provide critical medical infrastructure for their garrison and wounded personnel during the occupation.
- **Local Hong Kong Community**: For local residents, the transformation of a prominent civilian institution into a restricted military zone underscored the harsh realities of wartime governance and the complete subjugation of local civilian life to the occupying regime's needs.
