## Background
Following the fall of Hong Kong on **December 25, 1941**, the Japanese military imprisoned thousands of Allied military personnel, primarily British troops, at camps such as Sham Shui Po (深水埗). By **September 1942**, the Japanese authorities decided to transfer a large contingent of these prisoners to mainland Japan to serve as forced labor.

On **September 25, 1942**, over 1,800 British prisoners of war (POWs) were crammed into the hold of the armed Japanese freighter *Lisbon Maru* (里斯本丸) in Victoria Harbour (維多利亞港). The vessel was completely unmarked as a prisoner transport, violating the Geneva Convention regarding the identification of ships carrying POWs.

## What happened
On **October 1, 1942**, as the *Lisbon Maru* navigated the waters near the Zhoushan Islands (舟山群島) off the coast of Zhejiang province, it was struck by a torpedo fired by the American submarine **USS Grouper**. Unaware that Allied prisoners were aboard, the submarine targeted the vessel as a legitimate enemy military transport.

The ship did not sink immediately, but the Japanese military battened down the hatches, sealing the British prisoners in the dark, suffocating hold for over 24 hours without adequate ventilation, food, or water. During this time, the Japanese crew abandoned ship, leaving the prisoners trapped.

Eventually, some prisoners managed to break out of the hold as the ship finally sank on **October 2, 1942**. Japanese vessels fired upon some of the survivors swimming in the water. However, local Chinese fishermen from the nearby islands heroically rescued over 380 British survivors from the sea, sheltering them before eventually handing most over to the authorities, though the Imperial Japanese military ultimately recaptured and detained many of them.

## Significance
The disaster resulted in the deaths of over 800 British prisoners, making it one of the single largest losses of British military life during the Pacific War. The event left a deep, painful legacy in UK-Japan relations and became a prominent symbol of the brutality of the Japanese occupation of Hong Kong. In later decades, the rescue by Chinese fishermen has also been emphasized to highlight cross-national wartime solidarity between local Chinese communities and Allied forces.

## Perspectives

- **British Historical and Veteran Perspective**: Emphasizes the criminal negligence and brutality of the Japanese military in failing to mark the ship, sealing prisoners below deck, and firing on survivors in the water, viewing it as a major war crime.
- **Chinese Local and Historical Perspective**: Highlights the humanitarian rescue efforts of the Zhoushan fishermen who risked their lives to save drowning British soldiers, framing it as an act of international heroism during the war against Japanese aggression.
- **Japanese Wartime Perspective**: Viewed the transport as a military necessity under wartime conditions, often attributing the primary responsibility for the loss of life to the American submarine attack rather than the subsequent treatment of prisoners.
