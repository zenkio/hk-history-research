## Background

Following the surrender of the British garrison in **December 1941** (十二月), the Japanese military administration faced escalating supply shortages across the territory of Hong Kong (香港). As Allied blockades tightened across the Pacific and the war turned against the Japanese Empire, the import of crude oil, refined gasoline, and automotive spare parts dropped to near zero. By **1943** (一九四三), the maintenance of public buses, trams, and private automobiles became entirely unsustainable. 

## What happened

During **October 1943** (十月), the paralysis of motorized vehicles was formalized and visibly complete on the streets. Major transport operators, including the **China Motor Bus Company** (中華汽車有限公司) and **Hongkong Tramways** (香港電車有限公司), suspended or severely restricted their mechanized services due to a total lack of fuel and lubricants. 

In the absence of motorized transit, the urban landscape of Hong Kong underwent a profound physical transformation. The populace had to rely almost exclusively on human-powered transport. **Rickshaws** (人力車), which had previously been declining in number due to modern bus and tram expansion, experienced a dramatic resurgence. Bicycles (單車) also became vital tools for personal movement and light transport, despite strict Japanese military controls and registration requirements. For the vast majority of residents, daily commuting, commercial hauling, and inter-district travel were accomplished entirely on foot.

## Significance

The collapse of motorized transit severely disrupted the economic recovery and daily survival of Hong Kong's residents under the **Japanese occupation** (香港日治時期). The reliance on human-powered mobility heightened logistical bottlenecks for the occupation administration and deepened the physical hardships endured by ordinary citizens, who faced exhaustion, malnutrition, and prolonged travel times across the territory.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial perspective**: Wartime reports and post-war colonial assessments framed the breakdown of transport as a direct result of aggressive Japanese resource extraction and mismanagement, which destroyed the modern urban infrastructure built under British administration.
- **Local Hong Kong community perspective**: Oral histories and local accounts emphasize the sheer physical endurance required by ordinary residents to survive daily life, turning to walking, improvised bicycles, and rickshaws simply to secure scarce rations and maintain community survival.
