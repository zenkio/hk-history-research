## Background

Following the fall of Hong Kong to the Japanese in **December 1941**, the territory entered a period known historically as the "Three Years and Eight Months" (三年零八個月). Food supplies plummeted rapidly due to the disruption of maritime trade, Allied blockades, and the diversion of agricultural and marine resources to the Japanese war effort. By **1943**, severe inflation, hoarding, and rampant black-market activity left ordinary citizens unable to afford or legally source basic foodstuffs. In response to the worsening humanitarian crisis and dwindling reserves, the Japanese occupation authorities sought tighter control over the distribution of essential commodities.

## What happened

In **March 1943**, the administration rolled out a formal household rationing system centered on staple foods, primarily rice (米). Residents were required to register their households and obtain rationing coupons (配給券) issued through local neighborhood associations and designated distribution points. Allocations were strictly pegged to age, labor status, and availability, but the quantities provided were routinely inadequate for basic human sustenance. Official rations fell far short of daily caloric needs, compelling residents to supplement their diets through the black market, foraging, or trading personal possessions for food. The inability to survive on official rations alone drove widespread lawbreaking, with citizens risking severe penalties, imprisonment, or public execution for smuggling or hoarding unauthorized provisions.

## Significance

The implementation of the ration coupon system marked a turning point in the total mobilization and militarization of civilian life in occupied Hong Kong. It highlighted the complete breakdown of the colonial free-market economy and underscored the failure of the occupation administration to sustain the local population. The inadequacy of the system deepened the suffering of residents, directly contributing to soaring mortality rates, malnutrition, and a massive demographic flight through the forced and voluntary repatriation policies to mainland China. Long after the war, the memory of rationing remained a defining symbol of the hardship and deprivation experienced under Japanese military rule.

## Perspectives

- **Japanese occupation administration**: Authorities viewed the rationing system as a necessary administrative measure to manage dwindling resources equitably, curb inflation, and suppress illegal hoarding and black-market speculation during wartime constraints.
- **Local Hong Kong residents**: For ordinary citizens, the coupons represented starvation rather than relief, as official allocations were grossly insufficient, forcing them to take extreme personal risks on the black market simply to stay alive.
