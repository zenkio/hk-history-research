## Background

Following the British surrender of Hong Kong in **December 1941**, the Japanese military administration sought to reshape the local cultural landscape to align with the objectives of the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere. Cinemas and entertainment venues were temporarily closed during the immediate aftermath of the battle, then gradually reopened under strict state supervision. The administration viewed cultural spaces as vital tools for psychological conditioning, aiming to suppress British colonial influence and foster pro-Japanese sentiment among the residents of Hong Kong (香港).

## What happened

In **May 1943**, the **Dai To Theater (大東戲院)**—formerly known as the Pearl Theater—officially opened under its new Japanese-sanctioned management. The venue was designated for controlled entertainment, primarily screening Japanese military propaganda films, newsreels detailing imperial conquests, and approved cultural performances. Attendance at these screenings was often monitored, and local residents were expected to participate in public displays of obedience, such as bowing toward Tokyo and adopting Japanese cultural norms.

## Significance

The opening of the Dai To Theater exemplifies the broader policy of total mobilization and cultural assimilation enforced during the Japanese occupation (香港日佔時期). Entertainment was no longer viewed as a private leisure activity, but as a mandatory civic duty directed toward ideological indoctrination. While wartime records are scarce, the utilization of mass media highlights the regime's reliance on psychological warfare to maintain control over a resistant and starving urban population.

## Perspectives

- **Japanese Military Administration**: Viewed the theater as an essential instrument for enlightening the local population, fostering Greater East Asia solidarity, and replacing decadent Western colonial culture with virtuous imperial values.
- **Local Hong Kong Residents**: Generally regarded the propaganda screenings and enforced cultural events with profound cynicism, attending primarily out of survival necessity while attempting to avoid suspicion from the Kempeitai.
