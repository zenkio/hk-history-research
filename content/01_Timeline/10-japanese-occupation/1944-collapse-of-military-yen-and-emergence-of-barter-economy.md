## Background

During the Japanese occupation of Hong Kong (香港日治時期) starting in December 1941, the Japanese military administration imposed the military yen (軍票) as the sole legal tender, eventually banning the Hong Kong dollar (香港元). Initially, the exchange rate was pegged artificially, but as the Pacific War turned against Japan, the occupation authorities printed excessive amounts of military yen to finance wartime expenditures without any backing reserves.

## What happened

By **December 1944**, the hyperinflation of the military yen reached a catastrophic stage, rendering the currency virtually worthless. Prices for basic foodstuffs soared astronomically, and merchants increasingly refused to accept paper military yen. To stave off starvation, local residents were forced to resort to a primitive barter economy on the thriving black market. Families traded personal belongings, including clothing, jewelry, and ancestral heirlooms, directly for small quantities of rice, sweet potatoes, or firewood. Transactions often occurred in clandestine settings to evade Kempeitai (憲兵隊) surveillance and severe penalties for illegal trading.

## Significance

The total collapse of the monetary system exposed the complete failure of Japanese wartime administration and inflicted immense psychological and physical suffering on the civilian population. The widespread reliance on barter demonstrated the breakdown of formal economic structures, leaving deep memories of deprivation and hardship that shaped post-war attitudes toward currency stability and British colonial restoration.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial & Post-War Perspective**: British authorities and subsequent historical accounts viewed the collapse of the military yen as inevitable evidence of reckless Japanese fiscal mismanagement and economic exploitation, which deliberately impoverished the local population.
- **Local Hong Kong Resident Perspective**: For ordinary citizens, the collapse of currency represented a desperate daily struggle for survival, where traditional wealth and heirlooms lost all protective value against starvation, forcing communities to rely on mutual aid and clandestine black markets.
- **Wartime Japanese Administration Perspective**: Japanese occupation authorities officially blamed wartime shortages and speculative activities for the inflation, while struggling to maintain control over dwindling food supplies and distribution networks.
