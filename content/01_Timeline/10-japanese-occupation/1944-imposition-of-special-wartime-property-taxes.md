## Background
By **1944**, the Japanese military administration in Hong Kong faced severe financial strain as the Pacific War turned against the Japanese Empire. The local administration, operating under the Japanese military governor, struggled to cover the costs of occupation, infrastructure maintenance, and garrison upkeep. Having previously introduced the military yen (軍票) without adequate backing, the occupation authorities sought new methods to extract wealth from the local population and stabilize their fiscal operations.

## What happened
In **February 1944**, the occupation government instituted heavy special wartime property taxes and municipal levies targeting urban real estate owners. A defining feature of this tax imposition was the strict requirement that all payments be made in military yen rather than Hong Kong dollars or other currencies. Property owners who had already suffered immense losses from wartime destruction, plummeting rental values, and economic stagnation found themselves unable to meet the exorbitant demands. Failure to pay often resulted in the confiscation of real estate by the occupation authorities, further dislocating the urban property market and transferring ownership to entities favored by the military administration.

## Significance
This fiscal measure marked a critical phase of intensified economic exploitation during the three and a half years of Japanese occupation, often referred to by local residents as the "three years and eight months" (**三年零八個月**). The enforcement of taxes payable solely in military yen accelerated the collapse of the local economy and deepened the impoverishment of the urban middle class and property-owning elites. It demonstrated the desperate measures taken by the colonial administration to sustain its wartime apparatus at the direct expense of the local civilian population.

## Perspectives

- **Local Hong Kong Residents and Property Owners**: Viewed the property taxes as a predatory and confiscatory measure designed to strip locals of their remaining assets and real estate holdings amid widespread starvation and economic ruin.
- **Japanese Occupation Administration**: Justified the taxes as necessary fiscal policies to maintain municipal order, fund essential public services, and support the wartime defense of the territory.
