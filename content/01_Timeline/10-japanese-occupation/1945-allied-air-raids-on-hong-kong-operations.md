## Background

By **January 1945**, the Pacific War had shifted decisively against the Japanese Empire. Hong Kong (香港), occupied by Japan since December 1941, served as a vital naval and logistical hub for Japanese forces in South China and Southeast Asia. As part of a broader campaign to sever Japanese supply lines and support operations in the Philippines and South China Sea, the United States Navy's Task Force 38 prepared to strike strategic military and maritime targets within the territory.

## What happened

On **January 16, 1945**, American carrier-based aircraft—primarily from TF 38—conducted heavy bombing raids over Hong Kong in an operation known as **Operation Gratitude**. The strikes focused on military installations, harbor facilities, the **Kai Tak (啟德)** airfield, and docked shipping to cripple Japanese logistics. Anti-aircraft fire from Japanese positions engaged the attackers, while Allied pilots dropped high explosives and incendiary bombs. The raids resulted in significant damage to port infrastructure and shipping vessels, though they also caused substantial collateral damage and civilian casualties in surrounding urban areas.

## Significance

The air raids marked a turning point in the physical devastation experienced by Hong Kong during the Pacific War, directly bringing the frontline conflict to the occupied populace. While the strikes effectively degraded Japanese maritime capabilities and logistical throughput, they also underscored the heavy human cost borne by local residents. The bombings foreshadowed further Allied air campaigns that would continue until the Japanese surrender in **August 1945**.

## Perspectives

- **Allied Command**: From the perspective of the U.S. military, the raids were a tactical necessity required to destroy enemy shipping, disrupt supply lines, and neutralize a major Japanese naval base in support of Pacific theater offensives.
- **Local Hong Kong Residents**: For the civilian population enduring the harsh conditions of the Japanese occupation, the Allied air raids brought terror, destruction of homes, and heavy casualties, complicating the longing for liberation with the immediate trauma of bombardment.
