## Background
The surrender ceremony on **September 16, 1945** followed the sudden cessation of hostilities across the Pacific at the end of World War II. Following the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and the subsequent broadcast of the Emperor's surrender announcement in August, Allied forces moved quickly to reassert control over occupied territories. Rear Admiral **Cecil Harcourt** (夏慤) arrived in Hong Kong waters to accept the surrender of Japanese forces, heading the newly established British military administration.

## What happened
The formal surrender ceremony took place at Government House (港督府) in Central. Major General **Umetaro Nagano** (梅澤梅太郎) and Vice Admiral **Ruitaro Fujita** (藤田類太郎) signed the instrument of surrender on behalf of the Imperial Japanese Army and Navy. Rear Admiral Harcourt accepted the surrender on behalf of both the British government and the wider Allied powers. The proceedings marked the official transition from military occupation back to British colonial administration.

## Significance
This event symbolized the restoration of pre-war sovereignty and the closure of the occupation period, locally known as the "Three Years and Eight Months" (三年零八個月). However, the immediate post-war era brought significant socio-economic challenges, including severe food shortages, infrastructure devastation, and the complex task of repatriating displaced residents and surrendered Japanese troops.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Viewed the surrender and the subsequent resumption of control at Government House as the rightful restoration of legitimate governance and the rule of law after a period of illegal enemy occupation.
- **Local Hong Kong Residents**: Experienced the surrender primarily as a profound relief from severe wartime deprivation, forced inflation, and military terror, though they faced immediate post-war economic hardships and scarcity.
