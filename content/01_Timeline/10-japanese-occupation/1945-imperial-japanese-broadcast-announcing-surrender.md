## Background

By August **1945**, the Japanese military administration in Hong Kong, led by governors such as **Rensuke Isogai** (磯谷廉介), faced imminent collapse following the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and the Soviet entry into the war. The nearly four years of the Japanese occupation, locally remembered as the "Three Years and Eight Months" (三年零八個月), had brought severe economic hardship, food shortages, and forced deportations to the local population. Guerrilla forces, notably the **East River Column** (東江縱隊), had maintained active resistance in the New Territories, anticipating an Allied counter-offensive.

## What happened

On **August 15, 1945**, Japanese Emperor **Hirohito** (昭和天皇) delivered the *Gyokuon-hōsō* (玉音放送) radio broadcast, announcing to the Japanese populace and empire that Japan had accepted the terms of the Potsdam Declaration for unconditional surrender. In Hong Kong, the broadcast reached military officials and the civilian population under varying circumstances. The announcement initiated a tense interregnum. Vice Admiral **Fujio Fujita** (藤田類太郎) and remaining occupation forces retained administrative and police control for several weeks, while incarcerated Allied prisoners in camps like **Sham Shui Po** (深水埗) awaited liberation. Rumors and uncertainty gripped the streets as residents awaited British or Chinese Nationalist forces to reclaim the territory.

## Significance

The broadcast marked the cessation of active hostilities in Hong Kong and the termination of Japanese military rule. However, it also triggered a complex diplomatic and political scramble for sovereignty. The power vacuum left by the Japanese surrender forced a hurried transition, culminating in the formal liberation of Hong Kong on **September 30, 1945**, when British Rear Admiral **Sir Cecil Harcourt** (夏愨) arrived to accept the local Japanese surrender and re-establish colonial administration, setting the stage for post-war political developments.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Viewed the surrender as the legitimate restoration of British sovereignty over Hong Kong, moving quickly to re-establish pre-war administrative structures under Admiral Harcourt.
- **Chinese Nationalist Government (KMT)**: Argued that Hong Kong, as part of Chinese territory, should rightfully be restored to the Republic of China, creating immediate post-war jurisdictional tensions with returning British authorities.
- **Local Hong Kong and New Territories Residents**: Experienced the surrender primarily with immense relief from wartime starvation and oppression, while navigating the immediate uncertainties of a sudden change in governing authorities.
