## Background
Following the surrender of Japan on **August 15, 1945**, the status of Hong Kong became a focal point of international diplomatic maneuvering. For three years and eight months, the territory had been under the rule of the **Imperial Japanese Army** (日本陸軍). As the **Second World War** drew to a close, local residents faced severe food shortages and the collapse of infrastructure. The British government was eager to re-establish control to prevent a power vacuum, while the **Republic of China** (中華民國) government, led by **Chiang Kai-shek** (蔣介石), asserted that Hong Kong should be returned to Chinese sovereignty as part of the post-war order.

## What happened
On **August 30, 1945**, the **British Pacific Fleet**, led by **Rear Admiral Cecil Harcourt** (夏愨), arrived in Victoria Harbour. The transition of power was swift; **Harcourt** established a temporary military administration, the **British Military Administration** (香港軍政府), which immediately moved to restore order and intern Japanese personnel. The Japanese commander, **Tanaka Ryosaburo** (田中龍三), formally surrendered to **Harcourt** in **September 1945**. During this period, the administration focused on the distribution of essential supplies and the rehabilitation of the local population, who had suffered significantly under the occupation.

## Significance
The arrival of the fleet marked the conclusion of the Japanese occupation and the resumption of British colonial rule. However, this period served as a precursor to the long-term debate over Hong Kong's political status. The transition was not viewed uniformly; for many local residents, it meant the end of wartime hardship, yet for the nationalist government in China, it was seen as a missed opportunity to regain territory. The military administration set the foundation for the return of the civil government in **May 1946**, initiating a new era of colonial management that would persist until the late twentieth century.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Government**: The arrival was seen as the restoration of legitimate authority and a return to the rule of law. It emphasized the fulfillment of Britain's duty to protect its subjects and restore pre-war order.
- **Republic of China (Kuomintang)**: The Chinese government believed the territory should have reverted to China following the Allied victory. They viewed the swift British naval deployment as a pre-emptive action to deny Chinese sovereignty.
- **Local Hong Kong Residents**: For many citizens, the transition was primarily experienced as the cessation of the harsh Japanese military occupation. Priority was placed on physical survival, food security, and the stabilization of daily life rather than the abstract debate over sovereignty.
