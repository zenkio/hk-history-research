## Background
During the Japanese occupation from **1941** to **1945**, the colonial administration led by the Japanese military issued military yen (軍票) to replace the Hong Kong dollar (香港元). By the time of the Japanese surrender in August **1945**, these military notes had suffered catastrophic hyperinflation and were rendered completely worthless. The cessation of Japanese authority left the local population holding massive amounts of unbacked paper currency, causing severe shortages of food, daily necessities, and a total collapse of normal market exchange.

## What happened
Following the liberation of Hong Kong by the British Royal Navy under **Sir Cecil Harcourt** in August **1945**, the provisional government faced an immediate economic vacuum. The primary objective was to restore public confidence by invalidating the military yen and re-establishing the Hong Kong dollar as the sole legal tender. Emergency measures were implemented to stabilize prices, distribute essential relief supplies, and restart the banking system. Pre-war currency notes issued by authorized banks, such as the Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation, were gradually reintroduced, though verifying pre-war debts, deposits, and obligations proved to be an immensely complicated administrative challenge.

## Significance
The currency replacement marked the definitive return of British colonial rule and the capitalist financial order in Hong Kong. While the immediate crisis was managed through emergency relief and rationing, the transition period exposed the vulnerabilities of the colonial economy to external shocks. The resolution of the military yen issue—specifically the lack of compensation for local holders of the worthless notes—remained a long-standing grievance for many residents, shaping local attitudes toward wartime losses and governmental responsibility.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Viewed the immediate invalidation of the military yen and the reinstatement of the Hong Kong dollar as a necessary and urgent measure to restore order, fiscal stability, and legitimate governance. Compensation for worthless military notes was generally dismissed as the financial burden of a defeated enemy.
- **Local Hong Kong Residents**: Experienced severe economic hardship and destitution due to the sudden worthlessness of their accumulated savings in military yen. Many felt deeply aggrieved by the British government's refusal to honor or compensate for the currency forced upon them during the occupation.
