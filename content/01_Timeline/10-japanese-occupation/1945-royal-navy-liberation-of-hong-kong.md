## Background

Following the surrender of the Japanese garrison in August **1945**, the future governance of Hong Kong became a subject of competing geopolitical interests. The British government, led by **Winston Churchill**'s wartime cabinet and subsequently **Clement Attlee**'s administration, was determined to resume pre-war colonial rule. Simultaneously, the Nationalist government of the Republic of China under **Chiang Kai-shek** argued that Hong Kong should be returned to Chinese sovereignty alongside other recovered territories, though Allied command arrangements ultimately designated the British to accept the local Japanese surrender.

## What happened

On August 30, **1945**, a British Royal Navy task force—Task Group 111.2—steamed into Victoria Harbour. Led by **Rear-Admiral Cecil Harcourt** (夏愨) aboard the aircraft carrier HMS *Indomitable*, the fleet formally re-established British authority. **Rear-Admiral Harcourt** subsequently assumed the role of Commander-in-Chief and head of a military administration that governed the territory until civilian rule resumed in May **1946**. The Japanese forces formally surrendered aboard HMS *Devonshire* on September 16, **1945**.

## Significance

The arrival of the Royal Navy marked the abrupt end of the three years and eight months of the Japanese occupation, commonly referred to by local residents as the 'three years and eight months' (三年零八個月). While the event initiated the restoration of the pre-war British colonial order, it bypassed Chinese Nationalist aspirations for retrocession, setting the stage for the complex socio-political dynamics of post-war Hong Kong.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Perspective**: The return of the Royal Navy was viewed as a triumphant liberation and the legitimate restoration of lawful British administration over Crown territory, bringing stability and relief after years of wartime hardship.
- **Chinese Nationalist Perspective**: The British resumption of control was seen by some as a missed opportunity to reclaim Chinese sovereignty over the territory, complicated by broader Allied command decisions at the close of the Second World War.
- **Local Hong Kong Perspective**: For the local population, the arrival of the fleet primarily signaled the immediate end of severe wartime deprivation, forced military rule, and economic collapse under Japanese occupation.
