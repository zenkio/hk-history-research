## Background

Following the defeat of British forces in December 1941, Hong Kong endured **Three Years and Eight Months** (三年零八個月) of harsh Japanese occupation. During this period, the local population suffered from severe food shortages, hyperinflation, forced repatriation to mainland China, and widespread disease. As World War II drew to a close with the Japanese surrender announced on August 15, 1945, a power vacuum emerged in the territory.

## What happened

On **August 30, 1945**, a British Royal Navy task force commanded by **Rear-Admiral Cecil Harcourt** arrived in Victoria Harbour. The forces formally accepted the surrender of the Japanese garrison on **September 16, 1945**, at Government House. This event marked the immediate restoration of British colonial administration under a temporary military administration, which lasted until May 1946 when civilian governor **Sir Mark Young** resumed office.

## Significance

The liberation ended the immediate wartime suffering and initiated a rapid transition back to colonial rule. However, it bypassed the Chinese Nationalist government's aspirations to recover sovereignty over Hong Kong, setting the stage for complex postwar geopolitical dynamics. The return of British authority also paved the way for the massive influx of refugees and rapid industrialisation that would transform Hong Kong in the subsequent decades.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Perspective**: Viewed the return of British forces as the legitimate restoration of lawful administration and the successful rescue of a strategic imperial outpost from wartime devastation.
- **Kuomintang and Mainland Chinese Perspective**: Expressed disappointment and frustration that British forces reclaimed the territory rather than Chinese Nationalist troops, whose army had fought as an Allied power in the China Burma India Theater.
- **Local Hong Kong Population Perspective**: Experienced the liberation primarily as an immense relief from the starvation, fear, and oppression of the occupation years, despite the immediate resumption of pre-war colonial hierarchies.
