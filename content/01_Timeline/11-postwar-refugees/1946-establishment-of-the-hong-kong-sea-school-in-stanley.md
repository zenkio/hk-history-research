## Background
Following the surrender of Japan in **August 1945**, Hong Kong faced monumental challenges regarding postwar recovery, massive refugee influxes, and widespread juvenile destitution. Many children and youths were left without adequate care, education, or means of livelihood amidst the devastated urban infrastructure.

## What happened
On **September 16, 1946**, the Hong Kong Sea School was formally established in Stanley (赤柱) to address the plight of underprivileged and orphaned boys. The institution aimed to combine standard education with practical nautical and vocational training, preparing students for careers in the maritime industry and disciplined services. The initiative utilized available coastal facilities in Stanley to offer a structured environment focused on seamanship, character building, and self-reliance.

## Significance
The foundation of the school marked an important early step in Hong Kong's social welfare development during the immediate postwar period. By equipping disadvantaged youth with specialized maritime skills, the institution not only aided individual rehabilitation but also supplied trained personnel to support the revival of local and regional maritime trade, which was vital to Hong Kong's economic recovery.
