## Background
In the aftermath of the Second World War, Hong Kong experienced a massive influx of refugees, capital, and labor from mainland China. Amidst rapid industrialisation and harsh working conditions, the labor movement grew increasingly active. At the same time, the unfolding Chinese Civil War between the Nationalist Kuomintang (KMT) and the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) spilled over into the British colony, transforming local workers' associations into battlegrounds for partisan influence.

## What happened
In **1948**, the colonial administration enacted the Trade Union Registration Ordinance. The law mandated that all trade unions register with the government, subjected their financial accounts to official auditing, and prohibited unions from engaging in political activities outside Hong Kong or maintaining unauthorized external affiliations. While framed by officials as a measure to promote orderly collective bargaining and prevent subversion, the legislation effectively criminalized unregistered bodies and forced existing labor factions to openly declare their loyalties.

## Significance
The ordinance marked a turning point in the governance of labor in Hong Kong, establishing a precedent of executive-led intervention in civil society. Rather than dampening political agitation, the law deeply politicized the local labor movement along KMT-CCP lines, creating a lasting dual-structure between pro-Nationalist and pro-Communist unions that would shape the colony's socio-political landscape for decades, culminating in major confrontations such as the 1967 riots.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Government**: Viewed the ordinance as a necessary administrative tool to maintain public order, curb foreign political subversion, and foster a responsible, apolitical trade union movement focused purely on economic welfare.
- **Local Labor Activists and Partisan Factions**: Frequently regarded the registration requirements as an authoritarian infringement on workers' freedom of association, though competing KMT and CCP factions also utilized the legal framework to legitimize their respective influence.
- **Later Scholarship**: Analyzes the 1948 ordinance as a foundational moment in the colonial state's containment strategy, showing how labor policy was subordinated to broader geopolitical anxieties surrounding the Cold War and the Chinese revolution.
