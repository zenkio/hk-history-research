## Background

Following the conclusion of the Second World War in **1945**, Hong Kong faced a severe humanitarian crisis. An overwhelming wave of refugees fled the Chinese Mainland due to the ongoing civil war, leading to extreme overcrowding, widespread poverty, and a critical lack of social services. Traditional community structures had been largely fractured during the Japanese occupation (**1941–1945**), leaving vulnerable populations with limited institutional support from the colonial administration.

## What happened

In **November 1949**, local civic leaders formally established the Sham Shui Po Kaifong Association (深水埗街坊福利會), marking the modern rebirth of the historical *kaifong* (neighborhood) system. Originally rooted in Qing-era communal self-governance and charity, these revived associations quickly spread across various districts in urban Hong Kong. Operating under the encouragement and supervision of the colonial government's Secretariat for Chinese Affairs, the associations mobilized local merchants and elites to fund and organize grassroots welfare programs.

These initiatives directly addressed the acute needs of destitute residents by providing free or low-cost medical clinics, establishing schools for impoverished children, distributing emergency food and clothing, and offering disaster relief during fires and typhoons that frequently devastated squatter settlements.

## Significance

The revival of the *kaifong* associations represented a crucial turning point in Hong Kong's social fabric, bridging the gap between a strained colonial state and a destitute refugee population. While they successfully provided vital welfare, historians and political analysts interpret their broader role through differing lenses regarding colonial governance, social control, and community agency.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Colonial officials viewed the revived kaifong associations as valuable partners in maintaining social stability and administering local welfare without placing an unsustainable financial burden on the government. By co-opting local elites to manage grassroots relief, the administration fostered a cooperative channel of communication with the Chinese-speaking population.
- **Later Scholarship and Local Hong Kong Perspective**: Later scholars and local historians often analyze the kaifong movement as both a genuine expression of traditional Chinese communal philanthropy and a mechanism of social control that reinforced elite hierarchy. Some researchers suggest that the associations helped integrate refugees into a distinct local Hong Kong identity while simultaneously discouraging radical political mobilization during a highly volatile geopolitical era.
