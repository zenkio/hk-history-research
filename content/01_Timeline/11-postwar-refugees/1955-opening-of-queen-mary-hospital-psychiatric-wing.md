## Background
In the decade following the Second World War, Hong Kong experienced an unprecedented influx of refugees fleeing the Chinese Civil War and subsequent political upheavals. This rapid population growth severely strained public infrastructure, housing, and healthcare services. Living in overcrowded squatter settlements, many residents endured extreme poverty, malnutrition, and the lingering psychological trauma of wartime violence and displacement. Prior to the mid-1950s, institutional mental health care capacity in the colony was extremely limited, relying heavily on the Victoria Gaol or custodial care at the Mental Hospital on High Street, which struggled with severe overcrowding and limited therapeutic interventions.

## What happened
In **1955**, a dedicated psychiatric wing was opened at Queen Mary Hospital (瑪麗醫院) in Pok Fu Lam (薄扶林). This development introduced a more modern medical facility designed to separate psychiatric treatment from penal institutions and general overcrowding. The new wing aimed to integrate mental healthcare closer to mainstream general medicine, providing improved diagnostic and therapeutic capabilities for patients suffering from acute psychological distress, depression, and trauma-related conditions associated with the postwar refugee crisis.

## Significance
The establishment of the Queen Mary Hospital psychiatric wing represented a crucial turning point in the evolution of Hong Kong’s public health policy. It demonstrated a growing recognition by colonial authorities that mental illness required specialized medical intervention rather than mere confinement. However, historians note that despite this physical expansion, demand consistently outpaced capacity, and mental health services remained underfunded relative to the overwhelming social welfare challenges of the rapidly expanding industrializing colony.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration**: Viewed the expansion as a progressive modernization of the colony's medical infrastructure, demonstrating effective governance and humanitarian care in the face of overwhelming demographic pressures.
- **Local Hong Kong communities**: Perceived the new psychiatric facilities with a mix of gratitude for expanded medical access and enduring social stigma associated with mental illness, while navigating deeply stressful socioeconomic conditions.
