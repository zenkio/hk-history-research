## Background

Following the Second World War, Hong Kong experienced a massive influx of refugees fleeing the Chinese Civil War and the establishment of the People's Republic of China. This sudden population surge strained local infrastructure and led to severe overcrowding, particularly in squatter settlements. Poor living conditions, malnutrition, and inadequate sanitation created a fertile ground for infectious diseases, most notably tuberculosis (TB). The disease became a leading cause of death and placed an immense burden on the healthcare system, which was ill-equipped to handle the sheer volume of patients. 

## What happened

On **January 8, 1957**, the **Grantham Hospital** (葛量洪醫院) officially opened in Aberdeen (香港仔) to combat the crisis. Named after the serving Governor of Hong Kong, **Sir Alexander Grantham** (葛量洪), the institution was established as a specialized facility dedicated to the treatment and prevention of tuberculosis. Funded through a combination of government support, public donations, and charitable organizations, the hospital significantly increased the number of available beds for TB patients and introduced more advanced treatment regimens available at the time.

## Significance

The opening of Grantham Hospital marked a major milestone in Hong Kong's public health development during the post-war era. It reflected the colonial government's shifting policy from a largely laissez-faire approach toward a more interventionist stance in social welfare and healthcare, driven by the urgency of the health crisis. While the hospital could not eradicate the disease overnight, it provided critical relief to thousands of working-class patients and refugees, helping to stabilize public health as Hong Kong transitioned into a manufacturing-based economy.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration**: Officials viewed the establishment of Grantham Hospital as a humanitarian success and proof of the government's commitment to improving the welfare of all residents, while also containing a public health threat that could destabilize the colony's economic productivity.
- **Local Hong Kong working class and refugees**: For impoverished residents and refugees living in crowded squatter areas, specialized medical facilities like Grantham Hospital were desperately needed lifelines, though access to adequate long-term care remained a constant struggle amid widespread poverty.
