## Background

Following the Second World War, Hong Kong experienced an unprecedented influx of refugees from mainland China, causing the urban population to swell dramatically. By the mid-1950s, densely populated districts like Causeway Bay suffered from severe overcrowding and a critical lack of public open spaces. To address these infrastructural pressures, the colonial government undertook extensive reclamation projects along the waterfront. The site chosen for the new park was created through the reclamation of typhoon shelter spoils and mudflats in Causeway Bay (銅鑼灣).

## What happened

On **October 14, 1957**, **Victoria Park** (維多利亞公園), named after the statue of Queen Victoria located within its grounds, officially opened to the public. Spanning over twenty acres, the park was designed to serve as a major recreational hub for the urban populace. Initial facilities included football pitches, basketball courts, tennis courts, and expansive landscaped walking areas. The establishment of the park marked a significant milestone in urban planning, representing one of the earliest large-scale recreational developments undertaken by the government to improve living conditions in the crowded metropolis.

## Significance

Victoria Park quickly evolved into more than just a sports ground; it became a central social and political gathering place for the people of Hong Kong. Over subsequent decades, the park hosted numerous public events, cultural celebrations, and political demonstrations, cementing its status as a vital civic space. From the perspective of colonial urban development, the project demonstrated an early shift toward incorporating public amenities into densely built-up industrial districts, setting a precedent for future urban parks across the territory.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Viewed the construction of Victoria Park as a progressive achievement in urban planning and public health, demonstrating the government's commitment to improving the living standards and welfare of Hong Kong's residents amidst rapid post-war industrialisation.
- **Local Urban Residents**: Welcomed the park as a much-needed sanctuary from the cramped tenement housing of the era, though some working-class residents initially found access to certain sports facilities limited by prevailing social stratification and management policies.
