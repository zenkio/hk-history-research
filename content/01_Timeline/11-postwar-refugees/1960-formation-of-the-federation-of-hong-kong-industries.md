## Background
Following the end of the Second World War and the subsequent change in government in mainland China, Hong Kong experienced a massive influx of refugees and capital. This demographic shift provided a ready supply of labor, which, combined with the arrival of experienced entrepreneurs from cities like Shanghai, laid the groundwork for a transition from an entrepôt economy to a manufacturing hub. By the late 1950s, the Hong Kong government and the business elite recognized that the uncoordinated growth of small-scale factories required a more formal institutional structure to sustain long-term development and gain access to competitive international markets.

## What happened
The **Federation of Hong Kong Industries** (**香港工業總會**) was formally incorporated in **1960** following the passage of the Federation of Hong Kong Industries Ordinance. The primary objective behind its creation was to provide a unified voice for local industrialists in their dealings with the colonial government and to facilitate the implementation of standardized quality control measures. This was essential for restoring confidence in "Made in Hong Kong" goods among Western importers, particularly in the United Kingdom and the United States. The organization also focused on technical education, industrial research, and the promotion of commercial ties abroad.

## Significance
The formation of the Federation marked a maturation of Hong Kong's industrial policy. It signaled a shift from purely laissez-faire commerce toward a more organized framework where industry leaders collaborated with the government to manage exports. Academics often view this as a crucial step in the territory's economic miracle, as it enabled local manufacturers to shift from basic low-cost production to more specialized goods. The organization remains a central pillar of Hong Kong's industrial representation, reflecting the enduring influence of the manufacturing sector on the local economy.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: The colonial government viewed the Federation as a necessary tool to regulate quality control, ensuring that Hong Kong exports met international standards and protected the reputation of British-administered territories.
- **Local Industrialists**: For the entrepreneurs of the era, the Federation represented a vital platform to secure collective bargaining power against colonial trade policies and a means to modernize operations to remain competitive in global markets.
