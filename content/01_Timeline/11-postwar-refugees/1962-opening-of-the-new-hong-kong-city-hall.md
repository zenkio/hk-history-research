## Background

By the late 1950s, post-war Hong Kong was experiencing rapid industrialisation and population growth. The original Victorian-era City Hall, opened in 1869, had been partially demolished in 1933 to make way for the Hongkong and Shanghai Bank building. For decades, the colony lacked a dedicated central civic and cultural venue. In response, the colonial government commissioned British architects **Ron Phillips** and **Alan Fitch** to design a new modernist complex on newly reclaimed land in Central, intended to serve as a multi-purpose cultural center for the public.

## What happened

On **March 2, 1962**, the new Hong Kong City Hall (香港大會堂) was officially opened by the Governor, **Sir Robert Black** (柏立基). Comprising a Low Block and a High Block, the complex housed a 1,462-seat concert hall, a 463-seat theatre, a banqueting hall, exhibition galleries, a museum, and Hong Kong's first public library meeting international standards. Built with public funds, the facility was designed to offer affordable access to the arts, hosting local and international performers, art exhibitions, and civic gatherings.

## Significance

Historians generally view the opening of the City Hall as a milestone in the development of Hong Kong's public cultural life. By offering affordable venues and a public library, the institution helped democratise access to arts and education for a growing urban population. The complex became a vital gathering space during an era when the colonial government sought to foster a distinct local civic identity following the turbulence of the 1950s.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Viewed the project as a progressive achievement that demonstrated Britain's commitment to the social welfare and cultural enrichment of Hong Kong's residents during the postwar era.
- **Local Hong Kong Public and Scholars**: Emphasised that the City Hall provided rare, much-needed public spaces for ordinary citizens and laid the foundational infrastructure for modern local arts, theatre, and community life.
