## Background
During the postwar decades, Hong Kong experienced rapid population growth driven by refugees fleeing mainland China. This demographic surge severely strained local infrastructure, particularly the water supply. Traditional catchment areas and reservoirs proved completely inadequate during droughts, most notably during the catastrophic water crisis of 1963 when authorities enforced a drastic supply schedule of providing water for only four hours every four days.

## What happened
On **April 22, 1964**, the Hong Kong government, led by British colonial officials, reached a formal agreement with the Guangdong provincial authorities to purchase raw water from the Dongjiang (East River, 東江). The arrangement required significant engineering works on both sides of the border to construct a complex system of pipelines, pumping stations, and canals to channel water southward from Guangdong to Hong Kong's reservoirs.

## Significance
The **Dongjiang-Shenzhen Water Supply Agreement** fundamentally transformed Hong Kong's resource security, laying the physical foundation for the territory's subsequent industrialisation and economic boom. By securing a reliable external water supply, Hong Kong mitigated its most acute vulnerability, enabling uninterrupted urban development and population growth in the decades that followed.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration**: Viewed the agreement as a pragmatic and necessary arrangement to secure vital resources for economic survival and public health, successfully negotiating infrastructure cooperation across an otherwise tense geopolitical border.
- **Guangdong / Beijing authorities**: Framed the water supply initiative as an expression of patriotic kinship and practical support for compatriots in Hong Kong, demonstrating state capacity and solidarity despite broader Cold War tensions.
- **Local Hong Kong residents**: Experienced the agreement with immense relief, as it promised an end to the debilitating water rationing schedules that had crippled daily life and industrial production during the severe droughts of the early 1960s.
