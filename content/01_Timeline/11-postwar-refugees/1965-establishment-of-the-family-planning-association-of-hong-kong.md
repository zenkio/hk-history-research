## Background
Following the conclusion of World War II in **1945**, Hong Kong experienced a significant demographic surge. The influx of refugees from Mainland China, combined with a high birth rate, exerted severe pressure on the colonial government’s limited resources, including housing, healthcare, and education. By the late **1950s** and early **1960s**, the city’s population density reached critical levels, prompting discussions about the sustainability of rapid growth in the context of the territory's limited land mass and infrastructure.

## What happened
The Family Planning Association of Hong Kong (香港家庭計劃指導會), or FPAHK, trace its roots to the Eugenics League of Hong Kong, which was formed in **1936**. However, it was not until **1950** that it was formally reorganized as the FPAHK. By **1965**, the organization had achieved significant traction in public discourse. They began a concerted effort to distribute contraceptive information and products to the general public through a network of clinics. While the organization had operated for years, the mid-**1960s** represented a turning point where, with government support, the FPAHK pivoted from a private social initiative to a central pillar of public health strategy. The slogan "Two is Enough" (兩個夠哂數) became an iconic, though later, manifestation of these ongoing efforts.

## Significance
The work of the FPAHK is often seen as a critical element in the demographic transition of Hong Kong. By providing access to reproductive health services, the FPAHK helped families manage birth rates, which some scholars argue contributed to the improvement of individual quality of life and the socio-economic stability of the territory. However, the intervention into family structure also brought the private sphere of life into the domain of public policy. The success of these programs remains a subject of study, particularly regarding how local cultural values, including traditional views on family size and gender roles, interacted with colonial health policies.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Government**: The colonial administration largely viewed the expansion of family planning services as a pragmatic necessity for managing the 'population bomb' and ensuring that the territory's limited infrastructure could support its inhabitants. They prioritized economic stability and social order, viewing the limitation of births as a rational approach to modernizing the city's social welfare systems.
- **Local Hong Kong and New Territories Communities**: Many families held diverse views, often influenced by traditional values that favored larger, multigenerational households as a form of social security. While some welcomed the health benefits of family planning, others were initially skeptical of, or resistant to, Western-led interventions that challenged ancestral customs regarding family size and lineage.
