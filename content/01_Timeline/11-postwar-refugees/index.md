## Overview

The period from **1945** to **1966** represents one of the most turbulent and formative chapters in Hong Kong's history. Following the **Liberation of Hong Kong from Japanese Occupation** (香港重光) in August **1945**, the British resumed control and initially debated progressive governance reforms, such as the **Young Plan**, though these were ultimately shelved. The resumption of civil administration was quickly complicated by geopolitical upheavals on the Chinese mainland, particularly the **Mass Refugee Influx from Chinese Civil War** (國共內戰難民湧入) and the later **May Exodus Refugee Crisis** (五月逃港潮), which drastically swelled the territory's population and strained meager resources.

## Key themes

### Humanitarian Crises and Social Welfare
The sudden population boom led to acute housing shortages and severe living conditions. The tragic **Disastrous Shek Kip Mei Fire** (石硤尾大火) of **1954** left tens of thousands homeless overnight, prompting a monumental shift in government policy. This disaster led directly to the **Establishment of the Hong Kong Housing Authority** (香港屋宇建設委員會) and the birth of public housing estates.

Infrastructure and basic survival were further tested by natural and man-made calamities, including the devastation brought by **Typhoon Wanda** (溫黛) in **1962** and a **Severe Drought and Strict Water Rationing** (嚴重乾旱與制水) crisis. These challenges were partially mitigated by the **Signing of Dongjiang-Shenzhen Water Supply Agreement** (東深供水工程協議), securing a vital long-term water source.

### Economic Transformation and Industrialisation
Geopolitical isolation profoundly reshaped the local economy. The imposition of a **UN Embargo on China Triggers Industrial Shift** (聯合國對華禁令促成工業轉型), combined with an influx of capital and skilled entrepreneurs from Shanghai, transformed Hong Kong into a manufacturing powerhouse. The territory successfully transitioned from an entrepôt trade economy to the **Rise of Hong Kong as a Global Textile Hub** (香港成為全球紡織業樞紐).

### Social Tensions and Cultural Development
Rapid urbanization and economic disparity created fertile ground for social unrest. The **1956 Double Tenth Riots** (雙十暴動) highlighted lingering Kuomintang-Communist divisions within the population. A decade later, the **The Star Ferry Riots** (天星小輪加價暴動) of **1966** reflected deep-seated grievances among the local working-class youth regarding economic conditions and colonial governance.

Despite these tensions, the era also saw the dawn of modern mass media and culture, marked by the **Launch of Rediffusion Television** (麗的電視廣播) and the **Establishment of Commercial Radio Hong Kong** (香港商業電台), alongside major infrastructure upgrades like the **Completion of Kai Tak Airport Runway Extension** (啟德機場跑道延長工程) and the execution of the **First Modern Hong Kong Population Census** (香港首次現代人口普查).

## How it shaped Hong Kong

The dual pressures of mass migration and economic restructuring forced the colonial government to abandon its traditional laissez-faire approach in favor of active intervention in housing, infrastructure, and social administration. The establishment of secure refugee settlements, such as the **Establishment of Tiu Keng Leng Refugee Camp** (調景嶺難民營), and the accidental **Discovery of the Lei Cheng Uk Han Tomb** (李鄭屋漢墓) during public housing construction, highlighted the complex interplay between modern development and deep historical roots. Ultimately, the resilience forged during this era laid the socio-economic foundations for modern Hong Kong society.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Viewed the era as a remarkable administrative and humanitarian achievement in crisis management, transforming an unstable, refugee-swamped colony into a disciplined, economically viable capitalist showcase in Asia.
- **Qing/Beijing Perspective**: Regarded the mass influx of refugees primarily as compatriots displaced by civil war and foreign interference, while viewing Hong Kong's economic rise through the lens of mainland labor, capital, and resource dependency.
- **Local Hong Kong and New Territories Communities**: Remembered the period through the lens of immense daily hardship, overcrowded tenements, severe water shortages, and labor exploitation, balanced by a growing sense of a distinct, locally-forged identity.
