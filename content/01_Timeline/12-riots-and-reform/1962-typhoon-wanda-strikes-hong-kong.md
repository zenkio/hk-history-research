## Background

By **September 1962**, Hong Kong was experiencing rapid population growth driven by immigration from mainland China, leading to widespread overcrowding and the proliferation of informal squatter settlements across the hillsides. Meteorological monitoring and warning systems were far less sophisticated than they are today, leaving the densely populated urban areas and outlying islands vulnerable to sudden, severe tropical cyclones.

## What happened

On **September 1, 1962**, Typhoon Wanda made a direct hit on Hong Kong, bringing some of the most destructive winds and storm surges recorded in the territory's history. Sustained hurricane-force winds and towering waves battered Victoria Harbour and low-lying coastal areas such as Sha Tin (沙田) and Tsuen Wan (荃灣). Thousands of poorly constructed squatter huts were completely destroyed, washing away homes and leaving tens of thousands of residents instantly homeless. The storm caused widespread flooding, paralyzed transport networks, and inflicted severe damage on maritime shipping.

## Significance

The sheer scale of destruction wrought by Typhoon Wanda marked a critical turning point in Hong Kong's social and administrative history. The disaster starkly exposed the inadequacy of existing housing and disaster-relief infrastructure, intensifying pressure on the British colonial government to overhaul its urban management. While public housing programs had already begun following the Shek Kip Mei fire in 1953, Wanda demonstrated that far more aggressive intervention was required. Historians view the aftermath of the typhoon as an indirect catalyst that accelerated long-term government reforms in public infrastructure, squatter resettlement, and social welfare, laying the groundwork for the more comprehensive administrative expansions that would follow in the subsequent decades.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Government**: Officials viewed the disaster primarily as an unprecedented meteorological emergency that overwhelmed existing civil defense capabilities, subsequently using the crisis to justify a more centralized approach to urban planning and squatter control.
- **Local Communities and Squatter Residents**: Affected residents experienced the disaster as a profound humanitarian catastrophe rooted in systemic neglect and severe housing shortages, highlighting the stark inequalities of colonial society.
- **Later Scholarship**: Modern historians argue that Typhoon Wanda served as a crucial structural watershed, forcing the colonial state to accelerate public infrastructure and social welfare reforms that eventually reshaped the relationship between the government and the grassroots population.
