## Background
By the mid-1960s, Hong Kong's economy had transitioned from an entrepôt trade hub into a manufacturing powerhouse, driven by an influx of capital and labor from mainland China. Local industrialists produced textiles, garments, plastics, and electronic goods primarily for Western export markets. However, international trade carried substantial financial vulnerabilities, including buyer insolvency, foreign exchange restrictions, and geopolitical instability. Exporters often lacked access to commercial credit protection that could mitigate the risks of overseas buyers failing to pay.

## What happened
To address these systemic risks and support the export-driven economy, the colonial legislature passed the **Hong Kong Export Credit Insurance Corporation Ordinance**. This statute officially established the **Hong Kong Export Credit Insurance Corporation (香港出口信用保險局)** in **1966** as a statutory body. Backed by a government guarantee, the corporation was empowered to issue insurance policies to local exporters, covering commercial and political risks inherent in international trade transactions.

## Significance
The creation of the corporation provided a crucial institutional safety net for small and medium-sized manufacturing enterprises, encouraging them to explore new and unfamiliar overseas markets with greater confidence. Alongside other infrastructure and financial developments of the era, the initiative helped stabilize the industrial sector during a period marked by local civil unrest. It laid foundational support for the export boom that defined Hong Kong's economic takeoff through the 1970s.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: Officials viewed the statutory body as a pragmatic and necessary intervention to stabilize and expand Hong Kong's export trade, ensuring the economic viability and public revenue of the colony without resorting to direct industrial subsidies.
- **Local Manufacturers**: Industrialists and export merchants generally welcomed the corporation as a vital risk-mitigation tool that reduced the perils of international commerce and facilitated easier access to bank financing.
