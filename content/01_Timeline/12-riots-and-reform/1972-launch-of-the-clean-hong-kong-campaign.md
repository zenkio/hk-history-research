## Background

By the early **1970s**, rapid industrialization, population growth, and urbanization had left Hong Kong struggling with severe environmental and sanitation challenges. Overflowing rubbish, congested tenements, and inadequate municipal services created public health hazards across the urban areas and the New Territories. Following the social unrest of **1966** and **1967**, the colonial administration under Governor **Murray MacLehose** (麥理浩) recognized an urgent need to improve living standards, bridge the gap between the government and the populace, and foster a sense of shared community responsibility.

## What happened

On **November 1, 1972**, the government officially inaugurated the **Clean Hong Kong** campaign (清潔香港運動). The initiative moved away from purely punitive measures toward a sustained public education and community engagement strategy. Mass media, schools, and civic organizations were mobilized to promote anti-littering habits. The campaign's most enduring symbol was **Miss Lap Sap** (垃圾小姐), a cartoon monster-like mascot created to personify rubbish, alongside her companion **Lap Sap Chung** (垃圾蟲), designed to warn citizens against bad habits. Posters, television announcements, and community events flooded the territory, encouraging residents to take pride in their environment.

## Significance

The campaign marked a turning point in government-led civic mobilization and public hygiene awareness in Hong Kong. While initial skepticism existed regarding whether cartoon mascots could alter deeply ingrained social behaviors, subsequent decades saw a noticeable shift in public cleanliness standards. The initiative laid the groundwork for broader environmental policies and community-building efforts that defined the MacLehose era, contributing to the formation of a distinct local Hong Kong identity tied to urban modernity and civic pride.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration**: Viewed the campaign as a vital administrative success that improved public health, demonstrated effective governance, and fostered a cooperative relationship with the local population following the turbulence of the late 1960s.
- **Local Hong Kong residents**: Many citizens embraced the catchy media campaigns and mascots like Miss Lap Sap as entertaining and relatable, though some viewed the heavy-handed official messaging with initial amusement or cynicism regarding underlying systemic municipal issues.
