## Background
By the early **1970s**, Hong Kong faced rapid industrialization and a burgeoning population that relied heavily on street hawkers for daily necessities and livelihoods. Following the social unrest of **1966** and **1967**, the administration under Governor **Murray MacLehose** recognized that the existing system of managing street vendors—often handled by the police—was ineffective and prone to corruption. The presence of thousands of unlicensed hawkers caused significant traffic congestion and public health concerns in the eyes of colonial authorities, yet they remained an essential survival mechanism for the working class.

## What happened
In **1973**, the government formally established the **Hawker Control Force (小販管理隊)**. This body was created to professionalize the regulation of street vending, transitioning these duties away from the **Hong Kong Police Force (香港警務處)**. The unit was tasked with patrolling busy districts, controlling the spread of illegal stalls, and managing the allocation of designated hawker pitches. The reorganization aimed to standardize enforcement through a dedicated workforce that focused specifically on urban management rather than general law enforcement.

## Significance
The creation of the **Hawker Control Force** symbolized a shift in the colonial administration’s philosophy. It marked an transition toward 'modern' urban governance, emphasizing the regulation of informal economies over outright prohibition. By creating a specialized force, the government sought to formalize the street economy and integrate it into a structured framework. While intended to improve urban order, the transition was contentious; many hawkers viewed the new force as a more intrusive mechanism of state control, while others saw it as a necessary step in maintaining public hygiene and thoroughfare accessibility. This period of reform under **MacLehose** is often interpreted as a strategic attempt to gain social stability by addressing the logistical and administrative grievances that had exacerbated previous tensions.

## Perspectives

- **Colonial Administration**: The government viewed the Hawker Control Force as a necessary instrument of modernization, intended to improve public hygiene and traffic flow while professionalizing the civil service. They argued that separating hawker management from the police reduced opportunities for corruption and allowed for more consistent urban regulation.
- **Local Hawkers and Working Class**: Many hawkers perceived the new force as a direct threat to their livelihood, viewing the increased regulation as a disruption to long-standing community survival tactics. They often expressed frustration at the loss of flexibility and the imposition of a bureaucratic system that prioritized order over economic necessity.
