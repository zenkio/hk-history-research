## Background
Following the social unrest of **1966** and **1967**, the colonial government under Governor **Sir Murray MacLehose** (**麥理浩**) initiated wide-ranging social and administrative reforms to address public grievances and improve working-class living standards. Prior to this period, resolving employment disputes often required formal court proceedings that were financially prohibitive for ordinary workers, leaving many labor grievances unaddressed.

## What happened
In **1973**, the colonial administration established the Labour Tribunal (勞資審裁處) under the Labour Tribunal Ordinance. Designed as an alternative to the traditional court system, the tribunal aimed to provide a swift, inexpensive, and informal forum. Proceedings were conducted without the strict rules of evidence used in higher courts, and legal representation was generally not permitted, allowing workers and employers to present their own cases directly before a presiding officer.

## Significance
The introduction of the tribunal marked a major shift in Hong Kong's industrial relations and legal landscape, significantly improving access to justice for the workforce. It streamlined the resolution of monetary claims arising from breaches of employment contracts or the Employment Ordinance (僱傭條例), helping to defuse workplace tensions during a decade of rapid industrial growth.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration**: Viewed the tribunal as a progressive and pragmatic reform to modernize governance, institutionalize industrial peace, and demonstrate responsiveness to working-class welfare after the turbulence of the late 1960s.
- **Local workers and labor advocates**: Generally welcomed the venue as a practical, low-cost mechanism that empowered employees to challenge powerful employers without risking financial ruin through legal fees.
- **Employers and business owners**: Sometimes expressed initial concern over the informal nature of the proceedings and the potential bias toward employees, though many eventually adapted to the streamlined dispute resolution process.
