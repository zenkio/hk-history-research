## Background
Following the social unrest of **1966** and **1967**, the colonial administration under Governor **Sir Murray MacLehose** recognized an urgent need to bridge the gap between the government and the grassroots population. Urban areas, particularly rapidly expanding public housing estates, faced severe challenges regarding crime, sanitation, and social isolation. To combat these issues and foster a greater sense of belonging and civic responsibility, the Home Affairs Department sought mechanisms to encourage localized self-help and direct communication with residents.

## What happened
On **March 29, 1973**, the government formally initiated the Mutual Aid Committee (互助委員會) scheme. These committees were organized at the building level within both public housing estates and multi-story private tenements. Residents living in the same building were encouraged to elect executive committees to manage building security, cleanliness, and minor welfare activities. By establishing a dense network of hyper-local committees, the government aimed to decentralize urban management and provide a direct conduit for residents to report grievances and coordinate with district authorities.

## Significance
The MAC scheme rapidly expanded across the territory, eventually encompassing thousands of committees and fundamentally altering the administrative landscape of urban Hong Kong. While initially framed as apolitical administrative tools for neighborhood cleanliness and crime prevention, the network later provided a permanent infrastructure for community mobilization. The scheme set a precedent for institutionalized grassroots participation, directly influencing subsequent developments in district-level governance and urban administration.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration**: Viewed the MAC scheme as a progressive administrative innovation that successfully decentralized governance, improved urban hygiene, and enhanced community policing by encouraging residents to take responsibility for their own immediate living environments.
- **Later scholarship and local critics**: Argue that while the committees did improve municipal service delivery and crime prevention, they also functioned as mechanisms of state surveillance and social control, co-opting grassroots leadership and depoliticizing potential dissent during a volatile era.
