## Background
In the early **1973**, the Anti-Corruption Office of the Hong Kong Police Force began investigating **Peter Godber**, a high-ranking British expatriate Chief Superintendent. Investigators discovered that **Godber** had amassed wealth vastly disproportionate to his official salary, with bank accounts holding hundreds of thousands of dollars—a massive sum at the time. Widespread systemic corruption had long plagued the colonial civil service, particularly the police force, where bribery and protection rackets were an open secret. However, the active investigation of such a senior British officer marked a rare challenge to the status quo.

## What happened
Despite being under surveillance and instructed not to leave the colony, **Peter Godber** managed to bypass security at Kai Tak Airport (啟德機場) on **June 8, 1973**, and boarded a flight to London via Singapore. His ability to leave undetected created a profound scandal, exposing severe incompetence or complicity within the colonial administration and law enforcement agencies. The escape triggered immediate and widespread public outrage, bringing years of simmering resentment over official corruption to a boiling point. Students and citizens took to the streets under slogans such as "Anti-Corruption, Arrest Godber" (反貪污、捉葛柏), leading to mass demonstrations organized by activist students and community groups.

## Significance
Faced with an escalating crisis that threatened the legitimacy of colonial rule, Governor Sir **Murray MacLehose** (麥理浩) recognized that internal police investigations into corruption were no longer tenable. Responding to the intense public pressure catalyzed by **Godber**’s flight, **MacLehose** acted decisively. In **October 1973**, he announced the creation of the Independent Commission Against Corruption (ICAC) (廉政公署), an independent agency answering directly to the Governor and operating outside the police chain of command. **Godber** was eventually extradited from the United Kingdom back to Hong Kong in **1974**, where he was tried and convicted of bribery, serving a prison sentence. The incident is widely regarded as a watershed moment in Hong Kong history, transforming the territory from a society characterized by accepted graft into one of the most transparent administrations in the region.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: The administration viewed **Godber**'s escape as an administrative and security embarrassment that threatened public trust. However, Governor Sir **Murray MacLehose** successfully leveraged the ensuing crisis to push through radical institutional reforms, fundamentally cleaning up the civil service and strengthening colonial governance.
- **Local Hong Kong Public and Student Activists**: For the local population, **Godber**'s flight was the ultimate symbol of colonial impunity and the rottenness of the police force. The subsequent mass protests demonstrated a rising civic consciousness and a demand for accountability, laying the groundwork for modern Hong Kong civil society.
