## Background
By the early **1970s**, Hong Kong's urban areas, particularly Victoria Harbour and the Kowloon Peninsula, faced severe overpopulation, inadequate housing, and deteriorating living conditions following decades of rapid immigration. To address this crisis, the colonial government under Governor **Sir Murray MacLehose** launched a massive decentralization and public housing strategy. The plan aimed to transform rural regions of the New Territories into self-contained satellite new towns, capable of housing hundreds of thousands of residents.

## What happened
Tuen Mun (屯門), historically known as Castle Peak, was selected as one of the primary sites for this large-scale urban transformation. In **1974**, official development work commenced to convert the rural coastal area and scattered farming and fishing settlements into a modern industrial and residential hub. Massive land reclamation was undertaken along Castle Peak Bay, while infrastructure projects began laying down the foundations for high-rise public housing estates, transport links, and industrial zones designed to provide local employment.

## Significance
The opening and subsequent development of Tuen Mun New Town fundamentally reshaped the geographical and social landscape of Hong Kong. It shifted the center of gravity away from the traditional urban core towards the New Territories, establishing a new model of town planning centered on self-sufficiency. However, the rapid pace of development also brought logistical challenges, initial isolation from the urban center, and a profound transformation of traditional rural life.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Government**: Viewed the new town program as a triumph of progressive social engineering and pragmatic administration, successfully providing modern housing and relieving critical inner-city congestion.
- **Local New Territories Communities**: Often experienced the rapid urbanization with mixed feelings, as traditional ancestral lands, agricultural practices, and indigenous ways of life were rapidly displaced by massive concrete developments.
