## Background
By the mid-1970s, Hong Kong had transitioned from a refugee-driven entrepôt to a formidable manufacturing hub. Under the administrative guidance of Governor **Murray MacLehose** (麥理浩), the territory was shifting toward social stability and economic diversification following the civil unrest of 1967. The manufacturing sector, characterized by light industries such as textiles, plastics, and electronics, had become the primary engine of the local economy. With the government seeking to project an image of a modern, industrializing city, organizers moved to elevate the profile of local exports beyond mere low-cost manufacturing to higher-quality global trade goods.

## What happened
The First Hong Kong Industrial Trade Fair was held in **1975** at the Hong Kong Convention Centre. Unlike the smaller, localized street-based industrial exhibitions that preceded it, this fair was specifically designed to attract international buyers and trade delegations. The exhibition featured a curated array of products, highlighting advancements in precision manufacturing and design. Major industrial associations, including the **Federation of Hong Kong Industries** (香港工業總會) and the **Chinese Manufacturers' Association of Hong Kong** (香港中華廠商聯合會), played significant roles in organizing the event. Exhibitors showcased goods ranging from sophisticated timepieces to high-end apparel, emphasizing that Hong Kong was capable of meeting the rigorous standards required by European and North American markets.

## Significance
The event is widely regarded by economic historians as a milestone in the consolidation of the 'Made in Hong Kong' identity. It signaled a maturation of the territory's industrial policy, moving away from purely quantity-based production toward branding and quality assurance. For many local families, the fair was a source of pride, reflecting the collective upward mobility achieved in the post-war era. The success of the fair contributed to the stabilization of Hong Kong's economic reputation during a period of transition, helping to secure the territory's position as a key node in global supply chains. While the exact long-term influence on specific trade growth rates remains debated by some economists, it is universally acknowledged as a turning point in the professionalization of Hong Kong's export marketing efforts.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: The administration viewed the fair as a successful implementation of their 'positive non-interventionism' approach, demonstrating how government facilitation could foster private enterprise. They interpreted the event as proof of Hong Kong's political and economic stability under Governor MacLehose's reforms.
- **Local Manufacturers and Workers**: For the local industrial class, the fair was a triumphant display of self-reliance and the 'Hong Kong spirit.' They often emphasized the personal sacrifices of workers and small-scale business owners as the true architects of this industrial success.
