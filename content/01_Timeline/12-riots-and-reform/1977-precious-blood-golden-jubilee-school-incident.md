## Background

In **1977**, tensions boiled over at the Precious Blood Golden Jubilee Secondary School (寶血會金禧中學). Teachers and students voiced deep grievances against the school principal, **Sister Yee-kwan** (蘇羅斯), alleging financial mismanagement, arbitrary disciplinary actions, and authoritarian administration. Under the colonial education system of the **1970s**, grant-in-aid schools run by religious bodies wielded significant autonomy, leaving staff and students with few official channels to challenge administrative abuses.

## What happened

What began as internal dissatisfaction erupted into open confrontation in **late 1977 and 1978**. Teachers and students staged boycotts, sit-ins, and public demonstrations, demanding greater transparency and the removal of the principal. The situation escalated when the sponsoring religious body and the Education Department attempted to suppress the dissent by dismissing key teachers and threatening students with expulsion. 

Rather than backing down, the protesters took their fight to the streets, garnering unprecedented public sympathy and support from emerging pressure groups, social workers, and labor activists. The standoff eventually forced the government to intervene directly, leading to the closure of the original school and the eventual reopening under a new name and administration, though not without lingering bitterness among the dismissed staff and students.

## Significance

The incident is widely regarded by historians as a watershed moment in Hong Kong's social history. It marked a transition away from traditional, quiescent community structures toward active, rights-based social movements. The mobilization fostered solidarity among teachers, leading to the strengthening of independent professional unions, and provided crucial organizational experience for a new generation of local social activists who would later shape Hong Kong's civil society.

## Perspectives

- **Colonial Government / Education Department**: Authorities viewed the unrest primarily as an administrative and disciplinary breakdown within a private-grant school, attempting to restore order while minimizing political fallout. Officials sought to balance the autonomy of religious sponsoring bodies against mounting public pressure for institutional accountability.
- **Protesting Teachers and Students**: Participants and their supporters framed the movement as a righteous struggle against institutional corruption, authoritarianism, and bureaucratic indifference. They demanded democratic participation in school governance and protection for whistleblower rights.
