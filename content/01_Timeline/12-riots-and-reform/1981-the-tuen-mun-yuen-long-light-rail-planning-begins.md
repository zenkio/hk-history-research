## Background
By the late **1970s**, the British colonial administration had embarked on the 'New Town' development program to alleviate urban overcrowding in Kowloon and Hong Kong Island. **Tuen Mun (屯門)** and **Yuen Long (元朗)** were designated as major growth nodes, intended to house hundreds of thousands of residents. However, the geographic isolation of these areas from the central business districts of the Kowloon Peninsula necessitated a robust, high-capacity transport solution. Previous dependence on fragmented bus services had proven inadequate for the scale of population growth envisioned by the government.

## What happened
In **1981**, the government began formal planning for a rail-based transit system connecting **Tuen Mun (屯門)** and **Yuen Long (元朗)**. The decision to adopt a Light Rail Transit (LRT) system—rather than a heavy rail underground system—was influenced by both cost considerations and the specific urban layout of the Tuen Mun new town, which featured wide rights-of-way suitable for surface-level rail. The project was conceived as an internal feeder network that would later link into the broader transport framework of the New Territories. The planning phase involved complex negotiations regarding land resumption and the integration of the system into existing rural townships.

## Significance
The initiation of the light rail project marked a pivotal moment in the physical and economic integration of the New Territories into the broader Hong Kong metropolis. It reflected the government's transition toward state-led infrastructure projects as a tool for social engineering and regional development. The system was designed to provide a predictable, high-capacity link for commuters living in satellite cities, effectively tethering the once-peripheral farming regions to the industrializing core of the territory. By prioritizing rail, the government signaled a long-term commitment to high-density urban planning that would define the territory's spatial organization for decades to follow.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Government**: The administration viewed the Light Rail as a necessary technical solution to manage the logistics of a planned urban population. It was seen as an essential component of the 'New Town' social contract, providing modern amenities to improve the quality of life for new residents.
- **New Territories Local Communities**: For many long-term residents and indigenous inhabitants, the infrastructure projects often appeared as an imposition on traditional land rights. There was apprehension regarding the disruption of rural life and the prioritization of urbanized 'new town' migrants over the interests of existing village populations.
