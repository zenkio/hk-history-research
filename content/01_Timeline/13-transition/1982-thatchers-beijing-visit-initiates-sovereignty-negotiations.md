## Background
By the early **1980s**, the impending expiration of the **1898** Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory—which leased the New Territories (新界) to Britain for **99** years until **June 30, 1997**—created acute legal and economic uncertainty. Real estate investment and long-term financial planning in Hong Kong required clarity on land titles and governance beyond **1997**. While the British held sovereignty over Hong Kong Island (香港島) and Kowloon Peninsula (九龍半島) in perpetuity under the Treaty of Nanjing (**1842**) and the Convention of Beijing (**1860**), colonial officials maintained that administering Hong Kong Island and Kowloon separately from the New Territories was practically and economically unviable.

## What happened
On **September 24, 1982**, British Prime Minister **Margaret Thatcher** (**戴卓爾夫人**) traveled to Beijing to meet with paramount leader **Deng Xiaoping** (**鄧小平**). The meeting aimed to test Beijing's willingness to allow continued British administration while potentially transferring nominal sovereignty to China. Instead, **Deng Xiaoping** firmly stated that sovereignty was not a matter subject to compromise and that China intended to recover all of Hong Kong in **1997**. The public diplomatic friction following the talks contributed to a temporary drop in local financial and property markets.

## Significance
This high-level encounter marked the official commencement of formal Sino-British negotiations over Hong Kong's future. It set the stage for years of intense diplomatic wrangling that ultimately led to the signing of the Sino-British Joint Declaration in **1984**, establishing the framework for the 'One Country, Two Systems' (一國兩制) model.

## Perspectives

- **British Official Perspective**: London initially entered negotiations hoping to maintain British administrative control after **1997** based on the validity of the nineteenth-century unequal treaties, viewing continued British governance as vital for Hong Kong's stability and prosperity.
- **Beijing / Qing Historical Perspective**: Beijing regarded the nineteenth-century treaties concerning Hong Kong as historical products of imperialist aggression and 'unequal treaties,' maintaining that the entire territory rightfully belonged to China and must be fully recovered by **1997**.
- **Local Hong Kong Perspective**: The local population experienced profound anxiety regarding their future security and economic stability, with many residents beginning to consider emigration or seeking assurances about the preservation of their legal and social systems.
