## Background
During the late 1970s and early 1980s, the British colonial government in Hong Kong embarked on an ambitious New Towns program to alleviate severe urban congestion in Kowloon and on Hong Kong Island. **Tuen Mun (屯門)** was designated as a major satellite town to absorb a surging population driven by migration and natural growth. However, the existing coastal roads were winding, narrow, and entirely inadequate to handle the anticipated surge in commuters, industrial traffic, and commercial transport between the remote northwestern New Territories and the urban core.

## What happened
On **May 17, 1983**, the newly constructed **Tuen Mun Highway (屯門公路)** was officially opened to the public. The engineering project was formidable, requiring extensive blasting of coastal cliffs along Castle Peak Bay, massive land reclamation, and the construction of numerous bridges and viaducts to carve a high-capacity dual three-lane expressway through difficult terrain. The highway drastically cut travel times between Tuen Mun and Tsuen Wan, bridging the geographical divide.

## Significance
The completion of the highway was a critical catalyst for the expansion of Tuen Mun as a self-contained industrial and residential hub. By providing a reliable arterial route, it enabled the government to resettle hundreds of thousands of residents into public housing estates and private developments in the area. Yet, the road's immense popularity immediately brought challenges; traffic volume rapidly outstripped projections, leading to chronic congestion, frequent accidents, and ongoing widening works that would persist for decades.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Government**: Officials viewed the highway as an engineering triumph and an indispensable arterial link necessary for the success of the New Towns policy, demonstrating effective long-term urban planning.
- **Local Residents and Commuters**: While welcoming the connection to the urban center, daily commuters quickly grew frustrated by frequent traffic bottlenecks, vehicular accidents, and the high cost and reliability issues of public transport using the corridor.
