## Background

In the early **1980s**, as the governments of the United Kingdom and the People's Republic of China negotiated the future of Hong Kong, plans advanced for the construction of the Daya Bay Nuclear Power Plant (大亞灣核電站) in Shenzhen (深圳), Guangdong (廣東) province. Located approximately **50 kilometers** northeast of Hong Kong's urban center, the proposed plant aimed to supply electricity to both Guangdong and Hong Kong. Initially, public awareness was low, but anxieties mounted following the **April 1986** Chernobyl disaster in the Soviet Union. Local residents and professionals began to question the safety standards, proximity, and emergency preparedness associated with a nuclear facility so close to a densely populated metropolis.

## What happened

By **September 1986**, opposition to the project coalesced into a massive public campaign. Activists, professional bodies, social workers, and political figures organized signature campaigns, rallies, and forums to voice their concerns. A petition opposing the plant ultimately gathered over **one million signatures** from Hong Kong residents, presenting a monumental display of grassroots organization. Delegations traveled to Beijing (北京) to present petitions and meet with mainland officials, while local groups demanded independent safety reviews and greater transparency from both the colonial government and Chinese authorities.

## Significance

The Daya Bay protests are widely regarded by historians and political analysts as a milestone in Hong Kong's political and social development. The mobilization demonstrated the growing capacity of Hong Kong civil society to organize on a massive scale and express collective anxieties regarding decisions made across the border. Although the protests did not halt the construction of the plant—which eventually commenced operations in the **1990s**—the movement helped shape local political consciousness, fostering activism and community engagement that would influence subsequent debates surrounding public policy, environmental standards, and democratic representation during the transition period.

## Perspectives

- **Hong Kong activists and residents**: Viewed the protests as a vital assertion of public safety, democratic expression, and civic responsibility, demonstrating that the local population could successfully organize to demand accountability from both colonial and mainland authorities.
- **Beijing and mainland authorities**: Stressed that the project adhered to rigorous international safety standards and was essential for regional economic development, while cautiously accommodating some public consultations to address local anxieties without compromising state planning.
