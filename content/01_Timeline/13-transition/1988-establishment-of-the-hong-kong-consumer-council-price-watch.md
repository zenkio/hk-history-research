## Background
During the late **1980s**, Hong Kong experienced significant economic shifts characterized by rising inflation and cost-of-living pressures. As the Sino-British negotiations concluded and the **1997** transition approached, local residents faced heightened anxieties about economic stability, market fairness, and the potential for speculative price hikes on essential goods. The **Consumer Council** (消費者委員會), established earlier in **1974**, faced increasing demands from the public to take a more active role in protecting consumer rights and maintaining market transparency.

## What happened
On **April 12, 1988**, the Consumer Council formally intensified and structured its price-monitoring initiatives, commonly referred to as price watch mechanisms, to track the costs of basic foodstuffs and household necessities across various districts. By systematically collecting and publishing comparative price data through the media, the council aimed to deter unfair trade practices and curb unwarranted price inflation. This initiative relied on regular surveys of supermarkets, wet markets, and retail outlets, providing citizens with accessible information to make informed purchasing decisions during a period of economic uncertainty.

## Significance
The expansion of structured price monitoring marked a crucial evolution in Hong Kong's consumer advocacy landscape. While the colonial administration traditionally favored a laissez-faire economic approach with minimal market intervention, the growing socio-political consciousness of the late transition era necessitated institutional responses to public welfare concerns. Price watch mechanisms not only empowered ordinary citizens facing inflationary strains but also set a precedent for consumer protection policies that persisted beyond the **1997** handover, reflecting a broader societal transition toward civic engagement and institutional accountability.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration**: Viewed market monitoring as a pragmatic measure to address public anxiety and maintain social stability without abandoning the core philosophy of a free-market economy.
- **Local Hong Kong residents and consumer advocates**: Welcomed enhanced price transparency as a vital safeguard against inflationary exploitation and profiteering during the turbulent transition period leading up to 1997.
