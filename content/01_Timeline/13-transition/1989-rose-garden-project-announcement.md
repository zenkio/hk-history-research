## Background
In the wake of the 1989 Tiananmen Square crackdown in Beijing, public confidence in Hong Kong's future plummeted, leading to significant emigration waves and economic anxiety. To reassure the local population and signal long-term economic viability under British administration, the Hong Kong Government needed a bold initiative. Governor **David Wilson** (衛奕信) and his administration accelerated plans to overhaul the territory's aging transport and logistics infrastructure, culminating in the comprehensive Port and Airport Development Strategy (PADS).

## What happened
On **October 11, 1989**, during his annual policy address to the Legislative Council, Governor **David Wilson** formally announced the ambitious infrastructure blueprint. Dubbed the 'Rose Garden Project,' the plan proposed shifting the international airport from the cramped urban location of **Kai Tak** (啟德) to **Chek Lap Kok** (赤鱲角) off **Lantau Island** (大嶼山), alongside the construction of a new container port, massive land reclamation, suspension bridges, and a dedicated railway network. The total estimated cost of the project was unprecedented, running into tens of billions of Hong Kong dollars.

## Significance
While the project successfully laid the physical foundations for Hong Kong's modern aviation and logistics dominance, it immediately became a major diplomatic flashpoint between Britain and China. Beijing raised serious concerns about the financial reserves the project would leave behind for the future Special Administrative Region government, leading to intense negotiations that eventually culminated in the 1991 Memorandum of Understanding concerning the Construction of the New Airport. The Rose Garden Project fundamentally reshaped Hong Kong's geography and demonstrated the high-stakes interplay between local economic planning and Sino-British sovereignty politics during the transitional period.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration**: Viewed the project as an essential economic stimulus and a morale-boosting initiative designed to secure Hong Kong's future prosperity and stability before the 1997 handover.
- **Beijing and Qing/Beijing perspective**: Initially suspected the project of being a British plot to deplete the financial reserves of the future Special Administrative Region, leading to demands for a veto over financial commitments extending past 1997.
- **Local Hong Kong community**: Generally welcomed the modernization and economic stimulus, though many citizens and local businesses expressed anxiety over the astronomical public spending and the potential inflationary pressures.
