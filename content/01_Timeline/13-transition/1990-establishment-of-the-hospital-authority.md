## Background

By the late **1980s**, Hong Kong's public medical services faced severe challenges, including bureaucratic inefficiencies, aging infrastructure, and escalating public demand. Following a comprehensive review of the medical system, the **Woo Pak-chuen** (胡百全) committee and subsequent reports recommended a complete overhaul of public hospital management to separate policy-making from day-to-day hospital operations.

## What happened

On **December 1, 1990**, the Hospital Authority (醫院管理局) was formally established under the Hospital Authority Ordinance. It assumed governing responsibility for all public hospitals in the territory, taking them over in phases from the government's Medical and Health Department. The transition unified previously fragmented hospital management under a single corporate body with greater financial and administrative autonomy, supported by substantial government funding injections.

## Significance

The creation of the Hospital Authority marked one of the most significant institutional reforms in Hong Kong's civil service history before the **1997** handover. It aimed to professionalize hospital administration, streamline patient services, and raise overall medical standards. While it successfully modernized many aspects of public healthcare delivery, the long-term sustainability of its funding model and workload pressures on medical staff remain subjects of ongoing debate.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Government**: Viewed the reform as a progressive step to modernize public administration and ensure high standards of governance and service delivery in healthcare before the conclusion of British administration.
- **Local Medical Professionals and Staff**: Expressed initial anxieties regarding restructuring and corporatization, though many welcomed the increased autonomy and upgraded facilities aimed at improving patient care.
