## Background

By the late 1980s, the Sino-British Joint Declaration signed in 1984 had set the terms for Hong Kong's return to Chinese sovereignty in **1997**. This political transition generated deep anxieties and a sense of rootlessness among the local population, particularly the generation born and raised in the territory. Amidst a booming commercial film industry, a new wave of filmmakers began to explore these contemporary psychological anxieties rather than traditional genre tropes. Director **Wong Kar-wai** (**王家衛**), following his directorial debut *As Tears Go By* (**旺角卡巩**), sought to create a deeply atmospheric and introspective project that reflected the shifting emotional landscape of the city.

## What happened

*Days of Being Wild* (**阿飛正傳**) premiered in Hong Kong on **December 15, 1990**. Featuring an ensemble cast of prominent stars including **Leslie Cheung** (**張國榮**), **Maggie Cheung** (**張曼玉**), **Carina Lau** (**劉嘉玲**), **Andy Lau** (**劉德華**), **Jacky Cheung** (**張学友**), and **Tony Leung Chiu-wai** (**梁朝僉**), the film departed sharply from standard narrative conventions. Set in **1960**, it followed a restless young man named Yuddy and the people whose lives intersected with his. Despite its stellar cast and acclaimed cinematography by **Christopher Doyle** (**杜可風**), the film underperformed at the local box office, leading to the cancellation of its planned sequel.

## Significance

Though commercially unsuccessful upon its initial release, *Days of Being Wild* is now widely regarded as a landmark in Hong Kong cinema. Later film scholarship views the movie as a definitive artistic expression of the pre-1997 Hong Kong psyche, perfectly encapsulating the themes of displacement, temporal anxiety, and unfulfilled longing. The film established Wong Kar-wai's signature directorial style—characterized by lush visuals, fragmented storytelling, and haunting depictions of urban alienation—and cemented his status as a major auteur on the international film stage.

## Perspectives

- **Local Hong Kong Cultural Critics**: Critics and later scholars viewed the film as an essential mirror of the city's pre-1997 identity crisis, capturing the poignant feeling of being suspended between a receding colonial past and an uncertain future.
- **Mainstream Audiences (1990)**: At the time of release, many general moviegoers accustomed to fast-paced comedies and action films found the deliberate pacing, ambiguous narrative, and melancholy tone difficult to engage with, resulting in weak box office returns.
