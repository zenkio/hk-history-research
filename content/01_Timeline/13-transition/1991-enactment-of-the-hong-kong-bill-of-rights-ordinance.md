## Background

Following the signing of the Sino-British Joint Declaration in 1984, the future of Hong Kong (香港) was set for a 1997 transfer of sovereignty to the People's Republic of China. In the wake of the 1989 Tiananmen Square protests, local anxiety regarding civil liberties and the protection of fundamental rights under Chinese rule surged significantly. To address these concerns and stabilize the local population, the colonial administration under Governor **David Wilson** (衛奕信) moved to codify civil rights protections locally.

## What happened

On **June 8, 1991**, the Legislative Council enacted the Hong Kong Bill of Rights Ordinance. The primary mechanism of the ordinance was to domesticate the provisions of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR), as applied to Hong Kong, into domestic legislation. It bound all public authorities and government organs, and crucially included an overriding effect over prior inconsistent legislation, meaning older laws violating the ordinance could be challenged in court.

## Significance

The ordinance represented a watershed moment in Hong Kong legal history, shifting the territory toward a constitutional model centered on judicially enforceable human rights. It served as a vital precursor to the Basic Law (基本法), which also stipulated that the provisions of the ICCPR as applied to Hong Kong shall remain in force. However, its longevity and legal supremacy became a point of intense contention between British and Chinese authorities during the final years of the transition.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial government**: Viewed the ordinance as a necessary and responsible measure to anchor the rule of law and international human rights standards in local jurisprudence, providing reassurance to residents facing the 1997 transition.
- **Beijing and Qing/PRC authorities**: Criticized the legislation as a unilateral British attempt to alter the legal framework established by the Joint Declaration, arguing that the ordinance's overriding status improperly superseded local laws and the future Basic Law.
- **Local Hong Kong legal community**: Generally welcomed the ordinance for empowering the judiciary to protect civil liberties, though debates persisted regarding the exact scope of its overriding effect on existing legislation.
