## Background

Prior to **1991**, Hong Kong's domestic radio broadcasting market had long been dominated by a duopoly consisting of the government-funded Radio Television Hong Kong (香港電台) and the commercial broadcaster Commercial Radio Hong Kong (香港商業電台). As the territory approached its 1997 transition, the colonial government sought to broaden public choices and modernize telecommunications and media regulations. Hutchison Whampoa (和記黃埔), a major conglomerate controlled by **Li Ka-shing** (李嘉誠), successfully secured a new broadcasting license to operate a third commercial radio network, leading to the establishment of Metro Broadcast (新城廣播).

## What happened

On **July 1, 1991**, Metro Broadcast officially commenced its operations, introducing fresh competition into the airwaves. The station initially operated multiple channels targeting different demographics and musical tastes, aiming to capture the increasingly affluent and cosmopolitan local consumer base. By utilizing distinct formatting strategies, Metro sought to carve out a niche separate from the established programming of Commercial Radio and RTHK.

## Significance

The launch of Metro Broadcast marked a significant milestone in Hong Kong's media history by ending decades of a two-player radio market. It reflected the vibrant and expanding consumer culture of the early **1990s**, driven by economic growth during the Sino-British transition period. The entry of a new commercial player forced existing broadcasters to adapt their programming, ultimately offering listeners greater diversity in entertainment and news delivery.

## Perspectives

- **Local media analysts and commercial observers**: Many industry observers viewed the launch as a necessary and overdue modernization of Hong Kong's media ecosystem, demonstrating the viability of increased commercial competition and corporate investment in cultural industries during the transition era.
- **Cultural historians and critical scholars**: Some later scholarship suggests that while Metro Broadcast increased channel diversity, the commercial pressures of the new station also accelerated the trend toward mainstream, consumer-driven entertainment, sometimes at the expense of niche cultural programming.
