## Background

Originally established as a Chinese military outpost during the Song dynasty and later fortified in the 19th century, the Kowloon Walled City (九龍城寨) occupied a unique jurisdictional status. Under the 1898 Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory, the New Territories was leased to Britain for 99 years, but the Walled City was explicitly excluded from British jurisdiction unless its presence interfered with the defense of British Hong Kong. Over subsequent decades, governance effectively became a vacuum. Following the Japanese occupation of Hong Kong in World War II, squatters rapidly occupied the site. By the mid-20th century, it evolved into a labyrinthine, high-density cluster of unauthorized buildings largely untouched by colonial building regulations or effective policing.

## What happened

After years of tense negotiations, the governments of the United Kingdom and the People's Republic of China reached a formal agreement in January 1987 to demolish the Walled City and turn the site into a public park. Eviction and clearance operations began in earnest, accompanied by massive compensation and rehousing disputes. Residents and business owners staged various protests over valuation terms and relocation sites. Despite resistance, forced clearances proceeded. The structural demolition officially commenced on **March 23, 1993**, and was completed by April 1994, permanently erasing the physical enclave.

## Significance

The destruction of the Kowloon Walled City marked a pivotal moment during the transitional period leading up to the 1997 handover of Hong Kong. It eliminated a notorious symbol of lawlessness, transnational crime, and administrative neglect that had long challenged both colonial and mainland authorities. Culturally, the enclave's surreal architecture and vibrant underground economy captured global imagination, inspiring numerous cyberpunk aesthetics, films, and scholarly studies on urban resilience and autonomy. Today, the site is preserved as Kowloon Walled City Park (九龍城寨公園), which incorporates preserved artifacts and historical remnants of the old fort.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Government**: Viewed the Walled City as an intolerable breeding ground for crime, drugs, and unsanitary conditions, as well as an ongoing jurisdictional headache that complicated effective urban planning near Kai Tak Airport.
- **Qing and Beijing Authorities**: Historically maintained that the enclave remained under Chinese sovereignty, though twentieth-century Beijing largely deferred practical management to avoid diplomatic friction until the joint decision was made to clear the area prior to the 1997 handover.
- **Local Residents and Community**: Many residents developed a distinct sense of community, mutual aid, and self-reliance within the dense settlement, leading many to initially resist eviction or demand more favorable compensation and housing terms from the government.
