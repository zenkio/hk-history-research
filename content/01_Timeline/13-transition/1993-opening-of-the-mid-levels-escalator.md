## Background
By the late **1980s**, the densely populated residential area of Mid-Levels (半山) and the commercial core of Central (中環) faced severe traffic congestion. Navigating the steep slopes between Des Voeux Road Central and Conduit Road relied heavily on narrow winding roads and congested minibuses. To alleviate traffic and improve pedestrian transit, the Hong Kong Government commissioned the construction of an ambitious urban transport link. The project, officially known as the Central-Mid-Levels Escalator and Walkway System (中環至半山自動扶手電梯系統), was designed not only as a functional commuter link but also as an innovative engineering feat tailored to Hong Kong's unique vertical topography.

## What happened
On **October 15, 1993**, the escalator system officially opened to the public. Spanning over 800 meters in length and climbing an elevation of 135 meters, the interconnected system of 18 escalators and 3 inclined moving walkways became the longest outdoor covered escalator system in the world. Operating in a reversible flow to match peak commuting hours—downhill in the mornings and uphill from the afternoon until midnight—the system was projected to carry tens of thousands of daily commuters. The total construction cost ultimately exceeded initial budget estimates, drawing public scrutiny over public expenditure during the Sino-British transition period.

## Significance
The opening of the escalator system profoundly transformed the urban landscape of the affected historic neighborhoods, particularly along Hollywood Road (荷李活道) and Caine Road (堅道). Beyond its primary transit function, it catalyzed rapid commercial gentrification, paving the way for the emergence of the vibrant SoHo dining and entertainment district. While urban planners viewed it as a successful model of pedestrianized high-density transit, scholars and local residents later debated its mixed legacy of real estate displacement, rising living costs, and the alteration of traditional neighborhood character.

## Perspectives

- **Colonial Government and Urban Planners**: Viewed the project as a triumph of modern civil engineering that successfully solved complex topography challenges, eased traffic congestion, and modernized pedestrian infrastructure in a booming financial hub.
- **Local Residents and Small Businesses**: Experienced mixed outcomes; while some welcomed the improved connectivity and increased foot traffic for commerce, many long-term residents and traditional shop owners faced severe displacement due to rapid gentrification and soaring property values.
