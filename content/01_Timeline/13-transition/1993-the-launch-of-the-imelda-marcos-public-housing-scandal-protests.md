## Background

By the early 1990s, Hong Kong was experiencing rapid economic changes and soaring living costs during the Sino-British transitional period. The Hong Kong Housing Authority (香港房屋委員會) regularly reviewed and adjusted public rental housing rents to maintain financial self-sufficiency. However, many grassroots residents felt that these incremental increases placed an unbearable financial burden on working-class families, intensifying discontent over widening social inequality and housing affordability.

## What happened

On **May 12, 1993**, tenant groups and grassroots activists organized coordinated protests against the rent hikes. Protesters famously deployed the satirical moniker of former Philippine First Lady Imelda Marcos to target senior housing officials, drawing a public comparison between perceived bureaucratic extravagance or detachment and the controversial footwear collection of Marcos. Demonstrators marched to government offices, chanting slogans, submitting petitions, and demanding a freeze on public housing rent increases alongside structural reforms to the rent-setting mechanism.

## Significance

The protests marked a notable moment in the evolution of local civic engagement and urban governance in Hong Kong. By utilizing sharp political satire and mobilizing residents across various public estates, grassroots organizers demonstrated the growing capacity of working-class communities to challenge colonial administrative policies. The events underscored the deep anxieties of ordinary citizens regarding their socioeconomic future on the eve of the 1997 handover.

## Perspectives

- **British colonial administration**: The government maintained that public housing rent adjustments were necessary and formula-driven to ensure the sustainable development and maintenance of public estates, viewing the protests as a localized reaction to broader economic adjustments.
- **Grassroots tenant organizations**: Activists and local residents argued that relentless rent hikes disproportionately penalized the working class, serving as evidence of an unresponsive administration that ignored widening inequality while prioritizing bureaucratic fiscal policies.
- **Later scholarship**: Historians and sociologists often view the 1993 protests as part of a broader wave of rising civil consciousness and political mobilization during the transitional era, reflecting how urban governance and social welfare became key arenas for civic expression before 1997.
