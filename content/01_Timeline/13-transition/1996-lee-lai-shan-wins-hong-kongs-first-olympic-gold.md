## Background

By the mid-1990s, Hong Kong was in the final stage of its transition from British colonial rule to sovereignty under the People's Republic of China, set for July 1, 1997. Amidst widespread anxieties about the impending political handover, sports served as a rare arena where Hong Kong competed on the international stage under its own banner and identity. Windsurfing had grown in popularity across the territory's coastal waters, and **Lee Lai-shan** (李麗珊), often known affectionately as "San San," emerged as a world-class athlete through rigorous training and international competitions.

## What happened

On **July 29, 1996**, at the Olympic Sailing Competition held in Savannah, Georgia, during the Atlanta Olympic Games, **Lee Lai-shan** (李麗珊) competed in the women's Mistral windsurfing event. Having demonstrated strong performances in the preliminary races, she maintained her composure under pressure in the medal race to secure the gold medal. This victory marked a historic milestone: it was the first Olympic medal, and the first gold medal, ever won by an athlete representing Hong Kong since the territory began participating in the Olympic Games in 1952.

## Significance

The victory was met with immense jubilation across Hong Kong, temporarily overshadowing the prevailing anxieties surrounding the 1997 handover. The achievement was widely celebrated as a symbol of local resilience, hard work, and athletic excellence. Her famous post-victory remark highlighting that Hong Kong athletes were not "goods without an owner" (不是垃圾) resonated deeply with the public, helping to crystallize a distinct Hong Kong identity separate from both British colonial administration and mainland political integration. The government and various community organizations honored her with financial awards and public celebrations, cementing her status as a local sporting legend.
