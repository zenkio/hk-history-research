## Background
As the 1997 handover of Hong Kong approached, the structure of the future Special Administrative Region (HKSAR) government was established under the Basic Law. The position of Chief Executive (行政長官) would replace the British-appointed Governor (香港總督) as the head of government and head of the territory. To select the first leader, Beijing established a 400-member Provisional Selection Committee (推選委員會) composed of various local business figures, professionals, and community leaders. **Tung Chee-hwa** (**董建華**), a prominent local shipping tycoon and former Executive Councillor, emerged as the leading candidate alongside former Chief Secretary **Anson Chan** (**陳方安生**) and high court judge **Yang Ti-liang** (**楊鐵樑**), though the latter two had different levels of institutional backing.

## What happened
On **December 11, 1996**, the Selection Committee met in Shenzhen to vote for the first Chief Executive. **Tung Chee-hwa** secured a decisive victory, winning 320 of the 400 votes cast by the committee members. His closest rival, **Yang Ti-liang**, received 42 votes, while **Peter Woo** (**吳光正**) received 36. Following the vote, **Tung** was officially appointed by the State Council of the People's Republic of China. His selection marked a crucial milestone in the transition process, establishing the civilian leadership that would take office alongside the transfer of sovereignty on July 1, 1997.

## Significance
The selection of **Tung Chee-hwa** symbolized both continuity and change for Hong Kong. As a wealthy businessman from a traditional pro-Beijing family with international experience, **Tung** was viewed by Beijing as a steady hand capable of maintaining economic confidence and investor trust. His appointment set the precedent for the 'executive-led' governance model enshrined in the Basic Law. However, the indirect selection method—conducted by a committee rather than through public election—also highlighted ongoing debates about the pace and nature of democratic development in the post-colonial era.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and Pro-Beijing Establishment**: Beijing viewed Tung Chee-hwa as a patriotic, trusted figure with deep international business acumen who could ensure a smooth transition, maintain Hong Kong's economic prosperity, and implement the 'One Country, Two Systems' framework effectively.
- **Pro-Democracy Activists and Local Critics**: Many local pro-democracy groups criticized the Selection Committee process as an undemocratic 'small circle' election controlled by Beijing, arguing that the first Chief Executive lacked a genuine popular mandate from the Hong Kong public.
- **British Colonial Government and Western Observers**: British officials generally accepted the selection as a pragmatic reality under the Sino-Joint Declaration and Basic Law framework, while carefully watching to see whether the new leader would safeguard Hong Kong's rule of law and existing freedoms.
