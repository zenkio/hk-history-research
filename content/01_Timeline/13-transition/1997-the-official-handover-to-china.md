## Background
The transfer of sovereignty followed the signing of the Sino-British Joint Declaration in 1984, which set the framework for Hong Kong (香港) to become a Special Administrative Region (特別行政區) under the principle of "one country, two systems" (一國兩制). While the New Territories (新界) were held under a 99-year lease expiring in 1997, the British government ultimately agreed to negotiate the return of the entire territory—including Hong Kong Island (香港島) and Kowloon (九龍)—due to the perceived indivisibility of the region's infrastructure and water supply.

## What happened
On **July 1, 1997**, a formal handover ceremony was held at the Hong Kong Convention and Exhibition Centre (香港會議展覽中心) in Wan Chai (灣仔). Dignitaries including British Prime Minister Tony Blair, Prince Charles, Chinese President Jiang Zemin, and outgoing Governor Chris Patten attended. At midnight, the Union Jack and the Hong Kong colonial flag were lowered, and the Chinese national flag and the regional flag of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region were raised, marking the formal end of British administration and the establishment of the HKSAR government led by Chief Executive Tung Chee-hwa (董建華).

## Significance
This event marked a major milestone in modern Chinese history, concluding the era of Western colonial presence in major Asian port cities. It tested the viability of the "one country, two systems" model, designed to preserve Hong Kong's capitalist system, legal framework, and civil liberties for at least 50 years.

## Perspectives

- **British and Western colonial perspective**: Often framed as an orderly and dignified departure after more than a century of economic stewardship, emphasizing the successful transition of a global financial hub built on rule of law and institutional stability.
- **Beijing and official Chinese perspective**: Viewed as a glorious historic redemption, correcting the humiliation of unequal treaties imposed during the Opium Wars and reuniting compatriots with the motherland under the framework of national sovereignty.
- **Local Hong Kong community perspective**: Characterized by complex and mixed emotions, ranging from optimism about economic integration with mainland China to deep anxieties regarding future civil liberties, political autonomy, and the preservation of distinct local identities.
