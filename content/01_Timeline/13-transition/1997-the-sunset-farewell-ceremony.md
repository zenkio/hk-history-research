## Background
The transition of sovereignty over Hong Kong from the United Kingdom to the People's Republic of China was set in motion by the **Sino-British Joint Declaration** signed in **1984**. As **June 30, 1997**, approached, both governments prepared a series of high-profile events to mark the handover at midnight. Among these, the British farewell ceremony was planned as a formal military and civil acknowledgment of the end of the colonial era, concluding decades of British administration that had expanded from Hong Kong Island to the Kowloon Peninsula and the New Territories.

## What happened
On the evening of **June 30, 1997**, a heavy downpour soaked the proceedings at the **HMS Tamar** (添馬艦) naval base on the waterfront of Hong Kong Island. **Prince Charles** (查爾斯王子), representing **Queen Elizabeth II** (伊麗莎白二世), and outgoing Governor **Chris Patten** (彭定康) attended the ceremony, which included a final military parade, address, and the lowering of the Union Flag. Despite the adverse weather conditions, the event was broadcast globally, symbolizing the final hours before the establishment of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region.

## Significance
The ceremony served as the dramatic visual conclusion to the British presence in Hong Kong. For many observers, the rain-soaked proceedings added a poignant atmosphere to the departure of the last major colony of the British Empire. However, the historical meaning of the event remains interpreted through diverse lenses, reflecting the complex political and social dynamics of the transition.

## Perspectives

- **British official perspective**: The ceremony was viewed as an orderly, dignified conclusion to a century and a half of administration, highlighting the legacy of the rule of law, institutional development, and economic prosperity left behind.
- **Beijing and official Chinese perspective**: The event marked the long-awaited termination of foreign occupation and unequal treaties, representing the restoration of Chinese sovereignty and national dignity over territory historically part of China.
- **Local Hong Kong perspective**: Public reaction among residents was deeply mixed, reflecting anxieties over future political freedoms and the preservation of local autonomy alongside hopes for stability and continued economic success.
