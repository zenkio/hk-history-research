## Background

Following its implementation in 1983, the linked exchange rate system pegged the Hong Kong dollar (港元) to the United States dollar at a rate of approximately 7.8 to 1. This mechanism served as a pillar of monetary stability for the territory through the 1984 Sino-British Joint Declaration and the 1997 handover of sovereignty to the People's Republic of China. However, by mid-1997, the Asian Financial Crisis began sweeping through Southeast Asia, putting immense pressure on regional currencies. Speculators began employing a "double play" strategy, shorting both the local stock market and the currency simultaneously, anticipating that the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) would eventually abandon the peg.

## What happened

On **October 23, 1997**, speculative pressure on the Hong Kong dollar reached a critical intensity. In response to massive capital outflows, the HKMA, led by **Joseph Yam** (任志剛), rigorously defended the peg by allowing interbank interest rates to skyrocket, with the overnight Hibor briefly surging to extreme levels. While this mechanism automatically drained liquidity and forced speculators to pay exorbitant costs to borrow local currency, it simultaneously triggered a severe contraction in the local economy. The Hang Seng Index (恆生指數) plummeted drastically, and local property and equity markets experienced severe downturns, causing widespread financial distress among businesses and homeowners.

## Significance

The successful defense of the currency peg in October 1997 preserved the credibility of Hong Kong's monetary framework during the early months of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (香港特別行政區). Yet, the episode exposed the vulnerability of small, open economies to speculative capital flows and ignited intense debates over state intervention in free markets, culminating in the HKMA's unprecedented direct intervention in the stock market in August 1998.

## Perspectives

- **Hong Kong Monetary Authorities**: Defending the peg was viewed as an absolute necessity to maintain international confidence, financial stability, and Hong Kong's status as a global financial center, even if it required severe economic pain.
- **Local Business and Property Communities**: Many local enterprises and property owners felt that the rigid defense mechanism and soaring interest rates imposed an unnecessarily harsh burden on the domestic economy, exacerbating the recession.
