## Background

In May **1997**, a three-year-old boy in Hong Kong fell ill and later died from a respiratory illness that was eventually identified as the H5N1 avian influenza virus. This marked the first time the virus was known to transmit directly from birds to humans. As the year progressed, additional human cases emerged, causing widespread public anxiety just months after the territory's sovereignty transfer from the United Kingdom to the People's Republic of China on July 1, **1997**.

## What happened

By **December 1997**, health authorities recognized the live poultry markets as the primary reservoir and vector for the virus. To prevent a potential pandemic, the government under Chief Executive **Tung Chee-hwa** (董建華) made the unprecedented decision to order the complete culling of the territory's entire chicken population. Beginning on **December 29, 1997**, government workers rapidly slaughtered an estimated 1.3 million chickens, ducks, geese, and quail across local farms and retail markets, while also temporarily banning live poultry imports from mainland China.

## Significance

The drastic intervention successfully halted the immediate outbreak, with no further human cases recorded during that wave. The crisis fundamentally transformed Hong Kong's public health policy, leading to permanent structural changes in food safety, market hygiene regulations, and veterinary surveillance. The event established a precedent for aggressive government responses to emerging infectious diseases, heavily influencing later management of public health emergencies such as the SARS outbreak in **2003**.

## Perspectives

- **Hong Kong Government and Public Health Officials**: Viewed the massive cull as a decisive, necessary emergency action that prevented a catastrophic human pandemic through swift intervention in the agricultural supply chain.
- **Local Poultry Farmers and Vendors**: Experienced the cull as a severe economic blow and livelihood crisis, initially expressing deep concern over compensation and the sudden destruction of their businesses.
