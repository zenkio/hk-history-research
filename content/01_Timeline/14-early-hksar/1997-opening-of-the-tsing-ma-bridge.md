## Background
In the early 1990s, the colonial administration of Hong Kong initiated the Airport Core Programme (香港機場核心計劃) to relocate the territory's primary aviation hub from Kai Tak (啟德) to Chek Lap Kok (赤鱲角). As the new airport was located on an island, a robust road and rail network was required to connect Lantau Island (大嶼山) with the urban areas of Kowloon (九龍) and the New Territories (新界). The Tsing Ma Bridge (青馬大橋) was conceived as the central component of this Lantau Link (青嶼幹線), designed to span the Ma Wan Channel (馬灣海峽) and provide a dual-use facility for both road traffic and the Mass Transit Railway (地下鐵路).

## What happened
The bridge was constructed between 1992 and 1997, with the final structural elements completed shortly before the official opening ceremony. On **April 27, 1997**, the bridge was formally inaugurated. It featured a main span of 1,377 meters, making it, at the time of its completion, the world's longest span suspension bridge carrying both road and rail traffic. The bridge connects Tsing Yi (青衣) to Ma Wan (馬灣) and extends toward Lantau. The construction project utilized advanced engineering techniques, including massive anchorages and double-deck design to ensure stability during typhoons, which are frequent in the region.

## Significance
The completion of the Tsing Ma Bridge served as a major physical expansion of Hong Kong's infrastructure, signaling a modernization effort aimed at maintaining the city's status as a global logistics and aviation hub. For the colonial government, it represented a legacy of efficient infrastructure development in the final months of British administration. For the broader public, the bridge significantly reduced travel time to the western New Territories and Lantau, fostering new residential and commercial development in areas previously considered remote. Its engineering scale and iconic status made it an instant landmark, often featured in promotional materials showcasing the city's technical capabilities.

## Perspectives

- **Colonial Administration and Pro-development advocates**: The administration viewed the bridge as the crowning achievement of the Airport Core Programme, demonstrating the success of colonial-era long-term planning and technical expertise. They emphasized the project's ability to facilitate economic growth and connectivity during the territory's transition period.
- **New Territories local communities**: Residents in rural areas near the bridge approaches experienced significant disruption during the construction phase, including changes to land use and increased traffic. While the improved accessibility was welcomed, some communities expressed concerns about the preservation of traditional land rights and the encroachment of urban development on their established lifestyles.
