## Background

The status of Hong Kong was shaped by nineteenth-century imperial expansion. Following the First Opium War, the island of Hong Kong was ceded to Britain under the Treaty of Nanking in **1842**. The Kowloon Peninsula was added in **1860** by the Convention of Peking, and the New Territories (新界) were leased for 99 years under the Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory in **1898**. As the expiration date of the New Territories lease approached in the late **1970s** and early **1980s**, uncertainties regarding land leases and future governance prompted formal negotiations between London and Beijing. These talks culminated in the Sino-British Joint Declaration of **1984**, which outlined the "One Country, Two Systems" (一國兩制) framework, ensuring that Hong Kong's capitalist system and way of life would remain unchanged for 50 years.

## What happened

At midnight on **July 1, 1997**, a handover ceremony was held at the Hong Kong Convention and Exhibition Centre (香港會議展覽中心) in Wan Chai (灣仔). The Union Jack and the Hong Kong colonial flag were lowered, and the flag of the People's Republic of China and the regional flag of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (香港特別行政區) were raised. British troops departed, and the People's Liberation Army garrison (中國人民解放軍駐香港部隊) entered the territory. **Tung Chee-hwa** (董建華) was sworn in as the first Chief Executive of the newly established HKSAR, and the Provisional Legislative Council (臨時立法會) assumed legislative duties.

## Significance

This event marked a major milestone in global decolonization and modern Chinese history, symbolizing the end of foreign territorial concessions in China. Globally, it served as a unique constitutional experiment in sub-national autonomy under a socialist sovereign state. Domestically, it set a precedent for the subsequent handover of Macau in **1999** while initiating a complex transition period defined by legal harmonization, identity formation, and the ongoing implementation of the Basic Law (香港特別行政區基本法).

## Perspectives

- **British colonial perspective**: Often emphasized a successful, orderly withdrawal and the legacy of institutional foundations, rule of law, and civil service infrastructure left behind for the territory's future governance.
- **Beijing and Qing historical perspective**: Viewed the transfer as the historic correction of nineteenth-century unequal treaties and the triumphant return of lost territory to the motherland, restoring national sovereignty and dignity.
- **Local Hong Kong and New Territories communities**: Exhibited mixed feelings ranging from anxiety over political futures and emigration waves to optimism regarding economic integration with mainland China and local self-administration.
