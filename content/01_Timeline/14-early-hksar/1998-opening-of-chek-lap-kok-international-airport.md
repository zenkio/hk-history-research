## Background

By the late 20th century, the historic **Kai Tak Airport** (啟德機場), located in the densely populated urban area of Kowloon, was operating far beyond its capacity. Famous for its thrilling and technically challenging approach through the surrounding urban high-rises, Kai Tak could no longer accommodate the growing demands of international trade and passenger travel. To secure Hong Kong's future as a premier global aviation and logistics hub, the colonial government initiated the massive Airport Core Programme in the late 1980s and 1990s. The centerpiece of this grand infrastructure project was the construction of a brand-new, state-of-the-art airport on reclaimed land off the northern coast of **Lantau Island** (大嶼山), encompassing the leveled islands of **Chek Lap Kok** (赤鱲角) and Lam Chau.

## What happened

On **July 6, 1998**, the new **Hong Kong International Airport** (香港國際機場) officially commenced commercial operations, just a year after the sovereignty transfer of Hong Kong to the People's Republic of China. The transition from Kai Tak to Chek Lap Kok—dubbed the 'Airport Chek Lap Kok Transition'—was executed overnight. However, the opening day was infamously marred by severe logistical chaos. Delays in the cargo handling system, baggage mishandling, computer glitches, and transportation bottlenecks severely disrupted flight schedules, leading to widespread frustration among travelers and embarrassment for the government and airport management.

## Significance

Despite the initial operational hiccups, the opening of the Chek Lap Kok facility successfully expanded Hong Kong's capacity and reinforced its status as a vital international aviation gateway. The vast engineering feat of flattening islands and reclaiming land significantly boosted regional infrastructure and employment. Over subsequent years, the airport consistently ranked among the world's best, facilitating massive cargo throughput and passenger connections that underpinned the economic vitality of the **Hong Kong Special Administrative Region** (香港特別行政區).

## Perspectives

- **Government and Business Community**: Viewed the mega-project and the new airport as an essential triumph of engineering and a bold statement of confidence in Hong Kong's continued economic prosperity and global connectivity following the 1997 handover.
- **Travelers and Logistics Operators**: Remembered the initial opening weeks with frustration due to the severe baggage and cargo handling chaos, contrasting the high expectations of modern technological efficiency with the messy reality of Day One.
