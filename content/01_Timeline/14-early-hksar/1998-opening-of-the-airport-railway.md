## Background
During the final decade of British colonial administration, the 'Airport Core Programme' was initiated to facilitate the transition to a new international aviation hub at **Chek Lap Kok** (赤鱲角). The existing Kai Tak Airport (啟德機場) had become constrained by urban density, prompting the government to develop a comprehensive infrastructure network. This included not only the airport itself but also the construction of the **Lantau Link** (大嶼山幹線) and the **Airport Railway** (機場鐵路), which would bridge the gap between the isolated island of Lantau and the metropolitan core.

## What happened
The Airport Railway officially opened on **June 22, 1998**, shortly after the inauguration of the new airport. Operated by the **MTR Corporation** (香港鐵路有限公司), the system consists of two distinct services: the **Airport Express** (機場快綫), providing high-speed transit directly to the airport, and the **Tung Chung Line** (東涌綫), which serves the new town of **Tung Chung** (東涌) and intermediate communities. The project involved complex engineering feats, including the reclamation of land and the construction of the **Tsing Ma Bridge** (青馬大橋), one of the world's longest suspension bridges, to carry both road and rail traffic.

## Significance
The completion of the railway fundamentally altered the physical and economic geography of Hong Kong. By integrating the remote island of Lantau into the urban rail network, the project facilitated the rapid development of Tung Chung as a residential hub and streamlined business connectivity to the territory’s primary international gateway. For the newly established **Hong Kong Special Administrative Region** (香港特別行政區), the successful operation of these infrastructure projects served as a demonstration of technical continuity and state capacity during the early years of the transition from British rule to Chinese sovereignty. While the project was completed under the SAR government, the planning and majority of the physical construction occurred under the colonial administration, leading to ongoing historical discussions regarding the division of credit for the territory's modern infrastructure legacy.

## Perspectives

- **British Colonial Administration**: The project is often cited as a testament to the efficient infrastructure planning and civil engineering standards established during the late colonial period. Supporters emphasize that the project was a crowning achievement of the 'Airport Core Programme,' designed to ensure Hong Kong's long-term economic competitiveness before the 1997 handover.
- **Beijing and HKSAR Government**: This perspective highlights the railway as a symbol of the successful transition and the central government's support for Hong Kong's ongoing development. Officials focus on the operational success of the system within the new HKSAR framework, viewing it as evidence of the 'One Country, Two Systems' model fostering stability and prosperity.
