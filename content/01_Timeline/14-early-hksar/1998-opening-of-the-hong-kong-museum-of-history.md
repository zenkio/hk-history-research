## Background

Prior to the opening of the purpose-built facility, the **Hong Kong Museum of History** (香港歷史博物館) had occupied various temporary locations since its formal establishment in **1975**, following the split of the former City Museum and Art Gallery. As the **1997** sovereignty transfer approached, colonial authorities and local cultural institutions placed increasing emphasis on preserving distinct local heritage. Planning for a dedicated, permanent home in **Tsim Sha Tsui East** (尖沙咀東) began to take shape to accommodate a growing collection and to provide a more ambitious narrative of the region's development.

## What happened

On **July 1, 1998**, exactly one year after the establishment of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, the museum officially opened its new premises at **100 Chatham Road South** (漆咸道南100號). The centerpiece of the new facility was the permanent exhibition titled **"The Hong Kong Story"** (香港故事). Spanning multiple eras, the exhibition utilized extensive dioramas, multimedia presentations, and thousands of artifacts to take visitors on a chronological journey from prehistoric times through dynastic China, the British colonial period, the Japanese occupation, and up to the handover.

## Significance

The opening of the museum marked a significant milestone in institutionalizing a localized narrative of Hong Kong history. By dedicating substantial space to the everyday life, customs, and economic struggles of ordinary residents—including fishermen, farmers, and urban workers—the exhibits helped foster a distinct Hong Kong identity that went beyond the dual narratives of British administration and mainland Chinese dynastic history. However, the framing of specific historical eras, particularly the colonial period and the nature of Chinese sovereignty, remained subject to ongoing scholarly and political interpretation.

## Perspectives

- **Local Hong Kong community and scholars**: Many local historians and residents viewed the museum as a long-overdue validation of a distinct Hong Kong identity, focusing on the lived experiences and resilience of ordinary people rather than just colonial governors or dynastic rulers.
- **Official perspectives (British colonial legacy and Beijing)**: While colonial-era planners initiated the museum project to document regional history, the post-1997 HKSAR government utilized the space to integrate Hong Kong's narrative more firmly into the broader tapestry of Chinese national history, emphasizing the return to the motherland.
