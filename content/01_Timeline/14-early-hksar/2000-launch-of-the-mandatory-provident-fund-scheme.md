## Background
For decades, Hong Kong's social welfare system relied largely on family support, personal savings, and a modest public assistance framework, with relatively little formal retirement protection for the general working population outside of the civil service and certain large corporations. Proposals for a compulsory retirement scheme had been debated since the late colonial era, reflecting growing concerns about an aging population. However, previous initiatives faced strong resistance from employers and employees alike over cost and management concerns.

## What happened
On **December 1, 2000**, the Mandatory Provident Fund (強制性公積金) scheme officially came into operation following the passage of the Mandatory Provident Fund Schemes Ordinance during the colonial period and subsequent preparatory work by the HKSAR administration. Under the new system, most employers and employees aged 18 to 64 were legally required to contribute a percentage of their relevant income into privately managed investment funds registered with the newly established Mandatory Provident Fund Schemes Authority (積金局). The rollout marked a major structural shift in Hong Kong's approach to social security, moving away from reliance on discretionary welfare toward mandatory capitalization.

## Significance
The launch of the MPF represented a watershed moment in the development of Hong Kong's social security landscape. It institutionalized retirement savings for the vast majority of the working population. At the same time, the scheme's reliance on private financial institutions and exposure to market fluctuations generated ongoing public debate regarding management fees, investment risks, and the adequacy of retirement protection, setting the stage for decades of subsequent policy adjustments and political discussion.

## Perspectives

- **HKSAR Government and Pro-Business Sectors**: Authorities and many business representatives viewed the MPF as a sustainable, market-driven solution to the demographic challenge of an aging population, fostering a culture of personal financial responsibility without imposing an unsustainable tax-funded burden on public finances.
- **Labor Unions and Grassroots Advocates**: Labor groups frequently criticized the scheme for high administrative fees charged by private trustees, low initial yields, and mechanisms such as the 'offsetting arrangement' (對沖機制) that allowed employers to use severance and long-service payments from their MPF contributions, arguing that it undermined workers' retirement security.
