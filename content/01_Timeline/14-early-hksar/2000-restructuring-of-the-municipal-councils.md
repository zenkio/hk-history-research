## Background

Established during the colonial era, the Urban Council (市政局) and later the Regional Council (區域市政局) were responsible for municipal services such as public sanitation, cultural venues, and food safety licensing. Over time, these bodies evolved from appointed committees into fully elected institutions by the early **1990s**, serving as key training grounds for local politicians and a platform for municipal-level democratic participation.

## What happened

Following the handover of sovereignty in **1997**, the future of the two councils became a subject of intense debate within the HKSAR government. Led by Chief Executive **Tung Chee-hwa** (董建華), the administration proposed dissolving both councils to streamline municipal services and improve administrative efficiency. Despite strong opposition from various political parties and incumbent council members, the Legislative Council passed the legislation, leading to the formal abolition of the Urban Council and Regional Council on **January 1, 2000**. Their responsibilities were subsequently absorbed by newly formed government departments, namely the Food and Environmental Hygiene Department (食物環境衞生署) and the Leisure and Cultural Services Department (康樂及文化事務署).

## Significance

The restructuring marked a major turning point in Hong Kong's governance structure, shifting power away from autonomous municipal bodies back toward the central government bureaucracy. While the administration maintained that the reform eliminated redundancy and improved service delivery, critics and democratic advocates viewed the move as a significant rollback of local representative democracy.

## Perspectives

- **HKSAR Government**: The reorganization was presented as an administrative necessity to eliminate duplication, enhance efficiency, and ensure better coordination of municipal services under a unified civil service structure.
- **Democratic Politicians and Local Critics**: The abolition was widely interpreted as a deliberate regression in democratic development, stripping citizens of direct representation in local municipal affairs and dismantling established democratic institutions.
