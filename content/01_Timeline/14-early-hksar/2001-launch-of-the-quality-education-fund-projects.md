## Background
Following the transfer of sovereignty in **1997**, the government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region faced increasing pressure to reform an education system often criticized for rote learning and rigid pedagogical structures. To foster innovation and bottom-up school-based initiatives, the Chief Executive announced the establishment of the Quality Education Fund (優質教育基金) in the **1997** Policy Address, backed by a dedicated allocation of **$5 billion** Hong Kong dollars. By **2001**, the fund had become a primary vehicle for transforming day-to-day school operations, supporting thousands of projects aimed at upgrading facilities, supporting student holistic development, and integrating information technology into traditional classrooms.

## What happened
On **March 12, 2001**, and throughout that spring, various QEF-funded projects showcased how schools were utilizing grants to purchase computers, establish multimedia learning centers, and design specialized curricula. Educators applied for grants to pilot teacher-training workshops, psychological support services, and innovative teaching methods. While some schools successfully revolutionized their learning environments, others struggled with the administrative burden of grant applications and project evaluations, highlighting a digital and resource divide among different institutions across the territory.

## Significance
The widespread injection of QEF funding marked a pivotal shift in Hong Kong's educational governance, moving away from purely centralized directives toward more decentralized, school-based management. Supporters argue that the initiative laid the crucial technological and pedagogical groundwork for the 21st-century knowledge economy in Hong Kong (香港). Conversely, critics and educational researchers have debated whether one-off project funding produced sustainable, long-term structural improvements or merely created short-term cosmetic changes in schools.

## Perspectives

- **Government and Pro-Establishment View**: Officials viewed the Quality Education Fund as a vital catalyst for modernizing Hong Kong's education system, empowering frontline educators to innovate, and rapidly narrowing the technology gap in local schools.
- **Critical Educators and Scholars**: Some teachers and researchers argued that the heavy bureaucracy involved in applying for and auditing grants added undue workload, while failing to address deeper systemic issues within the examination-driven curriculum.
