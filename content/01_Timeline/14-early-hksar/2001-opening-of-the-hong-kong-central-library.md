## Background
During the mid-1990s, the Urban Council (市政局) identified a need for a central repository to serve as the hub for Hong Kong's library network. The project was conceived as a move to provide modern, high-tech intellectual infrastructure, replacing the older City Hall Public Library as the primary facility. Construction began in 1998, shortly after the transfer of sovereignty, on a site in Causeway Bay (銅鑼灣) situated near Victoria Park (維多利亞公園).

## What happened
The **Hong Kong Central Library (香港中央圖書館)** officially opened its doors on **May 17, 2001**. The building, designed with a distinct crescent-shaped facade, spans 12 floors and covers a gross floor area of approximately 33,800 square meters. At the time of its inauguration, it represented the largest library facility in the territory, offering a comprehensive collection of books, multimedia materials, and specialized research archives. The facility introduced digitized catalogs and high-speed information access points, marking a transition toward digital literacy for the general public.

## Significance
The opening of the library is often viewed as a milestone in the development of the Special Administrative Region's cultural policy. For many, it represented the maturation of public services in the post-colonial era, symbolizing an investment in knowledge-based economic development. The library acts as a central node for the Hong Kong Public Libraries (香港公共圖書館) network, coordinating inter-library loans and housing the central archives. While its architectural design was intended to be iconic, opinions on its aesthetic and functional success have remained varied among urban planners and the public since its completion.

## Perspectives

- **Government and Modernization Advocates**: The administration viewed the library as an essential component of 'Asia's World City' branding, demonstrating a commitment to high-tech infrastructure. Supporters argue that the library successfully centralized intellectual resources to democratize access to information.
- **Heritage and Architectural Critics**: Some local commentators questioned whether the massive, modern structure adequately reflected the traditional cultural history of Causeway Bay. Critics occasionally note that the emphasis on digital centralization potentially marginalized smaller, community-run reading spaces that served the grassroots population.
