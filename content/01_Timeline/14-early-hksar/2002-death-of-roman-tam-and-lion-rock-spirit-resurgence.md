## Background

Following the 1997 handover to the People's Republic of China, Hong Kong faced severe economic headwinds, compounded by the 1997 Asian financial crisis and the 2003 SARS outbreak. Property values plummeted, unemployment rose, and public anxiety grew regarding the territory's future under the 'One Country, Two Systems' framework. Amidst this atmosphere of despondency, **Roman Tam** (羅文), widely celebrated as the Godfather of Cantopop, passed away from liver cancer on **October 18, 2002**.

## What happened

**Roman Tam** (羅文) died at the Queen Mary Hospital (瑪麗醫院) in Hong Kong at the age of 52. His death triggered an outpouring of public grief across the territory. Media retrospectives heavily featured his extensive discography, notably his 1979 theme song for the television series *Below the Lion Rock* (獅子山下). Originally broadcast by RTHK (香港電台), the song and its associated imagery of the iconic hill in Kowloon became a focal point for mourning Tam and reflecting on the hardships facing ordinary citizens.

## Significance

In the wake of Tam's passing and ongoing economic distress, 'Below the Lion Rock' was increasingly embraced as an unofficial anthem for Hong Kong people. The phrase 'Lion Rock Spirit' (獅子山精神), which traditionally evoked hard work, perseverance, and mutual aid during the post-war industrialization era, was revitalized by politicians, community leaders, and the public. Later scholarship notes that while the revived slogan served to unite a demoralized populace, it was also deployed by various political factions—and later by the government itself—to foster social cohesion and encourage self-reliance during challenging times.

## Perspectives

- **Local Hong Kong community**: For many local residents, the widespread adoption of 'Below the Lion Rock' after Roman Tam's death provided emotional solace and a shared identity during a painful economic downturn.
- **Later scholarship**: Researchers often analyze the 2002 revival of the Lion Rock Spirit as a constructed cultural nostalgia, noting how a traditional working-class ethic was re-packaged to encourage civic endurance amidst structural economic changes.
