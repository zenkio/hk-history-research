## Background
Following the handover of sovereignty in **1997** and the economic shocks of the **2003** SARS outbreak, the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR) government sought new avenues for economic revitalization. Tourism and retail sectors had been severely depressed, prompting Chief Executive **Tung Chee-hwa** (董建華) to seek closer economic integration with mainland China. Discussions between the central government in Beijing and local officials led to the conceptualization of a policy that would dismantle the traditional requirement for mainland visitors to travel exclusively through government-approved group tours.

## What happened
On **July 28, 2003**, the Central People's Government officially launched the Individual Visit Scheme (個人遊), commonly known as the "Free Walk" (自由行). Initially rolled out as a pilot program for residents of four Guangdong (廣東) provincial cities—Guangzhou (廣州), Shenzhen (深圳), Zhuhai (珠海), and Foshan (佛山)—the scheme allowed eligible individuals to apply for exit endorsements to visit Hong Kong without joining a tour group. Over the subsequent years, Beijing progressively expanded the scheme to cover dozens of other mainland cities, including Beijing and Shanghai, drastically increasing the potential pool of travelers.

## Significance
The scheme triggered an unprecedented tourism and retail boom in Hong Kong, injecting much-needed capital into the local economy, boosting employment in service sectors, and driving up commercial property and retail rents, particularly in districts like Tsim Sha Tsui (尖沙咀) and Causeway Bay (銅鑼灣). At the same time, the massive influx of mainland visitors strained local infrastructure, public transport, and hospital resources, while altering the cultural landscape of traditional neighborhoods and setting the stage for subsequent debates about local capacity and cross-border integration.

## Perspectives

- **HKSAR Government and Business Sector**: Viewed the scheme as a vital economic lifeline that successfully rescued Hong Kong from the post-SARS recession, stimulated retail and property markets, and deepened mutually beneficial integration with the mainland economy.
- **Local Residents and Critics**: Expressed growing concern over resource allocation, crowding in public spaces, rising inflation, and the changing character of local retail which increasingly catered exclusively to luxury mainland shoppers.
