## Background
Following the 1997 handover to the People's Republic of China, Hong Kong faced severe economic headwinds. The Asian financial crisis of 1997-1998 deflated property and stock market bubbles, while the outbreak of Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome (SARS) in early **2003** severely impacted tourism, retail, and local commerce. To stimulate the economy and foster closer ties, discussions between the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR) government and the Central People's Government in Beijing intensified, leading to a preferential free trade agreement designed to grant Hong Kong goods and service providers advantageous access to the mainland market.

## What happened
On **June 29, 2003**, the Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement (內地與香港關於建立更緊密經貿關係的安排, **CEPA**) was officially signed in Hong Kong. The agreement was executed by **Tung Chee-hwa** (董建華), the Chief Executive of Hong Kong, and **Song Defu** (宋德福), Minister of Commerce of the People's Republic of China. CEPA focused on three main areas: trade in goods, trade in services, and trade and investment facilitation. It offered zero tariff treatment for a wide range of Hong Kong-origin goods and provided liberalized market access for various service sectors, including logistics, management consulting, and legal services, ahead of China's broader commitments to the World Trade Organization.

## Significance
CEPA represented the first free trade agreement concluded by Hong Kong and the first time Mainland China entered into such an arrangement under 'One Country, Two Systems'. While initially focused on kickstarting post-SARS economic recovery, it set a precedent for subsequent supplements and broader economic integration. Scholars and commentators note that while CEPA provided timely psychological and structural relief to the struggling local economy, its long-term impact on restructuring Hong Kong's service-dominated economy and its interplay with domestic political dynamics remain subjects of ongoing analysis.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and HKSAR Government**: Viewed CEPA as a tangible demonstration of the Central Government's steadfast support for Hong Kong's prosperity and stability under the 'One Country, Two Systems' framework, designed to leverage complementary strengths for mutual economic benefit.
- **Local Business and Critics**: While local businesses generally welcomed the preferential market access, some economists and commentators debated whether the benefits would primarily flow to large conglomerates rather than small and medium enterprises, while others expressed concern over increasing economic dependence on the mainland.
