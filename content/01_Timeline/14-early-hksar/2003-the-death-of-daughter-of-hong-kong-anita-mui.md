## Background

By the early years of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, **Anita Mui** (**梅豔芳**), widely celebrated as the "Daughter of Hong Kong," had established herself as a towering figure in both the Cantopop music industry and the Hong Kong film sector. Born in **Hong Kong** (**香港**), her career spanned decades, defining the golden age of local popular culture through her versatile vocal performances, groundbreaking fashion, and memorable acting roles. In September 2003, **Mui** publicly announced that she had been diagnosed with cervical cancer, choosing to face her illness with resilience while continuing her artistic commitments, notably performing a series of farewell concert in November 2003 at the **Hong Kong Coliseum** (**香港體育館**).

## What happened

On **December 30, 2003**, at the Hong Kong Sanatorium & Hospital (**香港養和醫院**), **Anita Mui** passed away at the age of 40 due to complications from cervical cancer, specifically respiratory failure. Her death occurred in a year that had already been exceptionally difficult for the territory, following the outbreak of Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome (SARS) and the suicide of fellow cultural icon **Leslie Cheung** (**張國榮**) earlier that spring. News of her passing led to an immediate and overwhelming outpouring of grief from the public, fans, and figures across the entertainment industry both locally and internationally.

## Significance

The death of **Anita Mui** became a defining cultural moment for the early HKSAR period. Coming at the end of 2003, it intensified the sense of collective sorrow experienced by a populace still grappling with public health crises and economic downturns. Her funeral, held in January 2004, drew tens of thousands of mourners to the streets of **Hong Kong**, uniting a grieving community. Later cultural scholarship often points to her passing, alongside that of **Leslie Cheung**, as symbolizing the end of an era for the golden age of Hong Kong's entertainment industry, while her final concerts and courageous final months cemented her enduring status as a symbol of local resilience and identity.

## Perspectives

- **Local Hong Kong community and fans**: For local residents, **Anita Mui** was viewed as the embodiment of the "Hong Kong spirit"—resilient, fiercely independent, and hard-working. Her death was deeply felt as a personal loss and a stark marker of the fading of the city's 1980s and 1990s cultural peak.
- **Mainland Chinese and regional observers**: Observers from mainland China and Chinese communities across Southeast Asia widely recognized **Mui** as a cultural ambassador who bridged different regions through her art, noting her active participation in charity work, including relief efforts for mainland disasters and the anti-SARS campaigns earlier in 2003.
