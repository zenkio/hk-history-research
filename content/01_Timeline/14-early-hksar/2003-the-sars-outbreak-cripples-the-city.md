## Background
In late **November 2002**, an outbreak of atypical pneumonia emerged in Guangdong Province, China, before spreading internationally. On **March 11, 2003**, the World Health Organization and local health authorities began tracking an escalating chain of transmission that traced back to a mainland doctor who had stayed at the Metropole Hotel in Kowloon, infecting several other guests who subsequently carried the virus worldwide.

## What happened
The virus entered local hospitals with devastating efficiency, most notably at the Prince of Wales Hospital (威爾斯親王醫院) in Sha Tin (沙田) and later sparking a massive community outbreak at the Amoy Gardens (淘大花園) residential estate in Ngau Tau Kok (牛頭角). Medical personnel, lacking initial knowledge of the pathogen or adequate personal protective equipment, bore the heaviest burden of infection. Schools were closed, public events canceled, and streets emptied as citizens wore surgical masks en masse. By the time the outbreak subsided later that summer, Hong Kong recorded 1,755 infections and 299 deaths, including several frontline healthcare workers.

## Significance
The epidemic severely crippled Hong Kong's economy, particularly tourism, retail, and hospitality, prompting the central government in Beijing to introduce the Individual Visit Scheme (個人遊) later that year to boost mainland tourism. The crisis also exposed significant communication failures and bureaucratic rigidity within the local government's Department of Health (衞生署) and the Hospital Authority (醫院管理局). It triggered sweeping reforms in public health infrastructure, disease surveillance, and emergency response mechanisms, while cementing the surgical mask as a lasting symbol of civic solidarity and health consciousness in local culture.

## Perspectives

- **Local Hong Kong Community**: Residents experienced profound trauma, widespread panic, and a strong sense of community solidarity. Grassroots mutual aid and heroic sacrifices by frontline medical staff became defining memories of resilience against institutional failures.
- **Beijing and Mainland Authorities**: From the perspective of the central government, the crisis highlighted the critical need for transparent cross-border health information sharing and led to closer economic integration measures, such as the Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement, to revitalize Hong Kong's economy.
