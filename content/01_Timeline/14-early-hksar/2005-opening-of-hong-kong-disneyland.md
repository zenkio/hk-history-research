## Background

Following the 1997 handover and the economic pressures of the Asian Financial Crisis, the Hong Kong Government sought new avenues to revitalize the local economy and maintain the territory's status as a premier regional hub. In **November 1999**, the administration under Chief Executive **Tung Chee-hwa** (董建華) announced an agreement with **The Walt Disney Company** to build the region's first Disney resort. Located on reclaimed land in Penny's Bay on Lantau Island (大嶼山), the project required substantial public investment through a joint venture named Hong Kong International Theme Parks Limited, in which the government held a majority stake.

## What happened

On **September 12, 2005**, **Hong Kong Disneyland** (香港迪士尼樂園) officially opened its gates. The grand opening ceremony was attended by key dignitaries, including Chief Executive **Donald Tsang** (曾蔭權) and Chinese Vice President **Zheng Qinghong** (曾慶紅). The initial phase featured a single theme park with four traditional lands—Main Street, U.S.A., Fantasia, Adventureland, and Tomorrowland—along with two resort hotels. The opening marked a major milestone for Hong Kong's tourism infrastructure, though it launched amid high public expectations and close scrutiny regarding its visitor capacity and financial projections.

## Significance

The opening of the park represented a key strategic effort by the government to transition Hong Kong into a premier family-oriented tourist destination, particularly targeting mainland Chinese visitors following the introduction of the Individual Visit Scheme in **2003**. However, the park's early years also sparked ongoing public debates regarding the use of public funds for foreign corporate partnerships, projected versus actual attendance figures, and labor practices within the resort. Later scholarship and economic analyses often view the project as a mixed venture that stimulated tourism infrastructure while exposing the complexities of government-led mega-projects in post-colonial Hong Kong.

## Perspectives

- **HKSAR Government and Business Sector**: Viewed the project as a vital long-term investment that would diversify Hong Kong's economy, enhance its international brand as a tourism capital, and create thousands of service-sector jobs.
- **Critics and Local Taxpayers**: Expressed skepticism regarding the substantial use of public funds to subsidize an American multinational corporation, pointing out potential financial risks and lower-than-expected initial attendance numbers.
