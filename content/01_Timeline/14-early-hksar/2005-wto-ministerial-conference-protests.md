## Background
In **December 2005**, Hong Kong hosted the Sixth WTO Ministerial Conference, drawing international delegates, journalists, and activists to the territory. The event marked a major diplomatic milestone for the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (香港特別行政區), showcasing its ability to host large-scale global events. However, it also attracted numerous international non-governmental organizations and activist groups opposing free trade policies, agricultural subsidies, and globalization.

## What happened
Beginning on **December 13, 2005**, protests escalated significantly around the Wan Chai (灣仔) area near the Hong Kong Convention and Exhibition Centre (香港會議展覽中心). A contingent of South Korean farmers, known for militant and highly organized activist tactics from previous global summits, led demonstrations that frequently clashed with the Hong Kong Police Force (香港警務處). Protesters attempted to breach police cordons, pushed through barricades, and threw makeshift projectiles. In response, police deployed riot gear, tear gas, and batons to contain the crowds and restore order, leading to numerous injuries and hundreds of arrests.

## Significance
The protests put local security forces and the 'One Country, Two Systems' framework to a severe public test, highlighting the challenges of policing international dissent in a dense urban environment. For local authorities, the events demonstrated a high level of operational readiness and crowd control capability, though the use of force also sparked debate regarding civil liberties and police tactics. Internationally, the clashes drew intense media scrutiny, embedding the 2005 summit into the collective memory of global anti-globalization movements.

## Perspectives

- **Hong Kong Government and Police**: Authorities emphasized the need to maintain public safety and protect critical international infrastructure against escalating violence, defending the use of riot control measures as proportional to the threat posed by aggressive demonstrators.
- **South Korean Activists and Protesters**: Demonstrators argued that their aggressive tactics were a necessary and legitimate response to destructive global trade policies that threatened their livelihoods, and some criticized the local police response as heavy-handed and restrictive of peaceful assembly.
