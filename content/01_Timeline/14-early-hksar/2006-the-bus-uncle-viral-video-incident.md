## Background
In the mid-2000s, the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR) was experiencing a period of economic recovery following the 1997 Asian Financial Crisis and the 2003 SARS outbreak. Daily life for many residents was characterized by intense work pressure, long hours, and the claustrophobic density of urban living. Mobile phone technology with video-recording capabilities was becoming increasingly widespread, laying the technical groundwork for user-generated content.

## What happened
On **April 27, 2006**, aboard a KMB double-decker bus traveling along Route 68X from Jordan (佐敦) to Yuen Long (元朗), a middle-aged man later dubbed the "Bus Uncle" (巴士阿叔) engaged in a lengthy, profanity-laced tirade against a younger passenger who had tapped him on the shoulder to ask him to quiet down. Another passenger, **Chong Lok-yin** (方穎恆), secretly recorded the confrontation using a camera phone. 

The video was subsequently uploaded to local online forums, most notably Hong Kong Golden Forum (高登討論區), and shared on YouTube. The footage quickly amassed millions of views globally, spawning remixes, parodies, and extensive media coverage across the world. The older man, identified as **Roger Chan** (阿叔 / 曾浩樂), and the younger commuter, **Alvin Leung** (梁耀權), briefly became subjects of intense public fascination.

## Significance
The incident is widely viewed by media scholars and sociologists as a watershed moment in Hong Kong's digital culture, marking the transition into the era of user-generated viral media. The widespread obsession with the video highlighted the alienation and high stress levels inherent in Hong Kong's fast-paced urban environment. Furthermore, it demonstrated the power of the internet and mobile technology in transforming ordinary citizens into instant celebrities, while raising early questions about privacy, public shaming, and digital ethics in the HKSAR.
