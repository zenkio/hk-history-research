## Background

Political activism in Hong Kong has long encompassed both moderate lobbying and confrontational street demonstrations. Following the handover in 1997, the Legislative Council (LegCo) became the primary arena where pro-democracy politicians contested executive dominance and the pace of democratic reforms. **Leung Kwok-hung** (梁國雄), widely known by his nickname "Long Hair" (長毛), rose to prominence as a radical activist associated with the League of Social Democrats (社會民主連線). Having first won a seat in **LegCo** (立法會) in 2004, Leung consistently utilized theatrical protests, props, and procedural disruption to draw attention to social inequality and demand universal suffrage.

## What happened

On **October 8, 2008**, during the swearing-in ceremony for the newly elected Legislative Council, Leung Kwok-hung took his oath of office while wearing a shirt bearing the image of Marxist revolutionary Che Guevara. As he recited the required statutory oath, Leung added unauthorized political slogans calling for the vindication of the **1989 Tiananmen Square protests** and the release of political prisoners in China. This performance built upon his earlier 2004 swearing-in protest, gradually establishing a predictable ritual of symbolic defiance during official legislative proceedings. Similar disruption tactics were subsequently adopted by other emerging localist and radical lawmakers in later years.

## Significance

Leung's actions institutionalized a new style of parliamentary politics in Hong Kong characterized by direct action and the subversion of formal legislative etiquette. Commentators note that these tactics transformed LegCo into a visible battleground for ideological confrontation between pro-establishment figures and pan-democrats. While supporters viewed these methods as essential tools for giving voice to marginalized citizens and resisting executive overreach, critics argued that such disruptions degraded the dignity of the legislature and hindered effective governance, ultimately contributing to the polarization that preceded major legislative overhauls in subsequent decades.

## Perspectives

- **Pan-democratic and radical activists**: Advocates viewed Leung's legislative stunts as a necessary and legitimate method to challenge an unrepresentative political system, amplify grassroots grievances, and inject genuine opposition into a chamber constrained by functional constituencies.
- **Pro-establishment and Beijing loyalists**: Critics and pro-establishment politicians condemned the theatrical protests as a disrespectful subversion of the rule of law and legislative decorum, arguing that such behavior paralyzed government business and undermined public trust in parliamentary institutions.
