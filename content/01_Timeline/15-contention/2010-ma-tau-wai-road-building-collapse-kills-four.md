## Background

On **January 29, 2010**, a five-storey residential building located at **No. 45J Ma Tau Wai Road** (馬頭圍道) in **To Kwa Wan** (土瓜灣), Kowloon, experienced a catastrophic structural failure. Built in **1955**, the tenement building—commonly known as a *tong lau* (唐樓)—was typical of mid-20th-century urban development in Hong Kong, where aging infrastructure often housed low-income residents and subdivided flats. Prior to the collapse, residents and local shop owners had reportedly noticed signs of structural distress, including falling concrete and shifting walls.

## What happened

At approximately **1:30 PM**, the structure imploded and collapsed into a pile of rubble within seconds, trapping pedestrians and residents. Emergency services, including the Fire Services Department (消防處), launched an extensive rescue operation involving hundreds of personnel. Search and rescue teams worked through the night using specialized equipment to navigate the unstable debris. Ultimately, the disaster resulted in **four fatalities** and several injuries. Initial investigations by government engineers indicated that unauthorized building alterations on the ground floor, combined with long-term structural degradation and water seepage, severely compromised the building's load-bearing columns.

## Significance

The tragedy sent shockwaves through Hong Kong society, laying bare the severe safety risks associated with the city's vast stock of aging private buildings. It prompted the **Urban Renewal Authority** (市區重建局) and the **Buildings Department** (屋宇署) to accelerate inspections of similar old tenements across districts like **Kowloon City** (九龍城) and **Sham Shui Po** (深水埗). The incident also intensified public debates regarding the efficiency of urban redevelopment, tenant compensation, and the regulation of unauthorized alterations in older urban quarters.
