## Background
In the years following the 1997 handover, the Hong Kong government sought to implement curriculum reforms aimed at fostering national identity among students. By **2011**, these efforts materialized as a proposed Moral and National Education (德育及國民教育科) curriculum. Critics increasingly worried that the curriculum would promote mainland-style patriotism and political indoctrination, sparking widespread debate across the city's schools and communities.

## What happened
On **May 29, 2011**, a small group of secondary school students, led notably by **Joshua Wong** (黃之鋒), established Scholarism (學民思潮). Initially starting as a modest platform utilizing online mobilization, the group rapidly gained momentum by organizing secondary students against the government's educational proposals. The organization argued that the curriculum infringed upon critical thinking and represented a form of premature political socialization. Through petitions, public forums, and eventually large-scale street demonstrations, Scholarism transformed a localized educational grievance into a major flashpoint for broader anxieties regarding autonomy and civic education.

## Significance
The foundation of Scholarism marked a turning point in Hong Kong political history by demonstrating that secondary school students could successfully organize and lead high-profile social movements. It served as a precursor to the larger youth-led mobilizations of the decade, including the 2014 Umbrella Movement. The group's eventual pressure contributed significantly to the government's decision to suspend the implementation of the mandatory national education curriculum in **September 2012**.

## Perspectives

- **Pro-Beijing and Government Perspective**: Pro-Beijing figures and government officials viewed the Moral and National Education curriculum as a necessary and standard measure to foster national awareness, cultural pride, and civic responsibility among the younger generation. From this perspective, opposition groups like Scholarism were seen as politicizing education and being influenced by external political agendas.
- **Localist and Activist Perspective**: Local activists and critics of the curriculum regarded Scholarism as a vital defensive line for protecting local autonomy, press freedom, and critical thinking from perceived mainland political interference. They celebrated the group's emergence as a triumph of grassroots youth participation and civil disobedience against perceived administrative overreach.
