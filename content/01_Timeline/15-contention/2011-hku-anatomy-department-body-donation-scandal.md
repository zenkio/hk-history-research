## Background

In **May 2011**, the management of human remains and body donations used for medical training and research in Hong Kong came under intense public scrutiny. Historically, the University of Hong Kong (香港大學) relied on unclaimed bodies and voluntary body donations to supply its Department of Anatomy (解剖學系) with cadavers for medical students to study human anatomy. The processes governing the receipt, embalming, retention, and ultimate disposal of these biological materials operated largely through internal departmental procedures rather than rigorous, transparent statutory oversight.

## What happened

On **May 15, 2011**, media reports surfaced revealing that human body parts, including limbs and internal tissues originating from the University of Hong Kong, had been improperly disposed of and discovered in a local landfill. The revelation shocked the public, raising profound ethical questions regarding the dignity afforded to deceased donors and the administrative competence of the institution. Following the discovery, university authorities launched internal investigations to trace how anatomical specimens intended for educational use ended up mixed with municipal waste.

## Significance

The scandal highlighted systemic vulnerabilities in how medical institutions handled anatomical waste and donor remains in Hong Kong. It triggered widespread public concern, particularly among families who had chosen to donate their loved ones' bodies to advance medical science. In response to the outcry, the university pledged to overhaul its protocols, improving tracking mechanisms, tightening chain-of-custody procedures, and establishing more respectful disposal methods such as cremation. Furthermore, the incident served as a catalyst for broader discussions across Hong Kong's medical education sector regarding the urgent need for standardized, transparent legislation governing tissue governance and body donation programs.

## Perspectives

- **Local Hong Kong Public and Donors**: The local community expressed deep outrage and betrayal, viewing the improper disposal as a severe breach of trust and a violation of basic human dignity that could discourage future body donations.
