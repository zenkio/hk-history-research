## Background
In the years following the 2008 global financial crisis, Hong Kong experienced significant economic shifts, including a rapid surge in property prices and an influx of Mainland Chinese visitors. The **Individual Visit Scheme (自由行)**, introduced in 2003, had been intended to boost Hong Kong's retail sector, but by 2012, many residents perceived that this increased volume of tourism strained local infrastructure, public services, and the cost of living. Tensions were further exacerbated by issues such as the rise of 'birth tourism'—where expectant Mainland mothers traveled to Hong Kong to give birth, securing right of abode for their children—and the competition for resources like infant formula.

## What happened
In **2012**, the term 'locust' (蝗蟲) began appearing prominently in local media, public discourse, and online forums. This dehumanizing metaphor compared Mainland visitors and migrants to a plague of locusts, suggesting they were consuming limited local resources and overwhelming social services. A highly publicized example occurred in **January 2012**, when a full-page advertisement was published in the *Apple Daily* (蘋果日報) by a group of local activists. The advertisement depicted a locust overlooking the Hong Kong skyline, explicitly calling on the government to restrict the influx of Mainlanders. This event marked a crystallization of a broader shift in Hong Kong identity, moving toward a more defensive stance regarding local boundaries and resources.

## Significance
The rise of this rhetoric signaled a profound transformation in the relationship between Hong Kong and the Mainland. While previously focused on political integration or democratization, public discourse became increasingly fixated on socio-economic protectionism. This period is often cited by scholars as a critical turning point where cultural and social grievances began to intertwine with political identity, laying the groundwork for the more organized socio-political movements that would follow in subsequent years, such as the rise of localist discourse.

## Perspectives

- **Pro-Beijing and Mainland Official Perspective**: Authorities and state media have historically viewed these expressions as xenophobic manifestations that undermine national unity. They argue that such rhetoric disregards the economic benefits the Mainland provides to Hong Kong and serves to artificially create division between compatriots.
- **Hong Kong Localist Perspective**: Proponents of this view argue that the 'locust' rhetoric was a necessary, albeit blunt, defensive reaction to structural inequality and the erosion of Hong Kong's unique social fabric. They maintain it was a symptom of the government's failure to regulate immigration and tourism in a way that prioritized the needs of local residents.
