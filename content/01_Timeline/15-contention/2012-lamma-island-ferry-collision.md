## Background
On **October 1, 2012** (October 1, 2012), Hong Kong celebrated the National Day alongside the Mid-Autumn Festival holiday. Many residents and employees of **Hong Kong Electric** (**HK Electric**, **香港電燈**) boarded the company-owned vessel *Lamma IV* (**南丫四號**) to travel from Lamma Island to Victoria Harbour to watch the annual fireworks display. The waters near **Lamma Island** (**南丫島**) and Yung Shue Wan (**榕樹灣**) were heavily trafficked by leisure boats, ferries, and other watercraft during the festive evening.

## What happened
At approximately 8:23 p.m., shortly after departing from Lamma Island, the *Lamma IV* was struck by the *Sea Smooth* (**海怡號**), a passenger ferry operated by **Hongkong and Kowloon Ferry** (**港九小輪**). The bow of the *Sea Smooth* tore a large hole in the stern of the *Lamma IV*, causing the company vessel to list rapidly and sink within minutes. A total of **39 people** (**39人**), including eight children, lost their lives, and over 90 others sustained injuries. While rescue vessels arrived to pull dozens of survivors from the water, the rapid sinking hindered evacuation efforts.

## Significance
The disaster was one of Hong Kong's deadliest maritime accidents in decades, profoundly shocking the local community. Subsequent investigations and a Commission of Inquiry revealed systemic failures within the **Marine Department** (**海事處**), including decades of lax enforcement regarding safety equipment, life jacket provisions, and crew manning standards on passenger vessels. The findings led to intense public scrutiny of government oversight, calls for bureaucratic accountability, and subsequent overhauls of maritime safety regulations across Hong Kong waters.

## Perspectives

- **Public and Survivors**: Local residents and survivors expressed profound grief and anger, viewing the tragedy not merely as an accident, but as the avoidable consequence of systemic regulatory negligence and complacency within government departments.
- **Government and Official Inquiry**: The official Commission of Inquiry and government authorities emphasized human error by the vessel captains alongside historical regulatory loopholes, prompting pledges to reform maritime inspection protocols and accountability measures.
