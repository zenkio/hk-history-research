## Background

Following the transfer of sovereignty in **1997**, the Hong Kong government sought to foster national identity and patriotism among students. In **2012**, the Education Bureau proposed the Moral and National Education (德育及國民教育科) curriculum as an independent subject. Critics, particularly pan-democrats and local concern groups, feared the curriculum would serve as a form of political brainwashing, pointing to teaching materials that portrayed the Chinese Communist Party favorably while avoiding controversial historical events.

## What happened

Tensions escalated as opposition grew throughout the summer of **2012**. On **September 8, 2012**, student activists, notably from the group Scholarism (學民思潮) led by **Joshua Wong (黃之鋒)**, initiated an indefinite sit-in protest outside the Central Government Offices (政府總部) in Tamar (添馬). The movement quickly gained momentum, drawing tens of thousands of citizens, parents, and students to nightly rallies. Several activists and teachers also staged a hunger strike. Faced with mounting public pressure and the prospect of the protests disrupting the upcoming Legislative Council elections, Chief Executive **Leung Chun-ying (梁振英)** announced on **September 8, 2012**, that the controversial guideline deadlines would be shelved, effectively leaving the implementation to individual schools.

## Significance

The suspension and subsequent indefinite shelving of the curriculum marked a major victory for civil society and student-led activism in Hong Kong. It demonstrated the profound capacity of grassroots mobilization to halt government policy. At the same time, the event foreshadowed rising ideological polarization over national identity and educational content, setting a precedent for youth-driven political resistance that would influence subsequent political movements in the territory.

## Perspectives

- **Pro-establishment and Beijing perspective**: Proponents argued that national education was a necessary and standard practice for any sovereign state to cultivate civic responsibility, patriotism, and understanding of the motherland among the younger generation. They viewed the opposition's fears as exaggerated and politically motivated by anti-communist sentiment.
- **Pro-democracy and localist perspective**: Critics and student activists maintained that the curriculum was an attempt at political indoctrination designed to suppress critical thinking and erase distinct Hong Kong identities in favor of unconditional loyalty to the central government. They celebrated the withdrawal as a triumph of civic defense against mainland political encroachment.
