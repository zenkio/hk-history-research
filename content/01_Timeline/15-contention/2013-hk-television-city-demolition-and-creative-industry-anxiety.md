## Background
For decades, the historic studio complex at Clear Water Bay (清水灣), operated by Television Broadcasts Limited (**TVB** (電視廣播有限公司)), served as the bustling cradle of Hong Kong's formidable television and film industry. Countless iconic dramas, variety shows, and star careers were launched from these lots, shaping Cantonese popular culture across Southeast Asia. By **2003**, however, TVB had relocated its operations to the Tseung Kwan O Industrial Estate (將軍澳工業邨). The former Clear Water Bay site was subsequently sold to property developers, laying the groundwork for eventual redevelopment into luxury housing.

## What happened
On **April 12, 2013**, demolition work on the former Television City accelerated, drawing media attention and public nostalgia. Bulldozers tore down soundstages and production facilities that had once defined the visual landscape of Hong Kong entertainment. The physical erasure of the studio coincided with a broader structural shift in the territory's creative sector, as production houses increasingly looked northward to the mainland Chinese market for funding, approval, and collaborative projects.

## Significance
The disappearance of the physical TVB studio complex became a powerful symbol for local observers of a wider cultural transition. While industry executives and investors viewed the transition pragmatically as a modernization and financial necessity tied to regional integration, many local residents and cultural critics interpreted the demolition as a metaphor for the erosion of distinct local identity. Critics argued that closer economic and administrative ties with Beijing were diluting the unique creative freedom and gritty authenticity that had long characterized Hong Kong cinema and television.

## Perspectives

- **Local Cultural Critics and Residents**: The demolition was viewed with profound nostalgia and sorrow, symbolizing the physical and cultural erasure of Hong Kong's golden era of independent creativity amidst increasing mainland assimilation.
- **Industry Executives and Developers**: The redevelopment of the obsolete studio land into housing was seen as a logical, highly profitable economic step that reflected the natural evolution of urban land use and the modernization of corporate assets.
