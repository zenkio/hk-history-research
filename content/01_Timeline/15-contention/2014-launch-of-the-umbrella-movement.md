## Background

Political contention in Hong Kong intensified surrounding the methods for selecting the Chief Executive. In August 2014, the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (NPCSC) issued a framework for the 2017 Chief Executive election, requiring candidates to be vetted by a nominating committee. Pro-democracy advocates argued this restricted genuine universal suffrage, leading to plans for civil disobedience led by campaigns such as Occupy Central with Love and Peace (讓愛與和平佔領中環) and student groups including Scholarism (學民思潮) and the Hong Kong Federation of Students (香港專上學生聯會).

## What happened

On **September 26 and 27, 2014**, student-led protests at the government headquarters in Admiralty (金鐘) escalated when demonstrators entered Civic Square. Police intervened, leading to numerous arrests. As crowds swelled over the weekend in support of the students, authorities deployed tear gas on **September 28, 2014**, around Admiralty, Central (中環), and later Causeway Bay (銅鑼灣) and Mong Kok (旺角). Rather than dispersing the demonstrators, the use of tear gas galvanized tens of thousands of citizens to join the streets. Protesters utilized umbrellas to shield themselves from pepper spray and tear gas, giving the widespread civil disobedience campaign its international moniker, the Umbrella Movement (雨傘運動).

## Significance

The movement marked one of the most protracted periods of civil unrest in Hong Kong's modern history, paralyzing key commercial and traffic arteries for 79 days until police cleared the final camps in December 2014. While the protests did not alter Beijing's electoral framework, the events deeply polarized society, reshaped the local political landscape, and gave rise to a new generation of political activists and localist groups.

## Perspectives

- **Pro-democracy participants and supporters**: Viewed the movement as a courageous, righteous stand for democratic rights and self-determination against encroaching control from Beijing, utilizing peaceful civil disobedience to demand universal suffrage in accordance with the Basic Law.
- **The Hong Kong Government and Beijing authorities**: Characterized the occupation as illegal assemblies that severely disrupted public order, damaged the rule of law, and harmed the local economy, arguing that the NPCSC framework was lawful and constitutional.
- **Business owners and affected residents**: Expressed frustration over the severe disruptions to daily commutes, logistics, and retail businesses caused by the prolonged road blockades in core commercial districts.
