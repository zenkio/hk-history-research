## Background

Following the handover of Hong Kong to the People's Republic of China in **1997**, the Basic Law (基本法) outlined a long-term goal of electing the Chief Executive and members of the Legislative Council by universal suffrage. Decades of political debate culminated in widespread public demands for genuine democratic reform ahead of the scheduled **2017** Chief Executive election. Activists and pan-democratic politicians campaigned heavily for 'civic nomination' (公民提名), which would allow citizens to nominate candidates directly without vetting by a designated committee.

## What happened

On **August 31, 2014**, the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (全國人民代表大會常務委員會) announced its official decision regarding the method for selecting the Chief Executive. The framework stipulated that while the Chief Executive could theoretically be chosen via universal suffrage, candidates must first be approved by a broadly representative nominating committee akin to the existing Election Committee. Furthermore, the committee would require a majority vote to nominate two to three candidates, effectively barring individuals deemed uncooperative with Beijing from running. This announcement is commonly referred to as the **831 Decision** (八三一決定).

## Significance

The decision shattered expectations among local pro-democracy groups that unhindered public nomination would be permitted. The announcement was widely viewed as a turning point in Hong Kong's political trajectory, deepening social polarization and accelerating plans for direct action. Protesters quickly mobilized, leading directly to the launch of the **Umbrella Movement** (雨傘運動) in late **September 2014**, where major thoroughfares were occupied for 79 days.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and the Hong Kong Government**: Authorities maintained that the framework strictly adhered to the Basic Law and ensured that the Chief Executive position would be held by someone who 'loves the country and loves Hong Kong' (愛國愛港). They argued that a restrictive nominating committee was necessary to preserve national security and maintain administrative stability.
- **Pan-democrats and Local Activists**: Critics and pro-democracy advocates condemned the decision as a betrayal of democratic promises and 'fake universal suffrage' (假普選). They argued that pre-vetting candidates rendered the vote meaningless and violated the principle of genuine political choice.
