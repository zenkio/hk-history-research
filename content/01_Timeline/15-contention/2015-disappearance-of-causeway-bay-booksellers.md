## Background

Causeway Bay Books (銅鑼灣書店), founded in **1994**, became famous for selling political gossip books and unauthorized biographies concerning elite Chinese Communist Party leaders. While these publications were banned in mainland China, they were legal under Hong Kong's publications ordinance. The shop catered to mainland tourists and local readers interested in sensitive political topics.

## What happened

Beginning in **October 2015**, people associated with the bookstore began disappearing under mysterious circumstances. **Lee Bo** (李波), **Gui Minhai** (桂民海), **Lui Por** (呂波), **Cheung Ji-ping** (張志平), and **Lam Wing-kee** (林榮基) vanished one by one across different locations, including Hong Kong and Thailand. 

Most alarmingly, **Lee Bo** disappeared from Hong Kong itself in **December 2015**, despite his travel documents remaining in his Hong Kong home, raising serious questions about whether mainland security agents had operated covertly in the territory in violation of the Basic Law.

Months later, some of the booksellers reappeared on mainland Chinese state television making televised confessions. In **June 2016**, **Lam Wing-kee** returned to Hong Kong and broke his silence, holding a high-profile press conference detailing his months of detention, psychological pressure, and interrogation by mainland authorities regarding the bookstore's customer lists and operations.

## Significance

The incident became a watershed moment for Hong Kong's political landscape. It severely undermined public confidence in the 'One Country, Two Systems' (一國兩制) principle, which guaranteed that mainland law enforcement agencies had no jurisdiction in Hong Kong. The event intensified long-standing fears regarding the gradual erosion of freedom of speech, freedom of the press, and personal safety in the territory.

## Perspectives

- **Hong Kong Civil Society and Pro-Democracy Figures**: Viewed the disappearances as a blatant violation of the Basic Law and an abduction by mainland security agents operating extrajudicially in Hong Kong, signaling the death of 'One Country, Two Systems.'
- **Beijing and Pro-Establishment Officials**: Maintained that mainland law enforcement agencies acted strictly within the law, and that the individuals involved had voluntarily returned to the mainland to assist with various criminal investigations.
