## Background

By **2015**, Hong Kong's public housing program accommodated nearly half of the territory's population through the Hong Kong Housing Authority (香港房屋委員會). The rapid development of new public rental housing estates placed immense pressure on contractors, sub-contractors, and government inspectors. Concerns regarding building material quality and sub-contracting practices had occasionally surfaced, but water safety had generally been taken for granted given the advanced state of the city's municipal infrastructure.

## What happened

On **5 July 2015**, the Democratic Party (民主黨) publicly revealed that water samples taken from the Kai Ching Estate (啟晴邨) in **Kowloon City** (九龍城) contained lead levels exceeding World Health Organization guidelines. Subsequent testing across multiple newly built public housing estates, including Kwai Luen Estate (葵聯邨) in **Kwai Tsing** (葵青), confirmed widespread contamination. The source was traced to lead-containing solder used in copper pipe joints. Residents were forced to rely on temporary water supplies provided by water trucks and bottled water, while affected families—particularly infants and pregnant women—underwent blood tests to check for lead poisoning.

## Significance

The scandal led to intense public outcry and demands for accountability from government officials and housing contractors. A Commission of Inquiry was established to investigate the root causes, revealing a fragmented regulatory framework, lax oversight of licensed plumbers, and inadequate acceptance testing by the Housing Department (房屋署). While the immediate crisis subsided through remedial pipe replacements and filtration measures, the incident severely dented public trust in public housing construction standards and government regulatory competence during a politically sensitive era.

## Perspectives

- **Local Residents and Pan-democrats**: Viewed the crisis as a manifestation of systemic government negligence, collusion with profit-driven contractors, and a total failure of bureaucratic oversight that directly threatened public health.
- **Government and Housing Authority**: Emphasized that the contamination stemmed from industry-wide malpractice by individual sub-contractors rather than systemic policy failure, while highlighting their prompt emergency response to supply safe drinking water and rectify faulty plumbing.
