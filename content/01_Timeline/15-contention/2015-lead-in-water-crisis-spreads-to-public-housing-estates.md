## Background
In **July 2015**, public anxiety regarding water safety in Hong Kong escalated significantly when Democratic Party members revealed that water samples from the recently completed Kai Tak Development area public housing estate, Kai Ching Estate (啟晴邨), contained lead concentrations exceeding World Health Organization guidelines. Prior to this discovery, public trust in Hong Kong's tap water quality, managed by the Water Supplies Department (水務署), had generally remained high.

## What happened
Following the initial disclosures at Kai Ching Estate, testing expanded rapidly to other public housing estates completed in recent years. Results soon confirmed that lead contamination was not an isolated incident but affected multiple newly built estates across the territory, including Kwai Luen Estate (葵聯邨) in Kwai Chung. Residents were forced to rely on alternative water sources supplied by government water trucks and bottled water donations. 

The crisis prompted the establishment of a Commission of Inquiry to investigate the root causes of the contamination. Investigations primarily pointed to soldering materials used in the plumbing systems rather than the municipal water supply itself. Subcontractors and licensed plumbers faced intense scrutiny over the installation of non-compliant components containing lead during the construction boom of these public housing projects.

## Significance
The lead-in-water crisis severely impacted public confidence in housing construction standards and government oversight. Lawmakers and affected residents demanded political accountability from relevant government officials and housing authorities. While the government implemented blood testing for vulnerable groups—such as children and pregnant women—and retrofitted affected estates with filtered water systems, the incident underscored systemic vulnerabilities in the regulation of subcontracting and quality control within Hong Kong's massive public housing construction sector.

## Perspectives

- **Local residents and pan-democratic legislators**: Viewed the crisis as a systemic failure of government oversight and corporate accountability, demanding immediate resignations of responsible officials and thorough independent investigations into public housing construction standards.
- **Government and housing authorities**: Emphasized that the municipal water source remained safe and attributed the contamination primarily to individual plumbing subcontractors failing to adhere to specified soldering materials.
