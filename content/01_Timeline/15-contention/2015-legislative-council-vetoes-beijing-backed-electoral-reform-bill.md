## Background

Following the handover of Hong Kong in **1997**, the Basic Law (基本法) outlined an ultimate aim of selecting the Chief Executive and all members of the Legislative Council (立法會) by universal suffrage. Decades of political debate followed regarding the pace and mechanism of democratic development. In **August 2014**, the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (NPCSC) issued a framework—commonly known as the "831 decision" (八三四決議)--stipulating that while the Chief Executive could be elected by universal suffrage starting in **2015**, candidates would first need to be vetted and nominated by a broadly representative nominating committee. 

Pro-democracy activists and lawmakers rejected this framework as "fake universal suffrage," arguing it did not meet international standards of genuine choice. This friction culminated in the massive **79-day** street occupations known as the Umbrella Movement (雨傘運動) in late **2014**, deeply polarizing society and setting the stage for the crucial vote in the legislature.

## What happened

On **June 18, 2015**, the Legislative Council brought the government's constitutional reform package to the floor. Passing the bill required a two-thirds majority of all **70** lawmakers (**47** votes) to amend the method for selecting the Chief Executive in **2017**.

In a bizarre and dramatic turn of events, moments before the division bell rang, a large faction of pro-establishment legislators walked out of the chamber en masse. Led by **Lau Kong-wah** (劉江華) and other alliance leaders, they reportedly intended to stall the vote momentarily to allow a delayed colleague, **Starry Lee** (李慧琼), to arrive. However, due to poor coordination and miscommunication, dozens of pro-establishment members exited, leaving only **8** pro-establishment lawmakers inside alongside the pan-democrats.

The vote proceeded rapidly. The bill was decisively defeated by **28** votes to **8**, with only a fraction of the legislature participating. The unexpected procedural blunder stunned political observers across the spectrum.

## Significance

The veto of the reform package froze Hong Kong's constitutional development, leaving the existing method of selecting the Chief Executive via an Election Committee largely unchanged. While pro-democracy groups celebrated the rejection of what they termed a restrictive framework, Beijing and the Hong Kong government expressed deep regret, blaming pan-democratic lawmakers for blocking democratic progress. The incident highlighted internal disorganization within the pro-establishment camp and widened the political chasm between local activists, the SAR administration, and the central government in Beijing.

## Perspectives

- **Pro-democracy camp**: Viewed the veto as a historic victory against a restrictive Beijing framework that would have institutionalized screening of candidates, preserving the integrity of future democratic resistance.
- **Beijing and Hong Kong governments**: Expressed disappointment and blamed pan-democratic lawmakers for destroying the opportunity for five million eligible voters to elect the Chief Executive by universal suffrage in 2017.
- **Pro-establishment legislators**: Faced severe internal embarrassment and public criticism after the botched walkout exposed poor coordination and discipline during a critical constitutional moment.
