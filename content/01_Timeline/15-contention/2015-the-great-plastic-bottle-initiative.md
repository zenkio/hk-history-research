## Background
In early **2015**, Hong Kong faced escalating tensions regarding environmental sustainability and waste management policy. While the Hong Kong government focused on large-scale infrastructure projects like incinerators, a growing segment of the population felt these top-down approaches ignored localized, community-led solutions. During this period, known for heightened political contention following the 2014 occupation movements, various civil society groups began shifting their focus toward practical, grassroots-led governance experiments.

## What happened
On **May 1, 2015**, a loose coalition of activists, students, and community organizers launched the Great Plastic Bottle Initiative. Participants established temporary, autonomous collection hubs across several districts, including **Central (中環)** and **Mong Kok (旺角)**, to address the perceived inadequacy of government recycling facilities. By mobilizing hundreds of volunteers to sort and transport plastic waste, the organizers aimed to demonstrate the viability of decentralized waste management. While the initiative was officially framed as an environmental effort, it operated within a political climate where the act of 'self-organizing' was increasingly viewed as a form of civic participation independent of state oversight. The initiative persisted for several months, though its logistical footprint diminished as volunteer momentum fluctuated.

## Significance
Historians identify the initiative as a hallmark of the 'localized civic engagement' era. By managing public resources outside of government-sanctioned channels, the movement challenged the state's monopoly on public service provision. Whether this represented a sustainable model for governance or a symbolic gesture of political frustration remains a subject of ongoing academic debate. It is widely accepted that the movement served as a catalyst for later discussions regarding municipal autonomy and the role of the citizen in environmental policymaking. The extent to which these grassroots efforts directly influenced subsequent government environmental policies remains uncertain, with scholars noting that the official response was largely characterized by administrative caution.
