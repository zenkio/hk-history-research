## Background

During the Lunar New Year holidays, particularly around **February 8, 2016** (the first day of the Lunar New Year), traditional street hawkers frequently set up stalls in densely populated districts like Mong Kok to sell local street foods such as fishballs. In previous years, government enforcement was often relatively lenient, but heightened regulatory efforts by the Food and Environmental Hygiene Department (FEHD) aimed at clearing unlicensed vendors drew pushback.

## What happened

On the night of **February 8, 2016**, localist groups and hawker supporters gathered in Mong Kok (**旺角**) to intervene against FEHD enforcement actions. The situation escalated rapidly as police were deployed to maintain order. Protesters, some associated with localist organizations advocating for Hong Kong autonomy or independence, threw bricks torn from pavements, glass bottles, and wooden pallets at police officers. Trash bins were also set on fire across several streets. In response, police deployed pepper spray, batons, and—notably—fired two warning shots into the air during the height of the chaos. Clashes continued into the early hours of **February 9, 2016**, resulting in numerous injuries among police officers, journalists, and participants, alongside widespread property damage.

## Significance

The events, frequently labeled by participants and media as the "Fishball Revolution" (魚蛋革命), represented a significant turning point in Hong Kong's political landscape. It signaled to observers a shift away from the largely peaceful civil disobedience seen during the 2014 Umbrella Movement toward more militant, confrontational street tactics among certain localist factions. The government and police heavily condemned the violence, leading to the arrest and subsequent prosecution of numerous individuals, including prominent localist figures, under charges of rioting and unlawful assembly.

## Perspectives

- **Government and Police Perspective**: Authorities and official bodies viewed the incident as a violent, premeditated riot that severely threatened public safety and the rule of law. They maintained that police officers exercised necessary restraint while responding to unprecedented levels of unlawful violence, property destruction, and arson.
- **Localist Perspective**: Some localist participants and sympathizers framed their actions as a desperate defense of local culture, grassroots livelihoods, and traditional ways of life against heavy-handed government regulation. Certain factions also viewed the clashes as a justifiable pushback against perceived police overreach and diminishing political freedoms.
