## Background
For years, the MTR Corporation (香港鐵路有限公司), which operates the primary rail network in Hong Kong, faced criticism regarding its Fare Adjustment Mechanism (票價調整機制), commonly known as the "fare-to-fare" formula. Critics argued that the formula, which linked fare adjustments to the Consumer Price Index and transport worker wage indices, frequently resulted in fare increases even when the corporation reported significant annual profits. This led to widespread public dissatisfaction during a period of escalating housing costs and stagnant wages in Hong Kong.

## What happened
On **2017-03-21**, the MTR Corporation announced a formal revision to the Fare Adjustment Mechanism. The move followed a series of public consultations and negotiations with the government. The revised framework included a 0.6 percentage point reduction applied to the annual fare adjustment rate. Additionally, the corporation committed to introducing new fare concession schemes and increasing the "profit sharing" mechanism, which required the company to set aside funds for passenger rebates whenever annual post-tax profits reached specific thresholds.

## Significance
The revision of the mechanism was widely viewed as a direct response to the political climate of the time, characterized by increasing public skepticism toward large infrastructure monopolies and the perceived disconnect between corporate prosperity and the quality of life for ordinary residents. While the MTR Corporation maintained that the formula provided an objective and transparent basis for financial stability, grassroots activists and various political parties viewed the 2017 changes as an insufficient, albeit necessary, concession. The event underscores the broader tension in Hong Kong between the operation of essential public services on a commercial basis and the public expectation of affordability in a high-cost urban environment.

## Perspectives

- **MTR Corporation and Government Supporters**: The MTR Corporation argued that the Fare Adjustment Mechanism was essential to ensure the long-term financial sustainability of the rail network without relying on government subsidies. They contended that the adjustment formula ensured predictable, incremental changes that allowed for continuous investment in system safety and infrastructure upgrades.
- **Public Advocacy and Political Opposition**: Grassroots organizations and legislative councilors frequently criticized the formula as being 'autonomously inflationary,' arguing that it lacked sufficient social consideration. They advocated for a mechanism that would directly tie fare adjustments to the corporation's actual profitability and the public's ability to pay, rather than external economic indices.
