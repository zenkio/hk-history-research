## Background
In the years leading up to **2018**, Hong Kong's retail payment landscape relied heavily on cash, Octopus cards, and traditional bank transfers that often required clearing delays for cross-bank transactions. To maintain the territory's status as a leading international financial center and foster financial technology innovation, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (**HKMA**, 香港金融管理局) initiated plans for a unified retail payment infrastructure. The initiative aimed to connect banks and stored value facility operators in real-time.

## What happened
On **September 30, 2018**, the **Faster Payment System** (**FPS**, 轉數快) officially commenced operations. Developed by **EPS Company (Hong Kong) Limited** (香港銀聯通寶有限公司) under the coordination of the HKMA, the system allowed consumers and merchants to conduct instant fund transfers 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Users could transfer money simply by using mobile phone numbers or email addresses across different participating banks and e-wallets, completely free of charge for most retail transactions.

## Significance
The launch of FPS marked a watershed moment in Hong Kong's financial technology development. By lowering transaction friction and bridging the gap between traditional banking and mobile payment applications, it accelerated the city's transition toward a cash-lite digital economy. Subsequent years saw widespread adoption across public transport, retail, and peer-to-peer transfers, fundamentally altering daily financial habits in the territory.

## Perspectives

- **Financial Regulators and Tech Industry**: Authorities and technology proponents viewed the FPS as a vital upgrade that successfully modernized Hong Kong's financial infrastructure, enhancing competitiveness against regional digital hubs like Shenzhen and Singapore.
- **Privacy Advocates and Consumers**: While consumers welcomed the convenience and speed of instant payments, privacy advocates and cybersecurity analysts raised concerns regarding data centralization, potential vulnerabilities, and the ease of unauthorized transactions linked to mobile identifiers.
