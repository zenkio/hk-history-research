## Background

Following the 2014 Umbrella Movement, localist and self-determination sentiments grew among younger residents in Hong Kong. In **July 2015**, **Andy Chan Ho-tin** (**陳浩天**) and other activists founded the **Hong Kong National Party** (**香港民族黨**), explicitly advocating for Hong Kong independence and the establishment of a "republic of Hong Kong." The platform called for rewriting the Basic Law and breaking away from the People's Republic of China. 

## What happened

On **September 24, 2018**, the Security Bureau gazetted an order made by Secretary for Security **John Lee Ka-chiu** (**李家超**) to formally ban the **Hong Kong National Party** under the **Societies Ordinance** (**社團條例**). The government invoked powers allowing the prohibition of societies deemed a threat to national security, public safety, or public order. This marked the first instance of a political party being officially outlawed in the territory since the 1997 handover. Prior to the ban, authorities had already barred **Andy Chan** from running in the **2016 Legislative Council election** (**2016年香港立法會選舉**), citing his political stance.

## Significance

The ban represented a watershed moment for political expression and freedom of association in Hong Kong. It signaled a hardening stance by Beijing and local authorities against advocacy for self-determination and independence, foreshadowing broader crackdowns on political dissent. While supporters of the decision argued that advocating secession violated the Basic Law and threatened national sovereignty, critics and civil rights organizations viewed the move as a dangerous precedent that severely eroded the freedoms guaranteed under the "One Country, Two Systems" (**一國兩制**) framework.

## Perspectives

- **Hong Kong and Beijing Governments**: Authorities maintained that advocating for independence directly violates the Basic Law, threatens national security and territorial integrity, and crosses a red line that justifies legal suppression under existing societies legislation.
- **Pro-democracy and Civil Liberties Advocates**: Critics argued that the ban weaponized administrative laws to suppress peaceful political speech, warning that it signaled the death of freedom of association and foreshadowed further erosion of civil liberties in the territory.
