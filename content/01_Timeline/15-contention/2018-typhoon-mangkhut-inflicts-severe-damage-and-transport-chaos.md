## Background
On **September 16, 2018**, Super Typhoon Mangkhut (山竹颱風), one of the most powerful storms to strike the region in decades, approached the coast of Guangdong and passed within 100 kilometers of Hong Kong. The Hong Kong Observatory (香港天文台) issued the highest tropical cyclone warning signal, Hurricane Signal No. 10 (十號颶風信號), as severe winds and storm surges threatened the low-lying areas of the territory.

## What happened
The storm brought ferocious winds and massive waves, resulting in widespread structural damage, uprooted trees, and coastal flooding in vulnerable districts such as Heng Fa Chuen (杏花邨) and Lei Yue Mun (鯉魚門). Skyscrapers swayed, windows were shattered, and scaffolding collapsed across various neighborhoods. Following the passage of the storm, the city faced unprecedented transport chaos on **September 17, 2018**. Blocked roads, suspended railway services, and massive crowds stranded at MTR (港鐵) stations paralyzed the morning commute as millions attempted to return to work amidst debris and fallen trees.

## Significance
The aftermath of Typhoon Mangkhut triggered intense public debate regarding government disaster management and labor protection. While employers expected normal business operations to resume quickly, workers faced perilous journeys and severe transit disruptions. This revived discussions on the lack of statutory guidelines for work-from-home policies or mandatory work suspensions during extreme weather events, highlighting the tension between economic continuity and public safety in the densely populated financial hub.

## Perspectives

- **Local Workers and Labor Advocates**: Many residents and labor unions argued that the government and employers prioritized economic output over safety by failing to declare an official work stoppage or public holiday, forcing workers to navigate hazardous, blocked transit networks.
- **Government and Business Communities**: Officials and business representatives maintained that existing advisory guidelines allowed for sufficient flexibility and that a rigid legal mandate for halting work would severely disrupt the economy and essential services.
