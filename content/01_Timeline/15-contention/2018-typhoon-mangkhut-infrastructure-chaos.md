## Background
On **September 16, 2018**, Super Typhoon Mangkhut (颱風山竹) approached the Pearl River Delta, prompting the Hong Kong Observatory (香港天文台) to issue the highest Hurricane Signal No. 10. As one of the strongest storms to hit the territory in decades, Mangkhut generated massive storm surges and destructive winds that threatened coastal and low-lying areas.

## What happened
The storm caused widespread devastation across the territory, uprooting tens of thousands of trees, shattering windows in high-rise commercial buildings, and flooding coastal neighborhoods like Heng Fa Chuen (杏花邨) and Lei Yue Mun (鯉魚門). Power supplies were disrupted for numerous households, and maritime and aviation services were severely curtailed.

The most acute friction arose on the Monday following the storm, **September 17, 2018**. With transport networks heavily blocked by fallen trees and debris, the government did not declare an official work suspension. This resulted in extreme congestion, dangerously overcrowded MTR (港鐵) platforms, and immense difficulties for millions of commuters trying to navigate blocked roads and partially suspended rail services just to reach their workplaces.

## Significance
The aftermath of Typhoon Mangkhut exposed vulnerabilities in Hong Kong's disaster response and urban planning, particularly regarding infrastructure resilience and public transport recovery. It catalyzed fierce socio-economic debates concerning labor rights during extreme weather events, contrasting the administration's reliance on voluntary employer guidance with demands for statutory protection and mandatory work-stop orders.

## Perspectives

- **Local Hong Kong Workforce and Commuters**: Many residents felt abandoned by the government's refusal to declare a general day off, forcing millions to brave hazardous conditions and gridlocked transit systems simply to satisfy employer attendance policies. This fueled widespread anger over the prioritization of economic continuity above public safety.
- **Government and Business Management**: Authorities and business representatives defended the decision to rely on existing administrative guidelines and employer discretion, arguing that a blanket work stoppage would unnecessarily stall the broader economy and complicate the phased restoration of essential services.
