## Background

The emergence of the Yellow Economic Circle (黃色經濟圈) in **late 2019** was rooted in the broader political polarization surrounding the anti-extradition bill movement. Consumers seeking to express political solidarity began categorizing businesses based on their perceived stance on the protests. Establishments supportive of or sympathetic to the demonstrators were labeled 'yellow' (黃), while those aligned with the Beijing and Hong Kong governments or police force were termed 'blue' (藍).

## What happened

Supporters utilized dedicated mobile applications, social media platforms, and online directories to identify and patronize 'yellow' shops, ranging from cafes and bookstores to retail outlets. Concurrently, some participating businesses offered discounts to students and protesters or displayed symbolic artwork associated with the demonstrations. The movement encouraged consumers to redirect their spending away from major pro-establishment conglomerates and mainland-linked chains toward independent local enterprises.

## Significance

The phenomenon represented a novel form of consumer activism and economic protest in Hong Kong. While proponents viewed it as a sustainable way to build community resilience and support local businesses, critics and government officials argued that the practice fractured society, constituted unfair discrimination, and potentially violated competition laws or national security regulations as the legal landscape shifted in subsequent years.

## Perspectives

- **Pro-democracy supporters and participants**: Viewed the economic circle as a legitimate and peaceful method of civil resistance and community building, empowering citizens to vote with their wallets and support local enterprise.
- **Pro-establishment figures and government officials**: Characterized the movement as a divisive practice that politicized commerce, excluded ideological opponents, and fostered social fragmentation and economic disruption.
