## Background
In the latter half of **2019**, widespread protests against a proposed extradition bill escalated into broader demands for democratic reforms and police accountability. University campuses across Hong Kong increasingly became fortified strongholds for protesters adopting more militant tactics, including the use of bricks, petrol bombs, and makeshift barricades to repel police advances.

## What happened
On **November 17, 2019**, the confrontation peaked when the Hong Kong Police Force moved to encircle the Hong Kong Polytechnic University (香港理工大學) in Hung Hom (紅磡). Clashes involved heavy volleys of tear gas and petrol bombs, alongside the police deployment of armored vehicles and water cannons. Hundreds of protesters, medics, and journalists became trapped inside the campus as police blockaded all exits, demanding the surrender of those inside. Over the subsequent days, numerous individuals attempted to escape through various means, including rappelling from pedestrian bridges and navigating the underground drainage system, while others remained holed up until the siege ended.

## Significance
The siege marked a turning point in the **2019** protests, resulting in a significant de-escalation of large-scale street mobilization as hundreds of participants were arrested on charges of rioting. It underscored the profound polarization of Hong Kong society, leaving deep scars on the city's educational institutions and international standing.

## Perspectives

- **Hong Kong Government and Police**: Authorities viewed the occupation of university campuses as violent criminal activity and domestic terrorism that threatened public safety, necessitating a firm containment strategy to restore law and order.
- **Protesters and Sympathizers**: Occupants and supporters regarded the defense of the campus as a necessary act of resistance against perceived police brutality and authoritarian overreach, framing their confinement as a humanitarian crisis.
