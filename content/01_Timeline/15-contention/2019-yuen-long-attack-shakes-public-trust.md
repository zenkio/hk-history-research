## Background

In the summer of **2019**, Hong Kong was engulfed in widespread anti-extradition bill protests. Tensions between demonstrators, police, and residents of various districts escalated significantly. **Yuen Long (元朗)**, a town in the New Territories (新界) with a distinct local identity and history of rural power structures, became a focal point of anxiety as protests increasingly spread outside the urban core.

## What happened

On the night of **July 21, 2019**, a large group of men wearing white shirts and armed with rods, canes, and metal pipes gathered at the **Yuen Long MTR station (元朗站)**. Following a large-scale protest march on Hong Kong Island, returning commuters, protesters in black shirts, journalists, and bystanders were indiscriminately attacked inside the concourse and train cars. 

Numerous emergency calls to the police met with delayed responses, and officers arrived at the scene only after the armed assailants had largely dispersed. Media broadcasts captured police officers walking away from attackers or failing to intervene promptly, leading to widespread allegations of police inaction and collusion with triad elements.

## Significance

The **July 21 incident** marked a watershed moment in the **2019** protests. While previous confrontations had primarily centered on clashes between protesters and riot police, the Yuen Long attack introduced vigilante violence involving non-uniformed actors into the public sphere. The perceived failure of law enforcement to protect citizens led to an unprecedented collapse of public confidence in the **Hong Kong Police Force (香港警務處)** and galvanized demands for an independent commission of inquiry.

## Perspectives

- **Protesters and opposition figures**: Viewed the event as a coordinated terrorist attack orchestrated by triads in collusion with the police and pro-establishment politicians to terrorize the anti-extradition movement.
- **Pro-establishment and rural New Territories communities**: Emphasized the context of months of escalating social unrest, arguing that local residents were defending their homes, businesses, and community from outside protesters bringing chaos to the district.
