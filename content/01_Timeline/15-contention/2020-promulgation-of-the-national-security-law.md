## Background

Following the widespread and sometimes violent social unrest of 2019, which was triggered by the controversial extradition bill, central authorities in Beijing and local pro-establishment figures argued that Hong Kong faced severe national security threats. Article 23 of the Basic Law stipulated that Hong Kong should enact its own national security laws, but local lawmakers had failed to pass such legislation since the 1997 handover due to fierce public opposition. Consequently, the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress decided to intervene directly.

## What happened

On **June 30, 2020**, the National People's Congress Standing Committee unanimously passed the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (香港特別行政區維護國家安全法). Bypassing Hong Kong's local legislature, the law was added to Annex III of the Basic Law and came into effect immediately at 11:00 PM local time via promulgation.

The legislation criminalized four main categories of offenses: secession, subversion, terrorist activities, and collusion with a foreign country or external elements. It also established new security agencies, including the Office for Safeguarding National Security of the Central People's Government in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (中央人民政府駐香港特別行政區維護國家安全公署), and granted the Chief Executive the power to designate judges for national security cases.

## Significance

The promulgation of the National Security Law marked a watershed moment in Hong Kong's history. Proponents argued it restored stability and order after months of disruption, ensuring the long-term prosperity of the city under the "One Country, Two Systems" (一國兩制) framework. Conversely, critics, foreign governments, and international human rights organizations contended that the broad definitions within the law severely curtailed freedom of speech, assembly, and the press, effectively dismantling the city's high degree of autonomy.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and Hong Kong Government**: Authorities maintained that the legislation was vital for closing national security loopholes, protecting public safety, and ensuring the successful implementation of 'One Country, Two Systems' after the 2019 protests.
- **Critics and Overseas Observers**: Many international governments and civil society groups argued that the law bypassed local legislative processes and severely eroded Hong Kong's judicial independence and civil liberties.
