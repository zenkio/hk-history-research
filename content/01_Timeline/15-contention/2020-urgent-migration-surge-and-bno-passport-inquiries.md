## Background

The status of British National (Overseas), commonly known as **BNO**, was established prior to the **1997** handover of Hong Kong to China. For decades, the status granted travel documentation without conferring the right of abode in the United Kingdom. As political tensions escalated during the **2019** anti-extradition bill protests, debates surrounding residency rights intensified. On **June 30, 2020**, the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress enacted the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (**National Security Law** (中華人民共和國香港特別行政區維護國家安全法)), altering the legal landscape of the city.

## What happened

Immediately following the implementation of the legislation on **July 1, 2020**, public anxiety catalyzed an unprecedented rush for alternative travel documents and foreign residency options. Citizens flooded courier services, post offices, and consulate websites to renew or apply for **BNO** passports. Concurrently, inquiries regarding migration pathways to countries such as the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, and Taiwan multiplied rapidly. The British government subsequently announced expanded pathways to citizenship for eligible **BNO** status holders and their dependents, prompting further departures and preparations for relocation among professionals, families, and young activists.

## Significance

This rapid wave of inquiries and subsequent departures contributed to a notable brain drain, affecting various sectors including education, healthcare, legal services, and information technology. It represented a psychological turning point for many residents, contrasting sharply with earlier waves of emigration by redefining the relationship between local populations and the changing political order. The demographic shifts prompted renewed discussions about the long-term economic and social composition of Hong Kong.

## Perspectives

- **British Government and Supporters**: Viewed the expansion of BNO rights as a moral and historical obligation to the people of Hong Kong in response to perceived erosions of autonomy under the Sino-British Joint Declaration.
- **Beijing and Hong Kong Governments**: Dismissed the migration wave and BNO pathway adjustments as external interference in China's internal affairs, asserting that the National Security Law restored stability and that those leaving would ultimately return or be replaced.
