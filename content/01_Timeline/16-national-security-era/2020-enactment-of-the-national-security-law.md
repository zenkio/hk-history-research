## Background
Following the widespread and often violent anti-extradition bill protests in **2019**, the central government in Beijing asserted that existing mechanisms in Hong Kong were insufficient to protect national security. Article 23 of the Basic Law had required Hong Kong to enact its own national security laws since **1997**, but local legislative efforts had repeatedly stalled amid intense public controversy, notably in **2003**.

## What happened
On **June 30, 2020**, the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (NPCSC) unanimously passed the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (中華人民共和國香港特別行政區維護國家安全法). Bypassing Hong Kong's Legislative Council (立法會), the law was added to Annex III of the Basic Law and promulgated locally late in the evening on the eve of the anniversary of the handover, taking effect immediately.

The legislation criminalized four main categories of offenses: secession, subversion, terrorist activities, and collusion with a foreign or external element. It also introduced specialized mainland-supervised security agencies and provisions allowing certain trials to be conducted behind closed doors or transferred across the border under specific circumstances.

## Significance
The enactment of the law fundamentally altered Hong Kong's legal framework and civil liberties, leading to the disbandment of numerous local civil society organizations, trade unions, and opposition political groups. It marked the beginning of a new era in the governance of the territory, redefining the application of the 'One Country, Two Systems' (一國兩制) framework.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and the Hong Kong Government**: Proponents argue that the law was necessary to restore public order, protect national sovereignty, and end the chaos of 2019, maintaining Hong Kong's long-term prosperity and stability.
- **Critics and International Observers**: Opponents and various foreign governments contend that the sweeping legislation severely eroded Hong Kong's high degree of autonomy, freedom of speech, and judicial independence guaranteed under international agreements.
