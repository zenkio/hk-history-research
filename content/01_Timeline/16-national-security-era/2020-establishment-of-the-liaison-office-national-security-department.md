## Background
Following the social unrest that characterized much of 2019, the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (NPC) enacted the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (The National Security Law) on **June 30, 2020**. This legislation was promulgated in Hong Kong under Annex III of the Basic Law. A core provision of this law was the establishment of a dedicated central-level office to oversee and coordinate national security work within the SAR, operating independently of the existing Hong Kong government structure.

## What happened
On **July 8, 2020**, the Office for Safeguarding National Security of the Central People's Government in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (中央人民政府駐香港特別行政區維護國家安全公署) was formally opened. The office was temporarily headquartered at the Metropark Hotel in Causeway Bay. During the inaugural ceremony, high-ranking officials including the Chief Executive of Hong Kong and representatives from the Central Government Liaison Office attended. The mandate of this office includes analyzing and evaluating the national security situation in Hong Kong, providing guidance for national security work, and exercising jurisdiction over specific cases defined under the National Security Law.

## Significance
The establishment of the office represents a fundamental change in the constitutional arrangement of the "One Country, Two Systems" model. Before this, the responsibility for maintaining order and security rested primarily with the Hong Kong Police Force and the local judiciary. The creation of this office formalizes the direct involvement of central state organs in local governance regarding national security matters. It grants the office authority to collect intelligence, process information, and initiate criminal proceedings for select national security crimes. Proponents argue this is a necessary structural correction to address security loopholes that existed since 1997. Conversely, critics suggest this development signals a significant reduction in the high degree of autonomy originally guaranteed to Hong Kong under the Sino-British Joint Declaration, as it introduces central state entities that are not subject to the jurisdiction of the Hong Kong courts in the same manner as local government departments.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and Pro-establishment perspective**: Authorities in Beijing and their supporters argue the office is essential to ending chaos and ensuring the long-term stability of the city. They maintain that national security falls under the purview of the sovereign state and that local institutions had proven unable to effectively prevent foreign interference and separatist activities.
- **Pro-democracy and international critics**: Pro-democracy activists and several international observers argue the office undermines the rule of law and the independent judiciary in Hong Kong. They view the direct presence of central security organs as an erosion of the 'One Country, Two Systems' framework and a threat to civil liberties protected by the Basic Law.
