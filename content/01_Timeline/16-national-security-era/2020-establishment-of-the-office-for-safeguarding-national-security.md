## Background
Following a period of sustained civil unrest in 2019, the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (NPCSC) passed the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR) on June 30, 2020. This legislation was promulgated in Hong Kong under Annex III of the Basic Law. A key component of this legal framework was the mandate to establish a permanent presence of central government authorities within the territory to monitor, supervise, and coordinate national security affairs, representing a departure from the previous administrative arrangement where local authorities held primary responsibility for internal security.

## What happened
On **July 8, 2020**, the Office for Safeguarding National Security of the Central People’s Government in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (中央人民政府駐香港特別行政區維護國家安全公署) was officially inaugurated. The office was temporarily headquartered at the Metropark Hotel in Causeway Bay (銅鑼灣) before transitioning to permanent facilities. The central government appointed **Zheng Yanxiong** (鄭雁雄) as the first Director of the Office. According to the law, the agency is tasked with analyzing and evaluating the situation regarding national security, providing opinions on major strategies, supervising the HKSAR government, and handling cases that fall under specific jurisdictional criteria outlined in the legislation.

## Significance
The establishment of the office fundamentally altered the legal and security architecture of Hong Kong. Historically, under the 'One Country, Two Systems' framework, Hong Kong maintained a separate legal system and internal security apparatus. The creation of this office provided a direct institutional link between Beijing and Hong Kong regarding national security enforcement. The office operates outside the jurisdiction of local Hong Kong courts for many of its internal functions, and its staff are exempt from local search and seizure in certain circumstances. This development is viewed by legal scholars as a transition toward a more integrated security framework between the HKSAR and the mainland, shifting the constitutional balance established at the 1997 handover.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and Pro-establishment supporters**: The establishment of the office is seen as a necessary and essential measure to plug loopholes in national security, restore long-term stability, and ensure the effective implementation of 'One Country, Two Systems.' They argue that it serves as a deterrent against external interference and internal subversion, safeguarding the constitutional order of the HKSAR.
- **International observers and legal critics**: Many international legal scholars and human rights organizations have expressed concern that the office bypasses local institutional checks and balances, potentially undermining the judicial independence and autonomy promised under the Sino-British Joint Declaration. They argue that the office's broad powers and lack of local accountability create ambiguity regarding the rule of law in Hong Kong.
