## Background
Following months of unprecedented social unrest in **2019**, the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress passed the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (**Hong Kong National Security Law**) on June 30, **2020**. Article 48 of the legislation mandated the establishment of a central national security agency in Hong Kong, directly answering to the Central People's Government.

## What happened
On **July 8, 2020** (七月八日), the Office for Safeguarding National Security of the Central People's Government in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (中央人民政府駐香港特別行政區維護國家安全公署) was formally established and inaugurated in a temporary headquarters at the Metropark Hotel Causeway Bay in **Hong Kong** (香港). **Zheng Yanxiong** (鄭雁雄) was appointed as the first Director of the Office. The establishment ceremony was attended by senior mainland and Hong Kong officials, including Chief Executive **Carrie Lam** (林鄭月娥) and Director of the Liaison Office **Liaoning** -- note, **Liaoning** is incorrect, Liaison Office Director **Luo Huining** (駱惠寧) alongside other dignitaries. The office began operating with its own personnel dispatched from mainland national security organs, possessing jurisdiction over specific, grave national security cases under designated circumstances defined by the new law.

## Significance
The creation of the Office represented a structural turning point in Hong Kong's legal and political landscape. It established a direct institutional presence of mainland Chinese state security apparatuses operating within the territory of the HKSAR, a development unprecedented since the 1997 handover. The agency's formation underscored a redefining of the "One Country, Two Systems" framework, asserting direct central oversight in matters of national sovereignty and security.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and HKSAR Government**: Proponents argue the office is a constitutional necessity to fill a long-standing legal loophole and safeguard national sovereignty, public safety, and long-term prosperity following the 2019 turmoil.
- **Critics and Overseas Legal Observers**: Conversely, critics and international legal scholars contend that the office's jurisdictional reach and immunity from local courts undermine Hong Kong's high degree of autonomy and traditional judicial independence guaranteed under the Basic Law.
