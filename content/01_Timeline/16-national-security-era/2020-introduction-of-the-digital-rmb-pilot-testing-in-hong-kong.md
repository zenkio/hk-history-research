## Background

Following the implementation of the **National Security Law** in mid-2020, Hong Kong's financial sector experienced significant structural shifts regarding its relationship with mainland China. The People's Bank of China (中國人民銀行) had been developing its sovereign digital currency, known as the Digital RMB or e-CNY (數字人民幣), through trials in several mainland cities since 2019. Given Hong Kong's status as an international financial center and the primary offshore Renminbi hub, integrating the city into digital currency testing became a logical step for cross-border monetary integration.

## What happened

On **December 4, 2020**, **Eddie Yue** (余偉文), Chief Executive of the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (香港金融管理局), publicly confirmed that the HKMA was collaborating with the Digital Currency Institute of the People's Bank of China to conduct technical tests for cross-border payments using the digital RMB. The initial testing phase focused on internal operations, involving designated bank staff and selected merchants. This initiative was designed to evaluate how a sovereign digital currency could facilitate trade settlement and consumer transactions between Hong Kong and the mainland.

## Significance

This pilot test represented a milestone in deepening financial connectivity between Hong Kong and the Greater Bay Area (粵港澳大灣區). While supporters viewed it as essential for maintaining Hong Kong's competitiveness and efficiency in international trade, observers noted it also underscored the steady alignment of Hong Kong's monetary infrastructure with Beijing's broader economic and technological strategies.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and Hong Kong Financial Authorities**: Proponents argue that integrating the digital RMB into Hong Kong enhances the city's role as the leading offshore RMB business hub, reduces cross-border transaction friction, and supports the internationalization of China's currency.
- **International Financial Analysts and Critics**: Some foreign analysts and local commentators express concerns that deeper integration of mainland financial infrastructure could gradually blur the distinct boundaries of Hong Kong's separate monetary system and raise data privacy considerations.
