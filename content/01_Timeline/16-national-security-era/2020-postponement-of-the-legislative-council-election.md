## Background

In the wake of intense political turmoil following the 2019 anti-extradition bill protests and the enactment of the **Hong Kong National Security Law** (香港國安法) in June **2020**, the political landscape of Hong Kong underwent profound changes. The pro-democracy camp had organized unofficial primary elections in mid-July **2020** with high voter turnout, aiming to secure a majority in the upcoming Legislative Council. Meanwhile, the government and pro-Beijing figures expressed concerns over the legality of the primaries and potential violations of the new security legislation.

## What happened

On **July 31, 2020**, Chief Executive **Carrie Lam** (林鄭月娥) announced that the seventh-term Legislative Council general election, originally scheduled for September 6, **2020**, would be postponed by one year to September 5, **2021**. Invoking the Emergency Regulations Ordinance (緊急情況規例條例), the administration officially attributed the decision to the severe third wave of the COVID-19 pandemic, citing the health risks of mass gatherings and the voting process for elderly residents and overseas voters.

The postponement created a constitutional vacuum regarding the legislature's composition for the intervening year. Consequently, the **Standing Committee of the National People's Congress** (全國人民代表大會常務委員會) subsequently ruled that the existing lawmakers would continue to serve for at least another year, though several pro-democracy legislators later chose to resign.

## Significance

The delay of the election marked a watershed moment in Hong Kong's constitutional history. While the government maintained that public safety was the sole motivation, critics and opposition politicians argued that the move was politically motivated to avert potential electoral losses for the pro-establishment camp. The subsequent electoral overhauls implemented in **2021** further reshaped the legislature, altering the trajectory of Hong Kong's representative governance.

## Perspectives

- **Hong Kong Government and Beijing**: The postponement was a necessary and prudent public health measure to protect citizens from COVID-19 transmission risks during an active pandemic wave, ensuring safety without political intent.
- **Pro-democracy Camp and Critics**: The health rationale served as a pretext to delay an election the pro-establishment camp feared losing, thereby disrupting democratic processes and extending the tenure of lawmakers without a fresh mandate.
