## Background

Established in **1990**, the Hong Kong Confederation of Trade Unions (香港職工會聯盟), commonly known as the **HKCTU**, grew to become the territory's largest independent labor coalition. Representing dozens of affiliated unions across various sectors, the organization was known for advocating labor rights, organizing collective bargaining efforts, and staging the annual **July 1st** marches alongside other civil society groups. 

Following the enactment of the **National Security Law** in **2020** and subsequent electoral system overhauls, the political environment for independent civil society organizations in Hong Kong shifted dramatically. Numerous prominent civil society groups, professional associations, and opposition political parties chose to dissolve rather than face potential legal action.

## What happened

On **October 3, 2021**, delegates from HKCTU member unions voted overwhelmingly to dissolve the organization. Prior to the vote, leaders of the coalition, including general secretary **Lee Cheuk-yan** (李卓人) who was already imprisoned for unauthorized assembly related to previous protests, reported facing intense pressure, threats, and state media criticism. Following the formal dissolution vote, the leadership cited safety concerns for its members as the primary reason for terminating operations, effectively ending three decades of independent labor advocacy.

## Significance

The disbanding of the HKCTU marked a major turning point for the local labor movement, leaving the field largely devoid of prominent pro-democracy or independent labor voices. Critics and labor rights advocates argued that the dissolution signaled the shrinking space for independent civil society and collective labor action in the territory. Conversely, supporters of the government's trajectory maintained that the removal of politically active trade unions would restore social stability and allow labor organizations to focus purely on workers' welfare without political interference.

## Perspectives

- **Pro-democracy and independent labor advocates**: Viewed the dissolution as a severe blow to workers' rights and freedom of association, arguing that the intense pressure from authorities extinguished the last major independent labor coalition in Hong Kong.
- **Pro-Beijing and government supporters**: Contended that the HKCTU had strayed far from traditional labor representation into disruptive political activism, and that its closure would help foster a more stable, constructive environment focused strictly on workers' economic welfare.
