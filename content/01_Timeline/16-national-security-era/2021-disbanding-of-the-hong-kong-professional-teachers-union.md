## Background
Founded in **1973**, the Hong Kong Professional Teachers' Union (香港教育專業人員協會), commonly known as the PTU (教協), grew to become the territory's largest single-industry trade union and a prominent voice in the pan-democratic camp. Beyond professional and welfare services for teachers, the union frequently engaged in broader political debates, participating in the alliance that organized the annual July 1 marches and supporting various democratic electoral campaigns.

## What happened
In **August 2021**, state-controlled media outlets in Beijing, including Xinhua and the People's Daily, launched a series of sharp attacks labeling the PTU a "poisonous weed" and demanding its eradication. Authorities subsequently severed ties with the organization. Citing an inability to navigate the rapidly shifting political environment and ensuring the safety of its staff and members, the union's leadership announced its decision to disband on **August 10, 2021**. A general meeting later voted overwhelmingly in favor of the voluntary dissolution.

## Significance
The dissolution of the PTU is widely regarded by observers as a major milestone in the contraction of Hong Kong's civil society following the enactment of the National Security Law in **2020**. As the largest professional body to dissolve, its closure signaled to other labor unions, professional associations, and non-governmental organizations that traditional forms of political advocacy and civil engagement carried severe institutional risks under the new legal framework.

## Perspectives

- **Pro-Beijing and Hong Kong Government**: Authorities and state media argued that the PTU had deviated from its educational mandate by engaging in radical politics and anti-government activities, viewing its disbanding as a necessary step to restore order and professional ethics in Hong Kong's education system.
- **Democratic and Civil Society Supporters**: Pro-democracy figures and civil society advocates viewed the dissolution as a heavy blow to freedom of association and expression, arguing that the pressure applied to the union demonstrated the narrowing space for independent labor representation and professional autonomy in the city.
