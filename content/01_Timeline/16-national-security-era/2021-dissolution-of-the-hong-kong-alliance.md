## Background

Formed on **May 21, 1989**, the Hong Kong Alliance in Support of Patriotic Democratic Movements of China (香港市民支援愛國民主運動聯合會) was a prominent civil society organization in Hong Kong. For over three decades, the Alliance was best known for organizing the annual candlelight vigil at Victoria Park (維多利亞公園) to commemorate the victims of the **1989 Tiananmen Square crackdown**. 

The organization's founding five goals included the release of dissidents, the accountability for the 1989 crackdown, the end of one-party rule, and the building of a democratic China. These objectives became central points of contention following the enactment of the **Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region** in June **2020**.

## What happened

Throughout **2021**, authorities increased scrutiny on the Alliance. In August, police and government officials designated the group as an "agent of foreign forces" and demanded extensive operational and financial records, which the Alliance's leadership initially refused to submit on legal grounds.

Key figures associated with the organization, including **Lee Cheuk-yan** (李卓人), **Albert Ho** (何俊仁), and **Chow Hang-tung** (鄒幸彤), faced arrest and prosecution under the National Security Law and other statutes. Amidst the detentions and the departure of many member organizations, a general meeting was convened on **September 25, 2021**. During this meeting, members voted overwhelmingly to dissolve the Alliance. Its official website and museum exhibits were subsequently removed.

## Significance

The dissolution of the Hong Kong Alliance marked a major turning point in Hong Kong's political history, signaling the effective conclusion of the city's organized, large-scale public movement demanding democratization in mainland China. Along with the earlier closures of the Civil Human Rights Front and numerous independent trade unions and media outlets, the event underscored a profound transformation in Hong Kong's civil society under the legal framework established in 2020.

## Perspectives

- **Pro-Beijing and Government Perspective**: Authorities and pro-establishment figures maintained that the Alliance's long-standing operational goals, particularly calling for the end of one-party rule, violated the National Security Law and threatened national sovereignty, asserting that law enforcement actions were necessary to uphold public order and the rule of law.
- **Pro-democracy and Civil Society Perspective**: Organizers and supporters argued that the Alliance operated peacefully for decades within the bounds of Hong Kong's previous legal framework, viewing its dissolution and the prosecution of its leaders as a severe contraction of civil liberties, freedom of speech, and the right to peaceful assembly.
