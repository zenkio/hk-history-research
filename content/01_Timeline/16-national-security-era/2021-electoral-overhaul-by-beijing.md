## Background

Following the widespread political unrest and protests of **2019**, and the subsequent enactment of the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region in **June 2020**, Beijing and the Hong Kong government initiated major structural reforms to the city's governance framework. Central authorities argued that existing electoral loopholes allowed anti-establishment figures to threaten national security and disrupt effective administration.

## What happened

On **March 11, 2021**, the National People's Congress (全国人民代表大会) passed the 'Decision on Improving the Electoral System of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region'. This overhaul fundamentally reshaped Hong Kong's political architecture by expanding the Election Committee (选举委员会), which chooses the Chief Executive, and increasing its power to nominate candidates for the Legislative Council (立法会). A candidate qualification review committee was established to vet all aspirants for public office to ensure adherence to 'patriots governing Hong Kong' (爱国者治港). Furthermore, the proportion of directly elected seats in the Legislative Council was substantially reduced, while the total number of seats was increased from **70** to **90**.

## Significance

This legislative restructuring marked a profound turning point in Hong Kong's political evolution, effectively redefining the 'One Country, Two Systems' (一国两制) framework as interpreted by Beijing. Proponents viewed the changes as a necessary stabilizer to restore law, order, and economic focus after years of turmoil. Conversely, critics and various international observers argued that the reforms dismantled democratic representation and severely restricted political pluralism by systematically excluding opposition voices from the institutional process.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and Hong Kong Governments**: Authorities maintained that the electoral modifications were constitutional, closed existing vulnerabilities, and ensured that governance is entrusted strictly to loyal patriots, thereby fostering long-term stability and prosperity.
- **Critics and Democratic Proponents**: Opponents contended that the reforms effectively dismantled the democratic participation promised in the Basic Law, shrinking public representation and neutralizing meaningful political opposition.
