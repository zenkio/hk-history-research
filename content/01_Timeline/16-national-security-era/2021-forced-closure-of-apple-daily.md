## Background
Founded in **1995** by media entrepreneur **Jimmy Lai** (黎智英), *Apple Daily* (蘋果日報) and its parent company, Next Digital (壹傳媒), established themselves as prominent critics of the governments in Beijing and Hong Kong. Known for its sensationalist reporting style combined with staunch investigative journalism and pro-democracy advocacy, the paper became a major forum for opposition voices. Following the enactment of the **Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region** (commonly known as the National Security Law) on **June 30, 2020**, the legal environment for local media shifted dramatically.

## What happened
Throughout **2020** and **2021**, authorities applied the National Security Law to several key figures associated with *Apple Daily*. In **June 2021**, police raided the newspaper's headquarters, arresting several top editors and executives on suspicion of conspiring with foreign forces. Concurrently, the Security Bureau froze company assets amounting to millions of Hong Kong dollars, citing articles published by the paper as evidence of an ongoing violation of the security legislation. Deprived of operating funds and facing insurmountable legal risks for its remaining staff, the board of directors announced that **June 24, 2021**, would be the publication's final operating day. Millions of copies of the final edition were printed and purchased by citizens across the city.

## Significance
The cessation of *Apple Daily* is widely regarded by observers as a watershed moment for the media landscape in **Hong Kong** (香港). For supporters and international critics, the closure symbolized the dismantling of independent journalism and the erosion of freedoms guaranteed under the Basic Law. Conversely, central and local government authorities maintained that the actions taken against the media outlet were strictly enforcement of the law against illegal acts endangering national security, emphasizing that freedom of the press is not absolute and cannot serve as a shield for unlawful conduct.

## Perspectives

- **Pro-democracy and international human rights observers**: View the closure as a devastating blow to press freedom and pluralism in Hong Kong, arguing that the freezing of assets without a prior judicial conviction bypassed standard legal protections and signaled the end of independent mainstream media in the territory.
- **Beijing and Hong Kong government officials**: Maintain that the legal actions taken against Next Digital and its executives were lawful measures to safeguard national security, asserting that the media outlet abused press freedom to incite subversion and collude with foreign forces.
