## Background
Following the enactment of the **Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region** in June **2020**, education policy shifted significantly. The Education Bureau (教育局) faced mandates to integrate national security concepts into the formal school curriculum. Prior to this period, civic education in Hong Kong schools often emphasized critical thinking, human rights, and local identity, reflecting a distinct educational lineage inherited from the British colonial era and adapted after **1997**.

## What happened
Starting in September **2021** at the beginning of the academic year, schools rolled out new guidelines emphasizing national security, Chinese sovereignty, and patriotism. The changes affected multiple subjects, including **Citizenship and Social Development** (公民與社會發展科), which replaced the former Liberal Studies (通識教育) curriculum. Teachers were provided with official frameworks outlining key areas such as ecological security, cultural security, and the avoidance of acts deemed subversion or secession. Co-curricular activities, flag-raising ceremonies, and school assemblies increasingly incorporated national symbols and themes.

## Significance
This educational reform marked a pivotal transition in Hong Kong's pedagogical landscape, realigning local curricula with mainland standards of political socialization. Proponents viewed the measures as essential for restoring social stability and correcting historical gaps in national identity following the unrest of **2019**. Conversely, critics and some overseas observers expressed concern that the new guidelines would restrict academic freedom, narrow the scope of classroom discourse, and diminish pluralistic viewpoints among youth.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and HKSAR Government**: National security education is a necessary and constitutional duty to ensure students develop a correct understanding of their country, respect national sovereignty, and become law-abiding citizens who contribute to social stability.
- **View**: The rapid implementation of national security curricula risks chilling open classroom discussion, eroding traditional academic freedoms, and suppressing the development of independent civic perspectives among students.
