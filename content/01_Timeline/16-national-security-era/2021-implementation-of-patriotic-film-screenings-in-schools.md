## Background
Following the implementation of the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (**2020**), the government sought to reform the education sector to emphasize national security education and patriotic sentiment. Education authorities argued that students needed a stronger sense of national identity, citing a perceived lack of understanding regarding the history and development of the People's Republic of China (中華人民共和國) as a challenge to stability. This period marked a transition from the previous emphasis on liberal studies toward a curriculum integrated more closely with national guidelines.

## What happened
In **July 2021**, the Education Bureau (教育局) issued guidelines to schools across the territory. These guidelines mandated that schools utilize film screenings as a pedagogical tool to facilitate 'value-based education.' The initiative encouraged schools to screen films depicting historical events in China, revolutionary figures, or themes of national development. While schools were given discretion in selecting specific titles from curated lists, the objective remained uniform: to ensure that students across all levels engaged with narratives that highlighted national achievements and the history of the Communist Party of China (中國共產黨). The screenings were often supplemented by classroom discussions or post-viewing assignments intended to solidify the lessons presented on screen.

## Significance
This policy represents a significant juncture in the evolution of Hong Kong’s educational landscape. By integrating media-based patriotic instruction, the authorities aimed to align local student perspectives with the official state-sanctioned view of national history. The move was part of a larger administrative effort to consolidate national security awareness in schools, which also included revisions to textbooks and the introduction of new standardized assessments. While proponents describe this as a necessary step to cultivate national consciousness, critics have raised concerns regarding the potential for narrowed historical discourse within the classroom. The long-term impact on student identity and the reception of these materials within the school community remain subjects of ongoing academic observation.

## Perspectives

- **Government and pro-establishment perspective**: Authorities and supporters maintain that these screenings are essential for fostering a correct understanding of national history and constitutional order. They argue that prior educational frameworks lacked sufficient emphasis on national identity, and that visual media is an effective tool to engage students with the successes of the nation.
- **Critical or pan-democratic perspective**: Critics and some observers argue that these mandates reflect a top-down effort to impose a singular ideological narrative on students. They express concern that the focus on patriotic films limits critical thinking and ignores alternative historical perspectives, potentially marginalizing the distinct local identity of Hong Kong.
