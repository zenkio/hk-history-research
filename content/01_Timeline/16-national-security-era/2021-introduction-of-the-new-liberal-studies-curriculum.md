## Background
Liberal Studies (通識教育科) was introduced as a core subject in the Hong Kong senior secondary curriculum in 2009. Designed to cultivate critical thinking, it encouraged students to analyze contemporary social, political, and cultural issues. Over the following decade, the subject became a focal point of public debate, with various stakeholders questioning its neutrality and its role in fostering student activism.

## What happened
On **April 1, 2021**, the Education Bureau (教育局) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region issued a circular detailing the replacement of Liberal Studies with the new subject, Citizenship and Social Development (公民與社會發展科). The curriculum for the new subject emphasizes national security, the "One Country, Two Systems" framework, and the development of mainland China. The content is designed to be more prescriptive, replacing the open-ended inquiry method of Liberal Studies with a syllabus focused on national identity and the Basic Law (基本法).

## Significance
This reform marked a fundamental pivot in Hong Kong's education policy. Proponents of the change argued that it was necessary to rectify the "misguided" critical inquiry encouraged by the previous curriculum, which they believed had been exploited to foster anti-government sentiment. Conversely, critics and some educators expressed concerns that the change curtailed the development of independent, critical reasoning skills. The transition was seen by many scholars as an institutional effort to strengthen patriotic education and ensure alignment with the political priorities established after the implementation of the National Security Law (香港國安法) in 2020.

## Perspectives

- **Pro-establishment and Beijing officials**: They viewed Liberal Studies as a source of instability that lacked clear guidelines and fostered radicalism among youth. The reform is seen as a necessary correction to restore educational focus on national identity and constitutional order.
- **Pro-democracy advocates and some educational groups**: They argue that the removal of open-ended inquiry undermines the student's ability to evaluate multiple perspectives independently. They contend the reform effectively turns education into a tool for political indoctrination.
