## Background

First introduced by the government in **November 2020** amid the COVID-19 pandemic, the 'LeaveHomeSafe' (安心出行) mobile application was initially designed as a voluntary tool to assist with digital contact tracing. Users would scan QR codes displayed at various venues to log their visit history. However, as part of the Special Administrative Region (SAR) government's strict adherence to a dynamic zero-infection strategy aligned with mainland China's pandemic protocols, authorities progressively tightened the requirements for entering public facilities.

## What happened

On **December 9, 2021**, the government's mandate took full effect, requiring citizens to scan the 'LeaveHomeSafe' QR code using a smartphone before entering almost all government buildings, restaurants, schools, leisure facilities, and other regulated premises. Exemptions were initially granted only to specific groups, such as the elderly, children under a certain age, and individuals with documented disabilities, though they often had to fill out paper registration forms instead. The policy effectively eliminated the option of simply providing personal contact details on paper for the general public, making smartphone ownership and app utilization a practical prerequisite for participating in daily public life in **Hong Kong** (香港).

## Significance

The enforcement of the mandatory app usage marked a critical intersection of public health governance and technological integration in the post-2020 National Security Law era. While framed strictly as a necessary epidemiological measure to protect public health and suppress virus transmission, the policy accelerated public discourse concerning the centralization of data, digital privacy, and the expanding infrastructure of surveillance. It underscored how public health emergencies could facilitate broader systemic changes in administrative control and digital tracking within the city.

## Perspectives

- **Government and Pro-Establishment Perspective**: Authorities emphasized that the universal adoption of 'LeaveHomeSafe' was an indispensable public health tool required to safeguard the community, swiftly trace potential chains of infection, and align Hong Kong's pandemic management with international standards and mainland protocols.
- **Critics and Civil Society Perspective**: Civil rights advocates and privacy concerned groups argued that the mandatory app encroached upon personal liberties, raised significant data privacy concerns regarding how collected information might be stored or accessed, and created practical exclusion barriers for marginalized populations without compatible smartphones.
