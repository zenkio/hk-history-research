## Background

In July 2020, the pro-democracy camp in Hong Kong organized an unofficial primary election (民主派初選) to select the strongest candidates for the upcoming Legislative Council election. The primary aimed to help the camp secure a legislative majority, which organizers hoped to use to veto government budgets and force the chief executive to address demands stemming from the 2019 protests. More than 600,000 residents cast votes. 

While organizers argued the primary was legal and protected under the Basic Law, central and local authorities warned that the exercise could violate the newly enacted **Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region** (Hong Kong National Security Law), specifically targeting subversion of state power.

## What happened

On **January 6, 2021**, the Hong Kong Police Force mobilized over 1,000 officers to conduct raids and arrests across the city. A total of **53** prominent activists, former lawmakers, social workers, and academics were detained. Among those arrested were figures such as **Au Nok-hin** (區諾軒), **Andrew Chiu** (趙家賢), and **Ben Chung** (鍾錦麟), alongside organizers of the primary. Authorities also froze property linked to the primary organization under national security provisions. 

Most of the arrested individuals were subsequently held without bail for months awaiting trial, marking the single largest application of the National Security Law since its implementation on June 30, 2020.

## Significance

The mass arrests marked a decisive turning point in Hong Kong's political landscape, effectively dismantling the institutional opposition and signaling a new phase of legal enforcement against political dissent. The event drew international condemnation from governments including the United States, the United Kingdom, and the European Union, which viewed the crackdown as a further erosion of Hong Kong's autonomy and freedoms under the 'One Country, Two Systems' framework. Conversely, mainland and Hong Kong officials maintained that the actions were lawful, necessary to restore public order, and aimed at preventing subversion and safeguarding national security.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and Hong Kong Governments**: Authorities viewed the unofficial primary as a coordinated, illegal scheme to paralyze the government and subvert state power, asserting that the arrests were necessary to uphold the rule of law and protect national security.
- **Pro-Democracy Activists and International Observers**: Critics and foreign governments regarded the mass arrests as a severe blow to political pluralism and democratic representation, arguing that participation in a primary election is a legitimate political activity protected by existing laws.
