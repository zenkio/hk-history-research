## Background

For decades, Hong Kong's urban development concentrated heavily around Victoria Harbour and the urban core of Kowloon and Hong Kong Island, while the border areas adjacent to Shenzhen remained largely rural or low-density. Proposals for closer integration with Shenzhen had circulated for years amid the rapid growth of the neighboring mainland technology sector.

## What happened

On **October 6, 2021**, Chief Executive **Carrie Lam** (**林鄭月娥**) announced the Northern Metropolis Development Strategy in her **2021 Policy Address** (**二零二一年施政報告**). The comprehensive plan envisioned a **300-square-kilometer** area spanning Yuen Long (**元朗**) and North District (**北區**) to house around **2.5 million** people. The strategy proposed expanding transport infrastructure, including cross-border railway links, and creating an innovation and technology zone around the San Tin (**新田**) area to parallel Shenzhen's tech industry.

## Significance

The announcement marked a major strategic shift in Hong Kong's urban planning paradigm, pivoting the city's geographical orientation from a harbor-centric model toward the northern frontier and deeper integration with the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area (**粵港澳大灣區**). Supporters viewed it as a vital step to alleviate chronic housing shortages and diversify the economy, while critics and urban planners raised concerns regarding financial feasibility, ecological impacts on wetlands, and the long-term governance framework.

## Perspectives

- **Hong Kong Government**: Authorities emphasized that the strategy would simultaneously address severe housing shortages and foster a new economic engine by synergizing with Shenzhen's technological capabilities.
- **Environmental Groups**: Conservationists expressed concern over potential threats to ecologically sensitive wetlands and fish ponds in the northern New Territories, calling for rigorous environmental impact assessments.
