## Background
Following the enactment of the **Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region** in June **2020**, the central authorities established the Office for Safeguarding National Security (中央人民政府駐香港特別行政區維護國家安全公署) to oversee local enforcement. Initially operating out of the Metropark Hotel Causeway Bay (銅鑼灣維景酒店) on a temporary basis, the agency required a permanent headquarters to carry out its statutory mandates, which include analyzing national security conditions, overseeing law enforcement, and exercising jurisdiction over specific complex cases under extraordinary circumstances.

## What happened
On **February 28, 2021**, a formal inauguration ceremony marked the establishment of the permanent headquarters at the former hotel building located on Causeway Road (高士威道). The conversion transformed a commercial hospitality venue into a fortified security installation. Alterations included the installation of enhanced security infrastructure, blast protection, and closed-circuit monitoring systems, visibly altering the urban fabric and streetscape of the bustling Causeway Bay district near Victoria Park (維多利亞公園).

## Significance
This event represented a tangible manifestation of Beijing's direct security apparatus taking physical root within the urban center of Hong Kong. While supporters viewed the fortified headquarters as a necessary and legitimate bastion for upholding state sovereignty and maintaining public order, critics and foreign observers interpreted the permanent conversion as a significant milestone in the integration of Hong Kong's legal and security framework with that of mainland China, altering the pre-1997 administrative demarcation between local governance and central state organs.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and pro-establishment perspective**: Proponents argued that the establishment of the permanent office was a constitutional necessity mandated by the National Security Law to ensure national sovereignty, security, and development interests were effectively protected in Hong Kong. They maintained that the agency operates strictly within the law and provides vital support to local authorities in maintaining stability.
- **Critics and international observers**: Critics and various human rights organizations expressed concern over the opacity of the agency's jurisdiction and the broad powers granted under the National Security Law. They viewed the prominent, fortified mainland security installation in a core commercial district as a symbol of the erosion of the 'One Country, Two Systems' framework and local autonomy.
