## Background
Plans for **M+** began over a decade prior as a flagship project within the West Kowloon Cultural District (西九文化區) to establish Hong Kong as a premier international arts hub. Conceived as a multidisciplinary institution dedicated to modern and contemporary visual culture—encompassing design, architecture, moving image, and visual art—the museum aimed to bridge Eastern and Western artistic narratives. However, by the time of its scheduled opening on **November 12, 2021**, the sociopolitical landscape of Hong Kong had transformed profoundly following the enactment of the **National Security Law** (香港特別行政區維護國家安全法) in June 2020.

## What happened
The museum opened its doors to the public featuring several inaugural exhibitions drawn from its core collections, including the high-profile **Sigg Collection** (希克藏品) of contemporary Chinese art. Before the opening, the institution faced controversy when certain politically sensitive artworks, notably photographs by **Ai Weiwei** (艾未未) depicting the Tiananmen Square crackdown, were scrutinized by pro-establishment politicians and local media for potentially violating the new security legislation. While museum leadership and the West Kowloon Cultural District Authority navigated these concerns by adjusting certain displays and emphasizing legal compliance, the institution maintained a vast collection representing decades of regional and international artistic expression.

## Significance
The launch of **M+** marked a watershed moment for Hong Kong's cultural sector. It materialized long-standing ambitions to build a world-class arts institution on par with London's Tate Modern or New York's Museum of Modern Art. Simultaneously, it became a prominent test case for how cultural institutions in Hong Kong would operate under tightened legal constraints, balancing international curatorial standards with national security mandates. The opening underscored the complex interplay between state-level legal frameworks and global contemporary art curation in the territory.
