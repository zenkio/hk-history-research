## Background

The **Pillar of Shame** (國殤之柱), created by Danish sculptor **Jens Galschiøt**, had been displayed at the University of Hong Kong (HKU) since **1997**. Standing roughly eight meters tall, the sculpture depicts twisted human bodies and served as a central symbol for annual commemorations of the Tiananmen Square crackdown. For decades, its presence on a university campus was often cited by observers as a testament to Hong Kong's relative freedom of expression compared to mainland China.

## What happened

Late in the evening on **December 22, 2021**, and into the early hours of the following day, HKU contractors and workers dismantled the monument. The university wrapped the statue's sections in protective material and transported them to a university-managed facility in Kam Tin (錦田). HKU officials stated that the decision was based on external legal advice and safety risk assessments concerning the aging sculpture, noting that the owner had never formally registered the statue with the institution.

## Significance

The removal of the Pillar of Shame marked a significant turning point in Hong Kong's cultural and political landscape following the enactment of the National Security Law in **2020**. Critics and democracy advocates viewed the dismantling as a severe blow to historical memory and academic freedom. Conversely, supporters of the action emphasized the autonomy of institutional property management and argued that public spaces should not host politically charged monuments that potentially conflict with local laws.

## Perspectives

- **Pro-democracy advocates and human rights organizations**: They viewed the removal as a calculated attempt by authorities to erase the history of the 1989 Tiananmen Square crackdown and stifle public mourning and political dissent in Hong Kong.
- **University management and pro-Beijing figures**: They maintained that the university acted independently to manage its campus property, resolve long-standing legal liabilities, and prevent the institution from becoming a platform for contentious political activities.
