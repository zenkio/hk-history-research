## Background

Radio Television Hong Kong (香港電台), commonly known as **RTHK**, had long operated as the public service broadcaster in Hong Kong. Established during the British colonial period, its editorial independence and operational structure were modelled loosely on the British Broadcasting Corporation (BBC). Over decades, RTHK produced investigative journalism and popular satirical programs that frequently critiqued government policies and figures. Following the implementation of the **National Security Law** in **2020**, the governance and editorial oversight of RTHK became a subject of intense public and political debate.

## What happened

On **May 19, 2021**, RTHK management, operating under the leadership of the newly appointed Director of Broadcasting **Patrick Li (李百全)**, introduced revamped editorial guidelines and accountability mechanisms. These measures were designed to align the broadcaster more closely with the principles of the Basic Law (香港特別行政區基本法) and to ensure accurate, impartial, and balanced reporting. Concurrently, several long-running satirical and public-affairs programs, including *Headliner* (頭條新聞), were canceled, and multiple episodes of other shows were pulled from public archives or prevented from airing before broadcast.

## Significance

The restructuring of RTHK represented a fundamental turning point in the landscape of Hong Kong's media and public broadcasting. Proponents of the changes argued that tighter editorial controls were necessary to restore professionalism, eliminate bias, and ensure that a publicly funded institution adhered strictly to the 'One Country, Two Systems' (一國兩制) framework. Conversely, critics and press freedom advocates viewed the reforms as a dismantling of editorial independence and a significant contraction of media pluralism. This event is widely cited by scholars as a key milestone in the broader transformation of Hong Kong's civil society and information ecosystem following the 2019 protests and the subsequent security legislation.

## Perspectives

- **Pro-establishment and Government Viewpoint**: Authorities and pro-establishment figures maintained that RTHK, as a government department and public broadcaster, had strayed from its charter by broadcasting biased content and failing to uphold national security. They argued that the new guidelines and management oversight were essential to restore editorial accountability and ensure the station served public interests responsibly.
- **Critics and Press Freedom Advocates**: Critics, including various journalistic associations and former RTHK staff, argued that the revamped guidelines and the cancellation of satirical shows effectively silenced critical voices and decimated investigative journalism. They contended that these measures undermined the core principles of public service broadcasting and severely restricted freedom of speech in the territory.
