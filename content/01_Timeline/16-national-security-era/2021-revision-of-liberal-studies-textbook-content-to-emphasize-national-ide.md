## Background

Liberal Studies (通識教育科) was introduced as a core senior secondary subject in **2009** under education reforms, designed to encourage critical thinking on contemporary social, political, and cultural issues. However, the subject became a lightning rod for controversy. Pro-Beijing figures and state media frequently criticized the curriculum, alleging it radicalized youth and contributed to widespread anti-government protests, most notably during the **2019** anti-extradition bill movement.

Following the enactment of the **Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region** (香港特別行政區維護國家安全法) in **June 2020**, the Education Bureau (教育局) initiated sweeping overhauls of the school system to embed national security education and patriotism across the curriculum.

## What happened

On **June 10, 2021**, textbook publishers released heavily revised editions of Liberal Studies materials vetted by the Education Bureau under a newly established review mechanism. Content addressing sensitive political topics—such as the 1989 Tiananmen Square crackdown, universal suffrage, the separation of powers, and systemic criticisms of the Chinese Communist Party—was systematically removed or rephrased.

In place of these topics, the revised textbooks introduced robust content emphasizing national security, China's constitutional order, national identity, and the achievements of the People's Republic of China. The subject was subsequently renamed and restructured to reduce the emphasis on independent political analysis.

## Significance

This curricular overhaul marked a watershed moment in Hong Kong's educational history, signaling the end of an era of permissive civic discourse in schools. By aligning curriculum content with mainland political standards, the reforms sought to cultivate patriotism and allegiance to the state among younger generations, permanently altering how contemporary history and governance are taught in the territory.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and Pro-Establishment Figures**: Proponents argue that the textbook revisions were a necessary corrective measure to purge biased and subversive materials, restore social stability, and instill a proper sense of national identity and legal consciousness among students.
- **Pan-Democrats and Civil Society Critics**: Critics contend that the curriculum changes represented a severe erosion of academic freedom and freedom of speech, replacing critical inquiry with state-sanctioned indoctrination and erasing pluralistic viewpoints from the classroom.
