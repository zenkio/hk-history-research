## Background

Founded in December 2014 following the conclusion of the Umbrella Movement, **Stand News** (立場新聞) established itself as one of Hong Kong's most prominent independent digital news platforms. Operating in an increasingly restrictive media environment following the implementation of the National Security Law in **2020**, the outlet frequently reported on sensitive political trials, protests, and local governance issues.

## What happened

On **December 29, 2021**, the National Security Department of the Hong Kong Police Force dispatched over 200 officers to raid the offices of Stand News. Police froze assets totaling approximately HK$61 million and arrested current and former senior staff members, including acting chief editor **Patrick Lam** (林紹桐) and former chief editor **Chung Pui-kuen** (鍾沛權), alongside several former board members such as **Denise Ho** (何韻詩) and **Margaret Ng** (吳靄儀). 

Later that same day, Stand News announced through social media that it would immediately cease all operations, take down its website and social media channels, and dismiss all employees. The police operation cited conspiracy to publish seditious publications under the Crimes Ordinance.

## Significance

The closure of Stand News marked a major turning point for press freedom in Hong Kong, following the earlier shutdown of the pro-democracy newspaper Apple Daily (蘋果日報) in June **2021**. It signaled the near-complete dismantling of the city's independent mainstream media landscape, accelerating concerns among international observers, journalists, and foreign governments regarding the protection of freedom of expression under the National Security Law framework.

## Perspectives

- **Hong Kong Police and Beijing Authorities**: Authorities maintained that the arrests and raid were targeted strictly at illegal acts involving seditious publications rather than legitimate journalistic work, emphasizing that freedom of the press is not absolute and cannot serve as a shield for national security violations.
- **International Observers and Press Freedom Advocates**: Foreign governments, the United Nations, and international press watchdogs condemned the raid and closures as a systematic erosion of press freedom, free speech, and civil liberties in Hong Kong.
