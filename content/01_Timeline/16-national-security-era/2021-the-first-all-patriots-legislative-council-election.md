## Background
Following the implementation of the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region in June 2020, the Central Government of the People's Republic of China initiated a significant restructuring of Hong Kong’s electoral system. In March 2021, the National People’s Congress Standing Committee (全國人民代表大會常務委員會) passed amendments to Annex I and Annex II of the Basic Law (基本法). The core of this reform was the "Patriots administering Hong Kong" (愛國者治港) principle, which established a Candidate Eligibility Review Committee (候選人資格審查委員會) to ensure that all individuals seeking public office were vetted for their loyalty to the state and the HKSAR.

## What happened
The election for the **7th Legislative Council** (第七屆立法會) was originally scheduled for September 2020 but was postponed for over a year, citing the COVID-19 pandemic. On **December 19, 2021**, the election proceeded under the new structure, which expanded the Legislative Council from 70 to 90 seats. These seats were allocated across three categories: 20 from geographical constituencies, 40 from the Election Committee (選舉委員會), and 30 from functional constituencies. Notably, the number of directly elected seats in geographical constituencies was reduced from 35 to 20. The vetting process resulted in the disqualification of several potential candidates, leading to a contest where almost all participants were identified as pro-establishment, with a near-total absence of traditional pro-democracy parties.

## Significance
The election represented a fundamental shift in Hong Kong’s political architecture. Supporters of the reform argued that it was necessary to restore stability, end legislative gridlock, and ensure that those holding public office would act in the interests of the nation and Hong Kong’s long-term prosperity. Conversely, critics viewed the exclusion of major opposition groups as a democratic regression, arguing that the system no longer provided a platform for diverse political representation or authentic public dissent. The voter turnout reached 30.2%, the lowest since the 1997 handover, sparking debate among observers over whether this reflected political disillusionment, a tactical boycott by voters, or a general lack of interest in the new electoral format. The resulting legislature shifted the focus of government-legislature relations toward a model of executive-led governance and collaborative policy development, signaling a departure from the confrontational political dynamics of previous decades.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and the HKSAR Government**: The reforms are presented as a necessary 'rectification' to close loopholes that previously allowed anti-China elements to sabotage governance. They maintain that the system ensures high-quality governance and long-term stability by requiring candidates to be vetted patriots.
- **Pro-democracy figures and international observers**: This perspective characterizes the changes as a dismantling of electoral competition and a violation of the promise of 'universal suffrage' outlined in the Basic Law. They argue that the vetting process effectively disenfranchised the majority of the pro-democracy electorate.
