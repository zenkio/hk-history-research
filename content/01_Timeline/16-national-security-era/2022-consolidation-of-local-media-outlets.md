## Background
Following the implementation of the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (香港國安法) in June 2020, the local media environment underwent significant structural changes. Throughout 2021, various news organizations faced intensified legal scrutiny. This period culminated in the closure of **Stand News** (立場新聞) in late December 2021, following a police raid and the arrest of several senior staff members under sedition charges.

## What happened
On **January 2, 2022**, the independent online news portal **Citizen News** (眾新聞) announced it would cease operations effective January 4, 2022. The management cited the inability to ensure the safety of its staff due to the prevailing legal environment as the primary driver for this decision. The closure was swift, occurring only days after the events involving **Stand News**. Unlike traditional broadcast media, **Citizen News** had relied on a subscription-based model and investigative reporting, which analysts noted had become increasingly difficult to sustain as legal interpretations of journalistic activities broadened.

## Significance
The closure of **Citizen News** is widely regarded by historians and observers as a definitive turning point for Hong Kong's press freedom. While the government maintained that these actions were necessary to uphold national security and the rule of law, the rapid departure of independent voices led to a reconfiguration of the media sector. Many observers note that this period saw a transition toward a media landscape increasingly aligned with state-sanctioned narratives, with remaining outlets adopting more cautious editorial stances to avoid potential legal liability.

## Perspectives

- **HKSAR Government and Beijing Officials**: The government asserts that media organizations must operate within the bounds of the National Security Law and that the rule of law is applied equally to all. They argue that the closure of such outlets is a internal management decision and that press freedom remains intact as long as it does not jeopardize national sovereignty.
- **Local Journalists and International Advocacy Groups**: Many local journalists and international observers argue that the legal climate created by the National Security Law has induced self-censorship, making independent investigative reporting functionally impossible. They view the closure of these outlets as evidence of a shrinking space for civil society and the erosion of the 'one country, two systems' framework.
