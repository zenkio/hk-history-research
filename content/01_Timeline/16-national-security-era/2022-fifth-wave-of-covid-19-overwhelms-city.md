## Background

Prior to **February 2022**, Hong Kong had largely maintained low case numbers through strict quarantine and tracing policies aligned with mainland China's "zero-COVID" strategy. However, the highly transmissible Omicron variant breached these defenses, exposing vulnerabilities in a population with suboptimal vaccination rates, especially among elderly residents.

## What happened

Beginning in **February 2022**, reported infections surged exponentially, severely straining public hospitals. Medical facilities were inundated, resulting in the distressing sight of patients lying on beds outside hospitals in cold weather. Public mortuaries quickly reached capacity, requiring the establishment of temporary storage facilities. The government implemented mandatory universal testing proposals, tightened social distancing restrictions, and imposed targeted lockdowns on residential buildings (圍封強檢), causing widespread anxiety and disruption to daily life.

## Significance

The fifth wave marked a critical turning point in Hong Kong's pandemic response. It severely tested the city's healthcare infrastructure and emergency management capabilities. The high death toll among unvaccinated seniors drew international scrutiny and prompted an urgent reevaluation of public health priorities, elderly vaccination campaigns, and the broader social and economic costs of maintaining strict containment policies.

## Perspectives

- **Hong Kong Government and Beijing**: Authorities emphasized the necessity of upholding the 'dynamic zero' strategy to protect public health and save lives, later accepting assistance from mainland Chinese medical and construction teams to build temporary isolation and treatment facilities.
- **Local Residents and Medical Professionals**: Many frontline healthcare workers and citizens felt overwhelmed by the suddenness and severity of the outbreak, criticizing the lack of clear communication, adequate planning for elderly care, and sudden policy shifts regarding testing and lockdowns.
