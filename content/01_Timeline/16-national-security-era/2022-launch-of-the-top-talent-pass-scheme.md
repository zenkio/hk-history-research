## Background

Following the social unrest of **2019** and the implementation of the National Security Law in **2020**, Hong Kong experienced a notable emigration wave involving professionals, educators, and middle-class families. This outflow exacerbated existing demographic challenges, including a low birth rate and an aging population, leading to severe talent shortages across various economic sectors. In response, Chief Executive **John Lee** (李家超) announced the creation of the Top Talent Pass Scheme (高端人才通行證計劃) during his **2022** Policy Address, signaling a strategic shift toward aggressive talent acquisition.

## What happened

On **December 28, 2022**, the Immigration Department (入境事務處) officially began accepting applications for the Top Talent Pass Scheme. The initiative aimed to bypass traditional, more cumbersome employment visa procedures for specific high-tier applicants. The scheme targeted three main categories: individuals with high annual earnings, graduates from the world's top universities, and those meeting specific professional criteria. The application process was designed to be streamlined, with approvals often granted within a short timeframe, allowing successful applicants to secure residency without first securing a local job offer.

## Significance

The launch of the scheme marked a pivotal moment in Hong Kong's post-pandemic governance and economic strategy. By the end of its initial rollout, the program received tens of thousands of applications, predominantly from mainland Chinese professionals. While supporters viewed the influx as a vital remedy for the labor shortage and a vote of confidence in the city's status as an international hub, critics and analysts debated its long-term impact on local workforce dynamics, housing demand, and the city's unique cultural identity.

## Perspectives

- **Government and Pro-Establishment Perspective**: Proponents argue that the scheme is a necessary and highly successful measure to replenish Hong Kong's talent pool, maintain its economic competitiveness, and secure its position as an international financial center.
- **Critical and Emigré Perspective**: Some observers and departing residents contend that the scheme primarily serves to integrate Hong Kong more closely with the mainland Chinese labor market, altering the local demographic balance rather than replacing the diverse international talent that previously left.
