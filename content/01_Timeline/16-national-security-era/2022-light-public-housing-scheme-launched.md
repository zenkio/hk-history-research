## Background
Hong Kong has long faced a severe housing crisis, characterized by prolonged waiting times for traditional public rental housing and an abundance of substandard subdivided flats (劏房) in the private market. Low-income families often endure years of high rents and poor living conditions while awaiting permanent allocation. 

## What happened
On **October 19, 2022**, Chief Executive **John Lee** (**李家超**) introduced the Light Public Housing (簡約公屋) scheme during his maiden Policy Address. The initiative aimed to construct approximately 30,000 temporary housing units within five years using standardized, modular construction methods. These units were planned for various sites across the territory, including urban and New Territories locations, to bridge the immediate supply gap for families on the public rental housing waitlist.

## Significance
The scheme represented a direct intervention by the government under the post-2020 National Security Law administration to tackle deep-seated livelihood issues. By focusing on speed and scale, the initiative sought to demonstrate governance capability and responsiveness to social welfare pressures, though its long-term effectiveness and the logistics of temporary site utilization remained subjects of public discussion.

## Perspectives

- **Government and Pro-Establishment Perspective**: Proponents argued that the scheme demonstrated decisive governance and a pragmatic approach to alleviating the suffering of grassroots citizens trapped in subdivided flats, effectively using transitional measures to address chronic supply shortages.
- **Housing Advocates and Community Critics**: Some critics and social workers questioned whether temporary housing could fully resolve systemic poverty, expressing concerns over the location of certain sites, transportation connectivity, and whether the policy might divert attention from the need for sustained, permanent public housing construction.
