## Background

In July **2022**, the popular local C-pop boy band **Mirror** (MIRROR) scheduled a series of 12 concerts at the **Hong Kong Coliseum** (香港體育館), located in **Hung Hom** (紅磡). The group, produced by **MakerVille**, had achieved immense mainstream success, sparking a major revival of local Cantonese pop culture. However, the rapid pace of production and elaborate stage designs placed heavy demands on technical crews and performers.

## What happened

On **July 28, 2022**, during the fourth concert of the run, a large suspended LED video screen detached from its rigging and plummeted onto the stage. The heavy display struck two dancers directly, most notably **Lee Kai-yin** (李啟言), who sustained severe spinal injuries, and **Chang Tsz-fung** (張梓峯), who was also injured. The incident was captured on video by numerous audience members and broadcast rapidly across social media, causing immediate shock and distress among the public and the entertainment industry.

## Significance

The accident exposed systemic safety oversights and regulatory gaps within the Hong Kong entertainment and event production sectors. Investigations by government departments, including the Labour Department and the Leisure and Cultural Services Department, revealed issues regarding subcontracting, load calculations, and the inspection of stage machinery. Several production personnel were subsequently arrested and charged in connection with the incident. Public discourse highlighted the precarious working conditions faced by performing arts professionals in Hong Kong, leading to calls for stricter enforcement of safety standards across all large-scale public events.
