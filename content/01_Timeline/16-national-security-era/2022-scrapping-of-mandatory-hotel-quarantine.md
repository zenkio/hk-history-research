## Background

For nearly three years, Hong Kong implemented some of the world's strictest COVID-19 pandemic control measures, closely aligning with mainland China's zero-infection strategy. Inbound travelers faced up to 21 days of mandatory isolation at designated quarantine hotels (指定檢疫酒店), alongside extensive testing requirements. While these policies successfully kept recorded case numbers low during earlier phases of the pandemic, they severely isolated Hong Kong internationally. Business chambers, foreign diplomats, and local residents increasingly voiced concerns that the restrictions were eroding the city's status as a global financial and aviation hub, leading to an outflow of talent and capital.

## What happened

On **September 24, 2022**, Chief Executive **John Lee** (李家超) announced that mandatory hotel quarantine would end, replaced by the **'0+3' scheme** effective from **September 26, 2022**. Under the new policy, inbound travelers no longer needed to quarantine in designated hotels or present a negative PCR test prior to boarding flights. Instead, the system permitted arrivals to go directly home or to accommodation of their choice, subject to three days of medical surveillance with movement restrictions. During this period, individuals were issued an amber health code preventing entry into high-risk venues like restaurants and bars, alongside mandatory polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and rapid antigen testing.

## Significance

The implementation of the '0+3' scheme represented a pivotal psychological and logistical shift for Hong Kong, easing the isolation that characterized the preceding years. It initiated the long-awaited process of reconnecting the territory with the global economy and facilitated a surge in outbound travel among local residents. However, full normalcy did not immediately return, as international carriers took time to restore flight routes, and strict masking mandates and testing protocols remained in place for several more months before subsequent policy rollbacks led to full reopening.

## Perspectives

- **Business and International Community**: Many multinational corporations and foreign chambers of commerce welcomed the '0+3' announcement as a vital first step to restore normal business operations, though many continued to advocate for an immediate '0+0' policy to match competing global financial centers.
- **Local Health Authorities and Beijing**: Proponents of cautious reopening emphasized that the gradual relaxation balanced the need for economic revitalization with ongoing public health protections, ensuring the healthcare system was not overwhelmed as international mobility resumed.
