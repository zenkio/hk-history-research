## Background

Opened in **1976** in Aberdeen Harbour, the Jumbo Floating Restaurant (珍寶海鮮舫) was a prominent symbol of Hong Kong's maritime dining culture and a major tourist attraction. Designed in the style of a traditional Chinese imperial palace, the multi-story barge could accommodate thousands of diners and hosted numerous international celebrities and dignitaries over the decades. 

However, the restaurant struggled financially in the years leading up to **2020**, exacerbated by the social unrest of **2019** and the subsequent COVID-19 pandemic. Despite attempts to find a new operator, the owner, Aberdeen Restaurant Enterprises, a subsidiary of Melco Resorts & Entertainment (新濠博亞娛樂), announced that the vessel would be towed away from Hong Kong as its license expired and maintenance costs mounted.

## What happened

On **June 14, 2022**, the Jumbo Floating Restaurant was towed out of Aberdeen Typhoon Shelter (香港仔避風塘). Five days later, on **June 19, 2022**, the parent company announced that the vessel had encountered adverse weather conditions near the Paracel Islands (西沙群島) in the South China Sea. Water entered the barge, causing it to list and eventually capsize. 

Maritime authorities and the towing company stated that water depth at the site made salvage operations extremely difficult. While the tugboat crew was uninjured, the main hull of the iconic structure sank.

## Significance

The loss of the floating restaurant sparked widespread discussion regarding the preservation of local heritage and collective memory in Hong Kong. For many residents, the sinking symbolized the fading of a distinct nostalgic era amid broader socioeconomic and political shifts under the National Security Law era. Critics of the process questioned the transparency of the relocation plans and whether adequate measures were taken to preserve the historic landmark locally, while defenders of the decision pointed to the insurmountable economic realities of maintaining a aging marine structure.
