## Background

In the years following the implementation of the National Security Law in **2020**, the financial sector of Hong Kong (香港) experienced significant integration into mainland China's economic framework while maintaining its status as a leading global financial hub. Long before **2023**, Hong Kong had established itself as the world's primary offshore Renminbi (人民幣) business clearing center. Financial authorities in both Beijing and Hong Kong sought to enhance the international usage of the Chinese currency and reduce transaction friction for mainland investors utilizing the Stock Connect scheme.

## What happened

On **June 19, 2023**, the Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (香港交易及結算所有限公司), commonly known as HKEX, officially launched the **HKD-RMB Dual Counter Model** (港幣人民幣雙櫃台模式). This framework enabled investors to trade designated major equities using two separate counters: one denominated in Hong Kong Dollars (HKD) and the other in Chinese Renminbi (RMB). At launch, over twenty major companies, including prominent technology and financial firms, participated in the initiative, utilizing a newly established market-maker mechanism to ensure liquidity and minimize pricing discrepancies between the two currency counters.

## Significance

Proponents argued that the dual counter model represented a crucial step in the internationalization of the Renminbi, providing offshore holders of Chinese currency with direct investment avenues in Hong Kong equities without requiring foreign exchange conversion. While Western financial analysts observed the initiative against a backdrop of shifting geopolitical tensions and foreign capital outflows from broader Chinese markets, local financial regulators emphasized that the model reinforced Hong Kong's unique position as a bridge between mainland China and international financial systems, supporting long-term liquidity and currency diversification.

## Perspectives

- **Hong Kong Financial Regulators**: Authorities viewed the launch as a major milestone that would strengthen Hong Kong's role as the premier offshore RMB hub, enhancing market liquidity and offering investors greater currency flexibility.
- **International Financial Analysts**: Observers noted that while the technical framework was robust, the actual trading volume in RMB counters would largely depend on broader macroeconomic confidence in China's currency and mainland economic growth.
