## Background
Historically, the **District Councils (區議會)** were established in the 1980s as the lowest tier of representative government in Hong Kong, serving as consultative bodies for local issues. Following the **2019 District Council elections**, which saw a record voter turnout and a landslide victory for the pro-democracy camp, the central government and the **Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR) Government** initiated a series of electoral overhauls. These reforms were described by authorities as necessary to ensure that only 'patriots' govern Hong Kong, following the implementation of the **National Security Law** in 2020.

## What happened
On **July 6, 2023**, the Legislative Council passed the District Councils (Amendment) Bill 2023. This legislation significantly altered the composition of the councils by reducing the number of directly elected seats from 452 to 88. The majority of the council seats were replaced by those filled via the **District Committees (地區委員會)**—specifically the Area Committees (分區委員會), the District Fight Crime Committees (地區撲滅罪行委員會), and the District Fire Safety Committees (地區防火委員會). Collectively referred to as the 'Three Committees' (三會), members of these bodies are appointed by the government. Furthermore, the reform mandated that all candidates for the remaining elected seats must secure nominations from members of these same three committees before they are permitted to run in the polls.

## Significance
This reform fundamentally altered the grassroots representation system in Hong Kong. By shifting power from direct public elections to a nomination process controlled by appointed committees, the system ensures that district-level representatives are vetted by government-aligned groups. Proponents of the change argue that it depoliticizes local governance, allowing district councillors to focus on livelihood issues rather than partisan politics. Conversely, critics and observers from various international human rights organizations argue that the move effectively eliminated the only remaining significant democratic platform in Hong Kong, thereby ending the city's experiment with grassroots electoral representation.

## Perspectives

- **HKSAR Government and Beijing**: The reform is presented as an essential step to return District Councils to their original function as non-political consultative bodies. Officials emphasize that this ensures stability and the 'patriots administering Hong Kong' principle, preventing the councils from being used to hinder government policy.
- **Pro-democracy advocates and international observers**: This system is viewed as an erosion of democratic accountability, as the 'Three Committees' are government-appointed and lack a public mandate. Critics argue that the nomination requirement creates an insurmountable barrier for any candidates who do not align with the political establishment.
