## Background

Following the implementation of the **National Security Law** in **2020** and the subsequent easing of pandemic-related restrictions, Hong Kong experienced an acute labor shortage. Key industries, particularly construction, transport, and hospitality, reported mounting vacancies driven by outward migration and an aging local population. Business chambers and industry stakeholders repeatedly pressed the administration of Chief Executive **John Lee** (李家超) to intervene, arguing that the deficit in human resources threatened infrastructure projects and economic recovery.

## What happened

On **June 13, 2023**, the Hong Kong government formally announced the introduction of streamlined importation of labor schemes. The framework relaxed previous restrictions under the Supplementary Labour Scheme (補充勞工計劃), allowing sectors such as construction and transport to bring in specified quotas of non-local workers, predominantly from mainland China. The policy included provisions for employer-provided accommodation and established monitoring mechanisms intended to safeguard local employment priority.

## Significance

The launch marked a significant pivot in Hong Kong's labor and economic policy, addressing structural demographic challenges through controlled regional integration. While proponents framed the measure as an economic imperative to maintain competitiveness and deliver public works, critics raised concerns regarding potential downward pressure on local wages and the integration of migrant workers into the community.

## Perspectives

- **Government and Business Chambers**: Authorities and business representatives argued that importing labor was a vital, pragmatic step to alleviate bottlenecks in critical infrastructure and services, ensuring Hong Kong's continued economic vitality.
- **Labor Unions and Local Workers**: Labor advocates expressed apprehension that expanding importation schemes could undermine local wage growth and job security, calling for greater transparency and stronger protections for resident workers.
