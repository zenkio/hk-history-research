## Background

Following the lifting of stringent COVID-19 pandemic restrictions in late 2022 and early 2023, the Hong Kong economy faced the challenge of reviving its retail, dining, and tourism sectors. Consumer confidence had been dampened by prolonged social distancing measures and broader economic uncertainties under the National Security Law era. To encourage residents to spend locally and inject vitality into the domestic market, the Financial Secretary announced various initiatives in the 2023-24 Budget.

## What happened

On **April 25, 2023**, the government officially launched the **Happy Hong Kong Campaign** (開心香港). The campaign centered around three main themes: gourmet experiences, vibrant marketplaces, and large-scale sea-and-land amusements. A key highlight was a series of large-scale weekend gourmet marketplaces held in various districts, offering diverse culinary options at affordable prices to attract families and residents. Additionally, the initiative included large-scale thematic events such as the Hong Kong Cinema Day, where movie tickets were heavily discounted to encourage theatergoing.

## Significance

The campaign marked a symbolic shift toward post-pandemic normalcy and government-led stimulation of the domestic economy. While international tourism recovery took time, the focus turned toward capturing local spending power. The long-term economic impact remains a subject of ongoing evaluation regarding whether short-term culinary events could structurally transform retail habits or sustainably boost consumption.

## Perspectives

- **Government Perspective**: Authorities viewed the campaign as a successful and timely intervention to boost local morale, encourage domestic consumption, and support local businesses recovering from the pandemic.
- **Critics and Analysts**: Some economists and commentators questioned the long-term effectiveness of short-term promotional events in addressing deeper structural economic challenges and weaker retail fundamentals.
