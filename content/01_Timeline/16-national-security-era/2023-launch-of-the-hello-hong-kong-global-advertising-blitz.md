## Background

Following several years of strict pandemic-related travel restrictions and the implementation of the National Security Law (香港特別行政區維護國家安全法) in 2020, Hong Kong's economy faced severe contraction. Tourism, retail, and aviation sectors experienced unprecedented downturns, leading to a decline in the city's international standing as a global financial and transit hub. By late 2022, as global travel resumed, local authorities sought methods to rebuild international confidence and attract visitors back to the city.

## What happened

On **February 2, 2023**, the Airport Authority Hong Kong (香港機場管理局) and the government officially launched the **'Hello Hong Kong'** (你好，香港) global promotional campaign at the Hong Kong Convention and Exhibition Centre (香港會議展覽中心). Presided over by Chief Executive **John Lee** (李家超), the campaign was backed by a massive budget allocated to international advertising, promotional events, and the distribution of approximately 500,000 free airline tickets to global travelers. The initiative was designed to showcase that the city had fully reopened and returned to the international stage.

## Significance

The campaign marked a pivotal symbolic and economic turning point for the territory, signaling the official end of pandemic isolation. While initial data showed a gradual return of regional and international visitors, analysts noted that the long-term recovery of Hong Kong's tourism and economic sectors would depend on a complex interplay of geopolitical factors, international perceptions, and shifting business environments under the evolving legal framework.

## Perspectives

- **Hong Kong Government and Pro-Establishment View**: Authorities and pro-establishment figures viewed the campaign as a resounding success in demonstrating government decisiveness, revitalizing the local economy, and confidently projecting Hong Kong's enduring status as Asia's world city.
- **International Observers and Critics**: Some international media and overseas commentators argued that despite generous giveaways, the lingering effects of the National Security Law and tightened political controls would continue to deter certain travelers and expatriate workers from returning long-term.
