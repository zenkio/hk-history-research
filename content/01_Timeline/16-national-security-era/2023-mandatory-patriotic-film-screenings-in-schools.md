## Background
Following the implementation of the **National Security Law** in 2020, the government of the **Hong Kong Special Administrative Region** (香港特別行政區) initiated a series of curriculum reforms intended to cultivate national awareness. The Education Bureau (**教育局**) sought to shift the focus of civic education, moving away from previous models that emphasized critical thinking and liberal studies towards a framework centered on national security and patriotic education. By the middle of **2023**, authorities emphasized that students should develop a deeper understanding of the history of the **People's Republic of China** (中華人民共和國) and the evolution of the **Chinese Communist Party** (中國共產黨).

## What happened
Around **July 1, 2023**, which coincided with the 26th anniversary of the Handover, reports indicated that schools were increasingly incorporating screenings of state-sanctioned patriotic films into their extracurricular and citizenship activities. These films often depicted historical milestones such as the **Second Sino-Japanese War** (中國抗日戰爭) or the technological achievements of modern China. While some schools had previously screened such content voluntarily, the new guidance encouraged a more systematic approach. Teachers were provided with resources and discussion guides to facilitate post-screening reflections, ensuring that the viewing experience aligned with the bureau's guidelines on national education.

## Significance
The introduction of mandatory screenings represents a key shift in the pedagogical landscape of Hong Kong. For proponents within the government, these screenings are viewed as a necessary step to correct what they characterize as historical omissions in the colonial-era education system and to instill a sense of pride and loyalty to the nation. Conversely, critics and some educators have raised concerns regarding the potential narrowing of academic discourse, arguing that the films prioritize a singular, state-sanctioned historical narrative. The long-term impact on student identity and the political atmosphere within classrooms remains a subject of ongoing discussion among sociologists and policy analysts.

## Perspectives

- **Government and Pro-Establishment Supporters**: Supporters argue that these measures are essential for rectifying historical misconceptions and fostering a cohesive national identity among the younger generation. They believe that understanding the nation's struggles and development is a fundamental responsibility of citizenship.
- **Critics and Education Activists**: Critics contend that mandatory screenings risk stifling critical inquiry and represent an attempt to enforce a monochromatic view of history. They worry that replacing open debate with state-sponsored narratives may discourage students from exploring diverse historical perspectives.
