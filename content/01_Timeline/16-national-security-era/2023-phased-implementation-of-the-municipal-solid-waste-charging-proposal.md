## Background

For decades, Hong Kong (香港) grappled with mounting pressure on its landfills. Proposals to introduce a municipal solid waste charging scheme had been debated through successive administrations under the Environmental Protection Department (環境保護署). The policy intended to harness the 'polluter pays' principle to encourage waste reduction and recycling among residents and businesses. However, prior legislative efforts stalled due to concerns over economic burdens, administrative complexity, and public readiness.

## What happened

On **July 13, 2023**, the Legislative Council (立法會) formally passed the enabling legislation to advance the phased implementation of the municipal solid waste charging proposal. The framework required citizens to purchase government-approved designated garbage bags or labels for waste disposal. While the policy was originally scheduled for roll-out, practical challenges, logistical readiness of recycling networks, and widespread public discussion regarding its implementation timeline led authorities to continuously evaluate and adjust the launch schedule amidst changing socio-economic conditions in the National Security Law era.

## Significance

The legislative approval marked a watershed moment in Hong Kong's environmental governance, transitioning from voluntary recycling to mandatory economic incentives. It brought issues of urban sustainability, civic responsibility, and infrastructural capacity to the forefront of public discourse. The policy highlighted the complexities of shifting social habits in a densely populated metropolis, serving as a critical case study for policymakers balancing ecological imperatives with public acceptance.

## Perspectives

- **Environmental advocates and government officials**: Proponents argued that charging for waste by volume is an indispensable economic tool to alter consumption patterns, reduce landfill strain, and align Hong Kong with international sustainability standards.
- **Community groups and affected industries**: Critics and grassroots representatives raised concerns about the financial impact on lower-income households, the sufficiency of recycling infrastructure, and whether the punitive mechanism addressed systemic waste issues effectively.
