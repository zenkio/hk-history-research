## Background
Hong Kong's subtropical climate makes it prone to heavy seasonal rainfall, typically managed by a robust drainage and civil engineering infrastructure developed over decades. Prior to **September 2023**, the highest hourly rainfall record was set on October 16, **1899**, at 158.1 millimeters. The SAR government and the Hong Kong Observatory maintain rigorous typhoon and rainstorm warning systems to mitigate disaster risks in the densely populated urban landscape.

## What happened
On the night of **September 7, 2023**, the remnants of Typhoon Haikui brought torrential rain to the region. The Hong Kong Observatory recorded 158.1 millimeters of rainfall in a single hour between 11:00 PM and midnight on **September 7**, matching the all-time hourly record from 1899. By the early hours of **September 8**, over 600 millimeters of rain had fallen in parts of Hong Kong Island, Kowloon, and the New Territories within 24 hours—amounting to roughly a quarter of the city's annual average precipitation.

The deluge overwhelmed the drainage systems, leading to severe and rapid urban flooding. Major thoroughfares, MTR stations—most notably Wong Tai Sin station—and underground tunnels were inundated with rushing muddy water. Landslides blocked several roads, particularly on Hong Kong Island and in outlying districts, while authorities issued widespread emergency warnings. The government announced 'extreme conditions' status, effectively shutting down schools, non-essential public services, and financial markets for parts of the day.

## Significance
The event severely tested the resilience of Hong Kong's civil infrastructure and emergency response mechanisms under extreme weather events intensified by global climate change. It prompted renewed public debate regarding urban planning, drainage capacity, and climate adaptation strategies. While the infrastructure largely prevented catastrophic structural failures, the disruption highlighted the increasing vulnerability of modern metropolises to unprecedented meteorological phenomena.

## Perspectives

- **Government and Infrastructure Authorities**: Officials emphasized that the rainfall was a 1-in-500-year event that exceeded the designed capacity of the drainage system, arguing that the rapid response and containment prevented greater loss of life and structural collapse.
- **Civil Society and Environmental Analysts**: Critics and environmental groups pointed to rapid urban development, aging drainage infrastructure, and the accelerating impacts of climate change as critical factors that exacerbated the severity of the flooding beyond mere natural causes.
