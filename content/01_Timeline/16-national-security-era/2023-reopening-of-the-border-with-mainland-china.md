## Background
Following the emergence of the COVID-19 pandemic in early **2020**, Hong Kong implemented some of the world's strictest quarantine, travel restriction, and border control measures. These policies largely severed routine cross-border movement between Hong Kong and mainland China, deeply impacting families, tourism, cross-border workers, and the local retail and transport sectors. During the latter half of **2022**, as mainland China began shifting away from its zero-COVID policy, discussions accelerated regarding the resumption of normal travel between Hong Kong and the mainland.

## What happened
On **January 8, 2023**, the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region government formally lifted quarantine requirements for travelers crossing the border into mainland China. An initial daily quota system was established for land boundary control points, such as Lok Ma Chau (落馬洲) and Shenzhen Bay (深圳灣), requiring travelers to make advance online reservations. Control points including the Hong Kong West Kowloon Railway Station (香港西九龍站) resumed high-speed rail services to mainland destinations shortly thereafter. Gradually, quotas were expanded and eventually eliminated as epidemic conditions stabilized and processing capacity normalized.

## Significance
The reopening marked a pivotal turning point for Hong Kong's post-pandemic recovery, restoring vital economic and social links with the mainland. For the business community and tourism sector, the return of mainland visitors was viewed as a critical step toward economic revitalization. Simultaneously, the resumption of unhindered cross-border travel facilitated the reunification of families separated for years, though it also brought logistical challenges related to public health monitoring and transport capacity.

## Perspectives

- **Pro-establishment and business community**: The reopening was welcomed as an absolute necessity for economic survival, restoring essential supply chains, financial flows, and tourism revenue that are vital to Hong Kong's status as an international financial center.
- **Local residents and families**: The policy change brought immense relief to families and cross-border workers who had endured years of separation, though initial booking systems and health declaration requirements caused temporary confusion and frustration.
