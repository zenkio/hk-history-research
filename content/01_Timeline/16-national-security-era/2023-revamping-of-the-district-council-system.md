## Background
For decades, the District Councils (區議會) served as the primary grassroots advisory bodies in Hong Kong. Following the 2019 District Council elections, which saw a landslide victory for the pro-democracy camp amidst the anti-extradition bill protests, the political composition of these councils shifted dramatically. Following the implementation of the National Security Law (香港國安法) in **2020**, many council members resigned or were disqualified due to oath-taking requirements introduced under the Public Offices (Candidacy and Taking Up Offices) (Miscellaneous Amendments) Ordinance **2021**.

## What happened
On **May 2, 2023**, the Chief Executive **John Lee** (李家超) announced the "Improving Governance at the District Level" proposal. The reform reduced the proportion of directly elected seats from over 90% to approximately 20%. The remaining seats were allocated to members appointed by the Chief Executive, members elected by District Committees (分區委員會), Area Committees (地區撲滅罪行委員會), and Fire Safety Committees (分區滅罪/防火委員會), as well as ex-officio members representing rural committees (鄉事委員會). Furthermore, a new qualification review mechanism was established to ensure that all candidates uphold the Basic Law (基本法) and pledge allegiance to the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region.

## Significance
This reform fundamentally altered the institutional landscape of local governance. By emphasizing a "patriots administering Hong Kong" (愛國者治港) principle, the government sought to depoliticize the District Councils, refocusing them on providing advisory services and community support rather than functioning as platforms for political advocacy. Critics argue this has removed the avenue for public participation in local democracy, while the government maintains it has restored the councils to their original intended function as advisory bodies, ensuring stability and effective communication between the administration and the public.

## Perspectives

- **The HKSAR Government and Beijing**: Authorities contend that the reform was necessary to correct the "chaos" caused by the previous political environment. They argue that the new structure ensures that District Councils remain focused on livelihood issues and prevents them from being used to incite social unrest or undermine national security.
- **Pro-democracy figures and civil society critics**: Critics view the reform as the dismantling of the final vestige of democratic representation in local government. They argue that the vetting process and the reduction of direct elections effectively exclude opposition voices, turning the councils into government-aligned bodies that lack genuine democratic legitimacy.
