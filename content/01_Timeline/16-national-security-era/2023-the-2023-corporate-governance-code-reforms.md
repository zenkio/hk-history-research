## Background
Following the implementation of the **National Security Law (NSL)** in 2020, the Hong Kong government and financial regulators sought to stabilize the city's status as a premier global financial hub. Amidst shifts in the geopolitical climate and economic uncertainty, regulators aimed to align Hong Kong's listing rules with international standards, particularly those regarding environmental, social, and governance (ESG) practices and corporate oversight.

## What happened
Effective **January 1, 2023**, the **Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing (HKEX)** mandated several updates to the Corporate Governance Code. Key reforms included the removal of 'gender-exclusive' boards, requiring all listed companies to appoint at least one director of a different gender. Furthermore, the rules introduced tighter requirements regarding the independence of long-serving independent non-executive directors (INEDs), specifically limiting the tenure of directors who have served on a board for more than nine years to ensure board renewal and objectivity. The reforms also emphasized the necessity for boards to maintain a clear policy on diversity and to disclose the progress toward achieving those diversity objectives annually.

## Significance
The reforms represented a concerted effort to modernize Hong Kong's business environment. By pushing for more diverse and independent leadership, regulators intended to bolster investor confidence and strengthen the resilience of listed entities. While the reforms were positioned as a technical enhancement of market quality, they also occurred within a broader framework of legislative and regulatory tightening overseen by the **Securities and Futures Commission (SFC)**. The changes reflected the ongoing dual focus on maintaining the city's international competitiveness while integrating more strictly into the regulatory expectations established in the post-2020 era.

## Perspectives

- **Financial Regulators and Pro-Beijing Analysts**: Regulators and proponents view these reforms as essential steps to align Hong Kong with global best practices, enhancing corporate accountability and attractiveness to international institutional investors. They argue that these measures demonstrate the maturity and adaptability of Hong Kong's regulatory framework under the new political order.
- **Liberal Critics and Market Observers**: Some market observers suggest that while the reforms are technically sound, they are overshadowed by the broader political climate impacting the city's rule of law. Critics question whether procedural governance improvements can effectively offset the systemic risks perceived by foreign investors following the implementation of the National Security Law.
