## Background

Following the prolonged border closures associated with the COVID-19 pandemic, travel restrictions between Hong Kong and mainland China were lifted in early **2023**. Over the ensuing months, the depreciation of the renminbi relative to the Hong Kong dollar, combined with the perceived higher value, broader variety, and superior service standards of mainland commercial establishments, created strong economic incentives for cross-border travel. Improved transportation infrastructure, such as the Guangzhou-Shenzhen-Hong Kong Express Rail Link and various land boundary control points, further facilitated movement between the two territories.

## What happened

By late **2023**, particularly around major holiday periods such as Christmas and the New Year, weekend and holiday outflows of Hong Kong residents to Shenzhen (深圳) reached record highs. Popular destinations included mega-malls in districts like Luohu (羅湖) and Futian (福田), where consumers engaged in routine activities ranging from grocery shopping to fine dining. Concurrently, local Hong Kong retail and catering businesses experienced a noticeable drop in weekend patronage, leading to diminished revenues for many small and medium-sized enterprises. Media reports and industry associations highlighted widespread weekend closures or reduced operating hours among local eateries facing high rents and labor shortages alongside declining foot traffic.

## Significance

The northbound consumption trend marked a notable shift in post-pandemic consumer behavior in Hong Kong. While cross-border integration had deepened steadily since the handover in **1997**, the sheer scale and demographic breadth of this retail migration—encompassing not only youth and cross-border families, but middle-class households as well—surprised many economic analysts. It prompted urgent debates among business owners, economists, and government officials regarding the structural competitiveness of Hong Kong's domestic service economy and the necessity of economic upgrading.

## Perspectives

- **Local Retail and Catering Sector**: Business owners and industry representatives view the trend as a severe crisis driven by high local operational costs, including exorbitant commercial rents, and labor shortages. They argue that structural disadvantages make it difficult to compete with mainland service providers on price and scale.
- **Economists and Policy Analysts**: Some analysts interpret the trend as a natural outcome of regional economic integration and market correction within the Greater Bay Area (大灣區). They suggest it highlights the need for Hong Kong businesses to pivot toward higher-value services and innovation rather than relying on traditional retail models.
