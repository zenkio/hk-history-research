## Background

Following the mass protests of **2019**, the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress enacted the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (National Security Law) on **June 30, 2020**. In **July 2020**, various pro-democracy figures organized an unofficial primary election to select candidates for the upcoming Legislative Council election, aiming to secure a legislative majority to veto government budgets. Authorities argued that this plan constituted an illegal scheme to paralyze the government and subvert state power.

## What happened

On **February 6, 2023**, the trial of the **Hong Kong 47** opened at the West Kowloon Magistrates' Courts. Out of the 47 defendants, **31** had pleaded guilty prior to the trial, while **16** maintained their innocence. The trial proceeded before a panel of three handpicked national security judges rather than a traditional jury, a departure from common law traditions explicitly authorized by the National Security Law. The courtroom was packed with diplomats, journalists, and members of the public, reflecting immense local and international interest.

## Significance

The trial represents the largest single prosecution under the National Security Law to date. It effectively targeted the leadership and organizing core of Hong Kong's institutional opposition movement. The proceedings served as a focal point for international debates regarding the erosion of judicial independence, civil liberties, and the autonomy promised under the Sino-British Joint Declaration, while Beijing and local authorities maintained that the prosecutions were a necessary defense of national sovereignty and the rule of law.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and Hong Kong Governments**: Authorities maintain that the primary election was a coordinated attempt to subvert state power and paralyze the government, and that the prosecutions are a lawful enforcement of the National Security Law to restore public order.
- **Democracy Advocates and International Observers**: Critics and foreign governments view the mass trial as a severe blow to political pluralism, freedom of association, and the rule of law, arguing that participating in an election primary is a normal political activity.
