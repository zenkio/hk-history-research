## Background
Since the establishment of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR) in 1997, the Court of Final Appeal (終審法院) has included non-permanent judges from other common law jurisdictions. This practice was codified under the Basic Law (基本法) to maintain international confidence in the city’s judicial independence and to bring global legal expertise to Hong Kong’s jurisprudence. For decades, these judges were viewed as a key indicator of the 'one country, two systems' framework, bridging Hong Kong’s legal heritage with its new constitutional order.

## What happened
In **June 2024**, several high-profile overseas non-permanent judges, including **Lord Jonathan Sumption** and **Lord Lawrence Collins** from the United Kingdom, resigned from the Court of Final Appeal. Their departures followed the conclusion of the trial in the case of *HKSAR v. Benny Tai Yiu-ting and others*, which concerned charges related to the National Security Law (國家安全法). While some judges cited the political environment in Hong Kong as a reason for their departure, the HKSAR government maintained that the judicial system remains robust, highlighting that many other overseas judges have chosen to remain in their roles.

## Significance
The resignations have prompted intense debate regarding the future of Hong Kong's legal system. Proponents of the city’s current direction argue that the judiciary remains independent and that the departure of individual judges does not diminish the rule of law. Conversely, international observers and legal scholars have expressed concerns that these departures reflect a weakening of the judicial safeguards originally intended to protect civil liberties under the Basic Law. The event has become a focal point in broader discussions about the impact of the National Security Law on Hong Kong's status as a global financial and legal hub.

## Perspectives

- **HKSAR Government and Beijing authorities**: The government asserts that the judiciary remains entirely independent and that the departure of a small number of judges does not impact the city's rule of law. They emphasize that the National Security Law has restored stability and that Hong Kong continues to attract international legal talent.
- **International legal observers and civil society groups**: Many overseas legal commentators view the resignations as a warning sign of the erosion of judicial independence under the National Security Law. They argue that the departure of these judges removes a critical layer of international scrutiny and undermines the impartiality of the court system.
