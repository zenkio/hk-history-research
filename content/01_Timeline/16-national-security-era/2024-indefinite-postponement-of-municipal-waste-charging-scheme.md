## Background

The municipal solid waste charging scheme, commonly known as garbage charging (垃圾收費), was first proposed to encourage waste reduction and recycling by making residents pay for their trash based on volume or weight. The legislative process spanned several years, culminating in the passage of the Waste Disposal (Charging for Municipal Solid Waste) (Amendment) Ordinance in **August 2021**. The policy aimed to tackle Hong Kong's notoriously overburdened landfills and align the city with international environmental standards.

## What happened

Originally scheduled to take effect late 2023, the implementation date was postponed to **April 1, 2024**, and later pushed further to **August 1, 2024**. To test readiness, a 'tryout' scheme was launched on **April 1, 2024**, involving selected government buildings, residential estates, and catering premises. However, the trial phase revealed significant confusion among the public regarding designated garbage bags, operational costs, and enforcement logistics. On **May 27, 2024**, Chief Executive **John Lee** (李家超) and Secretary for Environment and Ecology **Tse Chin-wan** (謝展寰) announced that the implementation of the scheme would be indefinitely postponed, with the government shifting its immediate focus back to public education and expanding recycling infrastructure.

## Significance

The indefinite postponement marked a rare policy retreat for the administration under the National Security Law era, demonstrating the government's responsiveness to intense livelihood concerns and public feedback. While environmental groups expressed disappointment over the setback in green policies, business sectors and ordinary citizens largely welcomed the decision, citing economic pressures and logistical impracticalities during a period of broader post-pandemic economic readjustment.

## Perspectives

- **Government and Administrative Perspective**: Authorities emphasized that the postponement demonstrated a pragmatic and responsive governance style, prioritizing public understanding and consensus-building over rigid implementation timelines when faced with complex logistical hurdles.
- **Environmental and Civic Perspective**: Green groups and conservation advocates expressed concern that the delay undermined Hong Kong's long-term sustainability goals and delayed necessary cultural shifts toward waste reduction and producer responsibility.
