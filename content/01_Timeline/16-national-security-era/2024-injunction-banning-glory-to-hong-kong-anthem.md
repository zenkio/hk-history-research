## Background

Following the widespread protests in **2019**, the song **'Glory to Hong Kong' (願榮光歸香港)** emerged as a prominent anthem associated with the anti-extradition bill movement. The track was frequently played at public assemblies, international sporting events, and online platforms. In **June 2023**, the Department of Justice applied for an interim injunction to prohibit the broadcasting, performance, printing, or dissemination of the song and its adaptations. The initial application was refused by the Court of First Instance in **July 2023** on grounds relating to freedom of expression and the potential redundancy of the order given existing criminal laws.

## What happened

On **May 8, 2024**, the Court of Appeal overturned the lower court's decision and granted the government's request for an injunction. The appellate judges concluded that the original judge had erred in assessing the necessity and effectiveness of the injunction. The ruling prohibited any person from broadcasting, performing, publishing, selling, or sharing the song with the intent to incite secession or with the intention of misrepresenting it as the national anthem of the HKSAR. Following the court's decision, major streaming platforms and digital stores began restricting access to various versions of the song within the Hong Kong jurisdiction.

## Significance

This legal action represents the first time a specific musical composition has been legally banned in Hong Kong since the handover in **1997**. It highlights the ongoing legal and judicial measures implemented following the enactment of the **National Security Law** in **2020** to curb perceived threats to national security. The ruling prompted widespread international debate regarding the balance between national security imperatives and freedom of speech, with technology companies navigating complex compliance issues concerning regional internet restrictions.

- **Government and Pro-Establishment View**: Authorities and supporting legal scholars argued that the injunction was a necessary measure to safeguard national security and prevent the misuse of a protest song to incite secession, defame the national anthem, or undermine public order.
