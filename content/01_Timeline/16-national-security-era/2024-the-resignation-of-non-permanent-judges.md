## Background
Since the handover of Hong Kong in 1997, the Court of Final Appeal (終審法院) has maintained a unique tradition of inviting prominent jurists from other common law jurisdictions to serve as Non-Permanent Judges (NPJs). This practice was intended to bolster international confidence in the impartiality of the judiciary and ensure the city’s legal system remained aligned with global common law standards. Following the implementation of the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (**2020-06-30**), the legal landscape underwent significant structural changes, leading to increased international scrutiny regarding the autonomy of the courts.

## What happened
On **2024-06-11**, the Hong Kong government announced that two prominent British judges, **Lord Sumption** and **Lord Collins**, had resigned from their positions as NPJs of the Court of Final Appeal. Their departures followed the conviction of 14 pro-democracy activists in a landmark national security case. While **Lord Collins** cited the "political situation" in Hong Kong as the reason for his departure, **Lord Sumption** subsequently authored an article in the Financial Times arguing that the rule of law was in "grave danger" due to the pressure exerted on the courts by the political environment. Shortly thereafter, the departure of the Chief Justice of the Supreme Court of Canada, **Beverley McLachlin**, was also confirmed, though she stated her departure was due to her reaching the end of her term.

## Significance
The resignation of these senior jurists is widely viewed by legal scholars as a turning point for the city’s judicial reputation. The presence of overseas judges was long considered a hallmark of the "One Country, Two Systems" (一國兩制) framework. Their departures prompted a broader debate about whether the common law system can effectively coexist with the requirements of the National Security Law. For the HKSAR government, these resignations were described as a loss for the judges themselves rather than a reflection of the court's integrity. Conversely, international observers and various legal professional bodies expressed concern that the trend of resignations might signal a retreat from the judicial independence guaranteed under the Basic Law (基本法).

## Perspectives

- **HKSAR and Beijing Authorities**: The government maintains that the judicial system remains robust and that national security laws are necessary to restore social stability. They argue that individual judges' decisions to resign are personal choices and that the court's overall integrity remains unaffected by the departure of international figures.
- **International Legal Critics**: Critics argue that the legal environment has become increasingly hostile to political dissent, effectively chilling the court's capacity to uphold fundamental rights. From this perspective, the resignations demonstrate an incompatibility between the National Security Law and the independent exercise of the common law tradition.
