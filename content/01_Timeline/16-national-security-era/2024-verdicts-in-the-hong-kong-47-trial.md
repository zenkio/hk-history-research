## Background

Following the widespread protests of **2019**, Beijing enacted the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (中華人民共和國香港特別行政區維護國家安全法) in June **2020**. In **July 2020**, the pro-democracy camp held an unofficial primary election to select candidates for the Legislative Council (立法會) election, aiming to secure a legislative majority to veto government budgets and force political concessions.

Authorities subsequently arrested **47** prominent politicians, academics, and activists, arguing that the primary strategy constituted an illegal attempt to subvert state power. The defendants were charged with conspiracy to commit subversion under the National Security Law.

## What happened

On **May 30, 2024**, a panel of three handpicked national security judges delivered verdicts in the West Kowloon Magistrates' Courts (西九龍裁判法院). Out of the **47** defendants, **14** were convicted, **2** were acquitted, and the remaining individuals had either previously pleaded guilty or were awaiting sentencing outcomes. The trial, which began in **February 2023**, lasted for more than **100** days and became a focal point for international scrutiny regarding the rule of law and judicial independence in Hong Kong.

## Significance

Legal scholars and international observers view the verdicts as a watershed moment in Hong Kong's legal and political history, effectively dismantling the organized opposition camp. The outcome highlights the profound shift in the territory's governance model following the implementation of the National Security Law, altering the framework of political participation and dissent.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and Hong Kong Governments**: Authorities maintain that the National Security Law is vital for restoring social stability and public order following the turmoil of 2019. They argue that the primary election was a coordinated scheme to paralyze the government and subvert state power, and that the judiciary acted independently and lawfully in rendering its verdicts.
- **Pro-democracy Supporters and Western Governments**: Critics and international governments argue that the prosecution and conviction of the activists criminalize normal political opposition and peaceful participation in elections. They contend that the verdicts signal a severe erosion of civil liberties, freedom of speech, and the independence of Hong Kong's judicial system.
