## Overview

The National Security Law era, beginning in **2020**, marks one of the most profound periods of political and social transformation in modern Hong Kong history. Following the widespread social unrest of **2019**, the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress enacted the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region on **June 30, 2020**. This legislative act, alongside subsequent electoral overhauls and the enactment of local security legislation under **Article 23**, significantly altered the governance landscape of the territory.

Throughout this period, civil society underwent rapid restructuring. Numerous political organizations, trade unions, and civil rights groups dissolved. Prominent media outlets, including **Apple Daily** and **Stand News**, ceased operations following law enforcement raids and arrests under sedition and national security charges. Legal and political institutions were reorganized, notably through the **2021** electoral reforms which introduced a vetting system for candidates to ensure governance by "patriots." High-profile legal proceedings, such as the trial of the **Hong Kong 47** pro-democracy activists, proceeded under new judicial frameworks.

## Key themes

Legal and institutional transformation stands as the central theme of the era. The implementation of the National Security Law introduced new criminal offenses—secession, subversion, terrorism, and collusion with foreign forces—and established specialized law enforcement and judicial mechanisms. Simultaneously, electoral systems were revised to reduce the proportion of directly elected seats, culminating in the **2023** District Council elections, which recorded the lowest voter turnout in the history of the SAR.

Public health crises also intersected with political changes. The **fifth wave of COVID-19** in early **2022** severely overwhelmed the city's healthcare infrastructure, leading to stringent containment measures before travel restrictions were lifted and the border with **Mainland China** reopened. Economic and social life faced further challenges, marked by events such as the **Mirror concert accident** in **2022** and extreme weather incidents, including record-breaking rainfall and flooding in **2023**.

## How it shaped Hong Kong

The cumulative effect of these developments has been a redefinition of Hong Kong's domestic environment and international standing. Proponents argue that the security laws and administrative reforms restored social stability, ended prolonged disruption, and ensured the principle of "patriots administering Hong Kong" (愛國者治港). Conversely, critics and various international observers contend that these measures severely curtailed civil liberties, freedom of the press, and political pluralism, leading to the emigration of numerous residents and shifts in the city's role as an international financial center.

## Perspectives

- **Beijing and the Hong Kong Government**: Authorities maintain that the National Security Law and subsequent electoral reforms were essential to restore public order, protect national sovereignty, and close legal loopholes after the instability of 2019. They argue these measures provide a stable environment necessary for Hong Kong's long-term economic prosperity and governance under the "One Country, Two Systems" framework.
- **Pro-Democracy and International Critics**: Critics, including various human rights organizations and overseas governments, argue that the sweeping security legislation and political overhauls dismantled Hong Kong's high degree of autonomy and fundamental freedoms. They contend these changes eroded judicial independence and suppressed peaceful political opposition contrary to international covenants.
