What's new and updated on the Gwulo website: General Thank you to everyone who attended my recent talks in York and Edinburgh. The audience was a good mix of long-term followers of Gwulo, and people who have a connection to Hong Kong but are new to the site. It was a pleasure to meet you all. After our visit to the North East, next month we're heading in the opposite direction, to give talks in Cheltenham and Exeter. Please use the following links for booking and details, and I look forward to seeing you there. Wed. 7 Oct, Gwulo talk in Cheltenham Sat. 10 Oct, Gwulo talk in Exeter The project to put Leo Landau's Prisoner Of War diary online is going well. Volunteers have already processed the pages from 1942 and 1943, and are making good progress with 1944. If you'd like to help, please reply to this newsletter by email with DIARY in the subject line, and I'll send you the files for the next page in the queue. Read the diary pages from 1941-43 See the steps required to help process a scanned page Recent news in the wartime diaries: 1942 25 Aug : The MS Gripsholm repatriation ship sails in to New York Harbour. It has been almost two months since the Americans left Japanese-occupied Hong Kong, but finally they are home. 8 Sep : The Japanese release the Chinese members of the HKVDC from the Sham Shui Po POW camp. (Eric MacNider in Stanley Camp writes about it later in his diary on 17 Sep: ‘124 Chinese, Malay & Annamite HKVDC men released from P.O.W. camp by Japs.’) 1945 30 Aug : The Allied fleet sails into Victoria Harbour. Admiral Harcourt visits Stanley Internment Camp where he gives a short speech, and there is a flag-raising ceremony. 11 Sep 1945 : The internees in Hong Kong and the POWs in Japan begin their long journey home. 16 Sep : The Japanese officials in Hong Kong sign their surrender in a ceremony at Government House. In the evening, ships in the harbour gave a fireworks display in celebration. While preparing for the Edinburgh talk I discovered not only did famous photographer John Thomson grow up in Edinburgh, but that his childhood home was just five minutes walk from our venue, at 6 Brighton Road. I went along to pay homage, and as you'll see in the photo below, there is a brass plaque on the wall commemorating him. It turns out that plaque is there because of the good work of Jamie Carstairs - he has written up how he tracked down Thomson's house and got the plaque installed .
