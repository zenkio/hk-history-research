The **Kowloon-Canton Railway** project was conceived to secure Hong Kong’s status as a primary trade hub by connecting it to the trunk line leading to Peking. According to an article from the **Far Eastern Review**, the **British and Chinese Corporation** initially held a concession granted in **1898** but failed to make progress, prompting the government and commercial interests of Hong Kong to intervene. By **1905**, the Hong Kong government moved to construct the line through British territory, including extensive earthworks, tunnels, and bridge construction. The project was significantly facilitated by a financial arrangement where Hong Kong loaned money to the Chinese government, effectively ensuring traffic connectivity and mitigating competitive threats from potential deep-water ports in Canton. Construction efforts in the British section officially commenced with embankment work and surveys completed in the autumn of **1905**.
