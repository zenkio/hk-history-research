On **9 September 2026**, the **Hong Kong History Centre** officially launched an open-access digital platform titled "Historical Photographs of Hong Kong." This initiative serves as a centralized repository for digitized slides, prints, and negatives that have been donated to the **Hong Kong Collections** held at the **University of Bristol Library**. The platform was developed to increase public and academic access to visual materials documenting Hong Kong's past. According to the centre, the project aims to preserve historical records through digitization and make them available for researchers and the general public. Accompanying the launch, the **Hong Kong History Centre** published an introductory blog post detailing the motivation behind the collection, its operational framework, and the scope of the archival efforts. The platform is accessible at hphkbristol.net. Additionally, the centre released its **2026 Impact Report**, which outlines the organization's activities and contributions to historical research during that year. The materials archived include various depictions of urban life, such as street markets featuring mid-to-late 20th-century attire and architecture, reflecting the broader scope of the collection.

## Historical context

> [!note] General background (AI, not from the source)

The digitization of archival materials is a critical component of contemporary historical preservation in Hong Kong. By utilizing institutional support from entities like the University of Bristol, these projects safeguard visual heritage that might otherwise deteriorate or remain inaccessible to the public. Such initiatives facilitate ongoing scholarly examination of Hong Kong's social and urban evolution throughout the 20th century.
