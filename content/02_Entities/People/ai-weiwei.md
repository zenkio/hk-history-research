## Introduction and Background
**Ai Weiwei** (艾未未) is an internationally recognized conceptual artist, sculptor, and human rights activist based internationally. Born in **1957**, his artistic practice often critiques government transparency, human rights abuses, and political authoritarianism, making his work both celebrated globally and politically sensitive in mainland China.

## Connection to the M+ Museum
Ai Weiwei's relationship with Hong Kong is prominently anchored in the development of the **M+ Museum** (M+博物館), a major visual culture museum located in the **West Kowloon Cultural District** (西九文化區). In **2012**, prominent Hong Kong collector **Uli Sigg** (希克) donated a vast collection of contemporary Chinese art to M+, which included several key works by Ai, such as his photographic series *Study in Perspective* (透視研究), where the artist gives the middle finger to significant landmarks, including Tiananmen Square and the **Hong Kong Convention and Exhibition Centre** (香港會議展覽中心).

## Censorship and Political Controversy
As political tensions shifted in Hong Kong following the passage of the **National Security Law** (香港國安法) in **2020**, Ai's works and presence in the city became subjects of intense debate. Pro-Beijing politicians and media outlets targeted the museum in **2021**, accusing some of Ai's artworks of violating the law and insulting China. Conversely, free-speech advocates and local art communities defended the inclusion of his works as essential to contemporary artistic expression and museum independence. M+ leadership ultimately stated that while some pieces would not be displayed immediately upon opening, the museum operates within the bounds of Hong Kong law while striving to maintain curatorial integrity.
