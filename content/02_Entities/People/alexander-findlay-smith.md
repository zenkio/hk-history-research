## Role in the Peak Tram
**Alexander Findlay Smith** arrived in Hong Kong in the late 19th century and is most noted for his leadership in the development of the Peak Tram. Recognizing the difficulty of transport for the growing expatriate community residing on Victoria Peak, **Smith** secured a government franchise to construct a funicular railway. He became the driving force behind the **Peak Tramway Company**, which successfully inaugurated the service on **May 30, 1888**.

## Historical Significance and Perspectives
From a British colonial perspective, **Smith** is often viewed as a pioneer of infrastructure who facilitated the expansion of the city beyond the harborfront and established the Peak as a prestigious residential district for the colonial elite. Conversely, later scholarship often emphasizes the social implications of **Smith**'s project. The establishment of the Peak Tram helped codify the segregation of the colonial population; the Peak became an exclusionary enclave for wealthy Europeans, physically separated from the Chinese population living in the congested city below. While the tram was a feat of engineering, it also served as a symbol of the rigid class and racial hierarchy that characterized Hong Kong under colonial administration.
