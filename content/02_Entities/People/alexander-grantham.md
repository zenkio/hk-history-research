## Governor of Hong Kong

Sir **Alexander Grantham** (葛量洪) served as Governor of Hong Kong from **1947** to **1957**. His tenure coincided with one of the most transformative decades in Hong Kong's history, marked by geopolitical upheaval in mainland China and rapid industrialization and population growth within the territory.

## Mass Refugee Influx and Housing

During the late **1940s** and early **1950s**, the victory of the Chinese Communist Party in the Chinese Civil War prompted hundreds of thousands of refugees to flee into British Hong Kong. This unprecedented migration strained local resources and created massive informal squatter settlements across the urban fringe.

Following the catastrophic **Shek Kip Mei fire** on **Christmas Day 1953**, which left over **50,000** people homeless, Grantham's administration fundamentally shifted colonial policy. Abandoning the previous laissez-faire approach to housing, the government initiated a massive resettlement program and established the **Hong Kong Housing Authority** in **1954** to build permanent multi-story public housing estates.

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of Grantham's governorship vary across different historiographical traditions. British colonial accounts typically portray him as a pragmatic and decisive administrator who successfully transformed crisis into modernization. Later critical scholarship, however, notes that while the public housing program was a major milestone, it was primarily driven by immediate security concerns, social stability, and the urgent need to clear land for economic development rather than pure welfarism. Local communities and mainland perspectives often emphasize how this era cemented Hong Kong's distinct socio-political identity as separate from mainland China while remaining deeply entangled in its geopolitical refugees.
