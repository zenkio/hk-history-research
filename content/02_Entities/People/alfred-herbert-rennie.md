## Business Ventures in Hong Kong
**Alfred Herbert Rennie** (艾爾弗雷德·赫伯特·倫尼) was a Canadian businessman who arrived in Hong Kong at the turn of the 20th century. He is primarily remembered for his significant, albeit short-lived, industrial initiative: the establishment of the Hong Kong Milling Company. In **1905**, **Rennie** constructed a large flour mill at a site then known as Tseng Lan Shue, located in the bay area later renamed Tiu Keng Leng (調景嶺) in his honor.

## The Flour Mill and Legacy
The mill was intended to compete with imported flour, representing an early attempt at local industrialization. However, the business faced severe financial difficulties. The company struggled against established competitors and shifting market conditions. By **1908**, the business failed, leading to **Rennie**'s sudden death, which was widely reported as a suicide. Following his death, the facility was abandoned, and the structures eventually fell into decay.

## Historical Interpretations
The legacy of the site remains complex. To early 20th-century colonial authorities and commercial interests, the mill was a failed industrial venture. Later, however, the site took on a new significance following the **1949** Chinese Communist Revolution. When thousands of Nationalist soldiers and their families fled to Hong Kong, they were settled in the derelict area formerly owned by **Rennie**. 

This led to a dual historical narrative: 
- **Colonial/Official perspective:** The site was viewed as a temporary refugee camp that required management and containment.
- **Local/Community perspective:** The residents of Tiu Keng Leng—often referred to by the Cantonese nickname 'Rennie's Mill'—developed a strong, distinct identity rooted in their anti-communist political alignment and communal life, which persisted until the area's urban redevelopment in the **1990s**. 

While **Rennie** himself had no connection to the later refugee community, the phonetic adaptation of his name and the physical remains of his failed mill provided the foundational geography for a defining chapter in Hong Kong's Cold War history.
