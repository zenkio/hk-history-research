## The Flour Mill Venture
**Alfred Rennie** (1857–1908) was a Canadian businessman who arrived in Hong Kong in **1905** with the ambition of establishing a large-scale flour milling industry in the region. He selected a coastal area in the Junk Bay (將軍澳) district, which subsequently became known as Rennie’s Mill (調景嶺). Rennie invested significant capital into the construction of the Hong Kong Milling Company (香港麵粉廠), aiming to capitalize on the local demand for flour.

## The Collapse and Legacy
The venture proved to be a commercial failure. By **1908**, the company faced severe financial difficulties due to poor quality control, competition from established flour imports, and technical challenges. In **April 1908**, shortly after the business declared bankruptcy, **Alfred Rennie** died. While some contemporary accounts attributed his death to suicide by drowning, the exact circumstances remain a subject of historical debate. Following the closure of the mill, the site remained largely undeveloped for decades.

## Significance to Hong Kong History
While **Rennie** himself was only a brief figure in Hong Kong's economic history, his name became permanently etched into the local landscape. Following the **1949** Chinese Communist Revolution, the abandoned site became the location of a large-scale refugee camp for former Kuomintang (國民黨) soldiers and their families. To the colonial government, the settlement was a temporary housing issue that required management; to the residents, it was a bastion of political identity. For local Hong Kong communities, the area—long referred to as Rennie’s Mill—became a symbol of the Cold War divide. The name persisted until the **1990s**, when the area was cleared for the development of the Tseung Kwan O New Town (將軍澳新市鎮). Despite the demolition of the camp and the official renaming of the area to Tiu Keng Leng (調景嶺), the historical moniker remains a primary point of reference for those studying the social and political complexities of post-war Hong Kong.
