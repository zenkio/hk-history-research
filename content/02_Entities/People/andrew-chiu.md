## Early Political Career
**Andrew Chiu** (趙家賢) served as a prominent member of the Democratic Party and was elected to the Eastern District Council in 2007, representing the Tai Koo Shing West constituency. He served as the convener of the Power for Democracy coalition, an organization that coordinated pro-democracy candidates during local elections.

## The 2020 Primaries and Arrest
In July **2020**, **Chiu** was a key organizer of the unofficial primary election held by the pro-democracy camp to select candidates for the subsequently postponed Legislative Council election. This event became the focus of a major legal challenge by the authorities, who argued that the coordination of such primaries sought to paralyze the government through mass vetoes of the budget, a move deemed to violate the **National Security Law**.

On **January 6, 2021**, **Chiu** was among the 53 activists and politicians arrested by the National Security Department of the Hong Kong Police Force. He was later charged with conspiracy to commit subversion. During his detention, **Chiu** became a prosecution witness in the high-profile *Hong Kong 47* trial, providing testimony regarding the internal deliberations and strategy of the organizers.

## Historical Perspectives
Interpretations of **Chiu**’s actions and the broader primary movement remain divided. Supporters of the democratic movement characterize the primary as a peaceful, civic exercise in political expression aimed at gauging public opinion within the constraints of the Basic Law. Conversely, the Hong Kong government and Beijing officials maintain that the primary was an illegal attempt to subvert state power and undermine the stability of the HKSAR government. Later scholarship often examines the case as a critical inflection point in the shift of Hong Kong’s political landscape following the implementation of the **National Security Law** in **2020**.
