## Early Career and Colonial Administration

**Anson Chan** (陳方安生) joined the Hong Kong government in **1962** as an administrative officer, rising steadily through the ranks. In **1993**, she achieved a historic milestone by becoming the first local Chinese and the first female to hold the position of Chief Secretary, the highest civil service post under British colonial rule. Known for her staunch defense of the civil service's integrity and the rule of law, she earned respect from both local residents and international observers.

## The Handover and Tung Chee-hwa Administration

Following the transfer of sovereignty in **1997**, Chan remained in office as Chief Secretary under the Special Administrative Region's first Chief Executive, **Tung Chee-hwa** (董建華). Her presence was widely interpreted by local and international commentators as a stabilizing factor that reassured the public and global markets regarding the continuity of Hong Kong's governance and autonomy.

However, political tensions gradually emerged between Chan and the Beijing-backed administration. Interpretations of her departure differ significantly; pro-democracy advocates viewed her early resignation in **2001** as a principled stand against mainland interference and administrative friction with Tung, while Beijing loyalists and other observers attributed it to policy disagreements and a natural turnover in leadership.

## Later Political Activity

In retirement, Chan remained a visible figure in Hong Kong public life. She briefly returned to the Legislative Council after winning a **2007** by-election, subsequently focusing her efforts on advocating for universal suffrage and constitutional development. While praised by pro-democracy supporters for her commitment to Hong Kong's autonomy, critics from pro-establishment circles accused her of aligning with foreign interests to challenge central authorities.
