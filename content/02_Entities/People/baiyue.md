## Origins and Identity
The term **Baiyue** (百越), meaning "the hundred Yue," refers to a loose confederation of maritime-oriented tribes that occupied the coastal regions of Southern China during the **late Neolithic** and **Bronze Age**. In the context of Hong Kong, these populations are understood as the ancestral architects of the region’s earliest social structures.

## Historical Interpretations
Historians offer varying perspectives on the **Baiyue** legacy:
- **Mainland Chinese historiography** often situates the **Baiyue** within the broader narrative of the integration of southern territories into the unified Chinese state, emphasizing their eventual assimilation during the **Qin dynasty** (秦朝) and **Han dynasty** (漢朝).
- **Local Hong Kong and New Territories scholarship** often highlights the unique maritime culture of these people, suggesting that their adaptation to the coastal environment provided the foundational agricultural techniques, such as early wet-rice cultivation, that defined regional life for centuries.
- **British colonial-era archaeology** previously tended to view these groups through a structuralist lens, focusing primarily on the transition from stone tool technology to bronze implements rather than cultural continuity.

## Archaeological Significance in Hong Kong
Evidence of **Baiyue** habitation is primarily found through archaeological excavations at sites such as **Sha Ha** (沙下) and **Sham Wan** (深灣). Excavators have uncovered sophisticated pottery shards—notably geometric-stamped ware—and evidence of early agricultural trial cultivation. While the specific linguistic and political organization of these groups remains uncertain due to the absence of indigenous written records, the prevalence of these artifacts suggests a highly mobile, seafaring society that utilized Hong Kong’s natural harbors for trade and resource gathering. Whether these groups were a monolithic entity or a cluster of distinct, loosely affiliated tribes remains a subject of ongoing debate among contemporary historians.
