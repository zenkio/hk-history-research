## Political Career
**Ben Chung** (鍾錦麟) served as a member of the **Sai Kung District Council**, representing the Shin Ming constituency. As a member of the Neo Democrats, a pro-democracy political party, he was elected to the council during the **2019** Hong Kong local elections, a period marked by significant civil unrest and record-high voter turnout. During his tenure, he focused on district-level administrative issues and the broader political climate of Hong Kong.

## The Mass Arrest of 2021
On **January 6, 2021**, **Ben Chung** was among 53 individuals arrested by the Hong Kong Police Force's National Security Department. These arrests were conducted under the National Security Law (NSL), which was implemented in **June 2020**. The authorities alleged that the detainees had participated in a primary election organized by the pro-democracy camp in **July 2020** with the intent to "subvert state power" by attempting to secure a legislative majority to veto the government's budget.

## Differing Perspectives
The arrests remain a subject of intense debate. Pro-establishment voices and the central government in Beijing assert that the primary elections were an illegal attempt to paralyze the government and destabilize the region, necessitating strict legal intervention. Conversely, pro-democracy advocates and international observers argue that the participation in primaries was a legitimate exercise of political expression and a conventional electoral activity. Critics suggest the application of the National Security Law in this context represents a significant contraction of political space in the city. Following his arrest, **Ben Chung** resigned from his position on the district council. Legal proceedings related to the case of the 'Hong Kong 47'—the group eventually charged from the original 53 arrested—have been central to discussions regarding the interpretation of judicial independence and political freedom in post-2020 Hong Kong.
