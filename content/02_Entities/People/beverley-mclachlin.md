## Role in the Judiciary
**Beverley McLachlin** (麥嘉琳) was appointed as a Non-Permanent Judge from another common law jurisdiction to the Court of Final Appeal (CFA) of Hong Kong in **2018**. Her appointment was widely viewed as a signal of the judiciary's commitment to maintaining the international character of Hong Kong's legal system under the 'One Country, Two Systems' framework. As a highly respected jurist who served as the Chief Justice of Canada from **2000** to **2017**, her presence was intended to bolster confidence in the rule of law and the independence of the Hong Kong courts.

## The Resignation of Non-Permanent Judges
In **June 2024**, **Beverley McLachlin** announced that she would not seek to renew her three-year term as a Non-Permanent Judge, which was set to expire in July. This decision followed the resignations of other overseas judges, including Lord Sumption and Lord Collins, who cited concerns regarding the political environment in Hong Kong following the implementation of the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (National Security Law) in **2020** and the subsequent Safeguarding National Security Ordinance in **2024**.

## Differing Perspectives
Interpretations regarding the impact of these departures vary significantly. Some legal scholars and international observers argue that the resignation of prominent overseas judges indicates a weakening of the 'firewall' between Hong Kong's common law tradition and mainland China's legal system, potentially undermining judicial independence. Conversely, the Hong Kong government and various legal proponents maintain that the judiciary remains robust, emphasizing that the Basic Law continues to guarantee judicial independence and that the resignation of individual judges does not reflect systemic failure. Supporters of the government’s position often point to the fact that many other overseas judges remain in their positions, arguing that the system remains functional and that legal processes are being followed according to the city’s post-1997 constitutional order.
