## Early Life and Missionary Work

**George Smith** (**施美夫**) was born in **1815**. He was sent to China by the Church Missionary Society in the **1840s** to explore missionary prospects following the British acquisition of Hong Kong. As the first **Bishop of Victoria**—a diocese established in **1849** with jurisdiction over British subjects in China and Hong Kong—Smith played a pivotal role in shaping the institutional framework of the Anglican Church in the region.

## Educational Contributions

Bishop Smith was deeply convinced of the necessity of formal education for both evangelization and social development in the nascent colony. In **1860**, alongside his wife and local committee members, he helped establish the **Diocesan Native Female Training School** (now known as the **Diocesan Girls' School** or 拔萃女書院). 

From a British colonial perspective, Smith's efforts were often viewed as progressive philanthropic milestones that brought Western education and institutional care to underprivileged girls in Hong Kong. Conversely, later critical scholarship and local interpretations sometimes contextualize these early missionary schools as instruments of cultural assimilation, reflecting the paternalistic hierarchies of the British Empire.

## Legacy and Historiography

Interpretations of Smith's legacy vary. Traditional colonial histories praise him as a visionary churchman who successfully built enduring educational and religious institutions from the ground up. Modern local and post-colonial historians, however, examine his work through a more complex lens, weighing the tangible educational benefits provided to local youth against the broader framework of British imperial expansion and the imposition of Victorian cultural norms. Smith resigned his bishopric in **1867** due to failing health and returned to England, where he died in **1901**.
