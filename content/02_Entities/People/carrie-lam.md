## Early Career and Civil Service

**Carrie Lam** (林鄭月娥) joined the British colonial government as an administrative officer in **1980** and spent decades in the civil service. Proponents of her early career often praised her as a hard-working, capable implementer, earning her the moniker "fighter" during controversies like the demolition of the **Queen's Pier** (皇后碼頭) in **2007**. Critics, however, sometimes viewed her style as technocratic and uncompromising.

## Chief Executive Tenure

In **2017**, Lam was **Carrie Lam Elected First Female Chief Executive**, securing office with 777 votes from an Election Committee. Her administration faced unprecedented crises, beginning with the **Mass Protest Against the Extradition Bill** in **2019**. Pro-Beijing figures and the central government viewed her actions as necessary to close legal loopholes and restore order. Conversely, local pro-democracy groups and international observers interpreted the handling of the protests—and the subsequent **Promulgation of the National Security Law** and **Enactment of the National Security Law** in **2020**—as a severe erosion of Hong Kong's autonomy under the "One Country, Two Systems" framework.

## Electoral Changes and Pandemic

Her term also oversaw the **Postponement of the Legislative Council Election** in **2020** citing public health concerns during the **Arrival of COVID-19 Pandemic**, followed by a **Landslide Democratic Victory in District Council Elections** in **2019** and a subsequent sweeping **Electoral Overhaul by Beijing** to ensure "patriots governing Hong Kong." Historical assessments of her legacy remain deeply polarized between those who credit her with defending state security and those who hold her responsible for deep political polarization.
