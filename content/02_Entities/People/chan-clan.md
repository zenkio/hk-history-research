## Origins and Settlement
The **Chan** clan is a lineage with deep roots in the Guangdong province, migrating into the region that would later become the New Territories of Hong Kong. Their history is inextricably linked to the establishment of **Sam Tung Uk** (三棟屋), a walled village originally located in present-day Tsuen Wan. The clan settled the area during the **18th century**, during the Qing dynasty, taking advantage of land reclamation and agricultural opportunities in the coastal plains.

## Sam Tung Uk Village
The construction of **Sam Tung Uk** represents a typical example of Hakka walled village architecture in Hong Kong. Local tradition held by the descendants suggests the village was designed to balance defensive needs with ancestral veneration. In contrast, colonial-era historical records often focused on the land usage rights and taxation disputes that arose as the British administration began surveying the New Territories after the **1898** lease. While the British viewed the site primarily through the lens of land registration and infrastructure development, members of the **Chan** clan emphasize the lineage's continuity and their role in the cultural development of the Tsuen Wan region.

## Modern Transformation
The village underwent significant changes in the **20th century** as Hong Kong shifted from an agrarian to an industrial economy. By the **1970s and 1980s**, the village was slated for redevelopment as part of the Tsuen Wan new town project. This necessitated the relocation of the residents. A distinct interpretation exists regarding this period: some academic observers highlight the government's systematic preservation of the village as a museum, while members of the clan emphasize the loss of the living communal environment that defined their ancestral home. Today, the original site is maintained as the **Sam Tung Uk Museum**, serving as a testament to the traditional lifestyle of the **Chan** clan and other Hakka settlers in the region.
