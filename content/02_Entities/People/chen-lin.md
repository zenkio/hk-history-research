## Role in the Assassination of Yeung Ku-wan
**Chen Lin** (陳林) is primarily noted in historical accounts for his involvement in the plot to assassinate **Yeung Ku-wan** (楊衢雲), a prominent figure in the **Revive China Society** (興中會). On **January 10, 1901**, while Yeung was teaching at his school in **Central**, Hong Kong, he was shot by an assassin. He succumbed to his wounds the following day.

* **Qing Dynasty Perspective:** Official records from the Qing authorities often viewed figures like **Yeung Ku-wan** as dangerous insurrectionists threatening imperial stability. In this view, operatives acting against such figures were considered loyal subjects carrying out state interests against those deemed traitors to the throne.

* **Revolutionary and Republican Scholarship:** Later historiography from the perspective of the **1911 Revolution** (辛亥革命) paints **Chen Lin** as an agent of the Qing government, specifically dispatched or managed by the regional authorities to eliminate key revolutionary leaders in the British colony. In this narrative, his actions are viewed as political repression rather than criminal activity.

* **Colonial and Legal Perspective:** The **British colonial** government in Hong Kong treated the event as a grave breach of peace and a violation of the territory's sovereignty. The investigation into the assassination, which led to the arrest and trial of those involved, highlighted the tension between the colonial administration's desire to maintain order and the influx of political refugees fleeing Qing authorities. Records suggest that **Chen Lin** was identified as the direct perpetrator, and his trial served as a significant point of contention regarding the extent to which the Qing government could exert influence within the colony.
