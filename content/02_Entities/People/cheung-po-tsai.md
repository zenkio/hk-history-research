## Origins and Rise
**Cheung Po Tsai** (張保仔) was a Cantonese pirate who rose to command a formidable fleet in the South China Sea during the early **19th century**. Born to a fisherman, he was captured by the pirate **Zheng Yi** (鄭一) and his wife **Zheng Yi Sao** (鄭一嫂). Following their deaths, Cheung assumed leadership of a major faction of the Red Flag Fleet. Historical accounts suggest his fleet at its peak commanded hundreds of vessels and thousands of followers, operating extensively around the **Pearl River Delta** and the islands of **Hong Kong**.

## Historical Interpretations
Perspectives on Cheung vary significantly depending on the narrative lens. To the **Qing Dynasty** (清朝) authorities, he was a dangerous outlaw whose persistent raids destabilized coastal commerce and necessitated large-scale naval engagements. In contrast, local oral traditions within Hong Kong fishing communities often frame him as a 'Robin Hood' figure who protected the poor and shared looted wealth. British colonial historians have traditionally focused on his surrender as a key event that cleared the shipping lanes, facilitating the eventual growth of Hong Kong as a trading port.

## Surrender and Legacy
In **1810**, after intense naval pressure from both the Qing navy and colonial powers, **Cheung Po Tsai** entered negotiations and accepted an amnesty offer. He surrendered his fleet to the Qing government, eventually serving as an official in the Qing naval hierarchy. Many maritime myths in Hong Kong associate him with various caves—most notably on **Cheung Chau** (長洲)—where he allegedly stored his treasure. While historians note that archaeological evidence for these caches remains elusive, these sites remain central to his local cultural legacy. Modern scholarship continues to debate the extent of his political agency versus his role as a tactical instrument of the late-Qing coastal defense strategy.
