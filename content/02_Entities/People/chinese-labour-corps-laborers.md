## Recruitment and Transit
During **1916** to **1918**, the **Chinese Labour Corps** (華工) were recruited primarily from Shandong province, but also from southern regions, to perform non-combatant labor on the Western Front. While most laborers arrived via ship from Weihaiwei (威海衛), **Hong Kong** served as a significant logistics hub and transit port. British colonial authorities facilitated the movement of these workers, often utilizing local infrastructure to process, house, and transport them toward Europe.

## Differing Perspectives
Historical interpretations of the laborers' roles remain multifaceted. From the perspective of the **British colonial government** at the time, the corps was a pragmatic solution to severe wartime manpower shortages, viewed through the lens of imperial utility. In contrast, the **Beijing government** of the era viewed participation as a strategic geopolitical move, hoping that contributing to the Allied effort would secure China's position at the postwar peace table and facilitate the recovery of sovereign rights. 

Within the **New Territories** and broader local communities, historical accounts—often passed down orally—reflect a complex legacy. Some narratives focus on the economic hardship that drove recruitment, while others emphasize the personal sacrifices made by those who never returned. **Later scholarship** has increasingly moved to decenter the British imperial narrative, focusing instead on the individual agency of the laborers and the long-term socio-political impact of their overseas experience on Chinese nationalism. 

## Legacy in Hong Kong
Because many records were scattered or destroyed during the turbulence of the early 20th century, the exact number of laborers recruited directly from Hong Kong is subject to academic debate. While it is widely accepted that the city was integral to the logistics of the corps, the extent to which local villagers were coerced or incentivized remains a point of historical uncertainty. Today, the role of these laborers is increasingly acknowledged in local historical discourse as a bridge between China's global participation in **World War I** and the city's role as a colonial entrepôt.
