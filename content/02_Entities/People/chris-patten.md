## Background and Arrival

**Chris Patten** (**彭定康**) arrived in Hong Kong in **July 1992** as the final British Governor. Unlike many of his predecessors who were career diplomats or civil servants, Patten was a senior British politician and former chairman of the Conservative Party. His appointment signaled a shift in London's approach to the impending **1997** handover, prioritizing a graceful exit and a strengthened democratic framework over quiet diplomacy with Beijing.

## Constitutional Reform Proposals

Shortly after taking office, Patten introduced sweeping political reforms in **1992** aimed at broadening the electorate for the Legislative Council (**立法局**). These proposals lowered the voting age, expanded functional constituencies, and created new electoral tiers. 

Interpretations of these reforms remain deeply divided:
* **British Colonial / Pro-Democracy Perspective:** Supporters viewed the changes as a necessary and belated effort to fulfill Britain's moral obligation to leave behind a robust, representative government that could protect Hong Kong's civil liberties.
* **Beijing / Qing Perspective:** The Chinese government condemned the reforms as a unilateral breach of the Sino-British Joint Declaration and the Basic Law, labeling Patten a 'sinner for a thousand years' and dismantling the elected council upon resumption of sovereignty in **July 1997**.
* **Local Hong Kong Perspective:** Public opinion was mixed; while many residents welcomed greater democratic representation, the business community and others worried the reforms would unnecessarily provoke Beijing and destabilize the local economy.

## Departure and Legacy

Patten's tenure culminated in the emotional **Sunset Farewell Ceremony** and the **Official Handover to China** on **June 30 – July 1, 1997**. His departure aboard the royal yacht Britannia marked the end of 156 years of British rule. Later scholarship often debates whether Patten's democratization efforts genuinely empowered Hong Kong civil society or primarily complicated the territory's integration into the People's Republic of China.
