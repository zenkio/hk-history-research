## Early Life and Ministry
Born in mainland China, **Chu Yiu-ming** (朱耀明) settled in Hong Kong and became a prominent figure in the Christian community. As a pastor at the **Chai Wan Baptist Church** (柴灣浸信會), he gained recognition for his social activism, particularly his efforts in the late 1980s to assist mainland Chinese dissidents fleeing after the **1989 Tiananmen Square protests** through 'Operation Yellowbird' (黃雀行動). 

## Occupy Central and Political Activism
In **2013**, **Chu Yiu-ming** co-founded the **Occupy Central with Love and Peace** (讓愛與和平佔領中環) initiative alongside legal scholar **Benny Tai** (戴耀廷) and sociologist **Chan Kin-man** (陳健民). The movement aimed to advocate for universal suffrage in Hong Kong, specifically pushing for a democratic nomination process for the Chief Executive election. 

Interpretations of these actions vary significantly. Supporters view **Chu** as a courageous human rights defender who championed democratic ideals through non-violent civil disobedience. Conversely, the central government in Beijing and various pro-establishment figures in Hong Kong have characterized his actions as destabilizing, accusing the movement of inciting illegal activity and challenging the rule of law. 

## Legal Consequences and Later Years

While the court acknowledged his peaceful intent, the verdict reflected the government's stance on unauthorized assemblies. **Chu** was handed a suspended sentence, citing his age and years of community service. Since the enactment of the **National Security Law** in **2020**, the political environment in Hong Kong has shifted considerably; **Chu** has largely retired from public life, with historical assessments of his legacy remaining a subject of ongoing debate between those emphasizing his contribution to civil society and those focusing on the challenges posed to the established political order.
