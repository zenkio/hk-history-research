## Role in the Expansion of Hong Kong
**Sir Claude Maxwell MacDonald** (1852–1915) was a British soldier and diplomat who served as the Minister to China between **1896** and **1900**. His most significant contribution to Hong Kong history was his role in negotiating the **Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong** in **1898**.

## Historical Interpretations of the Convention
Following the 'Scramble for Concessions' and the perceived weakening of the Qing dynasty, **MacDonald** negotiated the lease of the New Territories for 99 years. Interpretations of this event vary significantly among historians and political perspectives. From the perspective of the British colonial administration at the time, the lease was framed as a strategic necessity to secure the defense of Hong Kong's harbor and ensure the colony's economic viability. Conversely, modern scholars and commentators from Mainland China often characterize the agreement as part of the 'Unequal Treaties' imposed by Western imperial powers during a period of national humiliation. 

## Impact on Local Communities
The implementation of the agreement led to the 'Six-Day War' of **1899**, a period of armed resistance by local clan alliances in the New Territories who opposed the British takeover. While the British viewed the establishment of colonial governance as the introduction of rule of law and administrative modernization, local communities viewed the sudden change in sovereignty as an existential threat to their traditional land rights and customs. These conflicting historical memories persist in how the transition is perceived today. Some local Hong Kong perspectives emphasize the continuity of indigenous land-holding practices, such as the *Tso* and *Tong* systems, which were eventually protected under the British administration, while others highlight the displacement and loss of autonomy that occurred during the initial transition period. **MacDonald**'s diplomatic maneuvers thus set the stage for a century of complex socio-political integration that defined the territory's modern development.
