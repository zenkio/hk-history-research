## Role and Function
The **Cohong** (公行) were a guild of Chinese merchants authorized by the **Qing** (清) dynasty to manage all trade with Westerners in **Canton** (廣州). Under the **Canton System** (廣州體制), foreign traders—including those from the **East India Company** (東印度公司)—were required to deal exclusively with these licensed firms, which acted as intermediaries for both trade and diplomatic communication.

## Differing Interpretations
Interpretations of the Cohong's role vary by perspective. From the **Qing/Beijing** viewpoint, the guild was a tool of state security and fiscal control, designed to regulate "barbarians" and ensure the collection of customs duties. In contrast, **British colonial** records frequently characterized the Cohong as an inefficient and obstructive monopoly that hindered the natural flow of "free trade." Modern **scholarship** often presents the merchants as precarious intermediaries who bore the financial risks of foreign debts while remaining subject to the arbitrary demands of the imperial bureaucracy.

## Connection to Hong Kong
The frictions generated by the Cohong's monopoly were a primary driver for the British search for an independent trading base. The limitations placed on foreigners within the **Thirteen Factories** (十三行) and the lack of direct diplomatic access to the Qing court created a desire for a "free port" where trade could occur without the mediation of the Cohong. These economic tensions, compounded by the opium trade, contributed to the outbreak of the **First Opium War** (**1839–1842**). The **Treaty of Nanking** (**1842**) formally abolished the Cohong monopoly, opening several treaty ports and leading to the cession of **Hong Kong Island** (香港島). This transition shifted the center of Western trade in South China from the controlled environment of Canton to the open port of Hong Kong.

## Timeline

- **1720**: The Qing government restricts Western trade exclusively to Canton.
- **1834**: The British government ends the East India Company's monopoly on trade with China.
- **1842**: The Treaty of Nanking abolishes the Cohong and cedes Hong Kong Island to Britain.
