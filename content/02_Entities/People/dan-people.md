## Origins and Early Historiography
The Dan people (蜑家), often referred to in English as the Tanka, have long traditional roots as boat-dwelling inhabitants of the Pearl River Delta and coastal Guangdong. Historical perspectives on their origins vary significantly. Qing dynasty (清朝) and imperial Chinese records frequently marginalized the Dan as outsiders or non-Han populations, often restricting them from land settlement and imperial examinations. In contrast, later sociological scholarship and local Hong Kong oral histories emphasize their deep integration into the regional maritime economy, noting that their boat-based lifestyle was a practical adaptation to a rich fishing and trading environment rather than evidence of a distinct ethnic origin.

## British Colonial Era and Social Status
Following the British occupation of Hong Kong Island in **1841** and the subsequent expansion of the territory, the colonial administration encountered a stratified society where the Dan occupied a distinct socioeconomic niche. British colonial reports often documented the Tanka as a separate boat-dwelling class, distinct from land-based Punti (本地) and Hakka (客家) communities in the New Territories (新界). While imperial legal discriminations were formally dismantled under British rule, social barriers persisted well into the twentieth century. Traditional New Territories lineages often maintained strict social boundaries with the boat-dwellers.

## Modern Integration and Scholarship
In the decades following the Second World War, particularly from the **1950s** to the **1970s** modern urban development, typhoon shelter construction, and government housing schemes radically transformed the Dan way of life. Large-scale resettlement programs moved boat-dwelling families onto public housing estates on land. Contemporary scholarship views the history of the Dan people not as a narrative of sudden assimilation, but as a complex process of negotiation between maritime traditions, colonial governance, and the broader modernization of Hong Kong.
