## Early Life and Film Career
**Danny Lee** (李修賢) entered the Hong Kong film industry in the **1970s**, initially training at the Shaw Brothers Studio actor training course. Over the decades, he established a distinct screen persona, frequently casting himself as a dedicated, honorable, and disciplined police inspector. This archetype became a staple of the Hong Kong action and crime genres during the height of the local cinema boom in the **1980s** and **1990s**.

## Collaboration with John Woo and The Killer
Lee's career is closely intertwined with the development of the 'heroic bloodshed' genre. His production company, Magnum Films, played a vital role in supporting auteur **John Woo** (吳宇森). Lee famously starred alongside **Chow Yun-fat** (周潤發) in the **1989** masterpiece **The Killer** (喋血雙雄), portraying the principled Inspector Li Ying. The film premiered to significant local and international acclaim, cementing Lee's reputation as a producer and actor who could bridge gritty realism with stylized cinematic violence.

## Mentorship and Legacy
Beyond his acting credits, Lee is widely recognized in Hong Kong film history for his eye for talent. He notably offered **Stephen Chow** (周星馳) his breakthrough dramatic film role in **Final Justice** (霹靂先鋒) in **1988**, a collaboration that propelled Chow toward superstardom. While interpretations of Lee's directorial style range from conventional genre filmmaking to socially conscious crime drama, film historians generally view him as a central figure who shaped the golden age of Hong Kong cinema through his dedication to authenticity in police-themed narratives.
