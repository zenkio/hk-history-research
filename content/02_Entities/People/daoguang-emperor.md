## Reign and the Opium War

The **Daoguang Emperor** (道光帝) ruled the Qing dynasty from **1820** to **1850**. His reign is critically linked to Hong Kong's early modern history due to the outbreak of the First Opium War (**1839**–**1842**). Confronted with the escalating opium trade and the drain of silver, the emperor appointed **Lin Zexu** (林則徐) as Imperial Commissioner to suppress the trade in Canton (Guangzhou).

## Territorial Cessions and Negotiations

During the hostilities, local negotiations led to provisional agreements such as the Convention of Chuenpi in **1841**, negotiated by imperial plenipotentiary **Qishan** (琦善). However, the **Daoguang Emperor** rejected the terms, viewing them as excessively concessionary, and dismissed **Qishan**. Consequently, fighting resumed until the Qing empire was forced to sign the Treaty of Nanking in **1842**, which formally ceded Hong Kong Island to the British Crown.

## Historiographical Interpretations

Interpretations of the **Daoguang Emperor**'s role diverge significantly across historical perspectives:

*   **Qing and Mainland Historiography:** Traditional and mainland Chinese scholarship often portrays the emperor as a well-intentioned ruler caught in the decline of the dynastic cycle, struggling against aggressive Western imperialism and bureaucratic corruption.
*   **British Colonial Historiography:** Early British accounts frequently viewed the imperial court's rigidity and refusal to engage in Western-style diplomacy as the primary catalyst for the military conflicts that necessitated the acquisition of Hong Kong as a trading base.
*   **Local Hong Kong and Modern Scholarship:** Modern historians emphasize how the geopolitical decisions made in Beijing directly impacted the Pearl River Delta, setting off a chain of events that transformed a peripheral fishing region into a global commercial entrepôt.

## Timeline

- **1820**: Ascended the Qing throne.
- **1839**: Dispatched Lin Zexu to suppress the opium trade in Canton, precipitating the First Opium War.
- **1841**: Repudiated the Convention of Chuenpi and dismissed Qishan for ceding Hong Kong Island.
- **1842**: Ratified the Treaty of Nanking, formalizing the cession of Hong Kong Island to Britain.
