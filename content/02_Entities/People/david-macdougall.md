## Early Career and Wartime Service

**David MacDougall** entered the British colonial civil service in Hong Kong before the Second World War. During the Japanese occupation of Hong Kong (**1941**–**1945**), MacDougall served in the British Administration in London and subsequently with the planning units that prepared for the resumption of civil government. 

## Post-War Administration and Social Welfare

Following the liberation of Hong Kong in **August 1945**, MacDougall served as the Colonial Secretary (acting) and head of the Hong Kong Government's Civil Affairs Unit. In this capacity, he was instrumental in stabilizing the war-torn territory, addressing severe food shortages, and managing displaced populations.

His administration is particularly notable for the establishment of the **Social Welfare Office** in **1948**. While British colonial records emphasize this move as a progressive step toward modernizing government responsibility for public welfare, later scholarship and local historians often view it as a reactive measure to cope with the massive influx of refugees fleeing the Chinese Civil War, rather than a comprehensive social safety net.

## Historiographical Perspectives

In contrast, critical local scholarship suggests that early welfare initiatives were limited in scope and primarily designed to maintain social order and serve the strategic interests of the British Crown rather than address the structural needs of the local population.
