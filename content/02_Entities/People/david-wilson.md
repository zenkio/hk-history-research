## Early Career and Appointment
**David Wilson** (衛奕信), Baron Wilson of Tillyorn, was a career diplomat with extensive experience in mainland China. Before his appointment as Governor, he had played a key role in the negotiations leading to the 1984 Sino-British Joint Declaration. He assumed the governorship in **1987**, inheriting a society grappling with the complexities of the transition to Chinese sovereignty.

## The Rose Garden Project
In **1989**, following the Tiananmen Square protests, **Wilson** announced the 'Port and Airport Development Strategy,' later colloquially known as the **Rose Garden Project**. Public interpretation of this initiative remains divided. Pro-colonial scholars often characterize it as a visionary infrastructure plan designed to bolster Hong Kong’s economic status and restore local confidence. Conversely, critics and Beijing-aligned commentators frequently interpreted the project as a deliberate attempt to deplete the territory’s fiscal reserves before the 1997 handover, leading to protracted diplomatic friction between the British and Chinese governments.

## Legacy and Interpretation
**Wilson**’s tenure concluded in **1992**, followed by the arrival of **Chris Patten** (彭定康). His governance style is often contrasted with that of his successor; while **Patten** pursued more aggressive democratic reforms, **Wilson** favored a 'quiet diplomacy' approach with Beijing. Local historians and community groups in the New Territories (新界) often acknowledge his role in facilitating land-use developments, though his legacy is viewed through the varied lenses of colonial administrative pragmatism, democratic frustration, and the geopolitical pressures exerted by the People's Republic of China. His period in office remains a focal point for understanding the final decade of British administration in Hong Kong.
