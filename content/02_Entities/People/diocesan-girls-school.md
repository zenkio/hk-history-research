## Origins and Development

The school was founded in **1860** by the **Anglican Church** (specifically the Church Missionary Society) as the **Diocesan Female Training School** (拔萃女書院). Its establishment marked an early effort to provide formal education to Chinese girls in the colonial period. Initially situated in **Bonham Road**, the school aimed to foster a Christian-influenced curriculum, though its early operations faced challenges regarding enrollment and sustainability. 

## Historical Interpretations

Perspectives on the school’s founding often diverge based on historical context. From a traditional colonial administration viewpoint, the school is cited as an example of the British commitment to providing Western-style education and social uplift in the fledgling colony. Conversely, some post-colonial scholars and local historians note that such institutions were inherently tied to the missionary objective of Westernization, which sometimes operated at the intersection of, or in conflict with, traditional Confucian family values prevalent in the local Chinese community at the time. 

## Evolution and Modern Era

The school underwent several transformations, notably in **1899** when it merged with the **Diocesan Boys' School** (拔萃男書院) for a brief period before separating again. It eventually relocated to its iconic campus on **Jordan Road** in **1913**. Throughout the **20th century**, the school solidified its reputation as a leading academic institution in Hong Kong. Today, it remains highly regarded for its academic rigor and emphasis on extracurricular achievements, such as music and sports. Its history remains a significant case study in the influence of mission-run schools on the social mobility of women in Hong Kong throughout the **British colonial** period.
