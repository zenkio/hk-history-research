## Introduction
**Dorabjee Naorojee Mithaiwala** (德庇治·拿羅哲·米泰華拿) was a prominent nineteenth-century Parsi businessman who played a foundational role in Hong Kong's maritime transport history. As part of the vibrant Parsi community that established commercial roots in Hong Kong shortly after British occupation in **1841**, Mithaiwala contributed significantly to the colony's burgeoning infrastructure.

## The Star Ferry Service
In **1888**, Mithaiwala founded the **Star Ferry** (天星小輪) service, initially operating under the name **Morning Star** with a single wooden steamboat. Before this venture, cross-harbour transport relied on irregular and often unreliable sampans and steam-launches. By introducing a scheduled, affordable ferry service between Central and Tsim Sha Tsui, Mithaiwala met the growing demand for connectivity as Kowloon underwent gradual development. 

## Historical Interpretations
In British colonial historiography, Mithaiwala is typically remembered as an enterprising pioneer embodying the laissez-faire entrepreneurial spirit that transformed Hong Kong into a global trading hub. Later scholarship often contextualizes his success within the broader networks of Parsi merchants operating across the British Empire, who leveraged diasporic connections to secure commercial footholds in treaty ports and colonies. Local Hong Kong histories frequently celebrate the launch of the Star Ferry as a pivotal moment in the unification of the urban core on Hong Kong Island with the Kowloon Peninsula, cementing the harbour as the central axis of daily life in the territory.

## Legacy
Though Mithaiwala eventually sold his interests in the enterprise—which later became the **Star Ferry Company** in **1898** under the management of **Sir Catchick Paul Chater** (遮打)—his initial vision laid the groundwork for one of Hong Kong's most enduring symbols. Details regarding his early life and exact business networks remain sparse in mainstream historical records, reflecting the broader archival marginalization of non-elite and non-European commercial actors outside of official colonial reports.
