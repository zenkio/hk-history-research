## Background and Commercial Activities

**Dorabjee Naorojee** (爹核士·娜律哲) was part of the influential Parsi community that migrated from western India to **Hong Kong** (香港) during the mid-nineteenth century following the British establishment of the colony. Like many of his contemporaries, Naorojee engaged in trade, shipping, and hospitality. He was notably associated with the ownership and management of the Kowloon Hotel and various maritime ventures connecting the island of Hong Kong with the Kowloon Peninsula.

## Introduction of Regular Ferry Services

Before the late nineteenth century, cross-harbour transport relied primarily on irregular and often unreliable sampans and small wooden boats. In **1888**, Naorojee recognized the growing commercial and residential potential of the Kowloon Peninsula and founded the **Morning Star** ferry service. Operating a small steam-powered boat named the *Morning Star*, his enterprise provided scheduled, dependable transportation across Victoria Harbour.

## Historiographical Perspectives

In British colonial historiography, Naorojee's venture is typically framed as a prime example of nineteenth-century entrepreneurial initiative that modernized Hong Kong's infrastructure and facilitated urban expansion northward into Kowloon. Later scholarship often contextualizes his business within the broader network of Parsi diaspora merchants who played a crucial, albeit frequently overlooked, role in bridging British imperial trade networks with local Asian economies.
