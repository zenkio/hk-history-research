## Background and Arrival in Hong Kong

**Dr. James Cantlie** (康德黎) arrived in the British colony of Hong Kong in the late 19th century, serving as a prominent surgeon and educator. Recognizing the lack of Western medical training available to local populations in South China, Cantlie became a key driving force behind the establishment of formal medical institutions in the territory.

## The Hong Kong College of Medicine for Chinese

In **1887**, Cantlie, alongside fellow medical professionals and missionary figures such as **Dr. Ho Kai** (何啟), helped establish the **Hong Kong College of Medicine for Chinese** (香港華人西醫書院). The institution aimed to train Chinese students in Western medical and surgical practices. Among its earliest and most notable students was **Sun Yat-sen** (孫中山), who enrolled in **1887** and graduated under Cantlie's mentorship.

Cantlie's relationship with Sun extended far beyond the classroom. He became a close personal friend and political confidant to Sun, later famously assisting him during the **1896** London kidnapping incident, where Sun was held captive by the Qing legation.
