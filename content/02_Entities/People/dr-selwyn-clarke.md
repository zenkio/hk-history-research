## Medical Administration in Colonial Hong Kong

**Percy Selwyn-Clarke** (1893–1976) served as the Director of Medical Services in Hong Kong during the **1930s** and early **1940s**. His tenure spanned the period leading up to the Japanese occupation, during which he was interned at the Stanley Internment Camp. As a high-ranking British colonial official, his primary responsibilities involved overseeing public health infrastructure, sanitation, and hospital services for the territory's growing population. 

## Post-War Public Health and Tuberculosis

Following the liberation of Hong Kong in **1945**, **Selwyn-Clarke** played a critical role in the immediate public health reconstruction. His most enduring contribution to the territory was his involvement in the establishment of the **Hong Kong Anti-Tuberculosis Association** (香港防癆會). In the aftermath of the war, tuberculosis emerged as a severe crisis, claiming thousands of lives.
