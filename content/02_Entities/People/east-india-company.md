## Trade and the Canton System
The **East India Company (EIC)** operated under a royal charter, maintaining a monopoly on British trade in the East. For much of the 18th and early 19th centuries, its commerce with China was restricted to the **Canton System (廣州體制)**. The EIC established a factory (商館) in **Canton (廣州)**, focusing primarily on the import of tea, which became a staple of British consumption. Because the Qing (清) dynasty demanded payment in silver, a significant trade imbalance developed.

## The Opium Trade and Conflict
To rectify the silver outflow, the EIC expanded the cultivation of opium in India for sale to Chinese merchants. Although the EIC officially avoided direct delivery to China to circumvent Qing laws, it provided the opium to private traders who smuggled the drug into the mainland. This practice led to widespread addiction and social instability, eventually prompting a Qing crackdown in **1839**. This friction served as a primary catalyst for the **First Opium War (第一次鴉片戰爭)**.

## Connection to Hong Kong
The war concluded with the **Treaty of Nanking (南京條約)** in **1842**, which saw the cession of **Hong Kong Island (香港島)** to the British Crown. While the EIC's monopoly on China trade had been abolished by the British Parliament in **1833**, the company's previous commercial strategies and political influence were foundational to the conflict that created the colony.

## Differing Interpretations
Perspectives on the EIC's legacy differ across historical narratives. British colonial accounts historically framed the company as a pioneer of global trade and a harbinger of modernization. Conversely, Chinese perspectives, particularly from Beijing, view the EIC as an instrument of imperialist aggression and illegal narcotics trafficking, marking the start of the "Century of Humiliation" (百年國恥). Modern scholarship often analyzes the EIC as a "company-state," highlighting its unique ability to exercise sovereign powers—such as maintaining armies and governing territories—which blurred the distinction between corporate profit and state imperialism.
