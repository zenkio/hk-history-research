## Background and 1966 Protest
**Elliott Ngok-shun** (蘇守忠) gained public attention in **1966** during a period of economic strain in British-administered Hong Kong. On **April 4, 1966**, **Ngok-shun** staged a hunger strike outside the Star Ferry pier in Central, protesting the government's approval of a fare increase for the ferry service. He carried a banner criticizing the rise, arguing that the cost of living was already burdensome for the working class.

## Historical Significance and Interpretations

Others view his protest as a nascent moment of local identity formation, where residents began to challenge the authority of the British administration directly. The precise extent to which his individual action served as the sole driver for the broader riots remains a subject of academic discussion, as many believe the unrest reflected a broader accumulation of public frustration.
