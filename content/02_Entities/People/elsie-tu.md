## Early Life and Activism
Born **Elsie Hume** in **1913** in Newcastle upon Tyne, England, she arrived in Hong Kong in **1951** as a missionary. She later adopted the surname **Tu** after marrying her second husband, **Andrew Tu Hsueh-kwei (杜學魁)**. She became a vocal champion for the grassroots population, often challenging the colonial administration on issues ranging from housing and education to corruption and social welfare.

## The Star Ferry Riots
During the **1966 Star Ferry Riots**, which were sparked by a fare increase, **Elsie Tu** acted as a persistent critic of the colonial government. While official colonial reports often categorized the unrest as primarily the work of agitators, **Tu** argued that the riots were symptomatic of deep-seated frustrations regarding economic inequality, the lack of representative government, and the perceived indifference of British officials toward the working class. Her activism during this period cemented her reputation as a voice for the common citizen.

## Political Legacy
**Tu** served as a member of the **Urban Council** from **1963** to **1995** and later served on the **Provisional Legislative Council**. Her political career is viewed through multiple lenses. Some local supporters emphasize her role as a pioneering human rights advocate who stood up to colonial authority. Conversely, some later scholars and pro-democracy figures have noted that her later political alignment with the **Beijing**-backed establishment created a complex legacy, leading to debates regarding her shifting views on political reform in the years surrounding the **1997** handover.

Throughout her life, **Tu** maintained a firm belief in the importance of honesty in government, a position she championed through the **Hong Kong Civic Association** and later as an independent. Her work to expose corruption in public services was instrumental in the establishment of the **Independent Commission Against Corruption (ICAC)**. She remained a controversial yet respected figure until her death in **2015** at the age of 102.
