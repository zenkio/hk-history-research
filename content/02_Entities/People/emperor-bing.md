## Flight to the South

**Emperor Bing** (**宋帝昺**), personal name Zhao Bing (**趙昺**), was enthroned in **1278** as the last emperor of the Southern Song dynasty (**南宋**) while fleeing the advancing Mongol forces. Accompanied by loyal officials such as **Lu Xiufu** (**陸秀夫**) and **Zhang Shijie** (**張世傑**), the imperial court sought refuge along the southern coast of China.

## Presence in the Hong Kong Region

Historical records and local oral traditions indicate that the young emperor's entourage stayed in the territory now comprising Hong Kong. The **Establishment of Imperial Residence at Sacred Hill** (**聖山**), located in modern-day Kowloon City, is traditionally associated with this temporary court. Following the death of his older brother, Emperor Duanzong (**宋端宗**), who had passed away after falling ill near **Mui Wo** (**梅窩**), Emperor Bing became the symbolic center of the dying dynasty's resistance.

## Differing Historical Interpretations

Interpretations of Emperor Bing's legacy vary across different traditions:

* **Local Hong Kong and New Territories communities** historically integrated the court's flight into local identity, linking regional deities such as **Hau Wong** (**侯王**) to loyalist figures who accompanied the young monarch, as seen in the **Battle of Yamen and Local Veneration of Hau Wong** (**崖門戰役與侯王崇拜**).
* **Qing dynasty and imperial Beijing historiography** framed the loyalist resistance through the lens of Confucian orthodoxy, emphasizing absolute loyalty (**忠**) to the sovereign even in hopeless defeat.
* **British colonial** accounts and early Western antiquarians often treated these local legends as colorful folklore, documenting physical sites like Sung Wong Toi (**宋王臺**) primarily as architectural or epigraphic curiosities.
* **Later modern scholarship** views the events through a critical lens, separating verifiable dynastic movements from centuries of accumulated myth-making.

## The Fall at Yamen

The Song court's brief tenure in the region ended when they were forced further west to **Yamen** (**崖門**). In **1279**, the Song naval forces were decisively defeated by the Mongols. Rather than submit to capture, the official **Lu Xiufu** carried the eight-year-old Emperor Bing into the sea, ending the Southern Song dynasty.
