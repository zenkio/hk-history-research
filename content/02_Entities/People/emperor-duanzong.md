## Flight to the South

Emperor Duanzong (宋端宗), personal name Zhao Shi (趙昰), was born in **1269** and ascended the Southern Song throne in **1278** at a young age as the Mongol Empire pressed relentlessly southward. Fleeing the mainland, the retreating Song imperial court, accompanied by tens of thousands of officials, soldiers, and civilians, sought refuge along the southern coast. Historical records and local traditions indicate that the court arrived in the Kowloon (九龍) area, establishing a temporary imperial residence at Sacred Hill (聖山), located in present-day Ma Tau Chung (馬頭涌).

## Natural Disasters and Hardship

During their brief stay in the region, the fugitive court faced severe environmental challenges alongside military pressure. A devastating typhoon struck the Song imperial fleet, destroying many vessels and compounding the suffering of the displaced refugees. Accounts from the period and subsequent local oral histories emphasize the perilous conditions faced by the young emperor and his entourage.

## Death and Legacy

Emperor Duanzong's health, already fragile due to the hardships of flight and the trauma of the maritime storm, deteriorated rapidly. He passed away in **1278** at Mui Wo (梅窩) on Lantau Island (大嶼山) at the age of nine. He was succeeded by his younger brother, Emperor Bing (宋帝昺).

Interpretations of Duanzong's presence in Hong Kong vary across different historical traditions. Local Hong Kong and New Territories communities have historically preserved memories of the Song dynasty's flight through temples, place names, and lineage legends, often viewing the era through a lens of tragic loyalty. In contrast, Qing (清) dynasty historiography tended to frame the Southern Song loyalists through an orthodox Confucian framework of dynastic legitimacy, while British colonial interpretations often treated these events as obscure background history. Modern scholarship generally views the episode as a critical chapter in the southward migration of populations that shaped the demographics and early recorded history of the Pearl River Delta region.
