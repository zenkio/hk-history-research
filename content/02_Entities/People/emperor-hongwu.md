## Connection to Hong Kong

**Emperor Hongwu** (明太祖), born **Zhu Yuanzhang** (朱元璋), reigned from **1368** to **1398** as the founding emperor of the Ming dynasty. While his court was centered far to the north, first in Nanjing and later in Beijing, his sweeping economic and maritime policies directly impacted the coastal populations of the Pearl River Delta, including the areas that now constitute Hong Kong.

## The Pearl Diving Industry

The connection between Emperor Hongwu and Hong Kong is most notably preserved in local histories concerning the government pearl diving operations in **Tai Po** (大埔). During the early Ming dynasty, the imperial court sought to regulate and revive the extraction of southern pearls, which had been exploited since the Han dynasty. Local histories and later scholarship indicate that the dangerous state-mandated pearl diving placed a heavy burden on the indigenous coastal communities of the New Territories.

## Historiographical Interpretations

Interpretations of Emperor Hongwu's policies in the region vary across different perspectives:

* **Qing and Imperial Accounts:** Official dynastic records generally framed the emperor's maritime and industrial regulations as necessary steps to restore economic order and secure state monopolies after the fall of the Mongol-led Yuan dynasty.
* **Local New Territories Communities:** Oral traditions and local lineage perspectives often remembered early Ming state interventions, such as pearl diving levies, through the lens of hardship and forced labor, noting that diving conditions were so perilous that communities occasionally attempted to sabotage or halt the operations.
* **Modern Scholarship:** Contemporary historians view the Ming administration's early engagement with the Hong Kong region as part of a broader strategy of southern pacification and resource extraction, though the exact enforcement and local impact remain difficult to quantify due to sparse surviving local archives from the fourteenth century.
