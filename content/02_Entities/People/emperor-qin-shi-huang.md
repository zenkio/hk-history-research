## Historical Context and Conquest

Emperor **Qin Shi Huang** (**秦始皇**), who unified China in **221 BCE**, directed military campaigns to expand the empire's southern frontier into the region known as **Lingnan** (**嶺南**). Around **214 BCE**, Qin forces secured the territories comprising modern-day Guangdong and Guangxi, establishing administrative commanderies including **Nanhai** (**南海**), **Guilin** (**桂林**), and **Xiang** (**象**). 

## Connection to Hong Kong

While direct archaeological evidence specifically linking the modern territory of Hong Kong to the administration of the Qin court remains debated, traditional historiography associates this era with the integration of the region into the imperial fold. The establishment of the **Nanhai Commandery** (**南海郡**), governed from near modern Guangzhou, placed the coastal areas, including parts of present-day Hong Kong, under nominal imperial authority.

## Historiographical Interpretations

Interpretations of Emperor Qin Shi Huang's legacy in southern China vary across different historical perspectives:

* **Qing and Mainland Historiography:** Traditional and official Chinese histories emphasize the emperor as a unifying figure who brought the southern non-Han populations (**Baiyue**, **百越**) into the Chinese cultural and political sphere, laying the foundations for subsequent territorial integrity.
* **Local New Territories Communities:** Indigenous lineages in the New Territories historically placed less emphasis on early imperial integration, focusing instead on later migration waves from the Song (**宋**) and Ming (**明**) dynasties, though local folklore occasionally intersects with ancient imperial campaigns.
* **British Colonial and Modern Scholarship:** Western and modern archaeological scholarship often treats the early Qin presence in Hong Kong as peripheral, pointing to limited artifact recovery from the period and suggesting that actual administrative control was sparse and primarily focused on major transport routes rather than the outlying coastal islands.
