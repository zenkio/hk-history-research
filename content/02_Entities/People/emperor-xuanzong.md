## Imperial Administration and Tuen Mun
During the reign of **Emperor Xuanzong (唐玄宗)** in the mid-8th century, the Tang government established a naval supply depot at **Tuen Mun (屯門)**. This facility was designed to serve as a logistics and staging point for naval forces engaged in campaigns and diplomatic missions directed toward the Japanese archipelago and the Namba region.

## Divergent Interpretations
The nature and significance of the Tuen Mun installation are viewed differently across various historical perspectives:

* **Imperial/Beijing Perspective**: It is often framed as a symbol of the Tang Dynasty's maritime reach and its capacity to project power across East Asia during the height of its influence.
* **Local Hong Kong and New Territories Communities**: The depot is frequently cited as the earliest evidence of centralized imperial administration in the region, marking the beginning of the area's formal integration into the Chinese political sphere.
* **Later Scholarship**: Modern historians are more cautious regarding the scale of the site. Some argue it was a permanent military garrison, while others suggest it was a temporary supply depot used only during specific expeditions. The exact duration of its active operation remains uncertain.

## Strategic Significance
The selection of **Tuen Mun (屯門)** was likely based on its geography; the sheltered bay provided a secure anchorage for ships awaiting favorable winds before crossing the sea. This established a precedent for the region's role as a maritime gateway, a strategic characteristic that persisted through subsequent dynasties.
