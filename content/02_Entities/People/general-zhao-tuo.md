## Origins and the Nanyue Kingdom
**Zhao Tuo** (趙佗) was a military commander under the **Qin Dynasty** (秦朝) who led campaigns into the Lingnan region. Following the collapse of the Qin in **206 BCE**, he consolidated power and established the independent Nanyue Kingdom, with its capital at Panyu (modern-day Guangzhou). 

## Historical Interpretations
Historians offer varying perspectives on **Zhao Tuo**’s legacy regarding Hong Kong. Official historiography from the Qing Dynasty and modern Beijing emphasizes his role in integrating the Lingnan region into the broader Chinese cultural and political sphere, viewing him as a key figure in the Sinicization of the south. Conversely, some local New Territories communities and later scholarship explore a distinct 'Nanyue' identity, suggesting that the region maintained indigenous socio-political structures that were only partially altered by Nanyue administration. British colonial-era historians often framed the period through a lens of 'frontier' history, debating the extent of effective administrative control that reached the coastal areas and islands of present-day Hong Kong. While archaeological evidence, such as Han-style tomb artifacts found in the region, confirms the presence of Nanyue cultural influence, the exact nature and intensity of governance over the Hong Kong territory during this period remain subjects of historical inquiry.

## Connection to Hong Kong
The archaeological record in Hong Kong, particularly findings at **Lei Cheng Uk Han Tomb** (李鄭屋漢墓), provides the most tangible link to the era of **Zhao Tuo**. Although these artifacts date largely to the subsequent Western Han period, they underscore the lasting structural and material influence of the Nanyue Kingdom's foundation. Whether **Zhao Tuo** exercised direct authority over the islands remains uncertain, but his establishment of the Nanyue state provided the initial institutional link between the Lingnan region and the evolving Chinese imperial system.
