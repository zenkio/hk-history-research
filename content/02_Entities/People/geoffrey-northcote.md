## Governor of Hong Kong

**Sir Geoffrey Alexander Stafford Northcote** (**羅富國**) served as the 20th Governor of Hong Kong, taking office on **October 28, 1937**, shortly after the outbreak of the Second Sino-Japanese War. His administration was defined by the immense geopolitical pressures facing the British colony as Japanese forces occupied neighboring Guangdong and advanced across South China.

## Wartime Measures and Taxation

As the refugee crisis swelled and the threat of war grew, Northcote's government faced severe financial strains. To support defense preparations and manage the influx of displaced populations, his administration introduced significant fiscal reforms, most notably the **Introduction of the War Revenue and Income Tax**. While direct income taxation was historically resisted by the local commercial elite who favored indirect taxation and laissez-faire policies, the mounting existential threat compelled the colonial government to institute these emergency measures.

## Interpretations and Legacy

British colonial historiography generally portrays Northcote as a pragmatic administrator striving to prepare an indefensible outpost under impossible strategic conditions. Conversely, local Hong Kong and New Territories communities often remember this era through the lens of acute economic hardship, air-raid preparations, and the social dislocation caused by the war on the mainland. Later scholarship frequently examines Northcote's tenure as a transitional phase where the traditional British merchant-governed status quo was forced to yield to centralized wartime mobilization.

Northcote's health deteriorated due to the strain of office, and he left Hong Kong in **September 1941**, shortly before the Battle of Hong Kong and the subsequent Japanese occupation.
