## Governorship and Infrastructure
**Sir George William Des Vœux** (德輔) served as the Governor of Hong Kong from **1887** to **1891**. His administration was marked by rapid urban expansion and the initial planning of large-scale infrastructure projects, most notably the Praya Reclamation scheme. While colonial historians often laud these efforts as foundational to the city's modern commercial success, some local scholars and community researchers argue that these land reclamation efforts primarily served the interests of elite British trading houses, often at the expense of local fishing and boat-dwelling communities.

## Medical Education and Social Reform
In **1887**, **Des Vœux** presided over the opening of the **Hong Kong College of Medicine for Chinese** (香港華人西醫書院), an institution which would later become the core of the University of Hong Kong. He served as the college’s first patron. From a colonial perspective, this was viewed as a benevolent 'civilizing' endeavor aimed at introducing Western science to the local population. Conversely, later scholarship often emphasizes the agency of the local Chinese merchant class, such as **Ho Kai** (何啟) and **Kai Ho-kai** (何啟), who were instrumental in the founding and funding of the college. This reflects a shift in historiography away from viewing the governor as a singular benefactor toward recognizing the collaborative—and at times contested—nature of educational development in colonial Hong Kong.

## Legacy
**Des Vœux** is remembered through several enduring landmarks, most notably **Des Voeux Road** (德輔道), which serves as a major artery in the Central and Western Districts. While his administrative legacy is generally viewed as one of stabilization and growth, the impact of his policies remains a subject of study. Whether his governance represented a genuine step toward social inclusion or simply a reinforcement of the colonial order is a point of ongoing academic interpretation.
