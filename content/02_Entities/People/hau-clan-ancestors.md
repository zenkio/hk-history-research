## Historical Origins
The **Hau Clan** (侯氏) ancestors are traditionally recognized as one of the Five Great Clans of the New Territories. According to local genealogical records and oral traditions maintained by the community in **Ho Sheung Heung** (河上鄉), the clan traces its migration from the mainland to the region during the late **Southern Song dynasty** (1127–1279). It is widely accepted by local historians that the founding ancestor settled in the northern New Territories, establishing a lineage that has persisted for approximately 700 to 800 years.

## Differing Interpretations of Migration
While the Hau clan maintains a consistent local narrative of early settlement, scholarly interpretations regarding the exact timing and nature of their arrival vary. 

*   **Local Tradition**: The clan archives suggest the migration was spurred by the general instability of the Song dynasty, leading to the establishment of their ancestral seat in what is now the Sheung Shui area.
*   **Later Scholarship**: Some historians note that genealogical records compiled during the Qing dynasty were often used to solidify claims of land ownership and social status. While there is no doubt regarding the clan's long-standing presence, some researchers argue that the specific "founding dates" mentioned in clan records may reflect efforts to establish seniority within the complex social hierarchy of the New Territories clans.

## Connection to Ho Sheung Heung
The Hau clan is inextricably linked to the development of **Ho Sheung Heung**, a village that remains a key site for understanding rural history in Hong Kong. The clan established the **Hau Ancestral Hall** (侯氏祖祠), which serves as the physical and spiritual center of their heritage. Throughout the colonial period, the Hau clan maintained a distinct presence, managing agricultural lands and interacting with the colonial administration primarily through the **Heung Yee Kuk** (鄉議局). Their continuity in the region provides a vital link between the pre-colonial agrarian society of the New Territories and the modern development of Northern Hong Kong.
