## Early Role and Negotiations

**Sir Henry Pottinger** (1789–1856) was a British colonial administrator and diplomat who arrived in China in **1841** to replace **Charles Elliot** as the Plenipotentiary and Superintendent of Trade. His tenure is marked by the conclusion of the First Opium War. He played a critical role in the **Negotiation of the Convention of Chuenpi** (穿鼻草約), though the agreement was initially disavowed by both the British and Qing governments. He eventually led the British to victory, culminating in the **Treaty of Nanking** (南京條約) in **1842**, which officially ceded Hong Kong Island to Britain.

## Historical Interpretations

Interpretations of **Pottinger**’s legacy differ significantly based on historical perspectives:

*   **British Colonial Perspective:** Historically, British records celebrated **Pottinger** as the 'founding father' of Hong Kong, emphasizing his role in establishing the first colonial administration and laying the legal foundations for the free port trade.
*   **Qing and Beijing Perspective:** From the standpoint of Qing officials and contemporary Beijing historiography, the treaties negotiated by **Pottinger** are classified as 'Unequal Treaties' (不平等條約), viewed as the beginning of the 'Century of Humiliation' and the forceful imposition of imperialist interests upon China.
*   **Local and Modern Scholarship:** Modern historians often highlight that while the cession of Hong Kong occurred under his administration, the local reality for the indigenous inhabitants of the New Territories and the coastal regions was often secondary to the strategic imperial interests of the British and the political survival of the Qing state. Scholarship remains divided on the extent to which he understood local demographics or merely viewed the territory as a strategic naval base.

## Governance

In **1843**, **Pottinger** was sworn in as the first Governor of Hong Kong. He established the Legislative Council, though the administrative structure remained heavily centralized under his authority. While he successfully established the initial framework of the colonial government, his stay was relatively short, and he left office in **1844**.
