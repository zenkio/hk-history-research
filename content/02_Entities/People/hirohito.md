## Role in the Occupation
**Hirohito** (昭和天皇) served as the Emperor of Japan during the period of the **Japanese Occupation of Hong Kong** (香港日治時期), which lasted from **December 25, 1941**, to **August 15, 1945**. Under the Meiji Constitution, he was considered the supreme authority of the Japanese Empire, and the military administration in Hong Kong operated in his name. His reign during this era is a subject of complex historical interpretation.

## Differing Perspectives
Historians and local communities view his role through various lenses. For the British colonial administration, Hirohito was the ultimate architect of an illegal military aggression against a British Crown Colony. Conversely, some nationalist perspectives from the era framed the occupation as part of the 'Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere,' a narrative promoted by Japanese propaganda to justify the expulsion of Western colonial powers, though this was largely rejected by the local Chinese population who suffered under severe food shortages, hyperinflation, and brutality. 

Local Hong Kong oral histories often emphasize the 'Three Years and Eight Months' (三年零八個月) of hardship. While some later scholarship debates the extent of Hirohito’s personal tactical involvement in the decision-making of the Hong Kong campaign, others focus on his symbolic role as the ultimate head of the military apparatus responsible for the wartime conditions in the territory. Following the **August 15, 1945**, broadcast—known as the *Gyokuon-hōsō* (玉音放送) or 'Jewel Voice Broadcast'—in which Hirohito announced the surrender of Japan, the occupation officially concluded. The post-war transition and the return of British rule remain significant milestones in the city's collective memory, with the figure of the Emperor serving as a symbol of both the trauma of the occupation and the end of the hostilities.
