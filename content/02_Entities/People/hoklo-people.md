## Origins and Migration
The **Hoklo people** (福佬人), also known as **Hokkien** or **Holo**, historically migrated from the **Fujian** (福建) and **Guangdong** (廣東) coastal regions to the Pearl River Delta. While precise early migration waves remain difficult to date due to sparse demographic records from the imperial periods, scholarly consensus indicates that Hoklo fishermen and traders established permanent coastal settlements alongside the **Tanka** (蛋家) people well before the British colonial era.

## Colonial Administration and Historiography
Under **British colonial** administration, Hoklo communities were often categorized broadly alongside other indigenous boat and coastal populations. **Qing dynasty** (清朝) and imperial records typically managed these maritime populations through tax and administrative registers tied to coastal defense and anti-piracy measures. Local New Territories and coastal oral histories emphasize the distinct dialect, religious practices, and maritime traditions that set the Hoklo apart from both Punti (本地) land-dwellers and Tanka boat-dwellers.

## Maritime Economy
Historically, Hoklo communities in Hong Kong were heavily engaged in deep-sea fishing, inshore fishing, and coastal commerce. Their junk-building traditions and navigation skills played a foundational role in the maritime economy of early Hong Kong. While modernization and urbanization in the **mid-20th century** led to the gradual decline of traditional fishing villages, the cultural footprint of the Hoklo remains visible in regional festivals, coastal temples, and the intangible heritage of Hong Kong's maritime history.
