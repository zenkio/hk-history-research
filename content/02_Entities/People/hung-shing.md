## Historical Background and Identity

**Hung Shing** (洪聖), formally known as **Hung Shing Wong** (洪聖王 or King Hung Shing), is rooted in the historical figure **Hung Hei** (洪熙), a Tang dynasty (618–907) official in **Guangzhou** (廣州) known for his integrity and contributions to astronomy, geography, and trade. Following his death, he was granted posthumous imperial titles by successive dynasties for allegedly protecting coastal communities from natural disasters.

## Worship and Local Communities in Hong Kong

In **Hong Kong**, the veneration of Hung Shing holds significant importance for coastal and fishing communities. Local maritime traditions position him as a protector of fishermen and traders, akin to **Tin Hau** (天后). Temples dedicated to him were established across the region during the Qing dynasty, most notably the **Hung Shing Temple in Ap Lei Chau** (鴨脷洲洪聖廟), constructed in **1773**.

* **Qing and Imperial Chinese Authorities:** The imperial state promoted the cult through posthumous titles and official recognition, integrating local folk religion into the dynastic governance structure to secure loyalty in coastal Guangdong.
* **British Colonial Administration:** Early British colonial records generally categorized these temples as sites of local superstition or community gathering, often engaging minimally with the religious practices unless public health or land administration required it.
