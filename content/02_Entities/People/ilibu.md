## Role in the Opium War
**Ilibu** (伊里布) was a prominent Manchu official who served as the Viceroy of Liangjiang during the **First Opium War**. Tasked by the **Daoguang Emperor** with managing foreign relations, he became a central figure in the shifting diplomatic landscape between the Qing Empire and Great Britain. His primary connection to Hong Kong history lies in his involvement in the early, often aborted, peace negotiations that preceded the formal signing of the **Treaty of Nanking** in **1842**.

## Differing Historical Interpretations
Interpretations of **Ilibu**'s legacy vary significantly across historiographical traditions. In many traditional Qing-era records, he is often viewed through the lens of a pragmatic official caught between an uncompromising imperial court and the overwhelming military superiority of British forces. Conversely, early British colonial perspectives frequently characterized his diplomatic maneuvers as stalling tactics designed to deceive. 

Later scholarship often frames **Ilibu** as a representative of the 'appeasement' faction within the Qing court. While some modern historians view his willingness to negotiate as a realistic assessment of the Qing military's limitations, others argue that his inability to secure favorable terms facilitated the permanent cession of Hong Kong Island. Within local Hong Kong historical narratives, he is primarily remembered as a peripheral figure to the broader geopolitical shift that placed Hong Kong under British administration, serving as a reminder of the Qing government's inability to prevent the territory's detachment from the mainland. The extent to which he personally influenced the specific terms regarding the island's cession remains a subject of ongoing historical debate, as he was often operating under strict, fluctuating directives from Beijing that limited his executive autonomy during the final negotiations.
