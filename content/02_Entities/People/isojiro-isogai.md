## Background and Military Career

**Isojiro Isogai** (**磯谷廉介**) was a career officer in the Imperial Japanese Army. Prior to his assignment in Hong Kong, he held various command and staff positions, gaining prominence during the Second Sino-Japanese War. Following the surrender of the British colonial administration on **December 25, 1941** (commonly known in Hong Kong history as **Christmas Black Day**), Japan established a military government to administer the territory.

## Governor of Occupied Hong Kong

Isogai was appointed as the Governor of the Occupied Territory of Hong Kong, taking office in **February 1942**. His administration oversaw the bitter three-and-a-half-year period of Japanese occupation, characterized by severe food shortages, hyperinflation, forced repatriation of residents to mainland China, and a policy of Japanization. 

## Historical Interpretations

Interpretations of Isogai's administration and the occupation era vary significantly among historians and local communities. British colonial historiography traditionally emphasizes the harshness of the military regime, the collapse of public health, and the suffering of the local population under Isogai's decrees. In contrast, some Japanese military records and postwar accounts framed the administration as an effort to restructure the colony within the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere, though historians generally view these claims as apologetic. 

Local Hong Kong and New Territories communities often remember the occupation through oral histories focusing on survival, resistance by the **Hong Kong-Kowloon Independent Brigade** (**港九獨立大隊**), and severe economic deprivation. Isogai's administration also intersected with various educational and cultural initiatives, including the promotion of the Japanese language, though details regarding specific institutions like the Japanese Language School remain sparsely documented in mainstream historiography. 

## Postwar Trials

Isogai left Hong Kong before the Japanese surrender in **August 1945**. Following the war, he was classified as a Class B war criminal by the Nationalist government and extradited to Nanjing, where he was tried and sentenced to life imprisonment in **1947**. He was eventually released in the early 1950s and returned to Japan, where he lived until his death.
