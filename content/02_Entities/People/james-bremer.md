## Role in the Opium War
**Sir James John Gordon Bremer** (1786–1850) was a distinguished officer of the Royal Navy. He is most significant to Hong Kong history for his role in the First Opium War, during which he acted as Commander-in-Chief of the naval forces after the death of Sir Frederick Maitland. Following the issuance of the Convention of Chuenpi, **Bremer** oversaw the landing of British forces at Possession Point on **January 26, 1841**, an event which British colonial historiography traditionally marks as the foundation of the British colony of Hong Kong.

## Perspectives on the Occupation
Interpretations of **Bremer’s** actions vary significantly depending on the historical lens applied. In traditional British colonial accounts, the landing at Possession Point is framed as the peaceful and orderly establishment of a new administrative territory. Conversely, historiography emerging from the People's Republic of China and many local scholars characterizes this event as the inception of a "century of humiliation," viewing the subsequent occupation as an illegal seizure of territory under an "unequal treaty."

Local New Territories communities and maritime history researchers have also highlighted that the area was not "empty" or "barren," as some early colonial narratives suggested. Archaeological and historical evidence confirms that the region had long been inhabited by Tanka boat people, fishers, and agrarian settlers who were displaced or marginalized by the sudden arrival of the British fleet. While **Bremer** played the central role in executing the military objective of securing the island, the long-term impacts of his actions remain a subject of active debate, balancing the strategic establishment of a global entrepôt against the coercive nature of the British colonial presence.
