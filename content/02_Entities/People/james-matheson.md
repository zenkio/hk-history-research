## Early Career and Hong Kong Foundation
**James Matheson** (1796–1878) was a key figure in the pre-colonial and early colonial history of Hong Kong. Arriving in Canton in the early 19th century, he eventually partnered with **William Jardine** in 1832 to form **Jardine, Matheson & Co.**, which became the most influential British trading firm in the region. Matheson was instrumental in the firm’s reliance on the opium trade, utilizing floating storage hulks at **Lintin Island** (伶仃島) to bypass Qing restrictions on the direct import of the drug into Canton.

## Differing Perspectives
Historically, interpretations of Matheson’s legacy vary significantly. From a traditional **British colonial** perspective, he is often viewed as a pioneer of free trade and a driving force behind the development of Hong Kong as a strategic mercantile port. Conversely, in **Qing dynasty** and modern **Beijing-centric** narratives, Matheson is frequently characterized as a 'merchant adventurer' whose illegal opium activities were the primary catalyst for the **First Opium War** (1839–1842), leading to the forced cession of Hong Kong Island.

Local **Hong Kong** historiography often focuses on the tangible legacy of the 'hongs' (foreign trading houses). While some scholars acknowledge his role in establishing the infrastructure of the city’s early economy, others point to the social consequences of the opium trade on the local population. There is a broad historical consensus that Matheson was a chief architect of the British petition to the British government to secure a permanent territory, which culminated in the 1841 occupation of Hong Kong. His firm purchased the first lots of land auctioned in Hong Kong at **East Point** (東角), establishing a long-standing corporate presence in the territory that persists today.

## Timeline

- **1832**: James Matheson and William Jardine found Jardine, Matheson & Co. in Canton.
- **1839**: Matheson leaves Canton following the destruction of opium stocks by Lin Zexu.
- **1841**: Jardine, Matheson & Co. acquires the first land grant at East Point, Hong Kong.
- **1842**: Matheson returns to Britain to serve in Parliament, representing the interests of the merchant class.
