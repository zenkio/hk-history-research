## Background and Business Career

**Jehangir Ruttonjee** (律敦治) was a key figure in Hong Kong's small but influential Parsi community. Arriving from India, he established himself as a successful businessman, notably in the brewery and shipping industries. He was the founder of the Hong Kong Brewery, which played a notable role in the local manufacturing sector during the early **20th century**.

## Philanthropy and Public Health

Ruttonjee is best remembered for his immense contributions to healthcare and philanthropy in Hong Kong, particularly during the post-World War II period. Following the Japanese occupation, Hong Kong faced severe public health crises, among which tuberculosis was one of the deadliest. 

Recognizing the urgent need for specialized medical care, Ruttonjee donated heavily to convert the former French Mission Sanatorium into a dedicated tuberculosis treatment facility. This institution, which opened as the **Ruttonjee Sanatorium for Tuberculosis** (律敦治療養院) in **1949**, became a cornerstone of Hong Kong's early public health infrastructure. 

## Historical Interpretations

In British colonial historiography, Ruttonjee is frequently celebrated as a model philanthropist whose private wealth directly supplemented inadequate colonial healthcare resources. Later scholarship and local histories often contextualize his work alongside other minority entrepreneurs, examining how diasporic communities shaped colonial welfare before the government assumed a more comprehensive role in public health.

Today, his legacy endures not only through the evolution of the Ruttonjee Hospital—which grew out of the original sanatorium—but also as an example of early elite philanthropy in shaping modern Hong Kong's social welfare systems.
