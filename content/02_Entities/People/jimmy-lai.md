## Early Life and Business Career
Born in Guangzhou in **1947**, **Jimmy Lai** (黎智英) migrated to Hong Kong as a teenager. He initially worked in garment manufacturing before founding the clothing retailer **Giordano** in **1981**. His move into media began in **1990** with the establishment of *Next Magazine*, followed by the launch of the pro-democracy newspaper ***Apple Daily*** (蘋果日報) in **1995**. Throughout the late colonial period and post-1997 era, Lai became a polarizing figure. Supporters view him as a steadfast defender of free speech and democratic values, while critics, including representatives of the Beijing and Hong Kong governments, have characterized him as an agent of foreign influence and a destabilizing force in local politics.

## Media and Political Activism
Following the 1997 handover, ***Apple Daily*** became a influential platform for dissent, frequently criticizing the policies of both the Hong Kong government and the Chinese Communist Party. Lai’s participation in various pro-democracy protests, including the **2014** Umbrella Movement and the **2019** anti-extradition bill protests, solidified his role as a central figure in the local opposition movement.

## Legal Challenges and Recent Developments
In **2020**, following the enactment of the **National Security Law** (國安法), the offices of ***Apple Daily*** were raided by police on multiple occasions. In **2021**, the newspaper ceased operations after the freezing of its assets and the arrests of several senior executives. **Jimmy Lai** himself has faced numerous legal charges, ranging from participation in unauthorized assemblies to offenses under the National Security Law. 

Interpretations of these legal proceedings vary significantly. International observers, human rights organizations, and various Western governments have expressed concern, describing the arrests and the closure of his media outlets as a dismantling of press freedom in Hong Kong. Conversely, the Hong Kong and central Chinese authorities maintain that these actions are necessary exercises of the rule of law to protect national security and maintain public order, asserting that Lai’s activities transcended legitimate journalism and constituted illegal political agitation.
