## Early Career and Police Force
**John Lee Ka-chiu** (李家超) joined the **Hong Kong Police Force** (香港警察隊) in **1977** as a probationary inspector. Over a career spanning more than three decades, he rose through the ranks to become Deputy Commissioner of Police in **2010**, gaining extensive experience in criminal investigation, intelligence, and administration.

## Security Bureau and Political Rise
In **2012**, Lee transitioned to the political appointment system, becoming Under Secretary for Security. He was subsequently promoted to **Secretary for Security** in **2017** under Chief Executive **Carrie Lam** (林鄭月娥). In this role, he was a central figure in the government's response to the **2019 anti-extradition bill protests** (反修例運動).

As Security Secretary, Lee played a direct role in enforcing central and local government directives regarding national security. Notably, in **2018**, he exercised powers under the Societies Ordinance to officially ban the independence-leaning **Hong Kong National Party** (香港民族黨), citing national security grounds. This action marked a key precedent in the proactive application of security laws against local separatist movements prior to the enactment of broader national legislation.

## Chief Executive
Following the resignation of **Carrie Lam**, Lee ran as the sole candidate in the **2022** Chief Executive election and took office on **July 1, 2022**. His administration has focused heavily on security, governance overhaul, and economic integration with the Greater Bay Area, framed by Beijing as entering a phase of "governance and prosperity" (由治及興). 

Interpretations of Lee's tenure and legacy vary sharply. Supporters view him as a decisive leader who successfully restored public order and implemented necessary national security measures after the instability of **2019**. Conversely, critics and various international observers argue that his policies have severely curtailed civil liberties, press freedom, and political pluralism in Hong Kong.
