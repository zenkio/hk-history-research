## Early Career and Police Service

**John Lee** (李家超) joined the **Hong Kong Police Force** (香港警務處) in **1977** as a probationary inspector, eventually rising to become the Deputy Commissioner of Police in **2010**. His background in law enforcement shaped his later political trajectory, distinguishing him from many of his predecessors who came through the civil service administrative branch.

## Rise in Government and Security Policies

In **2012**, Lee was appointed Under Secretary for Security, transitioning into the political appointment system, and later served as Secretary for Security from **2017** to **2021**. During his tenure as security chief, he played a central role in handling the **2019 anti-extradition bill protests** and the subsequent implementation of the **Hong Kong National Security Law** in **2020**. Supporters viewed these measures as necessary to restore public order and stability after months of civil unrest. Conversely, critics and various international observers argued that these laws severely eroded civil liberties, freedom of speech, and the autonomy promised under the "One Country, Two Systems" framework.

## Chief Executive Tenure

In **May 2022**, Lee was selected as the sole candidate in the **Chief Executive election**, taking office on **July 1, 2022**. His administration prioritized economic revival and pandemic management, leading to the **Scrapping of Mandatory Hotel Quarantine** and the eventual **Reopening of the Border with Mainland China** in **2023**. 

Under his leadership, the Legislative Council unanimously passed the Safeguarding National Security Ordinance in **March 2024**, fulfilling the constitutional obligation under **Article 23 of the Basic Law**. While Beijing and local pro-establishment figures praised the legislation as vital for closing national security loopholes, Western governments and human rights organizations expressed concern over its broad definitions and potential impact on fundamental rights.
