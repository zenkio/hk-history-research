## Historical Context and Arrival

**Jorge Álvares** (若體·阿瓦雷斯) was a 16th-century Portuguese mariner. In **1513**, under the commission of Afonso de Albuquerque, he sailed to the southern coast of China. Historical records indicate that he landed at an island known to the Portuguese as **Tamão**, which modern scholarship and local traditions frequently associate with the **Tuen Mun** (屯門) area in present-day Hong Kong, though exact geographical identification remains debated among historians.

## Trade and Subsequent Conflicts

Following Álvares's initial voyage, Portuguese merchants established a trading presence in the Pearl River Delta. These early commercial ventures quickly led to geopolitical friction with the Ming dynasty. Álvares's legacy is closely tied to the ensuing conflicts, notably the **Battle of Tunmen** in **1521** and the **Battle of Xicaowan** in **1522**, where Ming naval forces ultimately expelled the Portuguese from the immediate coastal waters of Tuen Mun.

## Interpretations in Historiography

Interpretations of Álvares and early Portuguese contact vary across historiographical traditions. British colonial-era histories often framed these early voyages as the pioneering dawn of European trade and maritime globalization in East Asia. Conversely, traditional Chinese and later mainland scholarship have frequently contextualized these events through the lens of coastal defense against foreign incursions and maritime smuggling. Local Hong Kong and New Territories communities often view these early encounters as part of the region's long, complex maritime history at the periphery of imperial power struggles.
