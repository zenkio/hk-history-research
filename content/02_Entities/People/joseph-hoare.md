## Background and Ecclesiastical Role

**Joseph Charles Hoare** (**霍約瑟**) served as the Anglican Bishop of Victoria, a diocese that at the time encompassed Hong Kong and parts of South China, from **1898** until his death in **1906**. During his tenure, the Anglican Church expanded its missionary, educational, and social welfare footprint in the region, aligning with broader British imperial and ecclesiastical networks of the late Qing period.

## Connection to St. Stephen's College

Bishop **Hoare** is particularly significant in Hong Kong educational history for his role in the founding of **St. Stephen's College** (**聖士提反書院**). Established in **1903**, the school was envisioned to provide a high-quality Western education rooted in Christian values, primarily for Chinese boys. While colonial authorities and missionary societies often emphasized Anglicization, the establishment of **St. Stephen's** also reflected a growing local demand among the Chinese elite for modern secondary education.

Historiographical interpretations regarding missionary education during this era generally diverge. British colonial and church narratives often emphasize the philanthropic and educational uplift provided by figures like **Hoare**. In contrast, modern and post-colonial scholarship sometimes examines these missionary schools through the lens of cultural imperialism, noting how they functioned to shape a loyal local elite under British administration, while simultaneously providing social mobility for Hongkongers.

## Legacy and Death

**Hoare**'s episcopate was cut short when he drowned during a typhoon in **September 1906** while on a boat trip near the coast of Guangdong. His sudden death was widely mourned within the colonial and missionary communities, and his legacy endured through the continued expansion of the institutions he helped establish, notably **St. Stephen's College**, which later relocated to **Stanley** (**赤柱**).
