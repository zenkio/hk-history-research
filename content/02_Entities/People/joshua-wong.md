## Early Activism and Scholarism

**Joshua Wong** (黃之鋒) first came to public prominence as a teenager co-founding the student activist group **Scholarism** (學民思潮) in **2011**. The group successfully campaigned against the implementation of the Moral and National Education curriculum in **2012**, which critics argued was a form of Beijing-centric political indoctrination. This campaign marked a significant turning point in Hong Kong politics, demonstrating that organized student groups could effectively mobilize public resistance against government policies.

## The Umbrella Movement

In **2014**, Wong played a leading role in the **Umbrella Movement** (雨傘運動), a 79-day occupation protest demanding genuine universal suffrage for the Chief Executive elections. Interpretations of these events vary widely. Pro-democracy supporters and local activists view Wong and his peers as courageous defenders of civil liberties and autonomy against encroaching mainland control. Conversely, the central government in Beijing and the Hong Kong administration characterized the protests as illegal disruptions fueled by external forces that threatened public order and the rule of law.

## Subsequent Legal Challenges

Following the Umbrella Movement, Wong faced numerous legal challenges related to his activism. He co-founded the political party **Demosistō** (香港眾志) in **2016** to advocate for self-determination. In **2021**, Wong was among the defendants prosecuted under the Beijing-imposed National Security Law. His ongoing legal trials and subsequent imprisonment have drawn significant international scrutiny regarding the state of human rights and judicial independence in Hong Kong.
