## Role in Colonial Administration
**K. M. A. Barnett** (貝納特) served as a dedicated colonial civil servant in Hong Kong during the mid-20th century. He is best remembered for his expertise in statistics and demographic analysis, which were crucial to the British administration’s efforts to manage the territory’s rapid post-war growth. His work provided the empirical foundation for urban planning and public resource allocation during a period of intense migration and industrialization.

## The 1961 Census
**Barnett** was appointed to serve as the Commissioner for the **1961** census. While previous counts had been conducted in the territory, the **1961** census is widely regarded by historians as the first 'modern' population census in Hong Kong’s history. It was the first to employ rigorous, scientific data collection methods on a territory-wide scale, moving beyond simple headcounts to gather detailed information on social and economic conditions. 

## Historical Perspectives
Interpretations of **Barnett’s** work vary among scholars and stakeholders. From a **British colonial** perspective, his census data is often lauded for bringing administrative efficiency to a chaotic urban environment, providing the objective evidence needed to justify government spending on housing and infrastructure. Conversely, some **later scholars** and members of the **local community** have pointed out that colonial-era statistics were inherently tied to the colonial project, sometimes masking the distinct realities of indigenous New Territories populations or the specific needs of refugee communities who may have been wary of registering with government authorities. While it is widely accepted that the **1961** census was a logistical landmark, the extent to which these figures successfully captured the lived experiences of the entire population remains a subject of ongoing academic discussion.
