## Background and Coastal Threat

**Koxinga** (鄭成功), born **Zheng Chenggong** in **1624**, was a prominent military leader who maintained loyalty to the fallen Ming dynasty after the Manchu establishment of the Qing dynasty in **1644**. Operating from southeastern coastal bases and later Taiwan, Koxinga posed a significant maritime threat to Qing authority. His ability to mobilize fleets and command local coastal populations forced the Qing court to adopt extreme defensive measures to cut off his supplies and intelligence.

## Impact on the Hong Kong Region

To counter Koxinga's influence, the Qing government implemented the **Edict of the Great Clearance** (遷海令) starting in **1661**. This policy forced the evacuation of coastal populations along the Guangdong seaboard, including the areas that now form Hong Kong and the New Territories. Settlements were abandoned, and agriculture was disrupted as the population was ordered inland. 

## Historiographical Interpretations

Interpretations of Koxinga's legacy vary significantly across different perspectives:

*   **Qing/Beijing Perspective:** Traditionally framed the Qing naval measures as necessary actions against a rebellious element to restore imperial stability and territorial security.
*   **Local New Territories Communities:** Local oral histories and lineage records often remember the Great Clearance era as a period of immense hardship, displacement, and devastation for early inhabitants of the Hong Kong region.
*   **Later Scholarship:** Modern historians view Koxinga as a complex figure bridging trade, piracy, and anti-Manchu nationalism, while analyzing the Qing clearance policies as an early instance of state-directed demographic engineering with severe local consequences.

Following Koxinga's death and the eventual Qing conquest of Taiwan, the later **Qing Encouragement of Hakka Resettlement** (客家遷界) allowed people to return and repopulate the deserted Hong Kong region, fundamentally shaping its subsequent demographics.
