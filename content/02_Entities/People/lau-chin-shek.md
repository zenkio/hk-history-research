## Early Activism and Labour Leadership
**Lau Chin-shek** (劉千石) emerged as a significant figure in the Hong Kong labour movement during the 1970s and 1980s. He was a key leader within the Christian Industrial Committee, an organization that provided support and advocacy for factory workers during the city’s rapid industrialization. His work focused on improving labour rights and collective bargaining, often positioning him at the forefront of grassroots social advocacy.

## Formation of the HKCTU
In **1990**, **Lau Chin-shek** played a central role in the formation of the **Hong Kong Confederation of Trade Unions** (香港職工會聯盟, HKCTU). The organization was established as an alternative to the pro-Beijing Hong Kong Federation of Trade Unions (香港工會聯合會), prioritizing democratic representation and independent labour advocacy. This development marked a shift in the landscape of Hong Kong's labour politics, reflecting an increasing desire for unions to operate independently of influence from either the British colonial government or the government of the People's Republic of China.
