## Early Political Career

**Lau Kong-wah** (劉江華) emerged as a significant figure in Hong Kong politics during the transition period leading up to the **1997** handover. Aligning with the pro-establishment camp, he became a founding member of the **Democratic Alliance for the Betterment of Hong Kong** (DAB, 民建聯), which later became the **Democratic Alliance for the Betterment and Progress of Hong Kong**. Through his work in district councils and later the Legislative Council (立法會), Lau positioned himself as a key voice for pro-Beijing political interests in the territory.

## Legislative Council and Electoral Reform

As a DAB legislator, Lau was directly involved in the fierce political battles over Hong Kong's constitutional development. His political alignment placed him firmly in support of Beijing-backed policies within the legislature. A pivotal moment in his legislative career occurred on **June 24, 2015**, when the Legislative Council debated and ultimately vetoed the Beijing-backed electoral reform bill. The bill, which proposed a framework for the **2017** Chief Executive election widely criticized by the pro-democracy camp as offering "fake universal suffrage," was defeated after most pro-establishment lawmakers unexpectedly walked out of the chamber just before the vote. 

## Interpretations and Later Government Service

Interpretations of Lau's political trajectory differ depending on perspective. Pro-establishment supporters viewed him as a dedicated public servant working pragmatically within the constraints of Hong Kong's political system to ensure stability and cooperation with Beijing. Conversely, pro-democracy critics and local opposition groups often characterized him and his party as instruments of mainland oversight that hindered genuine democratic development in the territory.

Following his defeat in the **2012** Legislative Council elections, Lau transitioned into executive governance. In **2015**, he was appointed as the **Secretary for Home Affairs** (民政事務局局長) by Chief Executive **CY Leung** (梁振英), a role he held until **2020**. This appointment drew mixed reactions, reflecting the broader societal polarization over governance and political loyalty in post-handover Hong Kong.
