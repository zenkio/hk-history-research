## Early Career and Political Rise
**Lau Siu-lai** (劉小麗) was a sociology lecturer at the Hong Kong Polytechnic University before entering the political sphere. She gained public prominence as a social activist, particularly through her advocacy for grassroots rights and her involvement in the 'Democracy Groundwork' movement. In the **2016** Legislative Council election, she was elected to represent the Kowloon West geographical constituency, running on a platform emphasizing progressive social policies and democratic reform.

## Oath-Taking Controversy
On **October 12, 2016**, during the inaugural meeting of the Legislative Council, **Lau Siu-lai** took her oath of office at an exceptionally slow pace, pausing for several seconds between each word. She later stated that her intention was to highlight the performative nature of the oath; however, the Legislative Council Secretary-General refused to accept the oath. A second, standard reading was administered shortly thereafter.

## Disqualification and Legal Interpretations
Following the incident, the Hong Kong government initiated a judicial review. In **July 2017**, the Court of First Instance ruled that **Lau Siu-lai** had failed to take her oath in accordance with Article **104** of the Basic Law and the Oaths and Declarations Ordinance, leading to her disqualification from the legislature. 

Interpretations of this event vary significantly among political stakeholders. Supporters of the disqualification, often aligning with the perspective of the Hong Kong government and the National People's Congress Standing Committee (NPCSC), maintain that the oath is a constitutional requirement and that the judiciary had a duty to uphold the integrity of the Basic Law. Conversely, democracy advocates and legal scholars critical of the decision argue that the disqualification constituted an excessive interference in the legislative process and undermined the will of the voters who elected her. The incident remains a significant case study in the evolving relationship between Hong Kong’s legislative independence and the central government's oversight authority.
