## Background and Connection to King Yin Lei

**Lee Ming-shu** (李明述) is best known in Hong Kong history through his association with the historic mansion **King Yin Lei** (景賢里), located on Stubbs Road on Hong Kong Island. Historical records regarding the exact commissioning and initial ownership of the estate involve multiple overlapping narratives, particularly concerning Lee and his relative **Lee Kun-yiu** (李幹耀).

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of the property's origins and the roles of the individuals involved vary among local historians and heritage preservationists:

* **Local Hong Kong and Community Accounts:** Local oral histories and property records frequently associate the land acquisition and initial planning of the mansion with the Lee family, highlighting their prominence among the Chinese mercantile elite in mid-20th-century Hong Kong.
* **Architectural and Heritage Scholarship:** Later architectural studies generally attribute the completion of the mansion to **Mr. and Mrs. Mong Man-wai** (孟浩 - though the Mong family famously purchased it later in **1978**), while recognizing the earlier Lee family tenure for establishing the residential compound originally known as *Ho Lok Tsuen* (憩廬).

Due to the scarcity of exhaustive primary documentation specifically detailing Lee Ming-shu's private commercial and personal life outside of real estate transactions, detailed consensus on his broader public impact remains limited among historians. Modern official heritage assessments by the **Antiquities Advisory Board** primarily focus on the architectural significance of the mansion rather than the biographical specifics of its early occupants.
