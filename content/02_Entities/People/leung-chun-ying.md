## Early Life and Professional Career

Born in **1954**, **Leung Chun-ying** (梁振英) trained as a surveyor in the United Kingdom before returning to Hong Kong to pursue a career in real estate and property surveying. He rose to prominence through his professional expertise and civic involvement, participating in the drafting of the **Hong Kong Basic Law** in the late **1980s**. He served on the Executive Council under both **Tung Chee-hwa** (董建華) and **Donald Tsang** (曾蔭權), establishing himself as a key figure in the territory's governance.

## Chief Executive Tenure (2012–2017)

In **2012**, **Leung** defeated **Henry Tang** (唐英年) and **Albert Ho** (何俊仁) to become the third Chief Executive. His administration faced profound societal divisions. Pro-democracy critics and local communities frequently scrutinized his housing policies and integration initiatives with mainland China. Conversely, Beijing supporters viewed his tenure as a necessary period of national alignment and infrastructural integration into the **Greater Bay Area**.

Major crises defined his term, including the **2012** protests that led to the withdrawal of the proposed **Moral and National Education** curriculum, and the **2014 Umbrella Movement**, which was sparked by the **National People's Congress Standing Committee (NPCSC)** **831 Decision** regarding universal suffrage. Historical interpretations of his leadership remain sharply contested: critics argue his hardline approach eroded local autonomy, while supporters credit him with decisive governance and prioritizing livelihood issues.

## Post-Chief Executive Role

After declining to seek a second term in **2017**, **Leung** was succeeded by **Carrie Lam** (林鄭月娥). He subsequently assumed national-level duties as a Vice-Chairman of the **National Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference (CPPCC)**, continuing to comment actively on Hong Kong's political and economic affairs.
