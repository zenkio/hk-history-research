## Early Life and Arrival in Hong Kong

**Li Ka-shing** (**李嘉誠**) was born in Chaozhou, Guangdong, in **1928**, moving to British Hong Kong as a refugee in **1940** to escape the Second Sino-Japanese War. Like many refugees of his generation, he entered the workforce at a young age, initially working in a plastics trading company. Through diligence and an understanding of emerging manufacturing techniques, he gathered the experience necessary to establish his own enterprise.

## The Plastic Flower Boom

In **1950**, Li founded Cheung Kong Plastic Factory (長江塑膠廠), named after the Yangtze River. The timing coincided with Hong Kong's broader economic transition into a manufacturing powerhouse. During the mid-**1950s**, the factory capitalized on the global popularity of polyethylene plastics, notably producing plastic flowers on a mass scale. 

From a historiographical perspective, interpretations of this era vary. British colonial administration records often emphasize the laissez-faire economic environment and the entrepreneurial drive of refugees as the primary engines of growth. Conversely, later critical scholarship and local labor histories highlight the intense exploitation, long working hours, and precarious conditions faced by factory workers—many of them women and children—who fueled the boom. Beijing-leaning narratives have historically viewed industrialists like Li as patriotic Chinese capitalists who contributed significantly to modernizing the broader Chinese economy through subsequent mainland investments following the **1978** Reform and Opening-up.

## Transition to Real Estate and Legacy

By the late **1950s** and **1960s**, as labor costs rose and international competition increased, Li successfully transitioned away from plastics, laying the groundwork for Cheung Kong's expansion into property development. While his later real estate empire defined the modern Hong Kong skyline, his entry into industrial manufacturing during the plastic flower boom remains a foundational chapter in local economic history.
