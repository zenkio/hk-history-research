## Official Visit and the 818 Incident
**Li Keqiang** (李克強) served as the Vice Premier of the People's Republic of China from **2008** to **2013** and later as the Premier from **2013** to **2023**. His most notable intersection with Hong Kong history occurred in **August 2011**, when he visited the University of Hong Kong (HKU) to attend the university's centenary celebrations. 

## Differing Interpretations
The visit, now widely referred to in local history as the **HKU 818 Incident**, remains a subject of polarized interpretation. From the perspective of the university administration and Beijing-aligned officials, the visit was an appropriate diplomatic and ceremonial engagement intended to honor a prestigious institution's history. Conversely, many students, activists, and pro-democracy advocates viewed the security arrangements as an unprecedented overreach of state power into an autonomous academic space. 

During the visit, protesters gathered on campus to express dissent, leading to a physical confrontation between students and police. Critics argued that the heavy security presence and the designation of a "restricted area" on campus undermined the university's institutional independence and historical tradition of free expression. Supporters of the security measures maintained that such protocols were standard practice for the protection of high-ranking state officials and that the institution had a duty to ensure safety during a high-profile commemorative event. Later scholarship has frequently cited this incident as a pivotal moment that signaled a shifting political climate in Hong Kong, highlighting the ongoing tensions between the principle of "One Country, Two Systems" and the local expectation of civil liberties. The event catalyzed ongoing debates about how Hong Kong's universities navigate their roles as both global research hubs and local public spheres.
