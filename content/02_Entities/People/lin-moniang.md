## Historical Origins and Apotheosis

**Lin Moniang** (林默娘) was born in **960 CE** on Meizhou Island (滛洲岛) in Fujian province during the Song dynasty (宋朝). According to hagiographic tradition, she lived a brief life dedicated to helping fishermen and predicting weather before dying tragically at sea in **987 CE**. Over subsequent centuries, local folk beliefs fused with state recognition, leading to her elevation as the Daoist deity **Tin Hau** (天后, Empress of Heaven) or Mazu (媽祖). 

## Spread of Worship in Hong Kong

The veneration of **Lin Moniang** expanded into the coastal waters of present-day Hong Kong alongside waves of migration from Fujian and Guangdong. Fishing communities and salt-panning populations established small shrines to ensure safe navigation through treacherous waters.

* **Local New Territories Communities:** For indigenous lineages and boat people (Tanka and Hoklo), Tin Hau temples functioned as both spiritual anchors and secular community centers. Festivals celebrating her birthday on the **23rd day of the 4th lunar month** reinforced kinship ties and maritime identity.
* **Qing Dynasty and Imperial State:** Imperial authorities promoted the cult as part of a broader strategy of cultural integration and maritime pacification, periodically bestowing honorary titles upon the goddess to align local cults with orthodox state ideology.
* **British Colonial Administration:** Following the British occupation of Hong Kong Island in **1841** and the New Territories in **1898**, colonial officials generally adopted a policy of non-interference toward traditional Chinese religious practices, recording Tin Hau temples primarily as native customs while utilizing temple committees for local administration.
* **Later Scholarship:** Modern historians emphasize the adaptability of Tin Hau worship, viewing the network of temples not merely as religious sites, but as crucial economic and territorial markers that mapped out the shifting domains of coastal communities in the Pearl River Delta.

Today, dozens of Tin Hau temples persist across Hong Kong, serving as tangible links to the region's maritime heritage.
