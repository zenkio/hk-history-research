## Background and Role

**Lin Zexu** (林則徐) was an influential official of the Qing dynasty (大清), best known for his role as Imperial Commissioner appointed by the **Daoguang Emperor** (道光帝) in **1838** to eradicate the opium trade in China. His uncompromising enforcement of laws against foreign and domestic opium merchants in Guangzhou (廣州) formed the immediate backdrop to the **First Opium War** (第一次鴉片戰爭).

## Actions and Connection to Hong Kong

In **1839**, Lin oversaw the **Destruction of Confiscated Opium at Humen** (虎門銷煙), destroying vast quantities of British-owned opium. His blockade of foreign factories and subsequent measures against British merchants led to the **Evacuation of British Community to Hong Kong Anchorage** (英人撤離至香港錨地). Local escalations continued with the **Lin Weixi Affair in Tsim Sha Tsui** (尖沙咀林維喜事件) and subsequent military clashes including **The Battle of Kowloon** (九龍海戰) and **The Battle of Chuenpi** (穿鼻之戰).

## Historiographical Interpretations

Interpretations of Lin Zexu's legacy vary significantly across different perspectives:

* **Qing and Mainland Scholarship:** Traditionally views Lin as a patriotic national hero who bravely defended China's sovereignty and moral standards against imperialist aggression.
* **British Colonial Historiography:** Often characterized Lin as inflexible and culturally insular, portraying his strict enforcement methods as a catalyst for a conflict that London framed around free trade and diplomatic equality.
* **Modern Scholarship:** Tends to place Lin's actions within the broader structural crises of the late Qing dynasty, noting both his administrative diligence and the limitations of his understanding of Western maritime power.
