## Ancestral Origins and Settlement
The **Liu** (廖) clan traces its ancestral origins to Fujian province before migrating southwards during the **Song Dynasty** (960–1279). According to clan genealogies (*zupu*), the early settlers arrived in the Lingnan region and eventually established a permanent presence in the area now known as **Sheung Shui** (上水) in the New Territories. 

## Historical Significance and Land Control
Historically, the **Liu** clan gained regional prominence through successful agricultural practices, particularly in rice cultivation and irrigation. By the late **Ming Dynasty** (1368–1644) and into the **Qing Dynasty** (1644–1911), they had solidified their influence as major landowners. Their economic stability allowed them to construct ancestral halls and support lineages that governed local communal life, independent of direct imperial bureaucratic oversight.

## Interpretations of Clan Alliance
In the context of the **Five Great Clans** (五大氏族) alliance, historians observe differing perspectives regarding the group's formation. While popular local traditions in the New Territories emphasize a sense of regional solidarity based on ancestral rites and defense, colonial-era British records often framed these clan alliances through the lens of organized resistance to land surveys and administrative integration following the **1898** lease of the New Territories. 

Later academic scholarship suggests that these alliances were not static entities but fluid political mechanisms used to negotiate land rights, settle village disputes, and maintain autonomy against external pressures—whether from local officials during the Qing period or the colonial administration post-**1899**. Whether these clans formed a formal, unified bloc remains a subject of academic debate, with many scholars noting that competition between clans for water rights and village boundaries frequently occurred alongside instances of cooperation. Today, the **Liu** clan remains a vital part of the **Heung Yee Kuk** (鄉議局), maintaining traditional customs and ancestral practices that continue to define the cultural landscape of the New Territories.
