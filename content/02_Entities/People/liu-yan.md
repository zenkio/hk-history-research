## Origins and Rise
**Liu Yan** (劉龑), born **Liu Yan** (劉巖) and later adopting the name **Liu Yan** (劉龑), was a warlord who established the Southern Han kingdom during the **Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms** period. Following the collapse of the Tang dynasty, he consolidated control over the Lingnan region, which included modern-day Guangdong, Guangxi, and parts of northern Vietnam. His reign is dated from **917** to **942**.

## Connection to Hong Kong
Historically, the Southern Han regime's control over the Pearl River Delta is significant for Hong Kong because it marked a period of intensified administrative presence in the coastal regions. During this time, the area that is now Hong Kong saw continued development of salt production and maritime trade. Archaeological findings in the New Territories, including ceramics and coins associated with the Southern Han, suggest that the region was integrated into the kingdom’s economic network.

## Historical Interpretations
Historians offer varying perspectives on **Liu Yan**’s impact on local history. From the perspective of later imperial historiography, particularly during the Qing dynasty (清朝), the Southern Han was often dismissed as a regional, short-lived regime prone to extravagance and internal instability. Conversely, contemporary local historians and scholars in Hong Kong often view the Southern Han as a critical period in the region's development, arguing that it accelerated the permanent settlement of the Lingnan coast. 

Colonial-era British historians occasionally highlighted the maritime importance of the territory under Southern Han rule as a precursor to Hong Kong's role as a global entrepôt. However, details regarding the specific administrative status of the Hong Kong islands during his reign remain sparse in official records. While it is widely accepted that the region fell under Southern Han jurisdiction, the extent of local governance and the daily impact of his policies on the indigenous population remains a subject of ongoing academic investigation.
