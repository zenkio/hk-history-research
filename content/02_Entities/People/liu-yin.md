## Role in the Lingnan Region

**Liu Yin** (劉隱, 874–911) was a prominent military figure who rose to power in the Lingnan region (comprising modern-day Guangdong and Guangxi) during the chaotic decline of the **Tang Dynasty**. Appointed as the Military Commissioner (Jiedushi) of Qinghai (清海) in **907**, he exerted significant control over the coastal trade routes and regional administrative centers. While he nominally served the **Later Liang** (後梁) dynasty, he functioned effectively as an independent ruler, setting the stage for his brother, **Liu Yan** (劉龑), to later establish the **Southern Han** (南漢) kingdom.

## Connection to Hong Kong

Historical records from this era are sparse regarding specific administrative activities in the immediate territory of present-day Hong Kong. However, as **Liu Yin** consolidated power over the Guangzhou region, his influence over the coastal waters—including the area around the Pearl River Delta and the **Tuen Mun** (屯門) garrison—is generally recognized by historians as critical to maintaining maritime security and trade control.

## Historical Perspectives

Interpretations of **Liu Yin**'s legacy vary across different historiographical schools:

* **Traditional Mainland Historiography:** Often focuses on his role in stabilizing the Lingnan region amidst the fragmentation of the late Tang, viewing his governance as a precursor to regional autonomy.
* **Local Hong Kong and Regional Perspectives:** Many local historians view this period as a critical era where the coastal settlements of the New Territories and Lantau Island were increasingly integrated into the maritime networks of the Pearl River Delta. While direct documentation of his governance in Hong Kong remains elusive, his strengthening of the naval infrastructure in the region is seen as a formative moment for the area's development as a maritime gateway.
* **Modern Scholarship:** Scholars emphasize that the extent of **Liu Yin**'s direct administration over the coastal islands is uncertain, as the region was often prone to shifting loyalties and pirate activity. It is widely accepted that his efforts to control trade routes fundamentally shaped the regional power dynamics that allowed the Southern Han to flourish in the decades that followed.
