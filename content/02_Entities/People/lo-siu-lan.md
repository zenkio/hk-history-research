## Background and the Link REIT Challenge

**Lo Siu-lan** (盧少蘭) came to public prominence as a retired public housing resident living in **Choi Hung Estate** (彩虹邨). In **2004**, she initiated a landmark legal challenge against the Hong Kong Housing Authority's plan to divest its retail and parking facilities through the listing of the **Link REIT** (領匯房產基金, later 領展).

## Legal Battle and Implications

Acting as a litigant in person initially, Lo filed for a judicial review arguing that the Housing Authority lacked the statutory authority under the **Housing Ordinance** (房屋條例) to divest its assets. Her action temporarily halted the multi-billion-dollar initial public offering, which had been scheduled for **December 2004**, causing widespread political and financial debate.

While the Court of Final Appeal eventually ruled in favor of the Housing Authority in **January 2005**, allowing the listing to proceed, Lo's action became a powerful symbol of grassroots resistance against perceived corporate privatization of public goods.

## Historical Assessment

Interpretations of Lo's actions vary across different sectors of Hong Kong society. Critics of the divestment and local community groups lauded her as a courageous ordinary citizen standing up against institutional power to protect tenants' interests from rising rents and reduced services. Conversely, government officials and financial proponents viewed the legal challenge as a costly delay to necessary market reforms and public asset management restructuring. Later legal and political scholarship often cites the **Lo Siu-lan v. Housing Authority** case as a watershed moment highlighting the growing use of judicial review by ordinary citizens in Hong Kong to challenge major public policy decisions.
