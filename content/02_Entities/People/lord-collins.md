## Judicial Career and Hong Kong
**Lord Collins** (郝廉思), a distinguished British jurist and former Justice of the Supreme Court of the United Kingdom, served as a Non-Permanent Judge of the **Hong Kong Court of Final Appeal** (香港終審法院) from **2011** to **2024**. His appointment was part of a long-standing practice designed to maintain international confidence in Hong Kong's legal system through the inclusion of eminent common law judges from overseas jurisdictions.

## The 2024 Resignation
In **June 2024**, **Lord Collins** resigned from his position alongside **Lord Sumption**. While he provided no immediate public statement detailing the specific reasons for his departure at the time of the announcement, the resignations occurred against the backdrop of the city's implementation of the **Safeguarding National Security Ordinance** (維護國家安全條例). 

## Differing Perspectives
Interpretations of his resignation vary significantly. The **Hong Kong Government** and supporters of the current legal order emphasized that the city’s judiciary remains independent and that the departure of overseas judges does not undermine the rule of law, noting that many other non-permanent judges remain in their posts. Conversely, critics and some international legal observers argued that the resignations were a reaction to the restrictive political environment and the perceived erosion of judicial autonomy under the **National Security Law** (國家安全法). Some scholars suggest that the departure of such high-profile figures signals a shift in the perceived viability of maintaining the 'One Country, Two Systems' model through a hybrid legal framework, while others argue that the judicial system's functionality remains robust despite these departures.
