## Role in the Second Opium War and Hong Kong

**Lord Elgin** (額爾金), formally known as **James Bruce, 8th Earl of Elgin**, served as High Commissioner and Plenipotentiary in China during the **Second Opium War** (**1856**–**1860**). Operating from the strategic base of Hong Kong, Elgin directed military campaigns that culminated in the capture of Beijing and the destruction of the Old Summer Palace.

## The Convention of Peking

His actions led directly to the signing of the **Convention of Peking** (**1860**), which formalized the ceding of the **Kowloon Peninsula** (九龍半島) south of Boundary Street to Great Britain. This expansion provided the burgeoning colony of Hong Kong with vital military defense grounds and space for future commercial development.

## Historiographical Interpretations

Interpretations of Lord Elgin's legacy diverge significantly:

* **British colonial historiography** traditionally framed Elgin as a pragmatic diplomat who opened China to modernization and international trade, securing necessary territory to ensure the security and economic viability of Hong Kong.
* **Qing and mainland Chinese scholarship** views Elgin as an imperialist aggressor whose military campaigns enforced unequal treaties, devastated cultural heritage, and initiated a century of national humiliation.
* **Local Hong Kong and New Territories perspectives** often emphasize the abrupt displacement of indigenous residents and the geopolitical partitioning of the region without local consultation, framing the territorial expansion purely through the lens of colonial land acquisition.
