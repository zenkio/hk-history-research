## Role in Higher Education

**Lord Fulton** (富爾敦) was a prominent British educator appointed to chair a commission in **1963** tasked with advising on the creation of a new university in Hong Kong that would use Chinese as a primary language of instruction. At the time, the University of Hong Kong (香港大學) operated primarily in English.

## The Fulton Commission

The commission's report, published in **1963**, proposed federating three existing post-secondary Chinese-language colleges—New Asia College (新亞書院), Chung Chi College (崇基學院), and United College (聯合書院)—into a single federal institution. This recommendation led directly to the founding of the Chinese University of Hong Kong (中文大學) in **1963**. **Lord Fulton** subsequently returned to head a second review commission in **1976**, which recommended restructuring the university from a federal model into a more centralized unitary system. This later intervention sparked considerable debate among college traditionalists who favored preserving the autonomy of the individual founding colleges.

## Historiographical Perspectives

From a British colonial administrative perspective, **Lord Fulton**'s work was viewed as a successful modernization effort that expanded higher education access for the local population. However, interpretations differ regarding the degree to which the commissions respected local academic traditions versus imposing British institutional frameworks. Later scholarship often examines the Fulton reports as a critical turning point in the institutionalization and official recognition of Chinese-medium higher education in British-administered Hong Kong, balancing local cultural aspirations with colonial administrative goals.
