## Role in the First Opium War
**Henry John Temple, 3rd Viscount Palmerston** (巴麥尊) served as the British Foreign Secretary during the period leading up to and including the First Opium War. In **1839**, he ordered the **British Expeditionary Fleet** to assemble in East Asia to secure trade rights, address the destruction of opium in Canton (Guangzhou), and demand formal diplomatic equality with the Qing Empire.

## Territorial Ambitions
Historical interpretations of **Palmerston**’s objectives vary. Traditional British imperial scholarship often describes his actions as a necessary defense of free trade and British honor against the restrictive Qing tributary system. Conversely, historians associated with the Beijing and post-colonial traditions frequently characterize his policy as a calculated exercise in 'gunboat diplomacy,' designed to force the opening of Chinese markets through military aggression.

Regarding the cession of Hong Kong Island, **Palmerston** famously expressed skepticism in **1841** upon hearing of the island's potential acquisition. In a dispatch to **Charles Elliot** (義律), the Plenipotentiary in China, **Palmerston** dismissed Hong Kong as a 'barren island with hardly a house upon it,' famously stating it would never be a mart of trade. He preferred the acquisition of a more established commercial hub like Chusan (Zhoushan). However, once the **Treaty of Nanking** was signed in **1842**, the British government solidified its control over the island. Whether **Palmerston** eventually viewed Hong Kong as a strategic success remains a subject of academic debate, with some scholars suggesting he remained indifferent to its long-term development compared to more prominent treaty ports.
