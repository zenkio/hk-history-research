## Role in Nanyue Politics
**Lu Jia** (呂嘉) served as the Prime Minister (Chancellor) of the Nanyue (南越) kingdom during the reigns of the final three monarchs. He was a highly influential figure of Yue ethnicity, holding significant sway over the Nanyue court. Historically, he is recognized for his staunch opposition to the increasing political influence of the Han dynasty (漢朝) under Emperor Wu of Han.

## Conflict and Legacy
In **112 BCE**, after the death of King Zhao Xing, **Lu Jia** led a coup d'état to depose the pro-Han Queen Dowager Jiu and the monarch, installing Zhao Jiande on the throne. This act of defiance triggered the **Han conquest of Nanyue** in **111 BCE**. The resulting military campaign led to the collapse of the Nanyue state and the direct incorporation of the Lingnan region, including present-day Hong Kong, into the Han administrative system.

## Historical Interpretations
Interpretations of **Lu Jia** vary significantly across historiographical traditions. Traditional Qing-era (清朝) scholarship often depicted him as a treacherous rebel who disrupted the unification of China. Conversely, some modern local historical perspectives, particularly those focusing on the regional identity of the Lingnan area, view him as a symbol of indigenous resistance against northern centralized imperial power. British colonial-era historians generally framed his actions through the lens of early imperial expansion, noting the transition from a tributary state to a commandery (jundao) system. While specific details of his life remain sparse in primary sources like the *Records of the Grand Historian*, he remains a pivotal figure in understanding the administrative shifts that brought the Pearl River Delta under the governance of the Chinese imperial state.
