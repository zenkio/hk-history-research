## Role in the Final Years of the Southern Song

**Lu Xiufu** (陸秀夫) served as a high-ranking official and chancellor during the waning years of the Southern Song dynasty. As Mongol forces advanced, **Lu** and fellow loyalist **Zhang Shijie** (張世傑) escorted the imperial court southward, briefly establishing the **Establishment of Imperial Residence at Sacred Hill** (聖山行宮) in the area that is now Kowloon City in **Hong Kong** (香港).

## Departure and the Battle of Yamen

In **1277**, the **Song Court Departs Hong Kong Waters for Yamen** (宋朝朝廷離開香港水域前往崖門) under military pressure from the expanding Yuan dynasty. The loyalist fleet ultimately met its defeat at the **Battle of Yamen** (崖門海戰) in **1279**. Rather than surrender, **Lu Xiufu** strapped the young **Emperor Huaizong** (宋懷宗 / 趙昺) to his back and leaped into the ocean, an act of ultimate loyalty that defined the end of the dynasty.

## Historiography and Local Veneration

Interpretations of **Lu Xiufu** and the Southern Song loyalists vary across different historical perspectives. **Qing dynasty** (清朝) and later **Beijing** official historiography praised **Lu** as the epitome of Confucian loyalty and righteousness (*忠君*). **British colonial** accounts often viewed these events through a more detached administrative lens, recording local traditions without ideological endorsement.

Meanwhile, **local Hong Kong and New Territories communities** traditionally viewed these figures with reverence. Some oral histories and local belief systems connect the veneration of deities such as **Hau Wong** (侯王) to figures associated with the loyalist entourage, including **Yeung Ngai** (楊亮節), reflecting how imperial history became embedded in the ritual life of local lineages, though exact genealogical and historical links remain a subject of scholarly discussion and uncertainty.
