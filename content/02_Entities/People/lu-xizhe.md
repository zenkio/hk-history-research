## Historical Role
**Lu Xizhe** (魯希哲) is primarily recognized for his contribution to the local historiography of the Pearl River Delta region, specifically through his work on the **Xin'an County Gazetteer** (新安縣志). During the early **Qing Dynasty**, specifically in **1688**, **Lu Xizhe** acted as one of the primary compilers of this gazetteer, which remains one of the earliest official records documenting the administrative history and geography of what is now Hong Kong.

## Connection to Hong Kong
Before the **1841** British colonial acquisition of Hong Kong Island, the area was administered under **Xin'an County** (新安縣). The gazetteer compiled by **Lu Xizhe** provides researchers with critical data regarding the region's land use, coastal defenses, and cultural landmarks, such as the **Tuen Mun** (屯門) area. Historians utilize his work to establish the baseline for early maritime activities and ancestral lineage records in the **New Territories**.

## Interpretations and Scholarship
Interpretations of **Lu Xizhe**’s work vary across historiographical traditions. Scholars focusing on the **Qing Dynasty** state administrative apparatus often view the gazetteer as a project to solidify imperial control over a newly settled coastline following the end of the **Great Clearance** (遷界令). In contrast, local genealogists and researchers of the **New Territories** indigenous communities often analyze his work for its preservation of regional folk customs and ancestral village histories that predated the arrival of the British. While the accuracy of specific geographical boundaries mentioned in his text is subject to debate among modern cartographic historians, the document is widely accepted as a seminal source for understanding the pre-colonial administrative structure of Hong Kong.
