## Role at Causeway Bay Books
**Lui Por** (呂波) served as a business manager for **Causeway Bay Books** (銅鑼灣書店), a retail outlet in Hong Kong known for selling publications critical of the Chinese Communist Party leadership. These books, often categorized as political gossip or unauthorized biographies, were highly sought after by visitors from mainland China. In the context of Hong Kong’s history, the store functioned as a symbol of the city's unique legal environment under the "One Country, Two Systems" framework, which protected the rights to publish and distribute sensitive political materials that remained censored across the border.

## Disappearance and Interpretations
In **October 2015**, **Lui Por** went missing in Shenzhen. His disappearance occurred in tandem with that of other associates linked to the store and its parent company, Mighty Current, including **Gui Minhai** (桂民海) and **Lam Wing-kee** (林榮基). 

The circumstances surrounding these events are a subject of debate. Pro-democracy activists and international human rights organizations viewed the disappearances as extrajudicial abductions carried out by mainland Chinese security agents, effectively bypassing the Basic Law and undermining Hong Kong's judicial autonomy. Conversely, authorities in mainland China later described the individuals as having surrendered themselves voluntarily to assist in investigations regarding the illegal sale of books in the mainland. **Lui Por** eventually appeared on state-controlled media, stating that he had returned to the mainland of his own volition to cooperate with authorities. Critics argue that such public confessions are typically coerced, while supporters of the state position maintain that the legal processes were conducted within the bounds of mainland law. The episode remains a pivotal case study in the tension between mainland Chinese legal jurisdiction and the liberties traditionally enjoyed by Hong Kong residents.
