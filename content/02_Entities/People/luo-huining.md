## Background and Appointment

**Luo Huining** (駆惠寧) was born in October **1954** in Yiwu, Zhejiang province. Before his assignment to Hong Kong, he built an extensive career within the Chinese Communist Party apparatus, serving in senior provincial leadership roles including party secretary of Qinghai and Shanxi provinces. In January **2020**, Luo was appointed as the Director of the Liaison Office of the Central People's Government in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR), succeeding Wang Zhimin (王志沿c).

## Implementation of National Security Legislation

Luo's tenure coincided with a major turning point in Hong Kong's governance following the widespread social unrest of **2019**. Shortly after the enactment of the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region in July **2020**, Luo was appointed concurrently as the National Security Adviser to the newly established Committee for Safeguarding National Security of the HKSAR.

From the perspective of Beijing and official state media, Luo's leadership was characterized as crucial for restoring public order, ending prolonged political instability, and establishing a robust legal framework to defend national sovereignty. Conversely, critics, foreign governments, and various human rights organizations argued that the sweeping enforcement of the national security law under administrators like Luo severely curtailed civil liberties, freedom of speech, and political opposition in the territory.

## Later Career and Retirement

Luo stepped down from his role as Director of the Liaison Office in January **2023**, succeeded by Zheng Yanxiong (鄭雅雄). Shortly after his departure from Hong Kong, he was appointed to the Financial and Economic Affairs Committee of the National People's Congress.
