## Early Premiership and the Hong Kong Question

When **Margaret Thatcher** became British Prime Minister in **1979**, the impending expiration of the 98-year New Territories lease in **1997** loomed large over Hong Kong's economic stability. Property developers and investors increasingly sought clarity on the territory's long-term future. 

## The **1982** Beijing Visit and Sovereignty Talks

In **September 1982**, **Thatcher** travelled to Beijing to meet Chinese leader **Deng Xiaoping** (鄧小平). This high-stakes diplomatic encounter marked the official start of negotiations concerning Hong Kong's future. 

Interpretations of her visit and the subsequent talks vary widely:
* **British Colonial Perspective:** London initially hoped to maintain British administration after **1997** while acknowledging Chinese sovereignty, viewing continued British governance as vital for maintaining the rule of law and economic prosperity.
* **Beijing Perspective:** The Chinese government maintained that the 19th-century treaties concerning Hong Kong were unequal and non-negotiable. **Deng Xiaoping** firmly insisted on the complete recovery of sovereignty over the entire territory, rejecting any notion of continued British administration.
* **Local Hong Kong Perspective:** The negotiations caused considerable anxiety among local residents regarding their future political rights, civil liberties, and economic security, accelerating emigration waves among certain professional classes.

## Economic Policies and the Linked Exchange Rate

Beyond constitutional talks, her administration oversaw broader financial adjustments affecting the territory. Amid economic volatility in the early **1980s**, the Hong Kong government—with British backing—implemented the **Establishment of the Hong Kong Dollar Peg** in **1983**, pegging the local currency to the US dollar to restore financial confidence.

## The Joint Declaration

Following years of arduous diplomatic dialogue, **Thatcher** returned to Beijing in **December 1984** to sign the **Signing of the Sino-British Joint Declaration** (中英聯合聲明), which formally set the date for the transfer of sovereignty to the People's Republic of China on **July 1, 1997**.
