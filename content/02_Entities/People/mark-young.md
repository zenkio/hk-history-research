## Governorship and Post-War Reconstruction
**Sir Mark Young** served as the Governor of Hong Kong from **1941** to **1947**. His tenure was dramatically interrupted by the Japanese occupation of Hong Kong during the Second World War. Following his internment and subsequent liberation, he returned to the colony in **1946** with the intention of implementing significant administrative reforms to modernise the colonial government.

## The Young Plan
Upon his return, **Young** advocated for the creation of a municipal council with an elected element, a proposal widely referred to as the 'Young Plan'. Interpretations of his motives vary among historians: some colonial-era accounts suggest he aimed to provide Hong Kong with a greater sense of autonomy and political representation, while later scholars often argue these reforms were intended to bolster the legitimacy of British rule in a shifting post-colonial global landscape. These reforms were ultimately delayed and later scaled back, reflecting the complex political balancing act between local aspirations and British interests.

## Rent Control Ordinance
One of the most enduring legacies of his administration was the introduction of the **Rent Control Ordinance** in **1947**. As Hong Kong faced a massive influx of refugees and severe housing shortages in the immediate aftermath of the war, the government sought to prevent exorbitant rent hikes. From a local community perspective, this measure was essential for social stability; however, some contemporaneous business interests argued it discouraged the redevelopment of war-damaged properties. The ordinance remained a significant, albeit debated, component of Hong Kong’s housing policy for decades, highlighting the government’s shift toward more interventionist measures in the post-war period. His term ended in **1947** due to ill health, leaving a complex legacy regarding Hong Kong's path toward political and social reform.
