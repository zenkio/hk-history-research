## Role in Coastal Administration
During the **Ming Dynasty (1368–1644)**, officials administered the coastal areas of modern-day Hong Kong primarily through the jurisdiction of **Dongguan County (東莞縣)**, and later **Xinan County (新安縣)**, which was established in **1573**. These officials were responsible for the maintenance of maritime order, the collection of salt taxes, and the regulation of local industries, including the historically significant pearl harvesting activities in the Tolo Harbour region.

## Pearl Farming and Tax Reform
Interpretations regarding the efficacy and impact of these officials vary. Some scholars emphasize the role of local Ming officials in implementing tax reforms intended to stabilize the coastal economy and curb smuggling. Others suggest that the heavy bureaucratic oversight and the demands for high-quality pearls imposed significant hardships on local communities. 

## Differing Historical Perspectives
Perspectives on the intent of these officials differ between historical schools of thought. 
* **Local New Territories narratives** often portray these officials as distant figures whose tax policies necessitated resistance or negotiation by local clan groups. 
* **Mainland Chinese scholarship** frequently emphasizes the importance of these administrative structures in integrating the region into the broader imperial system and ensuring sovereignty over maritime borders. 
* **Colonial-era historians** often framed this period as one of relative administrative instability, arguing that the remoteness of the region meant that imperial control was frequently interrupted by piracy and local autonomy. 

Due to the loss of many regional administrative records during the **Great Clearance (遷界令)** of the early **Qing Dynasty (1661–1669)**, the precise day-to-day functions and personal names of many local officials stationed in the Hong Kong area remain largely obscured. While their influence on the development of land-use patterns and regional trade is recognized, much of their specific administrative reach remains a subject of historical debate.
