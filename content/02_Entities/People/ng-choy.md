## Early Legal Career
**Ng Choy** (伍才), also widely known as **Wu Tingfang** (伍廷芳), was born in Singapore in **1842** and raised in Hong Kong. He pursued legal studies in Britain and was called to the Bar at Lincoln's Inn in **1876**, becoming the first Chinese barrister in Hong Kong history. His professional entry into the colonial legal system was a significant milestone, as it challenged existing racial barriers in the legal profession.

## Legislative Council Appointment
In **1880**, Governor **John Pope Hennessy** appointed **Ng Choy** to the Legislative Council. This was a landmark event, as he became the first Chinese person to hold such a position. His appointment was a key part of **Hennessy**'s broader reforms, which aimed to soften the rigid racial segregation and discrimination pervasive in colonial administration. 

## Interpretations of His Legacy
Historical interpretations of **Ng Choy**'s tenure vary significantly. From a British colonial perspective, his appointment is often cited as a move toward progressive integration and meritocracy. Conversely, some later scholars and local historians view his role as a complex navigation between colonial co-optation and the burgeoning Chinese intellectual movement. While some emphasize his contribution to local administrative reform, others highlight his later career as a diplomat for the Qing government as evidence of his broader commitment to Chinese national modernization. His transition from a colonial appointee to a key official representing China's interests abroad remains a subject of study regarding the dual identity of Hong Kong's early Chinese elite.
