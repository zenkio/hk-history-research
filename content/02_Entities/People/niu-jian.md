## Role in the Opium War
**Niu Jian** (牛鑑) held the high-ranking post of Viceroy of Liangjiang during the **First Opium War**. His involvement in Hong Kong's history is inextricably linked to the cessation of hostilities between the Qing Empire and Great Britain. In 1842, following the British capture of **Zhenjiang** and their advancement toward **Nanjing**, the Qing court appointed **Niu Jian** alongside **Qiying** (耆英) and **Yilibu** (伊里布) to negotiate terms with the British plenipotentiary **Sir Henry Pottinger**.

## Historical Interpretations
Historians offer varying perspectives on **Niu Jian**'s role. Traditional Qing historiography often depicts officials like **Niu Jian** as being forced into disadvantageous treaties by overwhelming British naval force. Conversely, some later scholars and British colonial perspectives emphasize the diplomatic failures and strategic hesitation of Qing officials, suggesting their limited understanding of international law and maritime power accelerated the signing of the **Treaty of Nanking** in August **1842**.

## Connection to Hong Kong
The connection to Hong Kong remains indirect but significant. As a signatory and primary administrator overseeing the coastal provinces, **Niu Jian** was a party to the formal transfer of Hong Kong Island to the British Crown under Article III of the treaty. While **Qiying** is often the focus of scholarship regarding the treaty’s execution, **Niu Jian**’s presence was central to the Qing administrative response to the loss of Hong Kong. Because he was tasked with managing the immediate aftermath of the war, his correspondence provides insight into how the Qing bureaucracy attempted to stabilize the region following the establishment of the British colony. Whether his actions represented a pragmatic attempt to save the dynasty or a submission to imperialist encroachment remains a subject of ongoing academic debate.
