## Origins and Context
The **First Hong Kong Industrial Exhibition** was held in **1938** at the Tai Po Market (大埔墟). At the time, the event served as a pivotal showcase for locally produced goods, signaling a shift in the colony's economic identity from a mere entrepôt toward a rising center of light manufacturing. While there is no historical consensus regarding a specific individual organizer, the exhibition was the result of collaborative efforts by local business chambers and industrial advocates.

## Historical Interpretations
Perspectives on the significance of the exhibition vary depending on the historical framework applied:

* **Colonial Perspective:** British colonial records often framed the exhibition as a testament to the stability and economic potential of Hong Kong under the British administration, particularly as a response to the disruption of trade caused by the Second Sino-Japanese War.
* **Local Hong Kong Perspective:** For local merchants and the burgeoning industrial class, the exhibition was a grassroots demonstration of resilience and self-reliance, proving that Hong Kong could sustain itself despite the collapse of mainland markets during the wartime era.
* **Modern Scholarship:** Later historians often view the exhibition as a critical junction in Hong Kong's identity formation. It is frequently analyzed as the precursor to the larger **Hong Kong Products Exhibition** (香港產品展覽會) held in the post-war decades, which played an essential role in defining the 'Made in Hong Kong' brand.
