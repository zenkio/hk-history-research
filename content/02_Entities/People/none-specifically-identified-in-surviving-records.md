## Interpretations of Educational Expansion
Historians offer varying perspectives on the drivers of this movement. **British colonial** perspectives, as reflected in early administrative surveys, often framed these schools as vestiges of traditional order that persisted alongside colonial governance. In contrast, **New Territories** local genealogies often emphasize these institutions as indigenous efforts led by local gentry to preserve cultural continuity amidst rapid regional changes. **Later scholarship** has moved beyond these binary views, suggesting that these schools were sites of negotiation where local communities balanced adherence to imperial, scholar-official values with the pragmatic needs of an island population heavily engaged in salt production, agriculture, and maritime trade.
