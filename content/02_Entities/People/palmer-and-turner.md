## Origins and Development

**Palmer and Turner** was established in Hong Kong in **1868** by William Salway. It evolved into a partnership between **Clement Palmer** and **Arthur Turner** in **1891**. As the longest-established architectural practice in the territory, the firm played a pivotal role in shaping the physical environment of British Hong Kong during the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

## Architectural Legacy

The firm became the primary architect for the **Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation (HSBC)**, securing their reputation as the architects of Hong Kong's financial and colonial power. Their most notable contribution in this period was the **Third HSBC Building**, opened in **1935**. At the time of its completion, it was hailed as the most modern building in the Far East. 

Interpretations of their work vary among historians. British colonial accounts often frame the firm's output as a reflection of Hong Kong’s rapid modernization and professional advancement under British administration. Conversely, some post-colonial scholars and local historians observe that these structures served as symbols of institutional authority that reinforced the colonial hierarchy. While the aesthetic merit of their neoclassical and later Art Deco designs is widely acknowledged, the impact of these projects on the urban fabric—often involving the displacement of earlier local structures—remains a subject of nuanced discussion.

Beyond the **Third HSBC Building**, the firm designed numerous landmarks including the **Queen's Building**, the **Prince's Building**, and the **Bank of China Building** (the pre-war structure). Their influence was not limited to commerce; they were involved in various public and ecclesiastical projects that defined the aesthetic of the Central district. The firm continues to operate today, maintaining a continuous presence in Hong Kong's development that spans over 150 years.
