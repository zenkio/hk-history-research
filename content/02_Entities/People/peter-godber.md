## Career in the Hong Kong Police Force
**Peter Godber** (葛柏) served as a Chief Superintendent in the Royal Hong Kong Police Force during the 1960s and early 1970s. During this era, corruption within the police force and other government departments was widely regarded by contemporary observers and later historians as endemic. In **1973**, an investigation led by the Anti-Corruption Office of the police uncovered evidence that **Godber** had amassed unexplained wealth significantly exceeding his official salary, estimated by investigators at the time to be approximately HK$4.3 million.

## The Escape and Public Outcry
In June **1973**, while under investigation and forbidden from leaving the territory, **Godber** managed to bypass security at Kai Tak Airport and fled to the United Kingdom. This escape shocked the Hong Kong public, leading to widespread protests and student-led demonstrations under the slogan "Anti-Corruption, Arrest Godber" (反貪污，捉葛柏). The public's loss of confidence in the colonial government's ability to police itself became a defining political issue of the period.

## The Establishment of the ICAC
Facing intense domestic pressure, Governor **Murray MacLehose** commissioned a report from barrister **Alastair Blair-Kerr**. Following the subsequent recommendations, the Independent Commission Against Corruption (**ICAC**) was established in **1974**. In **1975**, **Godber** was extradited back to Hong Kong from England, tried, and sentenced to four years in prison for bribery. 

## Historical Interpretations
Perspectives on the **Godber** affair vary. Many in the Hong Kong public viewed his actions as the quintessential example of colonial indifference and systemic institutional rot. From the perspective of some colonial administrative officials, the scandal highlighted the urgent need to reform policing structures to ensure long-term stability. Later scholars often debate whether the ICAC's formation was a proactive attempt to clean up the government or a reactive measure necessitated by the intense public pressure that threatened the legitimacy of the British administration.
