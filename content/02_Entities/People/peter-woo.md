## Business Career
**Peter Woo** (吳光正) is a leading figure in Hong Kong’s corporate landscape, long associated with **Wheelock and Company** and **The Wharf (Holdings) Limited**. Through these conglomerates, **Woo** oversaw significant real estate and infrastructure developments, including major shopping malls like Harbour City in Tsim Sha Tsui. His leadership in these enterprises positioned him as one of the most influential tycoons during the final decades of British colonial rule and the early years of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR).

## Political Involvement
**Woo** emerged as a key political figure during the lead-up to the 1997 handover. In **1996**, he was a candidate for the first Chief Executive of Hong Kong, eventually losing the election to **Tung Chee-hwa** (董建華). Following the transition, **Woo** served as a member of the **Executive Council** of Hong Kong from **2000** to **2012**. 

## Historical Perspectives
Interpretations of **Woo**'s political legacy vary. Some analysts view his career as a continuation of the powerful alliance between the colonial-era business establishment and the newly established HKSAR government, providing stability during a period of transition. Conversely, critics and some segments of the public have at times scrutinized the concentration of economic power held by major conglomerates, debating the extent to which such influence has shaped public policy and housing accessibility in Hong Kong. Scholars often point to his candidacy in **1996** as a reflection of the selection process favored by Beijing at the time, which prioritized candidates with deep roots in the city's corporate sector.
