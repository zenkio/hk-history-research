## Role in the First Opium War

**Qishan** (琦善) entered the historical trajectory of Hong Kong following the dismissal of **Lin Zexu** (林則徐) in late **1840**. Appointed by the **Daoguang Emperor** (道光帝) as the imperial commissioner, **Qishan** was dispatched to Guangdong to negotiate an end to the First Opium War with British plenipotentiary **Charles Elliot** (查理·義律).

## The Convention of Chuenpi

Negotiations culminated in the unauthorized **Convention of Chuenpi** (穿鼻草約) in **January 1841**. Under this draft agreement, **Qishan** agreed to the ceding of Hong Kong Island to the British Crown, alongside an indemnity and the reopening of trade. However, the agreement was never formally ratified by either the Qing court or the British government.

## Historiography and Interpretations

Interpretations of **Qishan**'s actions vary significantly across different historical perspectives. 

* **Qing and Beijing official historiography** traditionally condemned **Qishan** as a traitor who capitulated to foreign aggression without imperial authorization, leading to his subsequent arrest, stripping of titles, and confiscation of property by the **Daoguang Emperor**.
* **British colonial narratives** often viewed him as a pragmatic, albeit constrained, official who recognized the military reality of Qing inferiority.
* **Later scholarship** suggests **Qishan** was attempting a stalling strategy to appease a superior foreign military force while preserving the Qing dynasty's core interests, fundamentally misjudging British determination and the geopolitical stakes of territorial cession.
