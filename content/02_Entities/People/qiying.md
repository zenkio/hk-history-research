## Role in Early Diplomacy
**Qiying** (耆英) was a member of the Manchu Aisin Gioro clan and served as a high-ranking official during the **Daoguang** (道光) and **Xianfeng** (咸豐) reigns. He is best known for his diplomatic efforts following the First Opium War. In **1842**, he represented the Qing court in the negotiations that led to the Treaty of Nanking, which formally ceded Hong Kong Island to the British.

## Historical Interpretations
Historians offer varying perspectives on **Qiying**'s career and diplomatic legacy. Traditional Qing scholarship often characterized him as a 'traitor' or a weak official who yielded excessively to foreign demands, facilitating the 'Century of Humiliation.' Conversely, some modern scholars argue that **Qiying** was a pragmatic realist who attempted to manage an impossible situation—a technologically superior foreign force—by using a strategy of 'managing the barbarians' (羈縻) to minimize disruption to the Empire.

From the British colonial perspective of the 19th century, **Qiying** was frequently viewed as a sophisticated, albeit duplicitous, statesman who sought to maintain the Qing status quo despite the shifting power balance. Local Hong Kong historical narratives often emphasize his role as the signatory who finalized the British occupation, marking the symbolic start of the colonial era. His subsequent return to the capital and eventual downfall in **1858** under the **Xianfeng** Emperor underscore the intense political pressure imperial officials faced when dealing with foreign encroachment.
