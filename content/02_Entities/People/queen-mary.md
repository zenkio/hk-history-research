## Introduction and Connection to Hong Kong

**Queen Mary** (瑪麗王后), born **Victoria Mary of Teck** (**1867**–**1953**), was the wife of King George V. While she never resided in Hong Kong, her royal patronage and title became deeply associated with the colony's medical history through the establishment of **Queen Mary Hospital** (**瑪麗醫院**) in **1937**.

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of British royal naming conventions in colonial Hong Kong vary depending on the historical perspective:

*   **British Colonial Narrative:** Colonial authorities viewed the naming of major public works and institutions after members of the royal family, such as Queen Mary, as a natural demonstration of loyalty, imperial connectivity, and the benevolent reach of the British Crown in providing modern infrastructure, education, and healthcare to the territory.
*   **Local and Modern Scholarship:** Later historians often analyze these naming practices through the lens of institutional history and urban development, noting how the naming of Queen Mary Hospital marked a significant decentralization and modernization of Hong Kong’s medical services away from the older **Alice Ho Miu Ling Nethersole Hospital** (**雅麗氏何妙齡那打素醫院**). From a contemporary perspective, the retention of the name post-1997 is frequently viewed as a reflection of Hong Kong's complex historical continuity rather than active imperial allegiance.

## Legacy in Healthcare

Opened in **1937**, **Queen Mary Hospital** in **Pok Fu Lam** (**薄扶林**) grew to become one of the most prominent teaching hospitals for the **University of Hong Kong** (**香港大學**) medical faculty. Although the institution bears her name, historical records indicate that the primary driving forces behind its establishment were colonial administrative needs, rapid population growth, and the necessity for advanced medical facilities in the western district of Hong Kong Island.
