## Early Career and Appointment

**Rensuke Isogai** (磯谷廉介) was a career officer in the Imperial Japanese Army. Following the British surrender of Hong Kong on **25 December 1941**, the Japanese military administration was established. Isogai was appointed as the Japanese Military Governor of Hong Kong, taking office in **February 1942**. His administration marked the beginning of the formal occupation era, often referred to locally as the "Three Years and Eight Months" (三年零八個月).

## Administration and Policies

Isogai's tenure was characterized by strict military control and severe resource scarcity. Under his command, the administration implemented the **Establishment of Mass Repatriation Policy**, forcing hundreds of thousands of local residents out of the urban areas into mainland China to alleviate food shortages. Concurrently, his regime initiated the **Launch of Cultural Japanization Campaign**, attempting to supplant British and local influences with Japanese language, customs, and education.

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of Isogai's administration and legacy vary significantly across different historical perspectives:

* **British Colonial Historiography:** Often focuses on the brutality of the military regime, the collapse of public health, and economic ruin during the occupation years.
* **Local Hong Kong and New Territories Communities:** Oral histories and local accounts emphasize the extreme survival struggles, food rationing, and the uneasy accommodations some rural communities had to make with the occupying forces.
* **Post-War and Later Scholarship:** Modern historians examine the structural constraints Isogai faced, noting that Hong Kong was treated primarily as a strategic occupied territory rather than an integrated part of the Japanese Empire, which heavily dictated his draconian economic and demographic policies.

Isogai remained in command until the **Imperial Japanese Broadcast Announcing Surrender** in **August 1945**. Following the war, he was tried and convicted as a war class-B/C criminal by a Chinese military tribunal in Nanjing, though his sentence was later commuted.
