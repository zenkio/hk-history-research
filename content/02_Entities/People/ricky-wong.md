## Background and Telecommunications Career

**Ricky Wong** (王維基) first rose to prominence in the Hong Kong business community as a telecommunications entrepreneur. In **1992**, he co-founded **City Telecom**, which later became **Hong Kong Broadband Network** (**HKBN**). His aggressive business strategies and challenge to the incumbent telecom monopoly established his reputation as a maverick competitor in the local market.

## HKTV License Denial and Mass Protests

In **2009**, Wong shifted his ambitions to the broadcasting sector, establishing **Hong Kong Television Network** (**HKTV** - 香港電視網絡) with the goal of operating a free-to-air television station to inject competition into a market dominated by **TVB** (電視廣播有限公司). 

On **October 15, 2013**, the Executive Council announced that HKTV's application for a free-to-air television license had been rejected, while two other applicants were granted approvals. The decision triggered widespread public anger, as Wong had already built a sizable production studio and filmed several high-budget dramas, cultivating significant public goodwill. Critics and citizens suspected political censorship behind the denial, given Wong's outspoken nature and the perceived preference of Beijing and the local administration for pro-establishment media dominance.

On **October 20, 2013**, an estimated tens of thousands of demonstrators gathered outside the government headquarters in **Admiralty** (金鐘) to protest the decision and demand transparency. These mass protests reflected broader anxieties regarding freedom of speech, shrinking civic space, and government accountability in post-handover Hong Kong.

## Subsequent Ventures

Following the denial of the television license, Wong pivoted HKTV into an online shopping platform, **HKTVmall**, which became one of the leading e-commerce services in the city. While interpretations of his television licensing battle range from a straightforward regulatory decision to politically motivated suppression of independent media, his legacy remains deeply intertwined with the shifting media landscape and civic activism of **2010s** Hong Kong.
