## Early Life and Missionary Work

**Robert Morrison** (馬禮遜) was born in **1782** in Northumberland, England. Appointed by the London Missionary Society, he arrived in **Guangzhou** (廣州) in **1807** as the first Protestant missionary to China. Operating under the restrictive rules of the Canton System, Morrison focused on linguistic and literary pursuits. He compiled the foundational *Dictionary of the Chinese Language* (**1815–1823**) and completed the first translation of the Bible into Chinese (**1819**), alongside his collaborator **Leang Afa** (梁發). These works significantly shaped Western scholarly engagement with the Chinese language.

## Connection to Hong Kong

Although Morrison died in **1834**—seven years before the British formal occupation of Hong Kong Island in **1841**—his institutional legacy deeply influenced the territory's early development. His son, **John Robert Morrison** (馬儒翰), served as a colonial official and interpreter. Furthermore, the intellectual and educational networks established by Morrison's associates directly led to the relocation of the **Morrison Education Society School** from Macau to Hong Kong in **1839**. 

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of Morrison's historical role vary significantly:

* **British Colonial and Missionary Historiography:** Traditionally portrays Morrison as a visionary pioneer who broke through cultural barriers, bringing modern education, printing technology, and Christianity to Chinese soil.
* **Qing and Mainland Chinese Scholarship:** Often contextualizes Morrison within the broader framework of 19th-century Western imperialism, viewing early Protestant missions as a cultural vanguard that accompanied economic and military aggression against China.
* **Local Hong Kong and Later Scholarship:** Tends to emphasize the agency of Chinese assistants like Leang Afa, examining how local converts utilized Western religious texts for their own indigenous social and spiritual interpretations.
