## Architectural Career and Hong Kong Identity

**Rocco Yim** (嚴迅奇) is one of Hong Kong's most recognized contemporary architects, having contributed extensively to the city's built environment over several decades. Born and educated in Hong Kong, Yim graduated from the University of Hong Kong's Department of Architecture in **1974**. He later established **Rocco Design Architects**, a firm responsible for numerous high-profile public, commercial, and cultural projects across the territory.

Yim's architectural philosophy often navigates the complex interplay between Hong Kong's dense urban reality, its colonial legacy, and its evolving identity as a Chinese global city. While his earlier works focused on commercial towers and public housing, his later career has become closely associated with major cultural institutions that redefine the city's public space.

## The Hong Kong Palace Museum

Yim's most notable recent contribution is the design of the **Hong Kong Palace Museum** (香港故宮文化博物館), which opened in **2022** in the West Kowloon Cultural District. Located on the Victoria Harbour waterfront, the museum was conceived to exhibit artifacts from Beijing's Palace Museum.

The project's inception in **2016** involved complex political and cultural dynamics. Critics and local commentators debated the transparency of the project's initial planning process and its growing integration with mainland Chinese cultural institutions. Conversely, supporters and government officials emphasized the museum as a unique bridge for cultural exchange, elevating Hong Kong's status as an international hub for arts and culture. Yim's architectural design sought to mediate these perspectives, reinterpreting traditional Chinese architectural concepts—such as the spatial hierarchy of the Forbidden City—through a contemporary, vertical urban lens suited to Hong Kong's dense environment.
