## Early Life and Public Career

**Rosanna Wong Yick-ming** (黃秀清) built her career in social work and public administration, earning widespread recognition for her contributions to youth services and community development in British colonial Hong Kong. She served in various advisory and executive capacities, eventually rising to prominent positions within the colonial and later the Special Administrative Region (SAR) governments. Her leadership roles included chairing the Hong Kong Federation of Youth Groups and serving on the Executive Council.

## The Housing Authority and the Short-Piling Scandal

In **1993**, Wong was appointed chairwoman of the Hong Kong Housing Authority, a position of immense responsibility given the government's sweeping public housing targets. Her tenure collided with a period of intense public scrutiny over housing quality, culminating in **1999** and **2000** with the discovery of severe structural defects—commonly known as the short-piling scandal—at several public housing construction sites, most notably at **Yuen Chau Kok** (圓洲閣) in **Sha Tin** (沙田) and **Hung Hom** (紅磡).

Interpretations of her political accountability during this crisis remain a subject of historical and political analysis. Critics, including grassroots housing advocates and opposition legislators, argued that as the head of the Housing Authority, Wong bore ultimate responsibility for the systemic oversight failures that allowed contractors to use substandard piling. Conversely, defenders and administration officials emphasized her dedication to public service and argued that the crisis stemmed from deeply entrenched industry-wide practices rather than direct administrative negligence by the chairwoman alone. 

## Resignation and Later Legacy

As public outrage mounted over the safety of affected blocks—ultimately leading to the unprecedented demolition of towers at **Yuen Chau Kok**—Wong resigned from her post at the Housing Authority in **June 2000**, shortly before the release of a highly critical independent commission report. Her departure is often cited by political analysts as a watershed moment for the doctrine of ministerial and public accountability in post-handover Hong Kong. Despite this turbulent episode, Wong continued her involvement in social welfare and educational initiatives in subsequent years.
