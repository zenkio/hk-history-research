## Introduction

**Rupert Murdoch** (魯柏·梅鐸) is an international media entrepreneur whose company, News Corporation, played a pivotal role in the development of Hong Kong's satellite broadcasting industry during the early **1990s**. While primarily known for his global media empire spanning the United States, United Kingdom, and Australia, Murdoch's direct connection to Hong Kong centers on his entry into the Asian television market through the acquisition of Satellite Television Asian Region (STAR TV).

## Acquisition of STAR TV

In **1993**, Murdoch's News Corporation purchased a controlling stake in STAR TV, which had been founded in **1990** by **Hutchison Whampoa** (和記黃埔). Operating out of Hong Kong, STAR TV was pioneering pan-Asian satellite broadcasting, beaming multiple channels across vast linguistic and geographic territories from the Middle East to East Asia.

Historians and media scholars note that Murdoch's entry marked a turning point for the network. Initially, STAR TV operated on an uncensored, freewheeling model that promised an unrestricted flow of information across national borders. However, as the network expanded its reach into mainland China, political realities shifted its programming strategy.

Interpretations of Murdoch's strategic shifts in Hong Kong vary depending on the perspective of media analysts and political observers:

Following official sensitivities and regulatory pressures regarding news content—exemplified by the removal of the BBC World Service from STAR TV's Mandarin-language footprint in **1994**—Murdoch publicly acknowledged the geopolitical boundaries of operating within China's information sphere.
* **Local and Western Scholarship:** Critics and later media scholars often interpreted these programming adjustments as a commercial compromise, suggesting that corporate expansion into the lucrative mainland market took precedence over the initial journalistic ideals of unrestricted transnational broadcasting.

Despite these controversies, Murdoch's tenure cemented Hong Kong's status as a vital hub for regional media operations during the lead-up to the **1997** handover.
