## Overview and Role in Hong Kong
Established in **1815**, **Russell & Company (羅素洋行)** became one of the most powerful American merchant firms operating in China. Following the cession of **Hong Kong (香港)** to the British in **1841**, the firm relocated much of its operations from the traditional factories of Canton to the new treaty port, where it functioned as a primary intermediary for trade between the United States, Britain, and the Qing Empire.

## Steam Navigation and Infrastructure
The firm is closely associated with the **Introduction of Steam Navigation on the Pearl River**. By investing in early steamship technology, **Russell & Company** helped transition the region's logistics away from a total reliance on seasonal monsoon winds and traditional sailing vessels. This technological shift accelerated the movement of goods—most notably opium, tea, and silk—between **Hong Kong (香港)**, **Macau (澳門)**, and the mainland.

## Differing Interpretations
Historical interpretations of the firm's legacy vary by perspective. **British colonial** accounts typically framed the firm as a catalyst for modernization and the expansion of "free trade" in East Asia. In contrast, **Qing (清)** and later **Beijing** perspectives characterize the firm as a central agent of imperialist aggression and a major distributor of opium, contributing to the social and economic destabilization of China. More recent **academic scholarship** tends to view the company as a case study in the early globalization of finance and shipping, noting that while they drove technological innovation in navigation, their profits were inextricably linked to the narcotics trade.
