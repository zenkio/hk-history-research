## Early Life and Arrival in China

**Samuel Robbins Brown** (勃朗) was born in **1810** in the United States and graduated from Yale College. Appointed by the American Board of Commissioners for Foreign Missions, he arrived in China in **1839** to serve as the headmaster of the **Morrison Education Society School** (馬禮遜教育會學校), which had been established to honor the pioneering missionary **Robert Morrison** (馬禮遜).

## Move to Hong Kong

Originally operating in **Macau** (澳門), the school relocated to the newly established British colony of **Hong Kong** (香港) in **1842** following the First Opium War. Under **Brown**'s direction, the institution became one of the earliest organized Western-style schools in the territory. 

Historiographical interpretations of this era often diverge. Traditional British colonial accounts emphasize the school as a benevolent foundation of modern education and Western learning in the territory. Conversely, modern scholarship and Qing-era perspectives often contextualize these early missionary schools within the broader framework of Western imperialism and cultural penetration, noting how educational institutions served to cultivate intermediaries for the colonial administration and foreign mercantile houses.

## Legacy and Later Work

Among **Brown**'s most notable students were **Yung Wing** (容閎) and other young boys who were sent to the United States for their education, making them precursors to the Chinese Educational Mission. Although **Brown** left China in **1847** due to failing health, his brief tenure in Hong Kong laid early foundations for the development of Western education in the colonial enclave.
