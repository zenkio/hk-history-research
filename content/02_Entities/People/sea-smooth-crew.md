## Overview

The **Sea Smooth crew** refers to the personnel operating the **Sea Smooth** (海泰號), a high-speed passenger ferry owned by **Hong Kong and Kowloon Ferry** (港九小輪). The vessel became centrally involved in one of Hong Kong's worst maritime disasters in decades on **October 1, 2012**, when it collided with the **Lamma IV** (南丫四號) near **Lamma Island** (南丫島).

## The October 1 Incident

On the evening of **October 1, 2012**, the **Sea Smooth** was travelling from **Central** (中環) to **Yung Shue Wan** (榕樹灣) on **Lamma Island** (南丫島) carrying dozens of passengers. At approximately **8:23 PM**, just off the coast of **Lamma Island**, the ferry collided with the **Lamma IV**, a vessel carrying employees of **Hong Kong Electric** (香港電燈) and their families to view the National Day fireworks in **Victoria Harbour** (維多利亞港).

The impact severely damaged the **Lamma IV**, causing it to sink rapidly within minutes. While the **Sea Smooth** sustained bow damage, its crew managed to keep the vessel afloat and subsequently sailed it to the nearby pier before reporting the severity of the accident. Questions immediately arose regarding the post-collision actions of the **Sea Smooth** crew, particularly concerning whether they realized the extent of the damage inflicted on the other vessel and their obligations to render assistance.

## Legal Proceedings and Findings

Following the disaster, a commission of inquiry was established to investigate the causes of the collision and the conduct of the crew members from both vessels. Public and media scrutiny focused heavily on navigation protocols, adherence to maritime safety regulations, and lookout procedures.

Subsequent criminal proceedings led to legal accountability for crew members from both the **Lamma IV** and the **Sea Smooth**. In **2015**, the master of the **Sea Smooth** was convicted of manslaughter and endangering the safety of others at sea, receiving a prison sentence. Discussions among maritime professionals, legal experts, and the local community centered on the shared responsibilities of vessel operators in congested waters and the systemic factors contributing to the tragedy.
