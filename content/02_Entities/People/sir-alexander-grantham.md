## Early Career and Appointment

**Sir Alexander Grantham** (**葛量洪**) began his career in the British Colonial Service, serving in Hong Kong, Bermuda, Jamaica, and Nigeria before returning to Hong Kong as Governor in **1947**. His tenure coincided with immense geopolitical upheaval in mainland China, notably the establishment of the People's Republic of China in **1949**, which triggered a massive wave of refugees into the British colony.

## Post-War Reconstruction and Public Health

Facing severe housing shortages, poverty, and public health crises, Grantham's administration oversaw the expansion of social services. Tuberculosis was a major public health menace in the overcrowded post-war environment. To combat this, the **Grantham Hospital** for Tuberculosis was established in **1957** in Aberdeen, funded by public donations and government support, becoming a cornerstone of respiratory care in colonial Hong Kong.

## Historiographical Interpretations

Interpretations of Grantham's governorship vary across different perspectives:

* **British Colonial Historiography:** Often portrays Grantham as a pragmatic and steady administrator who successfully laid the foundations for Hong Kong's economic miracle and managed delicate cross-border relations with Beijing without compromising security.
* **Local and Revisionist Scholarship:** Points out that despite infrastructural expansions, the colonial administration was slow to address deep-seated labor rights, political representation, and severe housing deficits, which eventually boiled over in the **1956** riots.
* **Mainland Chinese Perspectives:** Historically viewed the British colonial administration through the lens of imperial control, though Grantham's pragmatic handling of the border and local communists helped maintain a fragile stability during tense Cold War years.
