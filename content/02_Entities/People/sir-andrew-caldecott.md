## Early Administration and Public Works
**Sir Andrew Caldecott** (貝璐) served as the Governor of Hong Kong from **1935** to **1937**. His brief tenure is primarily noted for the completion of major infrastructure projects that aimed to alleviate the chronic water shortages facing the growing population. The most prominent of these was the **Shing Mun Reservoir** (城門水塘), often referred to as the 'Jubilee Reservoir' to commemorate the silver jubilee of King George V. The project involved the relocation of several indigenous villages, a process that reflects the tensions between colonial administrative goals and the rights of the **New Territories** (新界) communities.

## Historical Interpretations
Historians offer varying perspectives on **Caldecott**'s governance. British colonial records of the period often emphasize the modernization of urban infrastructure and the technical prowess required to complete the **Shing Mun Reservoir** as a triumph of civil engineering. Conversely, local history accounts and later academic scholarship frequently highlight the social displacement of the villagers forced to leave their ancestral lands during the reservoir's construction, noting that the colonial government often prioritized urban growth over local traditional land rights.

## Departure and Legacy
**Caldecott**’s time in Hong Kong was cut short when he was transferred to serve as the Governor of the Straits Settlements in **1937**. While his direct influence in Hong Kong was limited by the brevity of his appointment, his focus on expanding water supply systems remained a recurring theme in colonial planning throughout the mid-20th century. Scholarship remains divided on whether his policies were a necessary response to urgent public health needs or a continuation of a top-down administrative approach that marginalized local voices in the **New Territories**.
