## Role in the Second Convention of Peking
Sir **Claude MacDonald** (1852–1915) served as the British Minister to China during the late 19th century. His primary significance to Hong Kong history lies in his orchestration of the **Second Convention of Peking** (1898). Under instructions from the British Foreign Office, **MacDonald** negotiated with the **Zongli Yamen** (總理衙門) to secure the 99-year lease of the New Territories. This expansion was framed by the British colonial administration as a necessary measure to ensure the strategic defensibility and economic viability of Hong Kong Island and Kowloon.

## Differing Perspectives
Interpretations of **MacDonald**'s actions vary significantly across historical narratives. From the perspective of British colonial authorities, the acquisition was a logical extension of imperial interests meant to stabilize the region. Conversely, Qing (清朝) officials at the time viewed the pressure exerted by **MacDonald** as a manifestation of "gunboat diplomacy" and an erosion of Chinese sovereignty. 

Local communities in the New Territories, notably the **Punti** (本地) inhabitants, viewed the British takeover with deep suspicion and hostility, leading to the **Six-Day War of 1899** (新界六日戰). Modern scholarship often debates whether **MacDonald** truly understood the complexities of land ownership and lineage traditions in the New Territories, or if he viewed the region merely as a buffer zone for imperial defense. While **MacDonald** successfully achieved his diplomatic objectives, the legacy of his tenure continues to be scrutinized through the lenses of post-colonialism and regional geopolitical power shifts.
