## Administration and Public Works
**Sir George William Des Voeux** (德輔) served as the Governor of Hong Kong from **1887** to **1891**. His tenure is most notably associated with the period of rapid land reclamation and infrastructure growth that defined late 19th-century Hong Kong. During his term, he oversaw the planning and execution of the **Praya Reclamation Scheme**, which significantly expanded the waterfront of Central.

## Infrastructure and the Peak Tram
Beyond maritime expansion, **Des Voeux** was a vocal proponent of colonial infrastructure. He is frequently cited in historical records concerning the **Peak Tram** (山頂纜車). While the tram service commenced operations in **1888** under the administration of his predecessor, **Des Voeux** actively engaged with the colonial elite's move toward the **Peak**, facilitating the residential development of the area which continues to influence the geographic and social stratification of the territory.

## Historical Perspectives
Interpretations of **Des Voeux**'s legacy vary based on the historical lens applied. In traditional British colonial historiography, he is remembered as an effective administrator who laid the groundwork for modern trade and connectivity. Conversely, local Hong Kong and New Territories perspectives often highlight that such advancements were primarily designed to serve European commercial interests and the administrative elite. Some contemporary scholars point out that while his reclamation projects were engineering feats, they fundamentally altered the natural coastline and the socio-economic access for indigenous boat-dwelling communities. Whether his policies were primarily driven by progressive urban planning or exclusionary colonial ambitions remains a subject of ongoing academic discussion.
