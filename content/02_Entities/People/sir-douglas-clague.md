## Early Life and Military Career

**Sir Douglas Clague** (祈德尊) was born in **1917**. Before becoming a central figure in Hong Kong's corporate landscape, he served with distinction during the Second World War. As a British Army officer, he was involved in intelligence and resistance operations, notably with the British Army Aid Group (BAAG) aiding escapees from Japanese-occupied Hong Kong.

## Business Empire and Hutchison International

In the post-war era, Clague transitioned to commerce, eventually heading **Hutchison International**, one of the leading traditional British hongs in the colony. Under his leadership, Hutchison expanded aggressively through acquisitions and property development, transforming into a massive conglomerate. 

## The Cross-Harbour Tunnel

Clague's most enduring physical legacy in Hong Kong infrastructure is the **Cross-Harbour Tunnel** (海底隧道). Opened in **1972**, the project was a monumental engineering and financial undertaking that connected Victoria Harbour between Hung Hom and Causeway Bay. As chairman of the Cross-Harbour Tunnel Company, Clague was instrumental in pushing the public-private partnership forward, overcoming skepticism about its commercial viability to solve chronic cross-harbour transportation bottlenecks. 

## Historiographical Perspectives

From a British colonial and corporate perspective, Clague is often remembered as a visionary entrepreneur and quintessential 'taipan' whose risk-taking modernized Hong Kong's economy and infrastructure. Conversely, local and critical labor perspectives sometimes view figures like Clague as symbols of unchecked colonial corporate dominance, where British-managed hongs held disproportionate economic sway over the local population. Later scholarship tends to evaluate Clague as a transitional figure who bridged the gap between traditional British mercantile dominance and the modern, finance-driven Hong Kong economy that would emerge in the late 20th century.
