## Early Colonial Career
**Sir Francis Henry May** (梅含理) arrived in Hong Kong in **1881** as a cadet officer. Over the next three decades, he ascended through the ranks of the colonial administration, serving as Captain Superintendent of Police and Colonial Secretary. His deep involvement in police work and intelligence made him a pivotal figure in maintaining colonial stability during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. 

## Governorship and Assassination Attempt
In **1912**, May assumed the role of Governor. Shortly after his arrival, he survived an assassination attempt in Central, Hong Kong, when a Chinese man fired at his carriage. While colonial accounts of the era frequently framed the event as a reaction to anti-Qing political fervor spilling over into the colony, contemporary scholarship often emphasizes the complex socio-political climate in the aftermath of the **1911 Xinhai Revolution**. Some interpretations suggest the perpetrator was influenced by wider revolutionary ideology, while others view it as a localized protest against British colonial authority.

## The First World War
May’s tenure is most notably marked by the onset of the **First World War (1914–1918)**. His administration focused on suppressing German influence within the territory and maintaining order amid rising wartime economic pressures. Historians generally credit his policies with ensuring the continuity of the colonial administration during a period of global upheaval. However, different historical lenses provide varied assessments: British colonial records typically praise his steadfast governance, while later revisionist scholarship often critiques the lack of political representation for the local Chinese population and the harsh measures taken under the wartime emergency regulations. 

## Legacy in the New Territories
May’s interactions with the New Territories community remain a subject of nuanced study.
