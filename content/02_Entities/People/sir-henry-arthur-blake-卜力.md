## Governorship and Territorial Expansion

**Sir Henry Arthur Blake** (卜力) served as the Governor of Hong Kong from **1898** to **1903**. His tenure is most noted for the implementation of the **Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory** (展拓香港界址專條), which resulted in the British lease of the New Territories for 99 years. **Blake** arrived during a period of transition where the colonial administration sought to consolidate its authority over the newly acquired hinterlands.

## The Kowloon Walled City Controversy

One of the most complex aspects of **Blake's** administration was his role in the **British Annexation of the Kowloon Walled City** (九龍城寨). In **1899**, citing security concerns and the desire to maintain consistent governance, **Blake** ordered the expulsion of the Qing officials residing in the Walled City. 

Interpretations of this event vary significantly among historical viewpoints. From the perspective of the **British colonial** administration, the annexation was presented as a necessary administrative measure to ensure law and order within the leased territory. Conversely, officials from the **Qing Empire** (清朝) and later authorities in **Beijing** often viewed the expulsion as a breach of the agreement, which had originally exempted the Walled City from British jurisdiction. Local **New Territories** communities often found themselves caught between these conflicting claims of sovereignty, leading to early resistance movements, most notably the **Six-Day War** (新界六日戰) of **1899**. 

## Legacy and Scholarship

Later scholarship often examines **Blake's** actions through the lens of colonial expansionism and the geopolitical tensions between the British Empire and the declining Qing state. While he is credited with laying the foundations for modern colonial infrastructure, including early land registration in the New Territories, his legacy remains tied to the contentious unresolved status of the Walled City that persisted until the late 20th century.
