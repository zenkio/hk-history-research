## Background and Arrival

**Sir Henry Pottinger** (砵甸乍) arrived in East Asia during the First Opium War as the chief British plenipotentiary and superintendent of trade, succeeding **Charles Elliot** in **1841**. Tasked with enforcing British war aims against the Qing Empire, Pottinger combined military assertiveness with diplomatic maneuvering.

## The Treaty of Nanking and Hong Kong's Status

Following successful military campaigns along the Chinese coast, Pottinger negotiated the **Treaty of Nanking** in **1842**, which formally ceded Hong Kong Island to the British Crown. On **August 12, 1843**, he issued the Proclamation of Hong Kong as a Crown Colony, assuming office as its first Governor.

## Historiographical Interpretations

Interpretations of Pottinger's legacy vary significantly across different perspectives:

*   **British Colonial Perspective:** Traditionally framed him as an astute administrator and diplomat who laid the institutional foundations for Hong Kong's rise as a global free port and trade hub.
*   **Qing and Mainland Scholarship:** Often views him as an aggressive imperialist who coerced the weakened Qing court into signing the first of the "unequal treaties," initiating China's "Century of Humiliation."
*   **Local Hong Kong and New Territories Perspectives:** Indigenous and local communities often note that early British governance under figures like Pottinger focused primarily on European mercantile interests, initially impacting the surrounding maritime and agrarian populations in complex ways.

Pottinger departed Hong Kong in **1844**, leaving behind a nascent colonial administration that would shape the territory's trajectory for over a century.
