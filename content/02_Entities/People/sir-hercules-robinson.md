## Governorship and Land Reform
**Sir Hercules Robinson** (夏喬士·羅便臣) arrived in Hong Kong in **1859** to assume the governorship during a period of transition following the Second Opium War. His administration is noted for its focus on stabilizing colonial revenue through structured fiscal policies. 

One of the most significant developments during his tenure was the implementation of the first systematic land auctions for public development. Robinson oversaw the transition toward a more rigorous leasehold system, which sought to formalize land ownership and provide a consistent stream of income for the colonial government. While British colonial records emphasize this move as a triumph of administrative efficiency and urban planning, later historians have analyzed these policies as tools that tightened the Crown's control over territory while often marginalizing the customary land rights held by local indigenous communities in the rural areas and the New Territories.

## Socio-Political Climate
Robinson's time in office coincided with the establishment of the **Hong Kong General Chamber of Commerce** in **1861**. His administration maintained close ties with the merchant elite, reflecting the British priority of facilitating trade. Interpretations of his impact vary: colonial-era accounts often laud his role in laying the foundation for Hong Kong’s modern commercial infrastructure, whereas contemporary scholars and local community perspectives often highlight the growing social disparity and the rigid hierarchical structure imposed during this time. His tenure concluded in **1865**, marking a formative phase in the institutionalization of British rule in the territory.
