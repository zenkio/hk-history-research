## Early Life and Academic Career
**Sir Lindsay Ride** (1898–1975) was an Australian-born physician and educator. He moved to Hong Kong to serve as the Professor of Physiology at the **University of Hong Kong** (HKU) in 1928. His academic tenure was interrupted by the **Second World War**.

## The British Army Aid Group
Following the fall of Hong Kong in **December 1941**, **Ride** escaped to mainland China. In **July 1942**, he established the **British Army Aid Group** (BAAG), a military intelligence and humanitarian unit based in **Guilin**. The organization focused on gathering intelligence on Japanese movements in occupied Hong Kong, assisting Allied prisoners of war (POWs) in Sham Shui Po to escape, and smuggling medical supplies into the territory. Historians often emphasize the bravery of the local agents and guerrillas who facilitated these missions; however, debates persist regarding the extent of the unit's intelligence success versus the risks it imposed on local civilian populations who faced harsh reprisals from the *Kempeitai*.

## Post-War Contributions
After the war, **Ride** returned to HKU, serving as its Vice-Chancellor from **1949 to 1964**. He played a pivotal role in the post-war reconstruction of the university. His legacy is viewed through multiple lenses: to some, he is a symbol of resilience and post-war institutional restoration; others note the colonial administrative framework he operated within. The **Execution of British Army Aid Group Rescuers**, who were captured by Japanese forces, remains a somber chapter associated with the network he led. **Ride** was knighted in **1962** for his services to education in Hong Kong.
