## Governor of Hong Kong

**Sir Matthew Nathan** (彌敦) assumed office as the Governor of Hong Kong in **1904**, becoming the first Jewish governor of the colony. His administration is primarily remembered for accelerating urban development and infrastructure projects aimed at modernizing Hong Kong and integrating the expanding territories.

## Urban Development and Nathan Road

During his tenure, **Nathan** heavily promoted land reclamation and road expansion in Kowloon. He championed the construction of a major thoroughfare through the Kowloon Peninsula, originally named Coronation Road but later renamed **Nathan Road** (彌敦道) in his honor. While colonial authorities praised these initiatives for facilitating commercial growth and improving sanitary conditions, local communities and subsequent historians have noted that urban expansion often involved the displacement of indigenous villages and the reshaping of traditional landscapes in the New Territories.

## Peak Reservation Ordinance

**Nathan**’s administration was also tied to social stratification policies, most notably the discussions surrounding the **Peak Reservation Ordinance** (山頂reservation條例). This legislation aimed to legally reserve the exclusive residential Peak district for non-Chinese residents. British colonial justifications often framed such segregation in terms of public health and European living standards, whereas modern scholarship and contemporary Chinese critics view it as a formal institutionalization of racial discrimination and social hierarchy.

## Legacy and Historiography

Interpretations of **Nathan**'s governorship vary. Traditional British historiography often portrays him as an energetic and forward-thinking administrator who laid the groundwork for modern Kowloon. In contrast, local Hong Kong and critical scholarship emphasize the exclusionary nature of colonial governance during his term, highlighting how infrastructure and housing policies systematically favored European interests over the local population.
