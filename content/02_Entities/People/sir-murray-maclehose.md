## Background and Arrival

**Sir Murray MacLehose** (**麥理浩**) arrived as Governor in **1971**, succeeding Sir David Trench. A career diplomat with extensive China experience, MacLehose assumed office during a period of transition following the turbulent events of the late 1960s. From the British colonial perspective, his administration was viewed as a modernizing and enlightened era that successfully restored public faith in government through active intervention and social reform.

## Social Reforms and Infrastructure

MacLehose's tenure is best remembered for ambitious domestic programs aimed at addressing deep-seated social grievances. Following the **Ten-Year Housing Programme Announced** in **1972**, and the subsequent **Home Ownership Scheme Launched** in **1978**, the colonial government dramatically expanded public housing provision. Infrastructure projects also transformed the territory, most notably the initiation of the Mass Transit Railway (MTR) and the expansion of new towns.

To combat systemic corruption, MacLehose established the Independent Commission Against Corruption (ICAC) in **1974**. While widely praised for cleaning up the civil service and police force, the early operations of the ICAC generated severe friction, culminating in the **Police-ICAC Amnesty Declared** in **1977** to quell unrest within the police force.

## Cross-Border and Local Dynamics

MacLehose presided over major shifts in immigration policy, culminating in the **Abolition of 'Touch Base' Immigration Policy** in **1980**, which ended the practice allowing illegal mainland immigrants who reached the urban areas to stay. This reflected mounting demographic pressures and negotiations with Beijing. In **1975**, **Queen Elizabeth II Visits Hong Kong** (**伊利沙伯二世訪港**), marking a historic royal tour that underscored the British presence.

Historical interpretations of the MacLehose era vary. While mainstream narratives celebrate his governorship as Hong Kong's golden age of administrative competence and community building, critical scholarship and local community perspectives—particularly in the New Territories—sometimes highlight how rapid urbanization disrupted traditional rural land rights and local ways of life. His landmark **1979** visit to Beijing also initiated the formal diplomatic countdown regarding Hong Kong's sovereignty after **1997**.
