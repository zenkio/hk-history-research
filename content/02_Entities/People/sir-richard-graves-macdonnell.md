## Early Administration and Fiscal Policy

**Sir Richard Graves MacDonnell** (麥當奴) arrived in Hong Kong in **1866** during a period of severe economic depression. His administration is primarily remembered for its interventionist approach to the colonial budget. Faced with a fiscal deficit, **MacDonnell** implemented a series of retrenchments and introduced the territory's first government-controlled lottery, the 'Po Leung' lottery, to bolster revenue. While these measures stabilized the colonial treasury, they faced criticism from the merchant class who viewed government involvement in gambling as morally and economically dubious.

## The Contagious Diseases Ordinance

Perhaps the most controversial aspect of his tenure was the passage of the **Contagious Diseases Ordinance** in **1867**. Inspired by similar legislation in Britain, the ordinance established a system of compulsory medical examinations for prostitutes to control the spread of venereal disease among British troops and sailors. From a British colonial perspective at the time, this was a necessary public health intervention. However, later scholarship and contemporary critics have argued that the policy institutionalized the exploitation of Chinese women and failed to address the systemic inequalities inherent in the colonial social structure. Debates persist regarding whether the ordinance was motivated by genuine medical concern or primarily by the preservation of military readiness.

## Legacy and City Hall

**MacDonnell** is also notable for his support of civic infrastructure. In **1869**, he presided over the opening of the **First Hong Kong City Hall**, a project that symbolized the growing social aspirations of the colonial elite. While British historical accounts often emphasize his role as a 'strong-willed' reformer who rescued the colony from bankruptcy, local historical interpretations frequently highlight the increased social tensions and the imposition of top-down policies that characterized his administration. His efforts to centralize authority and expand the police force underscored the broader colonial objective of maintaining control over a rapidly expanding and diverse population.
