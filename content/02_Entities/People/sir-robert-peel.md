## Political Background and the First Opium War
**Sir Robert Peel** (1788–1850) served twice as the Prime Minister of the United Kingdom. His administration (1841–1846) coincided with the conclusion of the **First Opium War** (1839–1842). While **Lord Palmerston** is often credited with initiating the conflict, it was under **Peel** that the **Treaty of Nanking** (1842) was ratified, formally ceding Hong Kong Island to the British Crown.

## Influence on Colonial Governance
In the context of Hong Kong history, **Peel** is primarily recognized for his administrative approach to the new colony. Following the initial military occupation, his government emphasized the need for a formal civil structure to manage the territory. This led to the foundational period of colonial governance where the protection of British mercantile interests became a priority. 

## The Establishment of the Hong Kong Police Force
Historical accounts of the **Hong Kong Police Force** (香港警隊) often cite the early administrative influence of the British government under **Peel**. In **1844**, the Legislative Council passed the Police Force Ordinance. British colonial narratives traditionally frame this as a necessary step for maintaining law and order in a burgeoning port. Conversely, some local Hong Kong historians and critical scholars point out that these early policing efforts were primarily designed to secure British trade interests and control a population often marginalized by colonial regulations. From the perspective of the **Qing Dynasty** officials at the time, these legislative actions were viewed as an encroachment on Chinese sovereignty. The motivations behind the force's creation remain a subject of historical debate, with interpretations ranging from essential civil governance to instruments of imperial control.
