## Background and Role

**Sister Yee-kwan** (蘇羅斯) served as the principal of the Precious Blood Golden Jubilee School (寶血會金禧中學) during a period of significant administrative and social transition in **Hong Kong**. Her tenure intersected with growing demands for administrative transparency and student empowerment in grant-in-aid schools funded by the colonial government.

## The Golden Jubilee School Incident

In **1977** and **1978**, the school became the center of a major controversy involving allegations of financial mismanagement and authoritarian administration within the Precious Blood Sisters, the sponsoring body. **Sister Yee-kwan** was central to these disputes as students and some teachers launched protests demanding her removal and an investigation into school accounts. 

Interpretations of the crisis vary among different stakeholders. Critics and student activists viewed the protests as a righteous stand against institutional opacity and heavy-handed governance. Conversely, church authorities and conservative elements at the time often framed the unrest as a breakdown of discipline stoked by external political agitators. Later scholarship frequently analyzes the incident as a watershed moment for Hong Kong's civil society, highlighting the rising political consciousness of the city's youth and teachers during the pre-handover era.

## Historical Significance

The resolution of the dispute involved intervention by the **Roman Catholic Diocese of Hong Kong** under Bishop **John Baptist Wu** (胡振中) and the colonial **Education Department**. The fallout led to structural reforms in how religious sponsoring bodies managed aided schools, leaving a lasting impact on Hong Kong's educational administration.
