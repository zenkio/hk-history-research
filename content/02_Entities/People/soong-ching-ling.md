## Role in Hong Kong and the China Defense League

In **June 1939**, **Soong Ching-ling** (宋慶齡) established the China Defense League (保衛中國同盟) in **Hong Kong**, leveraging the colony's relative neutrality and status as a vital entrepôt to channel international medical supplies and financial aid into the wartime interior of China. Operating primarily from the British colony, the League successfully coordinated global fundraising efforts, working alongside notable figures such as **Liao Chengzhi** (廖承志) and international sympathizers like **Norman Bethune** and **Epstein**. 

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of Soong's wartime activities in Hong Kong vary across different political and academic frameworks. 

From the perspective of mainland Chinese historiography, Soong's leadership of the China Defense League is viewed as a vital patriotic endeavor that united patriotic overseas Chinese and international anti-fascist forces under the united front against Japanese aggression. 

British colonial authorities maintained a cautious stance toward Soong's activities. While officially committed to neutrality, the Hong Kong government monitored the League closely to avoid antagonizing the Japanese Empire while managing the massive influx of mainland refugees and political activists residing within the colony.

Later scholarship emphasizes Hong Kong's unique geopolitical position as a safe haven that enabled cross-border resistance networks to flourish before the Japanese occupation of the colony in **December 1941**, which ultimately forced the League's operations to relocate to Chongqing.
