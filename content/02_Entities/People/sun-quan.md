## Role in Southern Expansion
**Sun Quan** (182–252 AD) served as the founding emperor of the Eastern Wu state during the **Three Kingdoms** (三國) period. While his primary political center was located in the Yangtze River delta, his administration sought to consolidate control over the southern coastal regions, including the commanderies that encompass modern-day Guangdong. Historical records indicate that the Eastern Wu state established more robust naval infrastructures to secure maritime trade routes and suppress local unrest in the south.

## Historical Interpretations
Historians offer varying perspectives on the significance of this era for the Hong Kong region. Traditional Qing-era (清朝) scholarship often emphasizes the geopolitical necessity of these southern military campaigns, framing the period as a civilizing effort that brought the Lingnan territories into the administrative fold of the Chinese heartland. Conversely, later scholarship and local archeological studies in the New Territories (新界) often caution against assuming direct state control over the specific islands of Hong Kong during the **3rd century**. Some scholars argue that while Eastern Wu naval vessels likely traversed the waters near Lantau Island (大嶼山) for logistics and commerce, there is little evidence of a settled administrative presence or permanent urbanization in the region at this time.

## Maritime Legacy
British colonial-era historians frequently contrasted the maritime limitations of the Eastern Wu navy with the later expansion of global trade, viewing the period as a precursor to the region's long-term utility as a nautical transit point. Local community historians often focus on the oral traditions and folkloric associations tied to the coastal geography, though these are generally distinguished from verifiable historical records. It remains widely accepted that the Eastern Wu period marked the beginning of a sustained interest by northern dynasties in the maritime potential of the Pearl River Delta, establishing a pattern of strategic observation that would influence the region for centuries to follow.
