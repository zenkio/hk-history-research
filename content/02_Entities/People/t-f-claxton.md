## Early Career and Royal Observatory
**Thomas Folkes Claxton** arrived in Hong Kong to join the Royal Observatory (香港天文台) in the late 19th century. He served as the Director of the Observatory from **1912** until his retirement in **1932**. During his tenure, **Claxton** oversaw significant improvements in the colony's ability to track typhoons, a critical service for Hong Kong’s maritime-dependent economy. He was instrumental in refining the visual storm warning signals used in the harbour, which provided essential data to shipping interests and local authorities.

## Scientific Contribution
**Claxton** was widely recognized for his dedication to chronometric accuracy. He managed the Observatory's time-keeping services, ensuring that the firing of the 'Noonday Gun' and the signals sent to the shipping community were precise. His work was pivotal in maintaining the consistency of meteorological records in the region, which provided a foundation for later climatological studies. His reports are frequently cited by historians studying colonial-era disaster management, particularly regarding how the colonial government communicated weather threats to the public.

While British colonial records emphasize the technological limitations of the era, later scholarship and local historical accounts often highlight the disparity between the warnings issued by the Observatory and the tragic vulnerability of the boat-dwelling populations, who had limited capacity to seek refuge regardless of the alerts provided. **Claxton**'s legacy is defined by his contribution to a system that, while advanced for its time, operated within the complex social and political constraints of colonial Hong Kong.
