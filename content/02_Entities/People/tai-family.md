## Background and Local Prominence

The **Tai family** (戴氏家族) are recorded in local histories and temple epigraphy as key figures among the early Chinese communities of Hong Kong's coastal areas. While detailed genealogical records accessible in mainstream modern historiography remain sparse, their historical significance is primarily preserved through their enduring connection to the **Tin Hau Temple in Causeway Bay** (銅鑼灣天后古廟), one of the most important traditional religious sites on Hong Kong Island.

## Connection to the Tin Hau Temple

According to historical stone tablets and local tradition, members of the **Tai family** initiated and funded the construction of the Tin Hau Temple in the **early 18th century** (traditionally dated to around **1747**). Facing the waters of Causeway Bay—which was then an active fishing harbour—the temple served as a vital spiritual anchor for local Hakka and Cantonese fisherfolk, farmers, and traders.

Different historical perspectives interpret the role of local lineage families like the Tais in various ways:

* **Local and Community Perspectives:** Descendants and local villagers traditionally view the founding families as pious benefactors who established communal welfare, maritime safety, and social cohesion through the patronage of the Goddess of the Sea, Tin Hau (天后).
* **Colonial and Later Scholarship:** British colonial records and subsequent academic studies often view these early temple trusts through the lens of customary land tenure and community management. Scholars note that maintaining control over religious institutions allowed local clans to consolidate social influence and manage ancestral or communal trust properties (Tso and Tong, 祖嘗) during the transition periods of Qing imperial administration and the subsequent British colonial era beginning in **1841**.

Because comprehensive archival documentation prior to the 19th century is limited, the precise socioeconomic status of the early **Tai family** members remains partly obscured, though their legacy is firmly anchored in the architectural and epigraphic history of Hong Kong Island.
