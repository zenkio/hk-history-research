## Origins and Migration

The **Tang Clan** (鄧氏) trace their ancestral origins in the region to the **Song dynasty** (**宋朝**). Historical accounts generally indicate that members of the clan migrated southward from Jiangxi province into what is now the New Territories. Traditional genealogies emphasize elite lineage and early settlement, whereas later scholarship often contextualizes this migration within broader patterns of demographic shifts and land reclamation in southern China.

## Development of Walled Villages

As the clan established roots, particularly in areas like **Kam Tin** (錦田), they became central to the development of indigenous settlements. Facing security concerns from bandits, rival groups, and changing political dynamics, the Tangs constructed elaborate defensive structures. Projects such as **Kat Hing Wai** (吉慶圍) and **Kun Lung Wai** (昆lung圍) exemplify the **Development of Fortified Walled Villages** (**圍村**). 

## Historiography and Perspectives

Interpretations of the Tang clan's history vary across different traditions. 
- **Local New Territories Communities:** Indigenous oral traditions and lineage records often highlight ancestral prowess, ritual primacy, and long-standing autonomy over their lands.
- **British Colonial Administration:** Early colonial reports tended to view these walled communities through the lens of exotic local customs, occasionally characterizing their strong resistance during the **1898** takeover as lawlessness, though later colonial governance relied on rural leaders like the Tangs for indirect rule.
- **Modern Scholarship:** Contemporary historians focus on socio-economic networks, feng shui layout, and clan trusts (**tso**, **祖**), analyzing how lineage power was maintained across dynastic and colonial transitions.
