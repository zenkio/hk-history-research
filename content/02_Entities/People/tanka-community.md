## Origins and Identity
The **Tanka** (疍家) are a distinct maritime community that has historically inhabited the coastal waters and estuaries of Southern China and Hong Kong. Often referred to as 'boat people,' they have traditionally lived on vessels (junks or sampans). Their origins remain a subject of historical debate: local oral traditions and some scholarly accounts often link them to the ancient **Baiyue** (百越) peoples of Southern China, while imperial records from the **Qing Dynasty** (清朝) frequently marginalized them, categorizing them as 'low-born' or outsiders restricted from settling on land and barred from taking civil service examinations.

## Historical Economic Role
Historically, the **Tanka** were vital to the maritime economy of the Pearl River Delta. They were primary practitioners of deep-sea fishing and participated in the **pearl industry** (採珠) in the Tai Po (大埔) area. During the imperial era, their labor was heavily exploited for the collection of pearls, which were highly taxed by the central government. In the **First Recorded Pearl Farming Tax Reforms**, the Tanka were the primary source of labor, often subject to grueling and dangerous conditions to satisfy imperial quotas.

## Colonial Perspectives vs. Local Reality
Interpretations of the Tanka experience shifted significantly with the arrival of the **British colonial** administration. Colonial records often viewed the Tanka through a lens of 'social order' and maritime surveillance, sometimes pathologizing their nomadic lifestyle as primitive. Conversely, later scholarship often highlights the Tanka’s unique resilience and their sophisticated mastery of maritime ecology. Within the **New Territories** (新界), their presence was traditionally integrated into the local economy through trade and service, though they often existed on the periphery of the land-based clan power structures. Throughout the 20th century, modernization, government resettlement programs, and the decline of traditional fishing crafts led to a significant transition for the community from boat-based life to permanent land-based housing, fundamentally altering their traditional socio-economic structure.
