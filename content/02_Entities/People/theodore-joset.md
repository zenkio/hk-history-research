## Early Life and Arrival

**Theodore Joset** (西奧多·約瑟特) was a member of the Congregation of the Propaganda Fide and a priest belonging to the Institute of Charity (Rosminians). Following the British occupation of Hong Kong Island in **1841**, the Holy See sought to establish a formal ecclesiastical presence to serve the growing Catholic population, particularly soldiers, sailors, and local converts. In **1841**, Pope Gregory XVI established the Apostolic Prefecture of Hong Kong, separating it from the Diocese of Macao, and appointed **Joset** as its first Prefect Apostolic.

## Establishment of the Mission

Upon his arrival, **Joset** faced formidable challenges. The fledgling British colony lacked infrastructure, and anti-Catholic sentiment persisted among some British colonial administrators who favored the Church of England. Despite these hurdles, **Joset** worked to secure land grants from the colonial government for religious use, establishing early chapels and pastoral care structures.

Historiographical interpretations regarding his tenure often diverge. British colonial records generally frame early Catholic missions through the lens of administrative pragmatism, viewing clergy primarily as providers of moral and social welfare for the military and laborers. Conversely, Catholic church histories emphasize **Joset**'s pivotal role in carving out an autonomous spiritual jurisdiction independent of Portuguese ecclesiastical authority in Macao, a move that reflected broader geopolitical shifts as British Hong Kong developed a distinct identity separate from neighboring Portuguese and Qing territories. 

## Legacy

**Joset**'s tenure was cut short by his untimely death in **1842**, after which leadership of the prefecture passed to other members of his order and subsequently to the Congregation of the Immaculate Heart of Mary (CICM) and the Paris Foreign Missions Society (MEP). Although his time in Hong Kong was brief, **Joset** established the foundational institutional framework that enabled the Roman Catholic Church to expand rapidly alongside the colony's development.
