## Arrival and Medical Practice
**Thomas Boswell Watson** (湯馬士·屈臣) was a Scottish physician and pharmacist who arrived in the British colony of Hong Kong in the mid-19th century. During an era when the newly established colony struggled with tropical diseases and a lack of established medical infrastructure, **Watson** provided essential pharmaceutical services to the colonial administration, the military, and the local European population.

## The Hong Kong Dispensary
In **1841**, shortly after the British occupation of Hong Kong, **Watson** became associated with the Hong Kong Dispensary. While early commercial records are occasionally ambiguous regarding the exact timeline of his partnership, it is widely accepted that he took full control of the dispensary in **1858**. This business, located in the central district, served as a primary source for Western medicine and chemicals. The dispensary eventually became the cornerstone of the multinational retail giant **A.S. Watson & Co.** (屈臣氏), which remains a prominent entity in Hong Kong’s economic history.

## Historical Perspective
Historians generally characterize **Watson** as a representative of the mercantile class that drove the development of the colonial port city. From the perspective of British colonial history, he is often credited with helping to professionalize medical provisioning in the region. Conversely, local and revisionist scholars often examine his legacy through the lens of how Western commercial interests and colonial health initiatives influenced, and sometimes displaced, indigenous medical practices. While the exact scope of his influence on the broader local population remains difficult to quantify due to the limited archival records from the period, his name remains synonymous with the development of Western-style pharmacy in Hong Kong. He eventually handed over the management of his interests to his nephew, **Alexander Skirving Watson**, before departing the colony.
