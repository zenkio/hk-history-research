## Role in Hong Kong Commerce
**Thomas Sutherland** (1834–1922) arrived in Hong Kong in the mid-19th century as the superintendent of the Peninsular and Oriental Steam Navigation Company (P&O). During his tenure, he identified a critical deficiency in the local financial infrastructure: the lack of a bank managed by local merchants to serve the specific interests of the China trade, which at the time relied heavily on distant British-managed institutions.

## Establishment of HSBC
In **1865**, **Sutherland** spearheaded the creation of The Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation. While he is credited as the bank's visionary, historical perspectives on this initiative vary. Proponents of the colonial-era narrative often frame the establishment of the bank as a pivotal step in transforming Hong Kong from a precarious trading outpost into a sophisticated international financial hub. Conversely, critical scholars sometimes highlight how the institution reinforced the interests of British merchant houses, or 'hongs', at the expense of local Chinese financial networks, which operated on different traditional systems of credit and trust.

## Historical Interpretation
Interpretations regarding **Sutherland**'s legacy are nuanced. Within the traditional British colonial historiography, he is celebrated as a pioneer of Hong Kong's economic modernisation. From the perspective of later scholarship, his legacy is often contextualised alongside the broader expansion of British imperial influence in the Treaty Ports. While some suggest that **Sutherland** intended for the bank to be a truly local institution, others point out that the bank’s management remained firmly under British control for many decades. There remains some uncertainty regarding the exact extent to which he collaborated with Chinese merchants during the bank's initial formation, as primary records from local Chinese business communities of that period are less extensive than those of the British mercantile class.
