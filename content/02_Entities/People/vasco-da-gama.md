## Maritime Expansion and Hong Kong
**Vasco da Gama** (達·伽馬) is central to the history of the Pearl River Delta primarily due to his role in opening the sea route to India in **1498**. While there is no evidence suggesting **Vasco da Gama** ever visited the territory now known as Hong Kong, his expedition established the logistical and commercial framework that brought the Portuguese to the region. Following his success, the Portuguese crown sought to consolidate trade power in Asia, eventually leading to the arrival of **Jorge Álvares** at Tuen Mun (屯門) in **1513**.

## Historical Perspectives
Interpretations of these early interactions vary significantly among scholars. Early **British colonial** historians often characterized the Portuguese arrival as the dawn of 'modern' maritime trade in the region, emphasizing the transition from isolationism to global exchange. In contrast, some **Qing-era** and modern **Beijing-based** historiography often frames these early encounters as the beginning of a period of 'humiliation' by foreign colonial powers, focusing on the friction between the Ming dynasty and Portuguese naval arrivals. Conversely, local **New Territories** perspectives often highlight the resilience of coastal communities during these initial, often volatile, encounters. Contemporary scholarship remains divided on whether the 1513 landing at Tuen Mun should be viewed as a formal diplomatic attempt or an informal commercial expedition. The uncertainty surrounding these early records means the exact nature of the contact between Portuguese vessels and the Tuen Mun coastal populations remains a subject of ongoing debate among maritime historians.

## Timeline

- **1498**: Vasco da Gama reaches India by sea, initiating European interest in the South China Sea.
- **1513**: Jorge Álvares, following in the footsteps of established maritime routes, lands at Tuen Mun.
