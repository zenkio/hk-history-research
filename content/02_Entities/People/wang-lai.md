## Role in the Great Clearance
**Wang Lai** (王來) served as an official during the early **Qing dynasty** (清朝), a period defined by the consolidation of imperial power over the southern coastal regions. He is primarily known for his role in the enforcement of the **Great Clearance** (遷界令), an edict issued in **1661** aimed at isolating the pro-Ming loyalist forces of **Koxinga** (鄭成功) by forcing the evacuation of coastal populations.

## Impact on the Hong Kong Region
Under the orders associated with **Wang Lai**, residents along the coast of what is now the **New Territories** (新界) were forced to move inland, abandoning their homes, crops, and ancestral lands. The area became a 'no-man's land' intended to deny resources to naval resistance. Historical records from the **Xin'an County** (新安縣) annals suggest that this policy caused immense hardship for local clans and resulted in widespread destruction of settlements. 

## Historical Interpretations
Interpretations of **Wang Lai**'s actions vary significantly among historical schools. Traditional **Qing dynasty** historiography often frames officials like him as bureaucrats dutifully executing necessary state security measures to stabilize the empire. Conversely, local **New Territories** genealogies and community histories focus on the trauma of displacement, framing the era as a period of devastation that severed historical ties and altered the region's socio-economic landscape for decades. Later scholarship often debates the efficiency of the Great Clearance, questioning whether the policy achieved its intended military goals or if it primarily served to cripple local infrastructure. There is also scholarly uncertainty regarding the extent of **Wang Lai**’s personal agency in the severity of the evacuations, as he operated within a rigid imperial administrative hierarchy. His legacy remains tied to the period of the 'Coastal Watch Posts' (守禦所), as the subsequent re-population of the area, encouraged by the Qing government after **1669**, fundamentally reshaped the cultural and ancestral composition of the Hong Kong region.
