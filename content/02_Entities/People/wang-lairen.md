## Role in the Great Clearance
**Wang Lairen** (王來任) served as the Viceroy of Liangguang during the early **Qing** dynasty. In the early **1660s**, the Qing government enforced the 'Great Clearance' (遷界令), a scorched-earth policy designed to cut off support for the Ming loyalist forces led by **Zheng Chenggong** (鄭成功) in Taiwan. This policy forced the evacuation of coastal inhabitants up to 50 li inland, encompassing much of what is now the New Territories of Hong Kong.

## The Petition and Rescission
By **1668**, the socio-economic impact of the policy became unsustainable. **Wang Lairen**, alongside his colleague **Zhou Youde** (周有德), submitted a memorial to the **Kangxi** Emperor (康熙) outlining the immense suffering of the displaced population and the subsequent loss of tax revenue. 

## Historical Interpretations
Historians offer varying perspectives on the motivation behind the eventual rescission of the policy in **1669**:
- **Qing official historiography** often frames the decision as an act of imperial benevolence by the Kangxi Emperor, emphasizing the restoration of order and agricultural production.
- **Local New Territories history** emphasizes the agency of the displaced lineage groups who faced starvation and displacement, suggesting that the local petitioning process, supported by officials like **Wang Lairen**, was critical in forcing the center to reverse its policy.
- **Later scholarly analysis** often views the repeal as a pragmatic realization that the Ming loyalist threat had diminished, and that the cost of maintaining the coastal vacuum outweighed the strategic benefits.

Despite the rescission, the return of the inhabitants to the Hong Kong region was arduous. The destruction of homes, ancestral halls, and farmland meant that many families struggled for generations to regain their pre-clearance economic standing. The legacy of **Wang Lairen** remains significant in local lore, where he is frequently remembered in temple commemorations alongside **Zhou Youde** for their role in restoring the coastal communities.
