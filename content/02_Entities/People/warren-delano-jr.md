## Early Trade and Russell & Co.
**Warren Delano Jr.** (1809–1898) arrived in Canton (Guangzhou) in **1833** to join **Russell & Co.**, the largest American trading firm in China at the time. As a senior partner, he played a central role in managing the logistics of the 'Old China Trade,' which relied heavily on the export of tea and silk and the import of opium from British India. Delano was a central figure in the mercantile community that navigated the complex restrictions of the Canton System.

## Involvement in the Opium Trade
Delano's legacy remains a subject of historical debate. While he was a respected businessman and patriarch—notably the maternal grandfather of U.S. President Franklin D. Roosevelt—his wealth was substantially derived from the opium trade. Traditional Western scholarship often framed his activities within the context of 'free trade' and the expansion of global commerce. Conversely, modern scholars and historical accounts from a Chinese perspective emphasize the devastating social and economic consequences of the opium trade on the Qing Empire, highlighting the role of foreign merchants in exacerbating the crisis that led to the First Opium War (**1839–1842**).

## Impact on Hong Kong
Following the **1841** cession of Hong Kong to the British, **Delano** and his associates were among the first Western merchants to recognize the strategic value of the harbor. He was active in early land auctions in Victoria City, facilitating the shift of mercantile operations from the Pearl River ports to the newly established British colony. His career serves as a bridge between the merchant-dominated era of the Canton System and the more formalized colonial administration of Hong Kong. Historical interpretations vary on his role: colonial-era narratives often highlight his contribution to the development of the city's infrastructure and trade network, whereas post-colonial and local critical scholarship view him as a primary agent of imperial expansion who exploited the region for capital accumulation.
