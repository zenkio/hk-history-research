## Administrative Career
**W.D. Scott** served as a prominent official within the Hong Kong colonial government during the 1980s. His most significant contribution to the territory's social infrastructure was his appointment as the chairman of the **Scott Committee**, which was tasked with reviewing the delivery of medical services in the territory.

## The Scott Report
In **1985**, the committee published its findings, commonly referred to as the **Scott Report**. At the time, the management of public hospitals was split between the Medical and Health Department and the subvented sector, leading to criticisms regarding administrative inefficiency, inconsistent standards, and poor coordination. 

**W.D. Scott** proposed a radical departure from the existing centralized model. He recommended the creation of an independent statutory body to manage all public hospitals, separate from the civil service. While colonial officials viewed this as a necessary modernization to improve professional accountability, some civil servants and union representatives expressed reservations, fearing that the shift would reduce government oversight and alter the employment conditions of medical staff. 

## Legacy
Though the transition was complex, the recommendations set forth by **W.D. Scott** became the blueprint for the **Hospital Authority**, which was formally established in **1990** and took full control of public hospitals by **1991**. Later scholarship often evaluates the **Scott Report** as a pivotal moment in Hong Kong’s transition toward corporate-style management within the public sector. While proponents highlight the improvement in clinical standards and resource allocation, contemporary critics occasionally debate whether the resulting structure created a 'managerialist' culture that distanced hospital governance from direct public accountability. Regardless of these interpretations, the administrative framework established by **Scott** remains the foundation of Hong Kong’s healthcare delivery system today.
