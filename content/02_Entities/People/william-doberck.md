## Early Career and Appointment
**William Doberck** (杜佩克) was a Danish astronomer appointed in **1883** as the first Government Astronomer and Director of the **Hong Kong Observatory** (香港天文台). His recruitment was part of a broader colonial initiative to establish systematic meteorological observations to aid maritime navigation and improve typhoon warning systems in the South China Sea. His early work focused on establishing the observatory’s infrastructure and standardizing data collection across the region.

## Scientific Contributions and the 1906 Typhoon
Under **Doberck**’s leadership, the observatory became a central hub for reporting weather patterns. He is noted for his efforts in establishing a time-ball service and developing early theories on the movement of tropical cyclones. However, his tenure was marked by complex challenges regarding public warning efficacy. During the **1906** Great Typhoon, which caused devastating loss of life and property in the harbor, the observatory faced intense public scrutiny. British colonial administrators and the shipping community differed on the adequacy of the warning protocols of the time. While some contemporary accounts praised the observatory's scientific rigor, other segments of the Hong Kong public and local maritime communities criticized the perceived delay in forecasting the severity of the storm. Historical interpretation of this event often highlights the tension between colonial bureaucratic processes and the desperate need for actionable local intelligence.

## Legacy
**Doberck** remained in his post until his retirement in **1907**. His legacy is viewed through a dual lens: to historians of science, he is credited with building the essential foundations of Hong Kong’s meteorological capabilities. To social and local historians, his time in office remains a point of reference for studying the evolution of colonial disaster management and the subsequent development of more comprehensive warning systems tailored to the unique geography of Hong Kong.
