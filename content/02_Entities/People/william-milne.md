## Early Missions in the Delta
**William Milne** arrived in Southeast Asia in **1813** as part of the London Missionary Society. Because the Qing (清) dynasty strictly prohibited the entry of foreign missionaries, **Milne** based his operations in Malacca. From there, he sought ways to introduce Protestantism to the Pearl River Delta, often coordinating with local converts and utilizing the trade networks of the region.

## Linguistic Contributions
**Milne** is recognized for his significant efforts in translation and linguistics. He worked on translating the New Testament into Chinese and compiled one of the earliest Chinese-English dictionaries. These tools were critical for later missionaries who arrived in Hong Kong (香港) and the surrounding territories following the Treaty of Nanking in **1842**, providing the linguistic foundation necessary for Protestant proselytization in the region.

## Differing Interpretations
Interpretations of **Milne's** activities vary by perspective. British colonial and missionary archives generally portray him as a pioneering figure of faith and enlightenment who courageously sought to "open" China. In contrast, Qing imperial perspectives viewed his activities as illegal intrusions and violations of sovereign law. Later scholarship often frames **Milne** within the context of the "informal empire," suggesting that missionary efforts were closely linked to the expansion of British commercial interests and cultural hegemony in the South China Sea.

While his direct physical presence in what is now the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region was limited compared to later figures, his strategic focus on the Delta established the operational model for the Protestant missions that would later define the religious landscape of colonial Hong Kong.
