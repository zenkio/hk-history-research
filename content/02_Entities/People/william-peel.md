## Governorship and Colonial Policy

**Sir William Peel** (貝璐) served as the Governor of Hong Kong between **1930** and **1935**. His tenure coincided with the aftermath of the Great Depression, which placed significant fiscal strain on the colonial administration. Peel is often remembered for his efforts to stabilize the local economy and his administration’s move toward modernizing public infrastructure.

## Transport Reform

A defining aspect of **Peel**'s administration was the formalization of public transport services. Under his governance, the government established a franchised bus system in **1933**. This move curtailed the chaotic competition between smaller operators and consolidated transport services under two main franchisees, the China Motor Bus Company and the Kowloon Motor Bus Company. While British colonial records emphasize this move as a triumph of administrative efficiency and urban order, later scholarship and local commentators have sometimes questioned the extent to which these monopolies prioritized corporate interests over affordable public access for the working class.

## Historical Perspectives

The legacy of **Peel** remains a subject of varying historical interpretations. To the British colonial establishment of the era, he was a steady hand steering the territory through precarious global economic conditions. However, from the perspective of local communities and later critical historians, his administration often reflected the rigid social hierarchies of the time. While some local accounts acknowledge his contributions to public health and infrastructure, others emphasize that his policies were designed primarily to preserve the stability of the British colonial enterprise rather than to address the growing social needs of the Chinese population in the New Territories and urban districts. The degree to which his policies exacerbated local economic grievances or laid the groundwork for modern urban development continues to be discussed among historians of Hong Kong.
