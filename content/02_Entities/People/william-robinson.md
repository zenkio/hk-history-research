## Governor of Hong Kong

**Sir William Robinson** (羅便臣) served as the Governor of Hong Kong from **1891** to **1898**. His administration coincided with significant demographic pressures, sanitary crises, and growing imperial anxieties in Southern China following the expansion of the British sphere of influence.

## The Sanitary Board and Public Health

One of the most notable developments during Robinson's tenure was the evolution of the **Sanitary Board** (潔荮局). Established earlier to address chronic public health and sanitation issues—particularly in densely populated Chinese districts like **Tai Ping Shan** (太平山)—the Board became a focal point for political tension.

From the British colonial perspective, the administration viewed the Board's expansion as a necessary medical intervention to curb epidemics such as the bubonic plague of **1894**. British officials emphasized sanitation, quarantine, and Western medical practices to safeguard the colony's economic viability.

Conversely, local Chinese communities and merchant elites often viewed these heavy-handed sanitary regulations as an infringement on cultural practices and an unwanted intrusion into their internal affairs. Interpretations among later historians often examine this era through the lens of colonial governance, highlighting how public health policies served as instruments of social control and racial segregation, dividing the European and Chinese populations.

## Legacy

Robinson's term also laid groundwork leading up to the **Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory** in **1898**, which leased the **New Territories** (新界) for **99** years. While British colonial histories often lauded his administrative stability, later scholarship and local perspectives frequently re-evaluate his governance by emphasizing the enduring social divides and resistance to colonial authority during the late 19th century.
