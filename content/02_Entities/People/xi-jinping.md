## Leadership and Policy towards Hong Kong

**Xi Jinping** (習近平) assumed the leadership of the People's Republic of China in **2012**. Under his tenure, the central government’s approach to Hong Kong has been characterized by a more assertive integration into the national development strategy. 

## The National Security Law

In **2020**, the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress, under the direction of the state leadership, enacted the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (National Security Law). 

Interpretations of this legislative shift vary significantly. Supporters, including officials from Beijing and some segments of the pro-establishment camp, argue the law was a necessary intervention to restore order following the social unrest of **2019** and to close loopholes in national security. Conversely, critics, including various international observers and local pro-democracy figures, contend that the law has curtailed civil liberties and altered the 'One Country, Two Systems' framework by diminishing the high degree of autonomy originally guaranteed under the Basic Law.

## Regional Integration

**Xi Jinping** has actively promoted the Greater Bay Area initiative, which seeks to integrate Hong Kong and Macau more closely with nine cities in Guangdong Province. A physical symbol of this integration is the **Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macau Bridge**, which opened in **2018**. During the opening ceremony, **Xi Jinping** emphasized the bridge’s role in strengthening the connection between the mainland and the Special Administrative Regions. 

While some analysts view these projects as vital for Hong Kong's long-term economic relevance, others in the local community express concerns regarding the erosion of Hong Kong's distinct legal and socio-political identity. Historical assessments of this period remain in development, as scholars continue to analyze whether the shift toward deeper integration constitutes a structural evolution of the 'One Country, Two Systems' policy or a fundamental departure from the promises made during the **1997** transition.
