## Early Life and Educational Work
Born in **1861** in Guangdong, **Yeung Ku-wan** (楊衢雲) spent his formative years in Hong Kong, where he received an English-language education. He worked as an English clerk and later established the **Furen Literary Society** (輔仁文社) in **1892**. This organization, aimed at promoting modern knowledge and Western reform, served as an intellectual incubator for anti-Qing sentiment in the territory.

## The Revive China Society
In **1895**, **Yeung** merged his society with **Sun Yat-sen**’s (孫中山) group to form the Hong Kong chapter of the **Revive China Society** (興中會). **Yeung** was elected as the organization's first president. While **Sun** is frequently credited as the primary architect of the revolutionary movement, historical records indicate that **Yeung** held significant influence and prestige within the group during its early formation in Hong Kong.

## Perspectives on His Legacy
Interpretations of **Yeung**'s significance vary across historiographical traditions. Nationalist narratives often focus on his shared goals with **Sun Yat-sen**, though tensions between the two men regarding leadership and strategy are noted by later scholars. British colonial records from the era viewed him primarily through the lens of regional stability and immigration control, while local Hong Kong community accounts often emphasize his role as an educator and a pioneer of modernization efforts in the colony. In the People's Republic of China, he is officially recognized as a martyr of the **1911 Revolution**.

## Assassination
After the failure of the **1895** Guangzhou Uprising, **Yeung** went into exile but eventually returned to Hong Kong. On January 11, **1901**, he was assassinated in his office by an agent acting on behalf of the Qing government. His death was a significant event in Hong Kong, highlighting the extent of Qing intelligence operations within the British-administered territory. He is buried at the **Hong Kong Cemetery** in Happy Valley, where his tomb was long left unmarked for safety reasons before being restored to acknowledge his historical role.
