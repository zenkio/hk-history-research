## Early Career and Appointment
**Yeung Yung-kai** (楊永強), a medical doctor by profession, held a significant role in Hong Kong's public health administration during the early 21st century. He served as the Secretary for Health, Welfare and Food from **2002** to **2004**. His tenure coincided with the emergence of the Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome (**SARS**) epidemic, which brought unprecedented pressure to the healthcare system and the government.

## The SARS Crisis
In **2003**, the **SARS** outbreak placed **Yeung** at the center of a public and political firestorm. ## Legacy and Scholarship
In **2004**, a Legislative Council Select Committee investigated the handling of the outbreak. Historical interpretations of his tenure remain divided; some analysts view his departure as a necessary measure for political accountability in the aftermath of a public health catastrophe, while others suggest that his resignation reflected the immense difficulty of managing a global health crisis within the complex political structure of post-handover Hong Kong.
