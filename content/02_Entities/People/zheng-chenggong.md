## Introduction

**Zheng Chenggong** (鄧成功), also known historically in the West as **Koxinga**, remains a pivotal figure in the regional history of Southern China during the transition from the Ming (明) to the Qing (清) dynasty. While his most famous military achievement was the expulsion of the Dutch from Taiwan in **1661**, his protracted naval resistance profoundly affected the Pearl River Delta and the areas that now constitute Hong Kong.

## Impact on the Hong Kong Region

During the Fall of Ming and Coastal Chaos, Zheng's maritime forces frequently operated along the Guangdong coastline, using islands and secluded bays to launch raids, gather provisions, and challenge Qing naval authority. In response to the persistent threat posed by Zheng and his loyalists, the Qing court implemented the drastic Great Clearance (長界 or 遷界) policy starting in **1661**. This policy forced the evacuation of coastal populations several tens of li inland, devastating local settlements, agriculture, and fishing communities across the region, including parts of the modern New Territories.

## Historiographical Interpretations

Interpretations of Zheng Chenggong's legacy vary significantly across different historical traditions:

* **Qing and Mainland Historiography:** Traditionally viewed by the Qing state as a rebel and pirate, modern mainland scholarship often reinterprets him as a patriotic national hero who resisted foreign encroachment and recovered Taiwan for China.
* **Local and New Territories Perspectives:** Oral histories and local lineage records in the Hong Kong region historically reflected the immense suffering caused by the coastal evacuations, viewing the era through the lens of displacement and hardship rather than grand dynastic loyalty.
* **Colonial and Western Scholarship:** British colonial-era historians frequently focused on his maritime empire within the broader context of East Asian trade routes and geopolitical shifts in the South China Sea.

Uncertainties remain regarding the exact scale of Zheng's direct military engagements within the immediate waters of modern Hong Kong, as primary sources from the period often conflated various anti-Qing pirate bands with Zheng's official naval forces.
