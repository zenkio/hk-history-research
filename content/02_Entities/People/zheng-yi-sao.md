## Early Life and Rise to Power
**Zheng Yi Sao** (鄭一嫂), born **Shi Yang** (石陽), rose to prominence following the death of her husband, **Zheng Yi** (鄭一), in **1807**. She successfully consolidated control over the Red Flag Fleet, a massive pirate coalition that operated extensively along the coast of Guangdong and the waters that would later become Hong Kong. Her leadership was characterized by a strict code of laws, which governed the distribution of plunder and discipline within her ranks.

## Operations in Hong Kong Waters
During the early **19th century**, her fleet utilized the sheltered bays and islands of the Pearl River Delta, including areas around **Lantau Island** (大嶼山) and **Tung Chung** (東涌), as strategic bases. Her dominance disrupted regional maritime trade, forcing the Qing authorities and the British East India Company to contend with her decentralized naval power. 

## Interpretations of Her Legacy
Interpretations of her career vary significantly across different historical perspectives. 
*   **Qing/Beijing Records:** Official imperial archives traditionally categorized her as an outlaw and a destabilizing element to maritime order. These records emphasize the necessity of her eventual suppression to restore the authority of the Qing state.
*   **Local Communities:** Oral traditions in some New Territories fishing communities have occasionally characterized her as a protector who filled the vacuum left by ineffective governance, though these accounts are often folkloric rather than verifiable.
*   **Modern Scholarship:** Contemporary historians often focus on her organizational genius and her role as a precursor to modern maritime power structures. Her negotiation of a formal pardon in **1810**—which allowed her to retire without facing execution—is frequently studied as a unique political compromise between an informal non-state actor and the imperial government.

Following her surrender, her top lieutenant, **Cheung Po Tsai** (張保仔), entered into government service, further integrating the former pirate infrastructure into the Qing administrative framework. The exact extent of her personal role in these final negotiations remains a subject of ongoing historical investigation.
