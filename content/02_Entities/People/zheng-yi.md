## Role in Maritime History
**Zheng Yi** (鄭一) is historically recognized as the architect of the confederation of pirate fleets that plagued the South China coast in the early **1800s**. Operating during a period of transition in the region, he unified various independent pirate groups, most notably creating the Red Flag Fleet. His influence extended throughout the Pearl River Delta and the islands surrounding present-day Hong Kong, using the region’s complex coastline and sheltered bays as strategic bases for operations against both Qing imperial vessels and merchant traffic.

## Historical Interpretations
Historians offer differing lenses through which to view **Zheng Yi**. From the perspective of Qing Dynasty records, he was an outlaw and an agent of instability who challenged imperial authority and disrupted maritime trade. In contrast, local traditions in some New Territories coastal communities have occasionally viewed the pirate fleets with more nuance, sometimes portraying them as entities that operated within a complex local socio-economic hierarchy. Later scholarship emphasizes his role in creating a sophisticated, proto-state organization, noting that his leadership established a hierarchical structure that survived for several years after his death in **1807**. 

## Connection to Hong Kong
The geography of Hong Kong—with its numerous inlets and deep-water harbours—was central to the operational success of the fleet under **Zheng Yi**. While it is widely accepted that he used the Lantau and Hong Kong island waters as refuge, the exact extent of permanent land-based infrastructure remains a subject of academic debate. After his death, his wife, **Zheng Yi Sao** (鄭一嫂), took command, continuing the legacy of the coalition. The impact of their activities forced a shift in maritime security policies that predated the formal British colonization of Hong Kong, marking a period where the region was essentially governed by non-state maritime powers.
