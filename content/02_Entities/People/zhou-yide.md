## Role in the Great Clearance
**Zhou Yide** (周有德) served as the Governor of Guangdong (廣東巡撫) during the early **1660s**, a period defined by the Qing dynasty's implementation of the Great Clearance (遷界令). This policy sought to deny logistical support to the anti-Qing forces of **Koxinga** (鄭成功) by forcibly relocating coastal populations inland. 

## Historical Interpretations
Perspectives on **Zhou Yide**'s administration vary significantly among historiographical schools. Qing official records often frame his actions as necessary measures to restore order and state security in the southern coastal provinces. In contrast, local tradition within the New Territories of Hong Kong emphasizes the immense socio-economic trauma caused by the depopulation, which devastated agrarian livelihoods and left coastal villages abandoned for years.

Historians debate the extent of **Zhou Yide**'s personal agency regarding the eventual rescission of the order. Some scholars argue he was instrumental in relaying the catastrophic local reports to the **Kangxi Emperor** (康熙帝), highlighting the futility and economic cost of the policy. Others suggest he merely oversaw the implementation and eventual lifting of the policy as dictated by changing central imperial priorities. His collaboration with the Governor-General **Wang Lairen** (王來任) in the petition to repeal the order in **1668** is widely acknowledged in local historiography, though the degree to which he initiated the petition versus responding to local pressure remains a subject of academic nuance.

## Legacy
Following the rescission of the Great Clearance in **1669**, **Zhou Yide** was involved in the complex process of settling land disputes as residents attempted to return to their ancestral lands. While his name is frequently mentioned in historical accounts of this transition, details regarding his specific administrative methods during the repatriation process are limited. His involvement serves as a critical focal point for understanding the transition of the Pearl River Delta from a site of imperial conflict to a region of economic reconstruction in the late 17th century.
