## Role in the First Opium War
Amoy (廈門), known formally as Xiamen, played a strategic role in the conflict between the British Empire and the Qing Dynasty. Following the British capture of the city in **August 1841**, the port became a key transit point for the British expeditionary forces operating along the China coast. This occupation provided the British with a deeper understanding of the Chinese maritime landscape, which influenced their strategic considerations regarding the occupation of Hong Kong Island.

## Connection to Sir Henry Pottinger
Amoy served as a significant site for diplomatic movements during the tenure of **Sir Henry Pottinger**, the first British Plenipotentiary to China. In **1841** and **1842**, the British fleet utilized Amoy as a rendezvous point while moving northward to force the Qing government into negotiations. Historians note that the logistical support derived from ports like Amoy allowed the British to maintain the blockade of the Yangtze River, which ultimately compelled the Qing court to sign the **Treaty of Nanking** in **1842**.

## Historical Perspectives
Interpretations of Amoy’s role during this era differ significantly. British colonial records of the 19th century often framed the capture of Amoy as a necessary military action to secure 'free trade' and diplomatic equity. Conversely, Qing-era perspectives and subsequent mainland Chinese historiography typically categorize the event as part of the 'Century of Humiliation,' viewing the occupation of Amoy as an aggressive violation of sovereignty that paved the way for the unequal treaties. Local maritime communities in the region occasionally emphasize the disruption to existing trade networks, noting that while the British utilized the city for war efforts, the local populace faced profound instability. Modern scholarship often looks past these dichotomies to analyze how the opening of treaty ports like Amoy and Hong Kong fundamentally reconfigured the regional economic integration of the South China Sea, creating new patterns of migration and commerce that permanently altered the trajectory of the Pearl River Delta.
