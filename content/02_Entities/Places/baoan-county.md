## Historical Significance
**Baoan County** (寶安縣) served as the primary administrative authority over the region that is now Hong Kong for centuries. Originally established during the **Qin dynasty** as **Nanhai Commandery** (南海郡), the area was reorganized under the **Jin dynasty** as **Baoan County** in **331 AD**. For much of its history, the jurisdiction of this county included the Lantau Island, the New Territories, and the present-day city of **Shenzhen**.

## Administrative Evolution and Local Perspectives
The status of the region underwent significant changes, particularly the period in **757 AD** when it was renamed **Dongguan County** (東莞縣). Later, during the **Ming dynasty** in **1573**, **Xin'an County** (新安縣) was established, effectively separating the region from the larger Dongguan administrative umbrella. 

Interpretations of these shifts vary significantly across historiographical traditions. **Qing dynasty** and **Beijing-centric** narratives often emphasize the seamless continuity of imperial governance and the consolidation of maritime defenses against piracy. In contrast, local **New Territories** lineages often focus on the autonomy of clan-based social structures, viewing imperial administrative shifts as distant bureaucratic formalities that held little weight over their land-tenure systems. 

## The Colonial Transition
The relationship between the county and Hong Kong shifted dramatically following the **First Opium War**. Under the **Convention of Peking** in **1898**, the **New Territories** were leased to the British, creating a physical and political divide within what had previously been a unified administrative zone under **Xin'an/Baoan**. This split effectively severed the traditional ties between the rural villages in Hong Kong and the mainland administrative center. Later scholarship often highlights this as a pivotal moment where colonial border-making permanently disrupted local identity, while British colonial records typically emphasize the modernization and stabilization of the territory under a new legal framework. The name **Baoan** was briefly restored in **1914** and remained in use until the late 20th century, cementing its place as the ancestral administrative root of the Hong Kong region.
