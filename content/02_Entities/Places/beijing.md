## Imperial Diplomacy and Territorial Expansion
As the seat of the Qing (清) dynasty imperial government, Beijing (北京) was the location where unequal treaties affecting Hong Kong were concluded. Following military defeats, Qing officials negotiated and ratified agreements such as the Convention of Peking in **1860**, which formally ceded the Kowloon (九龍) Peninsula south of Boundary Street to Great Britain.

## Sovereignty Negotiations and the Joint Declaration
In the late 20th century, Beijing became the focal point for the future of the territory. British Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher visited Beijing in **September 1982** to meet with Chinese leader Deng Xiaoping (鄧小平), initiating formal sovereignty negotiations. While the British side initially emphasized the validity of the 19th-century treaties, the Chinese government maintained that all the Hong Kong areas were Chinese territory and must be returned. These high-level talks culminated in the signing of the Sino-British Joint Declaration in **December 1984**, setting the terms for the 1997 handover.

## Post-Joint Declaration and Financial Policy
Following the Joint Declaration, Beijing remained central to Hong Kong's transition. Governor Edward Youde engaged in ongoing diplomatic and administrative coordination with mainland authorities before his sudden death in Beijing in **December 1986**. Meanwhile, the establishment of the Hong Kong dollar peg to the US dollar in **October 1983** was managed locally by the Hong Kong government, though economic stability increasingly intersected with mainland policy.

## Legal Drafting and Historiography
Beijing was also the drafting ground for the Hong Kong Basic Law (香港特別行政區基本法), promulgated in **April 1990** to outline the "One Country, Two Systems" (一國兩制) framework. Interpretations of Beijing's role differ significantly: British and Western accounts often emphasize the safeguarding of international trade and legal continuity, whereas mainland historiography views the recovery of sovereignty as the correction of historical injustices stemming from foreign imperialism. Local Hong Kong perspectives have historically reflected a complex spectrum of anxieties, pragmatism, and aspirations regarding the city's political future under mainland administration.
