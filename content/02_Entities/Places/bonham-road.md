## Origins and Development
Named after **Sir Samuel George Bonham**, the third Governor of Hong Kong, Bonham Road was constructed in the mid-19th century to connect the expanding colonial administrative center with the higher elevations of the Mid-Levels. Historically, the area functioned as a buffer between the dense residential districts of Sai Ying Pun and the burgeoning academic enclave established later.

## The University of Hong Kong
The road is most notably connected to the **Inauguration of the University of Hong Kong** in **1912**. The university's main entrance on Bonham Road established the site as the premier location for tertiary education in the territory. While British colonial records emphasize the institution as a testament to the success of the 'civilizing mission' and an effort to cultivate a local professional elite, later scholars have debated the extent to which the university functioned as an instrument of British soft power in the Far East during the decline of the Qing dynasty.

## Historical Significance
Bonham Road has remained a focal point for the evolution of Hong Kong’s landscape. The presence of historic stone walls, built using traditional techniques to support the steep terrain, often sparks debate between preservationists who view them as essential heritage and urban planners who prioritize safety and infrastructure expansion. The road also serves as a transit corridor for several historic secondary schools, reflecting the area's identity as an educational hub. Interpretations of the road’s history are varied: colonial narratives often highlight the architectural achievements of the period, whereas local historians frequently document the displacement of earlier community dwellings required to clear the steep slopes for institutional construction. The significance of the thoroughfare is thus viewed differently depending on whether one prioritizes the administrative history of the British colonial government or the social history of the local communities who navigated these streets during the twentieth century.
