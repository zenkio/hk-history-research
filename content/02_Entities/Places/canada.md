## Historical Context of Migration
Canada has long been a significant destination for individuals departing from Hong Kong (香港). While early migration was often linked to labor and trade, the flow of people increased substantially in the late **20th century**. Scholars often point to the **1984** signing of the Sino-British Joint Declaration (中英聯合聲明) as a critical turning point that catalyzed concerns regarding the territory's future transition to the People's Republic of China. 

## Differing Perspectives
Interpretations of these emigration waves vary significantly depending on the vantage point. From the perspective of the **British colonial government** and some contemporary analysts, the outward movement was often viewed as a natural response to regional instability or a desire for Western security. Conversely, some **Beijing-aligned** observers at the time interpreted these departures as a lack of confidence in the 'One Country, Two Systems' (一國兩制) framework, or as the movement of capital and talent away from the developing regional economy. 

Within the **Hong Kong community**, the experience of 'astronaut families'—where one spouse worked in Hong Kong while the other resided in Canada to secure permanent residency—became a defining social phenomenon of the late **1980s** and **1990s**. Later academic scholarship highlights that these migrations were not merely political, but also represented complex decisions regarding education, family structure, and transnational identity. While some individuals returned to Hong Kong after obtaining Canadian citizenship, others established permanent roots in cities such as **Vancouver** (溫哥華) and **Toronto** (多倫多), creating enduring social and economic links that continue to shape the bilateral relationship today.
