## Historical Context and Establishment
The **Canton Bazaar (廣府墟)** emerged in the aftermath of the **1908** Canton-Hong Kong general strike and boycott, a major anti-British economic protest originating in Guangdong. To foster trade and counter boycotts, local merchants and authorities sought to establish a dedicated marketplace in Wan Chai to facilitate commercial ties with mainland traders, specifically from Canton (**Guangzhou**).

## Differing Historical Interpretations
Interpretations of the bazaar's purpose and impact vary among historians and contemporary observers:

*   **British Colonial Perspective:** Colonial authorities viewed the establishment through the lens of public order and economic pragmatism, seeking to stabilize trade while remaining cautious of potential political agitation emanating from nationalist networks.
*   **Qing and Mainland Perspective:** Contemporary mainland officials and nationalist organizers regarded commercial spaces like the bazaar as vital outposts for asserting Chinese economic influence and solidarity against British colonial dominance.
*   **Local Hong Kong Community:** Indigenous and immigrant Chinese merchant communities navigated these tensions pragmatically, balancing the commercial opportunities of the bazaar with the necessity of maintaining stable relations with the colonial administration.
