## Historical Significance
Castle Peak Bay (青山灣) has served as a sheltered anchorage for maritime communities for centuries. Geographically situated at the mouth of the Pearl River, the area was historically significant for the Tanka (蜑家) boat-dwelling communities and local fishing villages. During the British colonial era, the bay became a focal point for regional trade and maritime activity, particularly due to its proximity to the trade routes leading into the Pearl River Delta.

## Development and Transformation
In the late **1970s**, the area underwent profound changes as the colonial government initiated the **Opening of the Tuen Mun New Town** (屯門新市鎮發展). While British colonial records emphasize the necessity of these reclamation projects to accommodate Hong Kong's burgeoning post-war population and industrial growth, local New Territories communities and maritime scholars have highlighted the displacement of traditional fishing livelihoods. 

Interpretations regarding the impact of these developments remain multifaceted. Early government reports prioritize the success of the land reclamation in creating residential zones and modern infrastructure. Conversely, later historiography and oral histories from the Tuen Mun communities often emphasize the erosion of traditional maritime culture, citing the transition of the bay from an active fishing harbor into an urbanized environment as a central point of tension in Hong Kong's modernization narrative. Whether the transformation represents an inevitable progression or a cultural loss remains a subject of ongoing debate among historians of the New Territories. Today, the area remains an essential part of the Tuen Mun (屯門) landscape, balancing remnants of its maritime heritage with its status as a major residential and transit hub.
