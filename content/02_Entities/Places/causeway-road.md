## Introduction and Naming

**Causeway Road** (**高士威道**) is an important historical and transportation artery on Hong Kong Island, linking **Causeway Bay** (**銅鑼灣**) to **North Point** (**北角**). The road is named after **Sir William Causeway**, though historical records occasionally invite debate regarding colonial-era naming conventions and the precise timeline of its early construction phases.

## Urban Development and Reclamation

The history of Causeway Road is closely tied to the extensive land reclamation projects along the northern shore of Hong Kong Island. From the perspective of British colonial administration, the road was engineered to facilitate east-west connectivity and support the expanding commercial and residential needs of the Victoria City urban fringe. Conversely, local communities and later urban historians often emphasize how these infrastructural expansions altered traditional marine access, altered local Fung Shui, and reshaped the livelihoods of coastal villagers and fishermen who previously operated in the shallow bays.

## Connection to Government History

As infrastructure expanded eastward, the surrounding areas evolved from marshland and military recreation grounds into densely populated urban districts. While the road itself primarily served as a transit corridor, its vicinity has occasionally intersected with the broader administrative footprint of the government, including proximity to modern administrative shifts such as the utilization of facilities like the Former Metropark Hotel for official or quarantine purposes in contemporary times.

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of Causeway Road's legacy vary. British colonial accounts typically frame the road as a triumph of engineering over difficult geography. In contrast, modern critical scholarship and local Hong Kong studies often re-examine these infrastructure projects through the lens of colonial hegemony, questioning the displacement of indigenous and traditional coastal communities to make way for European-style urban planning.
