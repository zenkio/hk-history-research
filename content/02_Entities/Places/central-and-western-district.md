## Historical and Political Significance

The **Central and Western District** (中西區) has served as the administrative and financial heartbeat of Hong Kong since the early colonial period. Encompassing core areas such as **Central** (中環), **Sheung Wan** (上環), and **Western District** (西環), it houses the **Central Government Offices** (政府總部) and the **Legislative Council Complex** (立法綜合大樓). Because of its concentration of power, the district has frequently been the epicenter of major political developments, protests, and electoral contests.

## The District Council and Mass Resignations

Following the landslide victory of the pro-democracy camp in the **2019 District Council elections** (2019年區議會選舉), the Central and Western District Council, like many others across the territory, came under opposition control. However, the political landscape shifted dramatically with the enactment of the **Hong Kong National Security Law** (香港國安法) in **2020** and subsequent mandates requiring district councillors to take oaths of allegiance.

In **2021**, amid reports that unseated councillors might face retrospective financial penalties regarding their salaries and allowances, a wave of mass resignations swept through the territory. Pro-democracy councillors in the Central and Western District resigned en masse rather than take the oath, effectively ending the camp's local majority.

## Differing Perspectives

Interpretations of these events diverge sharply. Pro-Beijing authorities and supporters maintain that the oath-taking requirement and subsequent resignations were a necessary and constitutional measure to ensure that public office holders are patriotic and loyal to the state, thereby restoring administrative stability. Conversely, local democratic advocates and various international observers argue that the disqualifications and pressure tactics dismantled local democracy, effectively silencing the opposition and eroding the high degree of autonomy promised under the **One Country, Two Systems** (一國兩制) framework.
