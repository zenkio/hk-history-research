## Architectural Evolution and Completion
The completion of the Central Market building marked a significant milestone in the urban infrastructure of colonial Hong Kong. The current structure, completed in **1939**, replaced earlier market facilities that had occupied the site since the mid-19th century to serve the growing population of Victoria City. Designed in the Streamline Moderne architectural style, the building reflects the utilitarian yet progressive municipal planning of the pre-war British colonial administration.

## Historiographical Interpretations
Perspectives on the significance of the Central Market vary across different historical viewpoints. From a British colonial administrative perspective, the building represented the successful modernization of public health standards, hygienic food distribution, and orderly municipal governance in early 20th-century Hong Kong. Conversely, local Hong Kong and grassroots communities often remember the market through a social lens—as a vibrant, enduring hub of daily working-class life, small-scale entrepreneurship, and communal interaction that anchored the urban fabric of the Central district.

Later scholarship and heritage conservation advocates have focused on the building's architectural rarity as a surviving example of public Bauhaus-influenced architecture in the territory. While earlier planning phases in the late 20th century frequently targeted the site for commercial redevelopment, shifting public values eventually prioritized the preservation of its structural heritage, culminating in its recent adaptive reuse as a public-facing community and retail space.
