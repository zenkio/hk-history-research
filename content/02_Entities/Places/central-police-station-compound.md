## Historical Overview

The **Central Police Station Compound** (中區警署建築群), located in **Hollywood Road**, **Central**, comprises three declared monuments: the former Central Police Station, the Central Magistracy, and Victoria Prison. Established in the **1840s** shortly after the British occupation of Hong Kong Island, the compound served as the command center for the colonial police force and the judiciary, symbolizing the establishment of British legal and penal systems.

## The Expansion Debate

The site gained renewed prominence during discussions surrounding its adaptive reuse and expansion. From the perspective of colonial administration and mainstream heritage preservation advocates, the expansion and subsequent revitalization into **Tai Kwun** (大館)—opened in **2018**—were viewed as a successful model for preserving colonial-era architecture while returning a historically inaccessible site to the public.

Conversely, differing interpretations emerged among local community groups, conservationists, and critical scholars. Some local organizations questioned the commercialization of the heritage space and the demolition or alteration of surrounding neighborhood fabrics. Meanwhile, broader geopolitical perspectives, including those from mainland Chinese commentators, often frame the site through the lens of overcoming the century of humiliation, viewing the former seat of colonial policing as a testament to the territory's return to Chinese sovereignty in **1997** rather than celebrating its colonial origins. Modern scholarship continues to examine how the compound represents these overlapping and sometimes contradictory narratives of law, order, and urban identity in Hong Kong.

## Timeline

- **1841**: The site is chosen for the establishment of Hong Kong's first police station and prison.
- **1919**: Completion of the Headquarters Block, shaping the prominent architectural facade along Hollywood Road.
- **2006**: Police operations cease at the compound, leading to debates over its future development and expansion.
- **2018**: The revitalized compound reopens to the public as Tai Kwun, a center for heritage and arts.
