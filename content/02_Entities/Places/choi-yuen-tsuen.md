## Origins and Development
**Choi Yuen Tsuen** (菜園村) was a village located in the **Pat Heung** (八鄉) area of the New Territories. Established largely by immigrants arriving from mainland China in the mid-20th century, the community was characterized by its agricultural roots and rural lifestyle. Unlike traditional indigenous villages in the New Territories, its inhabitants held land titles that were subject to different administrative classifications, which later became a critical point of contention during land resumption processes.

## The Express Rail Link Controversy
In **2009**, the Hong Kong government announced plans to demolish the village to make way for the **Guangzhou-Shenzhen-Hong Kong Express Rail Link** (廣深港高速鐵路) stabling sidings and emergency rescue station. This proposal sparked the **Anti-Express Rail Link Protests** (反高鐵運動). 

## Differing Perspectives
The conflict reflected varying interpretations of development and heritage. For the **Hong Kong Government**, the project was framed as a vital infrastructural necessity to integrate Hong Kong into the national high-speed rail network, essential for long-term economic competitiveness. Conversely, activists and villagers argued that the project represented a top-down approach that sacrificed existing, organic communities for large-scale infrastructure. While some supporters of the project emphasized the need for modern connectivity, local advocacy groups highlighted the village as a symbol of 'right of abode' and sustainable rural living. The struggle gained significant public attention, particularly among the youth-led social movements of the time, leading to debates regarding land use policy and the preservation of New Territories villages versus developmental imperatives.

## Relocation
Following protracted negotiations and protests, the village was cleared, and residents were relocated to a new site, **Choi Yuen San Tsuen** (菜園新村), in **2011**. This relocation remains a landmark case in Hong Kong’s history concerning the balance between government-led urban development and the rights of existing rural communities.
