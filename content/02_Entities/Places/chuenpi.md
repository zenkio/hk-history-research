## Historical Significance
Chuenpi (穿鼻), sometimes romanized as Chuenpee, is a coastal location situated on the peninsula at the mouth of the Pearl River. It gained historical prominence in the mid-19th century due to its strategic position during the **First Opium War** (1839–1842). The area was the site of multiple naval engagements between the British Royal Navy and the forces of the **Qing Dynasty** (清朝).

## The Convention of Chuenpi
In **January 1841**, the **Convention of Chuenpi** (穿鼻草約) was drafted following negotiations between British Plenipotentiary **Charles Elliot** and the Qing Imperial Commissioner **Qishan** (琦善). The agreement ostensibly marked an early attempt to cease hostilities. 

## Divergent Interpretations
Interpretations of the Convention remain a subject of historical debate. British colonial records of the time frequently presented the document as a foundational, albeit incomplete, step toward the formal cession of **Hong Kong Island** (香港島). Conversely, Qing court narratives viewed the document as an unauthorized local agreement rather than a binding treaty, noting that the **Daoguang Emperor** (道光帝) ultimately repudiated the terms and dismissed **Qishan** from his post for failing to contain British demands. Later scholarship often emphasizes the gap between these two perspectives, highlighting how the failure of the Chuenpi agreement underscored the fundamental breakdown in diplomatic protocol and sovereignty claims between the British Empire and the Qing administration. While the specific legal status of the document is contested, historians generally agree that its collapse led to the continuation of military conflict, which eventually concluded with the **Treaty of Nanking** (南京條約) in **1842**.
