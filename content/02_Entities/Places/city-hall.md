## Historical Significance
The current **City Hall** (香港大會堂), opened on **March 2, 1962**, was built on the reclaimed land of the **Central** district. It replaced the original City Hall—the Victorian-era building that stood from **1869** to **1933**—which functioned as the focal point for the British colonial elite's social activities. The current modernist structure was designed to provide a multi-purpose space for a growing post-war population, housing a concert hall, theater, and public library.

## Cultural and Intellectual Legacy
The site holds importance in Hong Kong's intellectual history, specifically regarding the city's role as a nexus for regional political discourse. Before the current building existed, the original City Hall hosted various public forums and lecture series. Notably, in **1927**, the writer **Lu Xun** (魯迅) visited Hong Kong and delivered two significant lectures at the City Hall, titled "Silent China" (無聲的中國) and "Old Tune Has Been Played Out" (老調子已經唱完).
