## Geography and Context
The **Clear Water Bay Peninsula** (清水灣半島) extends into the South China Sea in the eastern New Territories. Its rugged coastline and proximity to the **Joss House Bay** (佛堂門) have historically made it a vital navigation point for vessels traveling along the coast of Guangdong. The peninsula is defined by its dramatic topography and historical coastal villages, which have shaped the area's development from a maritime passage into a blend of rural heritage and modern residential development.

## The Joss House Bay Rock Inscription
The most significant historical site on the peninsula is the **Joss House Bay Rock Inscription** (佛堂門天后廟石刻). Discovered in the 1970s, the inscription dates back to the **Southern Song dynasty** (1274 AD). It provides rare primary evidence of maritime activity in the region during the 13th century. 

Interpretations regarding the significance of the inscription vary. Some scholars emphasize its role as a testament to the early religious life of maritime communities, specifically the worship of the sea goddess **Tin Hau** (天后). Others focus on its administrative implications, suggesting it indicates the extent of imperial oversight or the influence of salt-tax administration in the coastal regions of the era. Among local **New Territories** (新界) communities, the site is viewed as a foundational element of the region's cultural identity and long-standing maritime traditions. Because the primary source is limited to the inscription itself, the precise circumstances of its commissioning remain a subject of academic debate.

## Historical Significance
For centuries, the waters surrounding the peninsula were a primary transit route. British colonial records often highlighted the peninsula's strategic importance for maritime surveying. In contemporary times, the peninsula is recognized as a key conservation area, balancing its rich archaeological heritage with the preservation of its natural landscape. While historical narratives have sometimes diverged between colonial-era administrative focus and local community-centered history, the peninsula remains a vital link to the maritime foundations of Hong Kong.
