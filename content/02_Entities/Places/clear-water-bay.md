## Geography and Early History
Clear Water Bay (清水灣) is located on the eastern side of the Clear Water Bay Peninsula in the Sai Kung District. Historically, the area was defined by its rugged coastline and several small Hakka (客家) villages, including Tai Hang Hau (大坑口) and Hang Hau (坑口). For centuries, these communities relied on fishing and subsistence farming. During the British colonial era, the area remained largely isolated from the rapid urban development of the Kowloon peninsula, preserving a rural character that persists in parts of the region today.

## Modern Development and HKUST
The most significant modern transformation of Clear Water Bay occurred in the late **1980s** and early **1990s** with the decision to establish the **Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST)** (香港科技大學) on the site of a former borrow area. 

Interpretations of this development vary. Proponents of the project, including the colonial administration at the time, viewed the construction of HKUST as a vital investment in the territory's transition toward a high-tech, knowledge-based economy. Conversely, some local New Territories residents and community advocates expressed concerns regarding the environmental impact of large-scale land development on the rural landscape and the potential marginalization of traditional village life. Later scholarship often positions this development as a case study in the tension between Hong Kong's rapid expansion and the preservation of its ecological and cultural heritage.

## Regional Status
While the area is synonymous with the university today, it remains a popular site for recreation, housing several public beaches and country park trails. Its history reflects the broader transition of the New Territories from a collection of peripheral agricultural and maritime outposts into an integrated component of Hong Kong's institutional and residential geography.
