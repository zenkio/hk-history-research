## Historical Development
Named after **Major-General George Charles D'Aguilar**, who served as the Lieutenant Governor of Hong Kong from 1843 to 1848, D'Aguilar Street (德己立街) has served as a vital artery in the Central district since the early years of the British colonial administration. Originally a slope connecting Queen's Road Central to the hillside, it was integral to the early layout of Victoria City. Historically, the area transitioned from a residential zone for colonial officials and merchants to a commercial hub.

## Urban Evolution and Cultural Significance
In the latter half of the 20th century, the street became synonymous with the development of the **Lan Kwai Fong** (蘭桂坊) nightlife district. The transformation of the surrounding area into a dense concentration of restaurants, bars, and entertainment venues fundamentally changed the social character of the street. While the colonial administration viewed the development as a successful revitalization of a historic district, some local observers and historians note that this commercialization frequently obscured the area's original colonial architectural heritage.

## The 1993 New Year Tragedy
The street is most somberly remembered for the **Lan Kwai Fong New Year Tragedy** on **January 1, 1993**. During a New Year's Eve celebration, a massive crowd surge occurred, resulting in 21 fatalities and over 60 injuries. Following the tragedy, debates emerged regarding urban safety and crowd control in dense, steeply sloped areas of Hong Kong. The incident led to significant changes in police protocol and event management in Hong Kong. While official reports identified the density of the crowd as the primary factor, later scholarly analysis has debated the relative weight of police mismanagement versus the unique architectural constraints of the street's intersection with **Lan Kwai Fong**.
