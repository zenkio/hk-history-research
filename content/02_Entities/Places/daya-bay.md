## Strategic Geography
**Daya Bay** (大亞灣) is situated on the coast of the Pearl River Delta, east of the **Kowloon Peninsula** and the **New Territories**. While technically outside the administrative boundaries of Hong Kong, its proximity to the region has historically influenced security and logistics. 

## The Japanese Capture of Guangzhou
During the **Second Sino-Japanese War**, **Daya Bay** became a primary site for the **Japanese** military offensive. In **October 1938**, the **Japanese 18th Division** launched an amphibious landing at **Daya Bay** as part of a campaign to isolate **Guangzhou** (廣州) and cut off supply lines to the Chinese interior. 

### Historical Interpretations
Perspectives on this operation vary significantly. **British colonial** records of the era emphasize the vulnerability of Hong Kong’s eastern flank and the resulting refugee crisis, as the collapse of southern Chinese defense lines accelerated the flow of displaced persons into the territory. Conversely, local **New Territories** community narratives often recall the event through the lens of local insecurity and the disruption of coastal trade networks. Later scholarship often focuses on the landing as a precursor to the **Pacific War**, interpreting the military logistics of the operation as a clear signal of Japanese intent to establish total dominance over the South China Sea. Beijing-centric historiography typically frames the event as a pivotal moment in the patriotic struggle against foreign encroachment, highlighting the difficulty of the resistance efforts in the region surrounding the bay.

## Modern Context
Following the **late 20th century**, the region around **Daya Bay** underwent significant industrialization, most notably with the establishment of the **Daya Bay Nuclear Power Plant** (大亞灣核電站). This facility has been a subject of extensive public discourse in Hong Kong regarding safety, environmental concerns, and cross-border energy cooperation, representing a shift from military-strategic relevance to modern economic and infrastructure interdependence.
