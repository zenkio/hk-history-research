## Geography and Early History
Deep Bay (**後海灣**), known in Mandarin as Shenzhen Bay (**深圳灣**), is located on the central-western coast of the Pearl River Delta. Historically, the shallow mudflats and brackish waters of the bay provided an ideal environment for marine life, most notably supporting the cultivation of oysters (**蠔**) for centuries. 

## Oyster Farming Tradition
Oyster farming in Deep Bay is widely documented as one of the oldest traditional industries in the region, traditionally centered around communities such as Lau Fau Shan (**流浮山**). Local lineage traditions and historical accounts suggest that oyster cultivation dates back several hundred years to the Qing dynasty (**清朝**), established by clans who migrated to the coastal areas. 

## Historiographical Perspectives
Interpretations of Deep Bay's economic and social history vary across different viewpoints:
* **Qing and Imperial Records:** Historical documents from the imperial era often emphasize the region through the lens of maritime defense, tax collection, and salt and oyster monopolies managed by local gentry and coastal families.
* **British Colonial Administration:** Following the Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory in **1898**, the British colonial government integrated Deep Bay's waters into the New Territories administration. Colonial records frequently highlight the legal frameworks surrounding fishing rights, crown ownership of foreshore and seabed, and cross-border border controls with Guangdong province.
* **Local Communities:** Oral histories and local New Territories narratives focus heavily on customary rights, lineage autonomy, and the communal organization required to maintain the complex, labor-intensive mudflat oyster beds.
* **Later Scholarship:** Modern historians and environmental studies examine Deep Bay as a space shaped by rapid urbanization, cross-border industrialization, and significant ecological transformation in the late 20th century.

## Timeline

- **1898**: Deep Bay waters and the surrounding New Territories were leased to Britain under the Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory.
- **1950**: Post-war border restrictions altered traditional cross-border movement and economic ties between Deep Bay fishers and the mainland.
- **1997**: Sovereignty over the Hong Kong portion of Deep Bay transferred to the People's Republic of China.
