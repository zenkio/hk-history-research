## Foundation and Early History

Founded in **1869** by the **Diocesan Home and Orphanage** (later renamed Diocesan Boys' School, 拔萃男書院), the institution originally served Eurasian and local orphans and students. Located initially in **Bonham Road**, the school later relocated to a sprawling campus on **Argyle Street** in **Mong Kok** (旺角) in **1926** to accommodate its growing student body and expand its facilities.

## Wartime Requisition and Military Use

During the Japanese occupation of Hong Kong (**1941**–**1945**), the school's premises, alongside numerous other prominent educational institutions across the territory, were seized by the Japanese military. While official records and local community recollections document the widespread conversion of schools into military barracks, hospitals, or prisoner holding areas, specific details regarding the exact operational units stationed at the Diocesan Boys' School remain partially obscured by the destruction of wartime archives.

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of the wartime repurposing of mission schools differ among historians and institutions. British colonial memory often emphasizes the tragic disruption of elite education and the resilience of mission traditions. In contrast, local Hong Kong community accounts and subsequent scholarship frequently contextualize these conversions as part of a broader, systemic mobilization of Hong Kong's urban infrastructure by the occupying forces. Beijing and mainland historiography traditionally view the wartime period through the lens of patriotic resistance against Japanese imperialism, encompassing the wartime experiences of Hong Kong institutions within the broader narrative of the War of Resistance.
