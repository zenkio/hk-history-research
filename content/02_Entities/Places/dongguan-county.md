## Historical Administrative Roots

Dongguan County (**東莞縣**) served as the primary administrative authority governing the region that encompasses modern-day Hong Kong during early imperial periods. Established and reorganized under various dynasties, its jurisdiction historically extended over a vast area of the Pearl River Delta, including the salt-producing and maritime trading posts situated within present-day Hong Kong waters.

## Connection to Hong Kong

Before the establishment of Xin'an County (**新安縣**) in **1573** (the first year of the Wanli reign in the Ming dynasty), the territory of Hong Kong was administered under Dongguan County. Local lineage research and Qing-era gazetteers document that many prominent New Territories clans trace their ancestral roots in the region back to migrations that occurred while the area fell under Dongguan's jurisdiction. Salt pans, military garrisons such as Tuen Mun (**屯門**), and early educational institutions in the area operated under Dongguan magisterial oversight.

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of Dongguan's administrative legacy differ among scholars and local communities. Qing dynasty and mainland Chinese historiography traditionally emphasize the gradual integration of the Pearl River Delta into imperial civil administration, viewing the region's development through the lens of Confucian education and tax incorporation. Conversely, local New Territories lineage studies and modern scholarship often highlight the semi-autonomous nature of these coastal and island communities, suggesting that imperial reach from the Dongguan county seat was frequently mediated by powerful local clans and maritime networks rather than direct bureaucratic control.
