## Historical Significance
Historically, the **Dongjiang (東江)** has been vital to the development of the Pearl River Delta. For Hong Kong, the river represents a critical geopolitical and environmental connection to mainland China, particularly regarding the territory's water security.

## Water Supply Agreements
Prior to the 1960s, Hong Kong relied heavily on local reservoirs. Frequent droughts, most notably in **1963**, necessitated an urgent agreement between the British colonial government and the authorities in Guangdong. This led to the signing of the **Dongjiang-Shenzhen Water Supply Agreement** in **1964**. 

Interpretations of this agreement vary. British colonial records often emphasize the pragmatic, humanitarian necessity of securing water to prevent civil instability during periods of severe drought. Conversely, official accounts from Beijing and the mainland focus on the integrated development of the region, framing the project as an expression of mainland support for the welfare of Hong Kong compatriots. Local residents in the New Territories, who had historically relied on smaller local streams and wells, experienced a shift in landscape management as the infrastructure required to transport **Dongjiang** water necessitated large-scale engineering projects and land acquisitions.

## Later Scholarship
Modern scholarship frequently examines the project through the lens of economic interdependence. Historians debate the extent to which the water supply was used as a tool of political leverage versus a strictly economic transaction. While some scholars argue that the reliance on mainland water solidified the integration of Hong Kong’s infrastructure with the Pearl River Delta long before the **1997** transfer of sovereignty, others caution against oversimplifying the complexity of these cross-border negotiations. The ongoing management of the water supply remains a point of academic interest, particularly concerning the ecological health of the river basin and the evolving environmental policies shared between Guangdong and the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region.
