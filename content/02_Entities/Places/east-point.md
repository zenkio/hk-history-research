## Establishment of the Settlement

**East Point** (東角), situated on the northern shore of Hong Kong Island, gained prominence following the British occupation of the territory in **1841**. The prominent Scottish trading firm **Jardine, Matheson & Co.** (怡和洋行) successfully acquired the headland at a public land auction in **1841**, moving their principal operations there from Stanley (赤柱). 

From the perspective of British colonial histories, East Point represents a foundational site of early enterprise where private commercial power directly shaped the urban geography of the nascent colony. The firm established its headquarters, godowns, wharves, and eventually repair yards at the location, transforming the promontory into a self-contained commercial enclave.

## Development and Integration

Initially separated from the growing administrative center of Victoria City by rough terrain and water inlets, East Point gradually integrated into the broader urban fabric. Later scholarship and local historical accounts emphasize how the expansion of **Jardine, Matheson & Co.** reshaped the coastline through extensive land reclamation, particularly during the late **19th century**. 

Interpretations of this development often highlight the unequal nature of colonial land allocation, where foreign agency houses secured prime waterfront property to dominate the China trade. Over time, the area evolved from an exclusive corporate enclave into a bustling commercial and residential district, eventually becoming known as part of **Causeway Bay** (銅鑼灣). Parts of the original settlement area remain associated with the Jardine Matheson group to this day.
