## Introduction and Development

**Edinburgh Place (愛丁堡廣場)** is a public square located in **Central (中環)**, Hong Kong Island, reclaimed from Victoria Harbour. Named after **Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh (愛丁堡公爵菲臘親王)**, the area developed as a civic hub alongside notable modernist buildings such as the old **City Hall (香港大會堂)** and the former **Star Ferry Pier (天星碼頭)**. 

## The Star Ferry Pier Controversy

The square gained intense public attention in **2006** during the **Demolition of the Central Star Ferry Pier**. As part of the **Central and Wan Chai Reclamation (中環填海計劃)**, the government proceeded to demolish the third-generation pier and its adjacent clock tower to make way for new roads and commercial land. 

Perspectives on the event varied significantly. **Local heritage activists and conservation groups** viewed Edinburgh Place and the pier as vital repositories of collective memory and architectural heritage, staging prolonged sit-ins and rallies on the square. Conversely, **government and pro-development proponents** emphasized the necessity of infrastructure upgrades, traffic alleviation, and land creation for the ongoing economic development of the Central business district. 

Later scholarship often marks the protests at Edinburgh Place as a watershed moment for Hong Kong's civil society, signaling a shift toward heightened public engagement in urban planning, heritage conservation, and local identity politics.
