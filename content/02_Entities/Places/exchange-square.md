## Development and Significance
Located on prime reclaimed land in **Central**, **Exchange Square** (交易廣場) was developed by **Hongkong Land** and officially opened in **1985**. Designed by the architectural firm **P&T Group**, the complex consists of three main towers and a podium, distinguished by its postmodern glass-and-granite aesthetic. It was built upon land reclaimed from **Victoria Harbour**, a process that has historically redefined the city's waterfront and fueled its expansion as a commercial center.

## The Stock Exchange Connection
In **1986**, the **Stock Exchange of Hong Kong** (香港聯合交易所) moved its trading hall into the complex, marking a pivotal moment in the unification of the territory’s four disparate stock exchanges. This relocation consolidated Hong Kong's financial infrastructure. While proponents of the era hailed the move as a necessary evolution for a modernizing economy, some local observers noted that the concentration of capital in **Central** further entrenched the dominance of elite financial institutions at the expense of regional business landscapes.

## Historical Interpretations
Perspectives on the significance of **Exchange Square** vary. From a British colonial perspective, the project represented the successful development of Hong Kong as a world-class financial center under the rule of law and international commerce. In contrast, some later academic critiques emphasize the environmental and social costs of continuous harbour reclamation, questioning the prioritization of high-density commercial space over broader public accessibility to the waterfront. Additionally, the role of the **Stock Exchange** in the transition period leading to the **1997** handover remains a subject of study; for some, the facility symbolizes the institutional continuity maintained through the transition, while for others, it represents the specific neoliberal economic structures inherited and subsequently adapted by the **Hong Kong Special Administrative Region**.
