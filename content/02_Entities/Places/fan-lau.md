## Geography and Settlement
Located at the southwestern extremity of Lantau Island, **Fan Lau** (分流)—meaning "diverging currents"—is named for the point where the waters of the Pearl River estuary meet the South China Sea. This strategic location has made it a focal point for maritime navigation and coastal defense for centuries. Historically, the area was settled by villagers engaged in fishing and agriculture, though its population has remained small due to the rugged terrain and isolation.

## The Fan Lau Fort
The most prominent historical site in the area is the **Fan Lau Fort** (分流炮台). The exact date of construction is subject to scholarly debate. While some local traditions suggest it was established during the **Ming Dynasty** (1368–1644) to combat piracy, colonial-era British records often attributed the structure to the **Qing Dynasty** (1644–1911), specifically dating its formal fortification to **1729** to protect salt production and maritime trade routes. Archaeological surveys have revealed remnants consistent with both periods, leading many historians to conclude that the fort was likely renovated and expanded over several centuries rather than built in a single instance. 

## Historical Significance
For the **Qing** authorities, the fort served as a deterrent against piracy and smuggling along the southern coast of the **Bao'an** (寶安) region. Following the **Convention of Peking** in **1898**, which ceded the New Territories to the British, the fort was largely abandoned by the military. 

Interpretations of the site's role vary significantly. British colonial historians often viewed the site through the lens of regional security and the suppression of piracy. Conversely, local New Territories communities and oral historians emphasize the site as a hub for indigenous maritime trade and a testament to the resilience of coastal settlements that existed independently of fluctuating imperial control. Today, the ruins remain a protected historical landmark, offering insights into the complex defense strategies of southern China's frontier regions.
