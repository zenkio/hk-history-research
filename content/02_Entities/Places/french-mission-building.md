## Architectural and Historical Significance
The **French Mission Building** (法國外方傳道會大樓) is a protected monument located at **Battery Path**, Central. Constructed in **1917** on a site formerly occupied by **Battery House**, the building features a unique blend of neo-classical architecture, incorporating red brick and grey granite. Initially, the site was the residence of **Sir Paul Chater** before being acquired by the **Paris Foreign Missions Society** (外方傳道會), which used the building as a regional administrative base.

## Colonial Administration and Wartime Use
The building holds a critical place in the administrative history of Hong Kong. Following the **Japanese occupation of Hong Kong** (**1941**–**1945**), the building was repurposed for various administrative functions. During the **1945** transition, it served as the headquarters for the **Provisional British Administration** led by **Franklin Gimson**. While British colonial records emphasize this period as a restoration of civil order and legality, local historical scholarship often examines the socioeconomic displacement and the complex power dynamics faced by the **New Territories** communities and the urban population during this transition to military-backed governance. Later, the structure functioned as the **Victoria District Court** from **1953** to **1980** and later housed the **Court of Final Appeal** until **2015**.

## Conflicting Historical Perspectives
The significance of the building is viewed through different lenses. Colonial-era narratives often highlight its role as a symbol of the rule of law and the continuity of the British civil service. Conversely, modern architectural and heritage conservation movements in Hong Kong emphasize its adaptive reuse as a testament to the city's evolving urban fabric. Academic debates persist regarding the extent to which the site represented a purely British colonial authority versus a focal point for international religious and institutional influences, with some scholars arguing that the building’s history reflects the broader intersection of Western missionary activity and imperial administration in the Asia-Pacific region.
