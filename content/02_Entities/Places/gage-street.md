## Historical Significance
Gage Street (結志街) is located in the Central district of Hong Kong Island. It was established in the mid-19th century as the colonial government began developing the area for urban settlement. Due to its proximity to the administrative and financial hub, it quickly became a commercial center for the Chinese community, hosting traditional markets and shops that supported the working population of the city.

## The Assassination of Yeung Ku-wan
The street is historically notable for the assassination of **Yeung Ku-wan** (楊衢雲), a prominent revolutionary and the first president of the Revive China Society (興中會). On **January 11, 1901**, while Yeung was working at a schoolhouse located on the second floor of **52 Gage Street**, he was shot by an agent dispatched by the Qing government. He died of his wounds the following day.

## Differing Interpretations
The event reflects the complex political climate of the turn of the 20th century. To the **Qing imperial authorities**, Yeung was a dangerous insurgent whose activities threatened the stability of the Chinese state, and his elimination was a matter of internal order. Conversely, in the narrative of **Hong Kong's revolutionary history** and **Sun Yat-sen's** (孫中山) movement, Yeung is remembered as a martyr for the cause of modernization and republicanism. British colonial authorities at the time viewed the event with concern, as it highlighted the inability of the colonial police to protect political dissidents seeking refuge in Hong Kong, leading to heightened tension regarding the security of political exiles within the colony. Modern historical scholarship often frames this incident as a pivotal moment that illustrated Hong Kong's role as both a sanctuary and a battleground for the political factions shaping the future of China.
