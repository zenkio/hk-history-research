## Role in Hong Kong Constitutional History
Located on the western edge of **Tiananmen Square**, the **Great Hall of the People** (人民大會堂) serves as the center of state power in the **People's Republic of China**. For Hong Kong history, it is most significant as the venue for the formal adoption of the **Basic Law** (香港基本法) on **April 4, 1990**, by the **Seventh National People's Congress** (全國人民代表大會).

## Differing Historical Interpretations
The enactment of the **Basic Law** within this space reflects divergent perspectives on the transfer of sovereignty. From the viewpoint of the **Beijing** authorities, the hall symbolizes the reassertion of Chinese sovereignty over Hong Kong, fulfilling the constitutional mandate to establish a **Special Administrative Region** (特別行政區). Conversely, many **Hong Kong** observers and international commentators viewed the proceedings as a pivotal shift in the territory’s legal identity, transitioning from British common law governance to a system anchored in the **PRC Constitution** (中華人民共和國憲法).

During the drafting period, the venue hosted intense negotiations involving members of the **Basic Law Drafting Committee** (基本法起草委員會). While the mainland participants emphasized the necessity of a stable legal framework that preserved national security, various local Hong Kong professional and civil sectors expressed concerns regarding the preservation of existing judicial independence and civil liberties. The final promulgation in this hall remains a defining moment that institutionalized the 'One Country, Two Systems' (一國兩制) principle, though the implementation and evolution of this framework continue to be subjects of debate among historians, legal scholars, and local activists.
