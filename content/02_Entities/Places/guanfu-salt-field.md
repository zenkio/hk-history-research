## Historical Context
**Guanfu Salt Field** (官富場) was one of the major official salt production sites in the **Guangdong** region during the **Song Dynasty** (960–1279) and **Yuan Dynasty** (1271–1368). It was located in the vicinity of present-day **Kowloon Bay** and **Kowloon City**. Salt production was a state monopoly, and the establishment of the 'field' or 'chang' (場) represented the formal extension of imperial administrative control over the coastal inhabitants of the region.

## Economic Significance
The site played a critical role in the regional economy, as salt was a vital commodity for food preservation and trade. Historical records suggest that **Guanfu** provided significant tax revenue for the imperial government. Scholars generally agree that the salt industry required a settled labor force, leading to the early development of local villages and maritime trade networks in the Hong Kong area. 

## Perspectives on Administration
Interpretations of the site's governance differ among various historical schools. **Qing Dynasty** (1644–1912) official gazetteers, such as the *Xin'an County Gazetteer* (新安縣志), emphasize the orderly collection of taxes and the integration of the area into the imperial bureaucracy. In contrast, local **New Territories** lineages often focus on the salt workers as the ancestors of the current indigenous populations, sometimes emphasizing the hardship of the salt labor compared to the prestige of the administrative officials sent from the central government. Some later academic studies suggest that while the salt fields were a central government project, the local population likely exercised significant autonomy in their daily operations. The exact geographic boundaries of the field remain a subject of scholarly debate, as coastal reclamation in modern **Hong Kong** has permanently altered the shoreline where the evaporation pans were once situated.
