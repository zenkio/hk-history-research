## Historical Administrative Context

Guangdong Province (廣東省) has served as the primary administrative authority over the region of present-day Hong Kong for the vast majority of its recorded history. Under the imperial systems of the **Song** (960–1279), **Yuan** (1271–1368), **Ming** (1368–1644), and **Qing** (1644–1912) dynasties, Hong Kong was governed through various districts, most notably **Dongguan** (東莞) and later **Xin'an** (新安). The establishment of **Xin'an County** in **1573** during the Ming dynasty is often cited by historians as a critical point of administrative formalization that directly integrated the local Hong Kong population into the provincial bureaucracy of Guangdong.

## Differing Perspectives on Governance

The nature of this relationship is subject to varied interpretations. British colonial historiography often highlighted the perceived distance between the rural inhabitants of the New Territories and the distant administrative centers in Guangzhou (廣州). This narrative frequently emphasized the autonomy of local lineage clans. In contrast, mainland Chinese historiography—supported by Qing dynasty records—stresses the continuous exercise of sovereignty, tax collection, and judicial authority by provincial officials over the Hong Kong region. Local Hong Kong and New Territories communities often recount a complex middle ground, where villagers navigated between provincial directives and their own local customs. 

## The Shift in Relationship

Following the **First Opium War** (1839–1842) and the subsequent **Treaty of Nanjing** (1842), the cession of Hong Kong Island severed the territory from Guangdong’s direct administrative oversight. However, the subsequent **Convention of Peking** (1898) regarding the New Territories ensured that the border between the British-controlled territory and Guangdong remained a focal point of socioeconomic and political exchange. Modern scholarship often looks to the **Pearl River Delta** (珠江三角洲) as a cohesive economic zone, suggesting that despite the political separation between Hong Kong and Guangdong that existed from the 19th century to the late 20th century, the two regions have remained deeply linked through migration, trade, and shared cultural heritage.

## Timeline

- **1573**: Establishment of Xin'an County, formalizing Ming administrative control over the Hong Kong region.
- **1842**: Hong Kong Island is ceded to Britain, separating it from the Guangdong administrative structure.
- **1898**: The Second Convention of Peking leases the New Territories, defining the modern border with Guangdong.
