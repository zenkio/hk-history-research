## Administrative and Geographic Context

Historically, the territory that now comprises Hong Kong was administered as part of **Guangdong (廣東)** province, specifically under Xin'an County (新安縣). Mainland historiography traditionally views Hong Kong's development as an extension of regional integration within Guangdong, emphasizing shared cultural, linguistic, and economic ties. In contrast, British colonial interpretations often sought to delineate Hong Kong's administration sharply from the surrounding Chinese mainland to justify its separate legal and political evolution.

## Demographics and Migration

Changes in Guangdong's interior heavily influenced Hong Kong's settlement patterns. During the **Qing dynasty (清朝)**, policies such as the **Great Clearance (遷界令)** in the **17th century** forcibly depopulated the coast, followed later by government encouragement of **Hakka (客家)** resettlement into the hills of the Pearl River Delta. Local lineage communities in the New Territories often preserve oral traditions and genealogies that record their ancestors migrating southward from various parts of Guangdong, establishing walled villages and clan alliances that persisted through dynastic transitions and the British colonial era.

## Economy and Trade

Guangdong's coastal geography made it a center for maritime commerce, salt production, and, at times, coastal piracy. The establishment of early salt fields and regional trade networks linked the Hong Kong region firmly to the broader markets of Guangzhou (廣州) and the Pearl River Delta. While colonial-era historiography highlighted the transformation of Hong Kong into a free port driven by Western enterprise, modern scholarship increasingly stresses that Hong Kong's early economic rise relied heavily on Chinese merchant networks, labor, and provisions originating from Guangdong.

- **1898**: Lease of the New Territories alters the administrative boundary between British Hong Kong and Guangdong.
