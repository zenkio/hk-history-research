## Administrative and Maritime Nexus

Guangzhou (廣州), historically known to Western traders as Canton, served as the primary administrative and economic hub of the Lingnan region. Throughout the **Tang dynasty** (**618–907**) and subsequent imperial eras, Guangzhou was the starting point and administrative anchor for the Maritime Silk Road. Its proximity to the Pearl River Estuary meant that the security and commercial activities of areas that are now part of Hong Kong—such as the Tuen Mun (屯門) military garrison—were directly supervised and supplied by Guangzhou-based authorities.

## Trade Systems and Regional Influence

During periods of fragmentation, such as the **Southern Han dynasty** (**917–971**), Guangzhou's rulers sought to project influence across the Pearl River Delta, incorporating the coastal areas of modern Hong Kong into their economic and political orbit. In later centuries, particularly under the Qing dynasty's Canton Trade System, Guangzhou was the sole legal port for maritime commerce with Western nations. This system profoundly influenced the geopolitical pressures that eventually led to the British colonization of Hong Kong in **1841**.

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of Guangzhou's relationship with Hong Kong vary across historiographical traditions. Traditional Qing and mainland Chinese scholarship often emphasizes Guangzhou as the central civilizing and administrative core that integrated the southern frontier into the imperial bureaucratic network. Conversely, local Hong Kong and New Territories historical studies frequently highlight the agency of indigenous lineages and maritime communities, viewing Guangzhou not merely as an absolute center of control, but as a distant authority whose policies local populations frequently navigated, adapted to, or evaded through maritime trade, such as the Guanxiang incense trade.

## Timeline

- **736**: The Tang dynasty establishes the Tuen Mun military garrison under the administration of Guangzhou.
- **917**: Establishment of the Southern Han dynasty centered in Guangzhou, extending regional influence over the Pearl River Delta.
- **1757**: The Qing dynasty restricts all Western maritime trade to Guangzhou, establishing the Canton Trade System.
