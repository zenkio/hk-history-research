## Introduction
Ha Tsuen (**廈村**), located in the northwestern New Territories, is one of the historic villages associated with the traditional Five Great Clans of Hong Kong. Its history is deeply intertwined with the migration and establishment of the **Ng Clan** (**鄧族**), who trace their lineage in the region back several centuries.

## The Arrival of the Ng Clan
According to traditional clan genealogies and local history, ancestors of the **Ng Clan** migrated southward from Jiangxi province during the late Song dynasty (**宋朝**). Oral traditions and local accounts often emphasize initial hardships and strategic settlement patterns as the clan sought arable land and security in the Pearl River Delta region.

## Historiography and Interpretations
Historians and local communities view the development of Ha Tsuen through different lenses:

* **Local Community Perspective:** Village histories emphasize lineage solidarity, ritual traditions, and the establishment of ancestral halls such as the **I Shing Temple** (**友恭堂**) as focal points of identity and defense against rival groups and pirates.
* **Colonial and Academic Scholarship:** British colonial records and later academic studies frequently analyze Ha Tsuen through the socio-economic structures of lineage society, land tenure, and the complex negotiations between the British administration and indigenous leaders following the 1898 Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory.

While precise founding dates and early demographic figures remain subject to scholarly debate due to the myth-making nature of traditional genealogies, Ha Tsuen remains a key site for understanding rural society in pre-colonial and colonial Hong Kong.
