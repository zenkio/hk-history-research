## Establishment and Context
Established during the **Han Dynasty** (漢代), **Hepu Commandery** (合浦郡) served as a southern territorial commandery in present-day Guangxi and Guangdong provinces. Within the historiography of early southern China, commanderies like Hepu represent the mechanisms of imperial expansion, taxation, and frontier control over non-Han indigenous groups.

## Connection to Yue Populations
In discussions regarding the **Han Dynasty Resettlement of Indigenous Yue Populations** (漢代重安置土著越人), commanderies such as Hepu are frequently analyzed as destinations or administrative hubs for the forced relocation, pacification, and assimilation of the **Baiyue** (百越) peoples. Mainstream historical scholarship generally views these policies as central to the integration of the Lingnan region into the imperial fold.

## Historiographical Perspectives
Interpretations of Han expansion into the southern commanderies vary across traditions. Traditional **Qing dynasty** (清代) and modern mainland Chinese scholarship often emphasize the civilizing mission, economic development, and eventual unification brought by imperial administration. In contrast, modern critical scholarship and regional studies sometimes highlight the violent displacement, demographic disruption, and cultural erasure experienced by indigenous communities. Because direct administrative records concerning the micro-movements of populations into and out of specific coastal enclaves like early Hong Kong remain scarce, the precise relationship between major commanderies like Hepu and the localized settlements in the Pearl River Delta is interpreted with varying degrees of caution by contemporary historians.
