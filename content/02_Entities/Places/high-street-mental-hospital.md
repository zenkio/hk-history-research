## Origins and Architecture

Constructed in **1892** and opened in **1894**, the building originally served as quarters for the nursing staff of the nearby Civil Hospital. Designed in the British colonial style with extensive use of granite and deep verandahs to combat the humid subtropical climate, the structure is locally remembered for its distinctive facade, which earned it the Cantonese moniker *Ghost House* (鬼屋) in later decades due to its haunting appearance and turbulent history.

## Transition to Psychiatric Care

By **1939**, the building was converted into an asylum to alleviate severe overcrowding at the existing psychiatric facilities on the island. During the Japanese occupation from **1941 to 1945**, the hospital experienced extreme shortages, and many records from this period remain incomplete or subject to varying historical accounts regarding the treatment of patients.

## Post-War Shift and Redevelopment

Following the Second World War, the facility functioned as a mental hospital until the opening of the **Queen Mary Hospital Psychiatric Wing** in **1961**, which absorbed most of the inpatient psychiatric services. The High Street building was subsequently downgraded to an outpatient clinic before being largely abandoned in the **1970s**.

## Preservation and Heritage

In **2015**, the remaining facade was declared a declared monument, preserving the granite stonework while the interior was redeveloped into the Sai Ying Pun Community Complex. Historical interpretations of the site vary; while colonial-era archives emphasize the incremental modernization of public health infrastructure, local oral histories and community memory often focus on its darker wartime associations and urban legends.
