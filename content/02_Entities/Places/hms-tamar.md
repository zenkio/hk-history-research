## Origins and Purpose
Originally launched in **1863**, **HMS Tamar** served as a troopship before arriving in Hong Kong in **1897** to act as a receiving ship. It was permanently moored at the naval dockyard on the northern shore of **Hong Kong Island**. The vessel became the administrative headquarters for the **Royal Navy** in the region, symbolizing British maritime power in the Far East.

## Colonial and Post-Colonial Interpretations
For the British colonial administration, the site—known as **Tamar**—represented the stability and strategic importance of Hong Kong as a gateway to East Asian trade and naval security. Following the vessel's scuttling in **1941** to prevent capture by invading Japanese forces during the **Battle of Hong Kong**, the name was transferred to the newly constructed shore-based naval facility. 

From the perspective of the **People's Republic of China**, the site was a persistent emblem of the unequal treaties and the period of colonial occupation. Conversely, many local Hong Kong residents came to view the **Tamar** site as a landmark of urban development and a familiar element of the **Victoria Harbour** waterfront. 

## The Sunset Farewell
On **April 11, 1997**, the site hosted the **Sunset Farewell Ceremony**, marking the formal end of the **Royal Navy's** presence in Hong Kong shortly before the **1997 handover**. Interpretations of this ceremony remain divided: some view it as a dignified conclusion to a century of history, while others see it as the final closing of a chapter of imperial influence. Today, the area has been transformed into the **Central Government Complex**, shifting the site's function from a symbol of naval authority to the heart of the **Hong Kong Special Administrative Region** government.
