## Early Development and Colonial Expansion

Constructed shortly after the British occupation of Hong Kong Island in **1841**, Hollywood Road (荷李活道) was among the first roads built by the colonial government. While British colonial records often emphasize the road as a triumph of early urban planning that connected Victoria City's burgeoning commercial and residential districts, local oral histories and community perspectives highlight the displacement of indigenous inhabitants and traditional land uses to make way for foreign administration.

## Religious and Cultural Landmarks

In **1847**, the **Man Mo Temple** (文武廟) was established on Hollywood Road through the funding of wealthy Chinese merchants. From the perspective of local Hong Kong communities, the temple served as a vital autonomous institution for community governance, arbitration, and the preservation of traditional Chinese cultural values under British rule. Colonial authorities, conversely, often viewed these local institutions through the lens of administrative convenience and social control.

## Education and Revolution

Hollywood Road also played a pivotal role in the intellectual and political transformation of Hong Kong. In **1862**, the **Government Central School** (中央書院) was founded nearby, representing the colonial government's effort to train a bilingual administrative class, though it simultaneously fostered a new class of critical Chinese intellectuals. Later, in **1894**, **Sun Yat-sen** (孫中山) founded the **Hong Kong Xingzhonghui** (香港興中會) in the vicinity, using the broader district as a base for revolutionary activities against the Qing dynasty. While Beijing's historiography emphasizes Sun's activities as part of the broader patriotic Chinese anti-imperialist movement, colonial accounts frequently tracked these same movements as potential security threats to British stability.
