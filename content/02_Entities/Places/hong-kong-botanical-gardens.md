## Establishment and Early Development

The **Hong Kong Botanical Gardens** (香港動植物公園), originally known simply as the Botanic Garden, was established in stages during the **1860s**, with portions officially opening to the public in **1864**. Located on the slopes of Victoria Peak above **Central (中環)**, the site was envisioned by colonial authorities not only as a recreational amenity for the European community in **Victoria City (維多利亞城)**, but also as a scientific station for acclimatizing economic plants and timber species from across the British Empire.

## Historiographical Interpretations

Interpretations of the gardens' function and symbolism diverge across different historical perspectives. British colonial records and contemporary Western accounts frequently emphasized the gardens as a civilizing triumph over a rugged landscape, framing them as a benevolent health resort and an educational showcase of botanical science. Conversely, later critical scholarship and local community analyses often highlight the site as an exclusionary spatial manifestation of colonial power, noting that access was initially restricted or culturally tailored to European norms, thereby reinforcing racial hierarchies within the urban environment of treaty-port Hong Kong. Meanwhile, mainland Chinese and Qing-era perspectives often contextualized such urban developments within the broader encroachment of British imperial administration on southern Chinese territory.

## Evolution and Legacy

Over the decades, the grounds expanded and underwent various re-landscaping efforts, eventually incorporating zoological exhibits and being officially renamed the **Hong Kong Zoological and Botanical Gardens** in **1975**. Despite shifts in governance and the physical transformation of surrounding **Central and Western District (中西區)**, the gardens remain a heavily studied site for understanding nineteenth-century colonial urban planning, landscape design, and public hygiene policies in Hong Kong.

## Timeline

- **1860**: Site selection and initial terracing begin on the slopes above Central.
- **1864**: The first phase of the Botanical Gardens officially opens to the public.
- **1871**: Charles Ford is appointed as the first superintendent, expanding scientific collection.
- **1975**: Officially renamed the Hong Kong Zoological and Botanical Gardens.
