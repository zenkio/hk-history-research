## Geographic and Maritime Context

Hong Kong Waters (香港水域) comprise the complex network of sheltered harbors, channels, and islands off the southern coast of Guangdong province. Historically, these waters provided crucial maritime routes for regional trade and rich fishing grounds that sustained generations of coastal inhabitants.

## The Tanka and Hoklo Communities

For centuries, these waters were primarily associated with boat-dwelling populations, notably the **Tanka** (蛋家 or 蜑家) and **Hoklo** (福佬). While official Qing (清) dynasty records often marginalized these communities as floating populations outside regular land-based household registration systems, they formed a vibrant maritime society deeply adapted to the coastal environment.

## Historiographical Interpretations

Interpretations of the history of Hong Kong Waters differ significantly across perspectives:

* **Qing and Imperial Perspectives:** Traditionally viewed the waters through the lens of maritime defense, anti-piracy operations, and border control, often attempting to regulate or pacify the autonomous boat populations.
* **Local and Indigenous Communities:** Oral traditions and local histories emphasize the self-sufficiency, religious customs, and distinct maritime culture of the boat-dwellers, who maintained their own networks independent of land-based gentry.
* **British Colonial Historiography:** Early colonial accounts often depicted the waters as lawless or infested with pirates prior to British intervention, a narrative that later scholarship has challenged by highlighting existing indigenous systems of marine resource management and coastal governance.
* **Modern Scholarship:** Contemporary historians focus on the socio-economic integration of the Tanka and Hoklo within the broader Pearl River Delta (珠江三角洲) economy, viewing Hong Kong Waters not as an isolated periphery, but as a dynamic maritime crossroads.

## Timeline

- **1661-1669**: The Great Clearance (遷海令) forces coastal populations, including those in Hong Kong Waters, to evacuate inland.
- **1841**: British forces occupy Hong Kong Island, initiating a new era of colonial jurisdiction over local waters.
