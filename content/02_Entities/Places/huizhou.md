## Connection to Hong Kong
Historically, **Huizhou** (惠州) maintained close socio-economic and cultural ties with Hong Kong, particularly through the diaspora of Hakka communities. During the late **Qing** dynasty, the region became a critical staging ground for the **Huizhou Uprising** (惠州起義) in **1900**, an event deeply intertwined with the political atmosphere of British Hong Kong.

## The Huizhou Uprising
In **October 1900**, the **Revive China Society** (興中會), led by **Sun Yat-sen** (孫中山), launched an armed rebellion in **Huizhou**. The uprising relied heavily on logistics, intelligence, and financial support coordinated from Hong Kong. While **Sun Yat-sen** hoped for support from the colonial government in Hong Kong to facilitate arms smuggling and provide a sanctuary for revolutionaries, the British administration—fearful of violating neutrality and destabilizing the colony—adopted a cautious and often restrictive stance. 

## Historical Interpretations
Historians offer differing views on the nature of these events. **Qing**-era officials viewed the uprising as a foreign-backed insurrection threatening imperial stability, often accusing British colonial authorities of tacitly encouraging the rebels. Conversely, nationalist historiography characterizes the **Huizhou Uprising** as a heroic, foundational effort to modernize China. Later scholarship suggests that the relationship between the Hong Kong-based revolutionaries and the British colonial government was complex and transactional; while the British prioritized colonial security, they occasionally provided indirect protection to political refugees if it served their regional geopolitical interests. Local communities in the New Territories, many of whom maintained ancestral links to the **Huizhou** region, navigated a difficult position between supporting revolutionary kinsmen and complying with the demands of the colonial administration. The extent of local New Territories support remains a subject of ongoing historical research due to limited documentation.
