## Origin of Local Lineages

According to traditional lineage genealogies, several prominent clans in the New Territories trace their ancestral origins to **Jiangxi (江西)** before migrating south through Guangdong (廣東). Notably, the **Liu Clan (廖氏)**, who eventually established major settlements in **Sheung Shui (上水)**, record roots in this region prior to their relocation. Local community oral histories and written genealogies emphasize these northern mainland origins to establish ancestral prestige and lineage continuity. However, later scholarship often treats these distant genealogical connections with caution, noting that lineage histories compiled centuries later frequently mythologized or rearranged ancestral migrations to enhance social standing.

## Hakka Resettlement and Regional Demographics

During the early **Qing dynasty (清朝)**, particularly in the aftermath of the **Great Clearance (遷界令)** between **1662** and **1669**, the Kangxi Emperor implemented policies to repopulate the devastated coastal areas of Guangdong. While the primary influx of migrant workers and farmers—commonly known as the **Hakka (客家)**—came from neighboring inland provinces like Fujian and Guangdong itself, the broader regional migrations involved populations shifting from broader interior zones, including **Jiangxi (江西)**. Qing administrative records present these resettlement schemes as orderly state-building and economic recovery initiatives. In contrast, British colonial accounts and local Punti (本地) community traditions often viewed the sudden influx of competing ethnolinguistic groups from the interior with suspicion, frequently attributing subsequent land disputes and violent territorial clashes to these state-sponsored migrations.

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of Jiangxi's role in Hong Kong history differ largely by perspective. Imperial Qing historiography highlights the benevolence of imperial edicts in stabilizing the southern frontier. Modern historians and anthropologists focus instead on how push-and-pull demographic pressures in inland provinces like Jiangxi drove centuries of cyclical southern migration, fundamentally shaping the agrarian landscape, land tenure systems, and cultural mosaic of the pre-colonial New Territories.
