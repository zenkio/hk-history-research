## Historical Significance
Located at the southern tip of the Clear Water Bay Peninsula, **Joss House Bay** (大廟灣) is best known for the **Tin Hau Temple** (佛堂門天后古廟), one of the oldest and most revered temples dedicated to the Goddess of the Sea in Hong Kong. The temple is a focal point for local maritime communities, particularly during the **Tin Hau Festival**.

## The Rock Inscription
The site is home to the **Joss House Bay Rock Inscription** (大廟灣刻石), which is officially recognized as a declared monument. Dated to **1274** during the **Southern Song Dynasty**, the inscription is the earliest dated historical document of its kind in Hong Kong. It records a visit by officials to the site, then known as **Fat Tong Mun** (佛堂門), and documents the construction of the temple. 

## Historical Interpretations
Interpretations regarding the site’s early history vary. Some scholars suggest the inscription serves as evidence of an established Southern Song administrative presence in the region, aimed at patrolling maritime trade routes and salt production. Conversely, colonial-era historiography often focused on the inscription as a rare relic of pre-colonial Chinese activity in an area later integrated into British administration. Local New Territories traditions emphasize the continuity of the **Tin Hau** cult, arguing that the site has functioned as a spiritual anchor for regional fishing communities long before and after the written records of the **Song Dynasty**. Due to the fragmented nature of archival records from the 13th century, the exact political status of the area at the time of the inscription remains a subject of ongoing academic discussion.
