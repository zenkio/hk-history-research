## Introduction
Kat Hing Wai (**吉慶圍**) is a historic walled village located in Yuen Long, New Territories, Hong Kong. It is one of the most famous walled settlements constructed by the **Tang clan**, who are among the earliest and most powerful of the Five Great Clans of the New Territories.

## Construction and Architecture
Traditionally associated with the **Ming dynasty** (**明朝**), the village's origins and exact construction timeline are subjects of ongoing historical discussion. Local oral traditions and clan records often emphasize deep-rooted lineage defense against bandits and rival groups. Architecturally, the village is characterized by its grid layout, enclosing brick walls, and four corner cannon towers, which were later modified.

## The Six-Day War of 1899
Kat Hing Wai gained major historical significance during the **Six-Day War of 1899** (**新界六日戰**). As the British colonial administration prepared to take formal control of the newly leased New Territories under the Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory, indigenous villagers mounted armed resistance. Kat Hing Wai served as a stronghold for local fighters. Following the defeat of the local militias by British forces, the colonial government notably removed the village's wrought-iron gates as a punitive and symbolic measure of subjugation. 

## Historiography and Interpretations
Perspectives on Kat Hing Wai and the events of 1899 vary significantly:
* **British Colonial Accounts:** Typically framed the resistance as lawless opposition to modernization, order, and legitimate colonial administration.
* **Local New Territories Communities:** View the resistance as a heroic defense of ancestral land, sovereignty, and traditional property rights against foreign encroachment.
* **Later Scholarship:** Often contextualizes the conflict within broader patterns of late-Qing imperial dynamics, local militarization, and the complexities of British colonial expansion.
