## Introduction

King Yin Lei (景賢里), originally known as **Crescent Gardens** (香港傲廬), is a historic mansion located at **45 Stubbs Road** (司徒拔道), Mid-Levels, Hong Kong Island. Completed in **1937**, the property is widely recognized for its striking Chinese Renaissance architecture, featuring traditional glazed Chinese tiled roofs combined with Western construction techniques.

## Ownership and Heritage

The mansion was commissioned by a wealthy merchant couple, **Mr. and Mrs. Shum Lee See** (岑日初與夫人). During the Japanese occupation of Hong Kong (1941–1945), the building reportedly served alternative uses, though specific official records from this period remain limited. In **1978**, the property was acquired by **Yau Wing Co. Ltd.** (祐榮有限公司).

## Preservation Controversy

In **September 2007**, public alarm was raised when partial demolition work began on the mansion, with workers stripping away roof tiles and architectural features. This triggered an immediate outcry from local heritage groups, concerned citizens, and scholars who emphasized the building's historical and architectural significance.

In response to public pressure, the Hong Kong Government intervened under the **Antiquities and Monuments Ordinance** (古物及古蹟條例), gazetted the mansion as a proposed monument, and later reached an agreement with the owners to exchange the site for an alternative plot of land. King Yin Lei was formally declared a monument in **July 2008**.

## Subsequent Restoration and Interpretations

Following its declaration as a monument, extensive restoration works were undertaken by the HKSAR Government to repair the damaged structures. 

Interpretations of the site's preservation vary among stakeholders. Local heritage advocates view the case as a landmark victory for grassroots conservation over pure property development rights, highlighting the shift in public consciousness regarding colonial-era and vernacular architecture. Conversely, debates persist in urban planning circles regarding the financial costs of land swaps and the long-term sustainable use of preserved heritage assets in high-density Hong Kong.
