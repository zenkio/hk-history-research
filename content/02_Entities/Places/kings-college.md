## Introduction
Established in **1926**, **King's College** (英皇書院) in **Bonham Road** (般咸道), **Sai Ying Pun** (西營盤), is one of Hong Kong's oldest and most prestigious English-medium secondary schools. Throughout its history, the institution has reflected the turbulent shifts in Hong Kong's political and social landscape, most notably during the Pacific War.

## Wartime Requisition and Military Use
During the Japanese occupation of Hong Kong (**1941**–**1945**), the school buildings—like many other educational facilities across the territory—were subjected to the **widespread conversion of schools into military barracks** and strategic installations by the **Imperial Japanese Army**. 

From the perspective of British colonial records and post-war historiography, this requisition represented a devastating disruption to local education and a stark symbol of wartime hardship. Facilities were repurposed to serve the occupying military's logistical needs, resulting in severe damage to the school's infrastructure, loss of educational records, and the dispersal of its student body and faculty. 

Conversely, wartime Japanese administrative records typically framed the repurposing of public and educational infrastructure through the lens of military necessity and total mobilization for the establishment of the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere. Local oral histories and community accounts from Hong Kong residents emphasize the abruptness of these conversions, which stripped communities of vital civic spaces and subjected the urban population to severe privation.

## Post-War Restoration
Following the surrender of Japan in **1945**, the colonial government undertook extensive efforts to reclaim and rehabilitate former military sites. **King's College** underwent major repairs and reopened for classes, symbolizing the resumption of British civil administration and the gradual rebuilding of Hong Kong's education system. Later scholarship often views the post-war reopening of institutions like King's College as part of a broader British effort to re-establish legitimacy and social order through traditional colonial administrative structures.
