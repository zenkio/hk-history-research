## Coastal Salt Industry and Early Settlement

The history of Kowloon Bay is closely tied to the **Emergence of the Coastal Salt Industry** and the **Establishment of Guanfu Salt Field** (官富場) during the Song dynasty. The sheltered waters and tidal flats provided an ideal environment for salt production, making the area a vital economic hub for the region under imperial administration.

## The Southern Song Imperial Court

In **1278**, the area gained significant historical resonance when the **Southern Song Imperial Court Flees to Kowloon** (九龍) pursued by Mongol forces. Local traditions and later scholarship emphasize this period as a foundational moment in local identity, linking the geography of Kowloon Bay to the final chapters of the Song dynasty.

## Aviation Reclamation and Urban Expansion

For centuries, Kowloon Bay remained a natural indentation along the coastline. However, twentieth-century modernization dramatically altered its geography. As the **Kai Tak Expands into Commercial Airfield**, and through the subsequent **Completion of Kai Tak Airport Runway Extension** extending into the bay, large portions of the original marine area were reclaimed.

Interpretations of these transformations often diverge. British colonial records highlight the engineering achievements in expanding aviation infrastructure to connect Hong Kong globally. In contrast, local Hong Kong and New Territories communities, alongside later scholarship, frequently reflect on the profound loss of traditional fishing grounds and the erasure of historical maritime landscapes due to rapid, state-directed reclamation.
