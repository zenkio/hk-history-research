## Imperial Refugee Haven and Sacred Hill

According to local tradition and historical accounts, Kowloon City holds early historical importance linked to the end of the Song dynasty (宋朝). In **1277**, fleeing Mongol forces, the imperial court briefly established an imperial residence at Sacred Hill (聖山), located in modern-day Kowloon City. While the physical traces of this era are largely gone, local communities long venerated this connection. Later scholarship and archaeological finds have debated the exact scale and permanence of this royal presence, but the legend deeply shaped the identity of the area.

## Maritime Defense and the Hau Wong Veneration

The region's strategic coastal location led to increased military attention during the Qing dynasty (清朝). During the tumultuous transition periods and maritime threats, local communities established traditions honoring loyalty and protection. The veneration of **Hau Wong** (侯王), a loyal official of the late Song dynasty, became a focal point of local spiritual life. The construction of the Hau Wong Temple in Kowloon City reflects the blending of local Hong Kong and New Territories community devotion with broader dynastic loyalties.

## Modern Transformation and Aviation

In the 20th century, the landscape of Kowloon City was radically transformed by infrastructure and geopolitics. The area became globally famous for its proximity to **Kai Tak Airport** (啟德機場). The completion of the Kai Tak Airport Runway Extension jutting into Victoria Harbour brought low-flying aircraft directly over the densely packed tenements and the unique enclave of the Kowloon Walled City (九龍城寨).

Interpretations of the Walled City's status differed sharply: under the Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory of **1898**, Qing officials retained jurisdiction within the walled enclave, a point of contention that persisted under British colonial administration and later Beijing's perspective. While the British colonial government often viewed the area as an uncontrollable lawless zone, many local residents and later scholars emphasized its vibrant community self-governance and resilience before its demolition in the early 1990s.

- **1950s**: Rapid postwar population surge transforms the Kowloon Walled City and surrounding urban areas.
- **1990s**: Demolition of the Kowloon Walled City and the eventual relocation of Kai Tak Airport.
