## Origins and Urban Planning

**Kowloon Tong** (九龍塘) was originally a small village located north of the boundary street of the Kowloon Peninsula. Under the **Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory** in **1898**, the area came under British lease as part of the **New Territories**. 

In the **1920s**, the colonial government attempted to develop the area into an upscale garden city to alleviate urban congestion in Victoria and southern Kowloon. British urban planners envisioned low-rise residential housing surrounded by greenery, modeled after English suburbs. However, financial difficulties plagued the original development company, leading the government to take over the project.

## Wartime and Post-War Transformation

During the Japanese occupation of Hong Kong (**1941**–**1945**), Kowloon Tong suffered from neglect and severe deforestation as trees were cut down for firewood. Following the war, the district experienced rapid rebuilding.

From the **1950s** onward, local and international interpretations of the district's zoning shifted. While the British colonial administration maintained low-density zoning regulations, post-war economic pressures and housing demands gradually transformed parts of the area. Kowloon Tong became renowned for its distinct cluster of low-rise houses, private schools, and later, hourly-rate boutique hotels colloquially known as "romantic hotels."

## Educational and Institutional Hub

By the late **20th century**, Kowloon Tong emerged as a premier educational center in Hong Kong. It hosts numerous prestigious primary and secondary schools, as well as the **Hong Kong Baptist University** (香港浸會大學) and the **City University of Hong Kong** (香港城市大學). 

The opening of the **Kowloon Tong Station** interchange, connecting the **Kowloon-Canton Railway** and the **Mass Transit Railway**, cemented the district's status as a vital transportation node linking the urban core with the New Territories. The area gained further prominence as a creative industries cluster, frequently intersecting with modern cultural initiatives such as the promotion of local design, fashion, and innovation through organizations like the **Hong Kong Design Centre** (香港設計中心).
