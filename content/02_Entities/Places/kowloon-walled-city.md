## Origins and Early Status
The **Kowloon Walled City** (九龍寨城) originated as a Qing dynasty military fortification. Following the **1842** Treaty of Nanking, which ceded Hong Kong Island to Britain, the Qing government expanded the outpost to maintain administrative presence. Under the **1898** Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory, the New Territories were leased to Britain, but the Walled City was specifically excluded, remaining under Qing jurisdiction provided it did not interfere with British military management.

## The Legal Vacuum
In **1899**, British colonial authorities attempted to occupy the site, but the legal status remained ambiguous. While Britain asserted that the Qing had abandoned their rights, the Qing government and later Chinese authorities maintained that sovereignty never transferred. This resulted in a 'lawless' period where the site became an enclave largely ignored by both the British colonial police and Chinese authorities. During the **1940s** and **1950s**, the population surged as refugees arrived from mainland China.

## Life and Demolition
By the **1980s**, the Walled City had become the most densely populated place on Earth, characterized by high-rise 'stacking' construction and a lack of formal urban planning or government services. Interpretations of the enclave vary significantly; colonial officials often focused on the area as a center for organized crime and vice, while later scholarship and local accounts emphasize the resilient, self-governing community structures that emerged in the absence of state intervention. Following the **1984** Sino-British Joint Declaration, the two governments agreed to demolish the site. Clearing began in **1991**, and the site was officially turned into **Kowloon Walled City Park** (九龍寨城公園) by **1995**.
