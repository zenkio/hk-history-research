## Background and Development

**Kwai Luen Estate** (**葵聯邨**) is a public rental housing estate located in Kwai Chung (**葵涌**), New Territories (**新界**). Developed by the Hong Kong Housing Authority (**香港房屋委員會**), the estate was constructed to help meet public housing demands and consists of multiple residential blocks completed in the 2010s.

## The 2015 Lead-in-Water Scandal

The estate gained major prominence in **July 2015** when it became one of the primary epicenters of the territory-wide public housing drinking water contamination crisis, commonly known as the lead-in-water scandal. Routine water tests initiated by local residents and district councillors revealed elevated lead levels in the drinking water supply of **Kwai Luen Estate**'s **Kwai Luen Phase 2** (**葵聯邨第二期**), specifically in Luen King House (**聯欣樓**) and Luen Lok House (**聯樂樓**).

Subsequent investigations revealed that lead solder used in plumbing joints within newly constructed public housing estates was the primary source of the contamination. The discovery sparked widespread public panic, political debate, and health screening initiatives for affected residents, particularly young children and pregnant women. 

## Interpretations and Legacy

The incident severely impacted public trust in the Hong Kong government's quality assurance mechanisms for public works and housing construction. While government-appointed independent commissions and housing officials attributed the issue primarily to sub-contractor workmanship and industry-wide plumbing practices, local communities, concern groups, and opposition legislators argued that systemic regulatory oversight failures and inadequate government monitoring were also to blame. The crisis led to long-term remedial measures, including the retrofitting of water filters and stricter testing protocols across public estates.
