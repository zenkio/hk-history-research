## Introduction and Administrative Context

**Kwai Tsing** (葵青) comprises the **Kwai Chung** (葵涌) and **Tsing Yi** (青衣) areas in the southern New Territories. Historically part of Tsuen Wan, the district was formally separated in **1985** to manage its rapid population growth and intense industrial development. The area contains extensive public housing estates, which house a significant portion of Hong Kong's working-class population.

## The 2015 Public Housing Lead Contamination Crisis

In **July 2015**, Kwai Tsing became the epicenter of the **Discovery of Toxic Lead in Public Housing Drinking Water** (公屋食水含鉛超標事件). Routine water testing at the newly completed **Kai Ching Estate** in Kowloon initially exposed the crisis, but subsequent investigations quickly spread across the territory, heavily impacting public housing estates within Kwai Tsing, such as **Shek Lei Estate** (石籬邨).

Laboratory tests revealed that solder used in copper pipe joints contained excessive levels of lead, which leached into the drinking water supply. This discovery sparked widespread panic and health concerns among residents, particularly regarding the long-term developmental impacts on young children and pregnant women.

## Historiographical Perspectives and Official Response

The crisis prompted differing interpretations regarding institutional accountability and public safety:

* **Local Communities and Residents:** Affected tenants and local concern groups viewed the contamination as a severe systemic failure of government oversight, negligence by main contractors, and inadequate initial transparency from the **Housing Authority** (房屋委員會).
* **Government and Official Inquiries:** A government-appointed commission of inquiry investigated the root causes, attributing the issue primarily to sub-contractor non-compliance and industry-wide practices rather than deliberate regulatory evasion.

Ultimately, the incident led to the implementation of mandatory water testing for all new public housing projects, extensive plumbing replacements, and heightened public scrutiny over the safety of Hong Kong's infrastructure.
