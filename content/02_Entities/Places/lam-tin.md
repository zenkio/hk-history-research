## Introduction and Geography

Lam Tin (**藍田**) is situated in New Kowloon, east of Kwun Tong (**觀塘**). Historically, the area was characterized by rugged terrain and small, rural settlements before mid-20th-century urbanization transformed the landscape.

## Urbanization and Public Housing

During the post-war population boom, the colonial government identified Lam Tin as a prime location for mass public housing to accommodate refugees and low-income families. The construction of the Lam Tin Estate (**藍田邨**) in the **1970s** reshaped the demographic and social fabric of the district. Later redevelopment projects in the **1990s** and **2000s** replaced older, slab-block buildings with modern high-rises, altering the local community structure.

## Transport Connectivity

As a crucial transit corridor, Lam Tin connects urban Kowloon with the eastern New Territories. The opening of infrastructural projects, including the Tseung Kwan O Cross Bay Link (**將軍澳跨灣連接路**) and associated tunnel systems, further integrated Lam Tin into the broader territorial road network, significantly easing commuter congestion between East Kowloon and Tseung Kwan O (**將軍澳**). Local communities and urban planners have often debated the long-term environmental and traffic impacts of these massive arterial developments on older residential neighborhoods.
