## Prehistoric and Early Settlements
Lantau Island (大嶼山) holds some of the earliest evidence of human activity in the territory. Archaeological findings, including the **Introduction of Coarse Cord-Marked Pottery**, indicate prehistoric settlement and maritime exchange networks connecting the Pearl River Estuary to broader coastal cultures.

## Imperial Era and Maritime Conflict
During the Song dynasty, the island became a crucial refuge and battleground. Historical records note the **Devastating Typhoon Strikes the Song Imperial Fleet** and the subsequent **Death of Emperor Duanzong at Mui Wo** (梅窩). These events are closely tied to the broader **Battle of Yamen and Local Veneration of Hau Wong** (侯王), reflecting how local populations memorialized loyalist resistance against the Mongol invasion.

## Economy and Local Uprisings
In the post-Song and imperial periods, Lantau's coastal geography supported distinct rural economies. The region saw significant salt production, which culminated in the **Lantau Island Salt Farmers' Uprising**, reflecting socioeconomic tensions under imperial administration. Local communities also participated in regional commodity production, as evidenced by the **Expansion of the Guanxiang Incense Trade** (官香樹/沙螺湾等), which linked local villagers to wider markets.

## Strategic and Modern Interpretations
Due to its remote and rugged terrain, Lantau has frequently served as a strategic frontier. While colonial records and local oral histories often emphasize its role in coastal defense and piracy—including encounters such as the **Battle of Xicaowan** (西草灣之役) in the Ming period—later historiography and local New Territories traditions frequently reframe these narratives. Modern scholarship highlights the island's dynamic indigenous communities, balancing the perspectives of British colonial archives with mainland Chinese administrative histories.
