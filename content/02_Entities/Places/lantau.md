## Early Maritime Context
Lantau (大嶼山) has played a pivotal role in the maritime history of the Pearl River Delta. Archaeological evidence suggests that the island was part of a broader network of **Austronesian** maritime trade routes that connected coastal communities across the South China Sea. These early inhabitants utilized the island's sheltered bays for navigation and exchange, marking it as a node in prehistoric regional commerce.

## Shifting Hegemonies
Historical interpretations of Lantau's governance differ significantly based on the perspective of the era. From the perspective of the **Qing Dynasty** (清朝), the island was a strategic outpost for coastal defense, particularly against piracy. During the **19th century**, the island became a point of contention between the British colonial administration and local indigenous communities of the **New Territories** (新界). 

British colonial historiography often emphasizes the island's role in the expansion of maritime trade and the eventual formalization of colonial control after the **1898** Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory. Conversely, local New Territories communities often view this period through the lens of land rights and the preservation of traditional social structures, which frequently resisted external administrative oversight. Modern scholars suggest that Lantau served as a buffer zone rather than a centralized administrative center, reflecting the complex, localized nature of authority in the region prior to the mid-20th century.
