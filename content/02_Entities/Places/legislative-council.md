## Origins and Colonial Era
Established in **1843** under British colonial rule, the Legislative Council (立法會) initially functioned as an appointed advisory body consisting entirely of official members, later including unofficial members representing British commercial interests. For much of its early history, the council operated without direct public representation. 

## Democratization and Handover
Pressures for democratic reform grew following the Sino-British Joint Declaration in **1984**. The first indirect elections were introduced in **1985**, and limited direct elections to geographical constituencies finally took place in **1991**. Throughout the transition period leading up to the **1997** transfer of sovereignty, the legislature became a focal point of intense political maneuvering between the British administration, local pro-democracy movements, and Beijing.

## Post-Colonial Evolution and Protests
Under the **1997** Basic Law, the Legislative Council was structured to balance geographical and functional constituencies, a system criticized by pro-democracy advocates for skewing representation toward business elites. Over the post-handover decades, LegCo became a stage for fierce legislative battles and procedural disruption. Figures such as **Leung Kwok-hung** (梁國雄), known as "Long Hair," pioneered confrontational protest tactics inside the chamber starting in the **2000s**, challenging traditional parliamentary decorum to protest government policies and limited electoral democracy.

## Interpretations
Historiographical and political perspectives on LegCo remain divided. British colonial narratives often emphasize the steady evolution toward representative governance. Conversely, Beijing and pro-establishment interpretations frequently frame rapid democratization efforts late in the colonial period as a disruptive strategy designed to complicate Chinese resumption of sovereignty. Meanwhile, local Hong Kong civil society and later scholarship often view the council's structural limitations as a root cause of prolonged political polarization culminating in major institutional overhauls in the **2020s**.

- **1997**: Hong Kong sovereignty transferred; LegCo reconstituted under the Basic Law.
