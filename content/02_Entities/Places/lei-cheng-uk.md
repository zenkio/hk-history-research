## Geography and Early Settlement

**Lei Cheng Uk** (李鄭屋) is situated in New Kowloon (新九龍), bordered by Sham Shui Po (深水埗) and Cheung Sha Wan (長沙灣). Historically, the area was rural and rocky, characterized by traditional agricultural practices and small-scale granite quarrying before mid-20th-century urbanization.

The locality gained major historical prominence in **August 1955** during the **Construction of Lei Cheng Uk Tomb** (李鄭屋古墓發現), when government workers leveling a hillside for resettlement housing uncovered a brick-built ancient tomb.

## Archaeological Discovery and Interpretations

The **Discovery of the Lei Cheng Uk Han Tomb** (李鄭屋漢墓發現) sparked significant academic and political interest regarding Hong Kong's early integration into Chinese imperial history. The tomb's structure, featuring four chambers arranged in a cross shape, and the recovery of dozens of pottery and bronze artifacts strongly indicate a burial dating from the Eastern Han dynasty (**25–220 AD**).

Interpretations of the tomb's presence vary among historians and political observers:

* **Mainland Chinese and Traditional Scholarship:** Proponents of this view see the tomb as definitive archaeological proof of early Chinese administration and cultural presence in the Lingnan region, aligning with the narrative of continuous Chinese sovereignty and civilizational reach.
* **Colonial and Modern Scholarship:** British colonial authorities at the time, alongside later independent historians, approached the site through a purely archaeological lens, emphasizing trade routes, migration patterns of refugees or officials from the central plains, and local frontier dynamics rather than direct political control.
