## Geography and Early Societies

Lingnan (嶺南), literally meaning "south of the mountain ranges," encompasses the watershed of the Pearl River system. In archaeological and historical contexts, this geographic barrier separated the central plains of China from the coastal and tropical territories inhabited by various indigenous peoples collectively referred to in ancient texts as the **Baiyue** (百越).

## Technological and Cultural Transitions

Historically, the integration of Lingnan into broader East Asian networks occurred gradually. Archaeological evidence from the **Introduction of Bronze Metallurgy** and the subsequent **Emergence of Advanced Bronze Weapons** indicates local adaptation and exchange. The **Widespread Adoption of Stilt-House Architecture** reflects common environmental adaptations to the humid, flood-prone climate across the delta, including areas that would later become Hong Kong. As external pressures mounted, particularly through the **Defensive Tribal Alliances Form**, local communities organized to navigate shifting regional dynamics.

## Imperial Incorporation

Perspectives on Lingnan's incorporation into the imperial sphere vary. Traditional historiography, reflected in both **Qing/Beijing** official histories and later dynastic records, frames the **Qin Dynasty Conquest of Lingnan** and the **Qin Dynasty's Southern Expansion** under **Qin Shi Huang** as a civilizing unification that brought administrative order to the southern frontier. Conversely, modern local Hong Kong and New Territories scholarship, alongside post-colonial interpretations, often re-examines this process as imperial expansion and colonization that subjugated indigenous polities. Following the collapse of the Qin, this geopolitical space enabled the **Foundation of the Nanyue Kingdom** (南越國) by **Zhao Tuo** (趙佗), establishing a distinct regional identity that blended Han and Baiyue cultures.
