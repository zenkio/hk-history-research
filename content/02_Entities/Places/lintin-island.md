## Strategic Anchorage and the Opium Trade
Located in the Pearl River Estuary, **Lintin Island** (內伶仃島) gained historical prominence in the early **19th century**. Because the **Qing Dynasty** (清朝) authorities strictly enforced prohibitions on foreign vessels entering the **Pearl River** (珠江) to trade opium, British and American merchants established **Lintin Island** as a floating depot. From the **1820s** onwards, large armed receiving ships, known as store-ships, were permanently stationed in the anchorage. Small, fast craft would ferry opium from these ships to various coastal points, bypassing the **Canton System** (廣州制度).

## Historical Perspectives
Interpretations of this period differ significantly depending on the historical lens. British and Western colonial records often framed the activities at **Lintin Island** as an expression of the necessity of free trade against the perceived restrictive and corrupt practices of the **Qing** bureaucracy. Conversely, mainland Chinese historical scholarship emphasizes the event as an act of colonial intrusion and the illicit facilitation of drug trafficking that caused profound social and economic damage to China. Local historians and scholars in **Hong Kong** often highlight the island's role as a precursor to the development of the **Victoria Harbour** (維多利亞港) as a superior deep-water port, noting that the logistical reliance on **Lintin Island** eventually proved insufficient for the growing demands of British mercantile interests.

## Impact on Hong Kong
The failure of the **Qing** government to effectively suppress the trade centered at **Lintin Island** led to escalating tensions, culminating in the **First Opium War** (1839–1842). Following the **Treaty of Nanking** (南京條約), the shift of trade and administrative focus to the newly ceded **Hong Kong Island** rendered the role of **Lintin Island** as a primary depot largely obsolete. While now under the jurisdiction of the **People’s Republic of China** (中華人民共和國), the island remains a symbol of the transitional era that saw the shift from the **Canton System** to the rise of British maritime power in the region.
