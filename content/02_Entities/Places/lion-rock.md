## Geography and Early Context

Lion Rock (獅子山) is a 495-meter granite peak situated on the boundary between **Kowloon** (九龍) and the **New Territories** (新界). Historically, the mountain served as a physical divider between the urbanized peninsula and the rural northern territories. Local villagers in the surrounding areas, such as **Kowloon Tsai** (九龍仔) and **Wong Tai Sin** (黃大仙), regarded the terrain through traditional agricultural and religious lenses, though detailed records specific to the mountain prior to the 20th century remain sparse.

## The 'Lion Rock Spirit' and Television

During the post-war decades, as Hong Kong experienced rapid industrialization and an influx of refugees, the mountain became deeply embedded in the social fabric of the city. Its cultural significance was largely popularized by the **RTHK** (香港電台) television drama series **Below the Lion Rock** (獅子山下), which premiered in **1972**. The series portrayed the daily struggles, resilience, and mutual aid of ordinary working-class residents.

Interpretations of what the mountain represents vary across different political and social perspectives:

* **Critical Scholarship:** Later historians and sociologists note that the concept of the 'Lion Rock Spirit' was partly constructed and promoted through government-backed media to foster social cohesion and stability during a turbulent economic and political era.
