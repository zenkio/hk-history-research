## Historical Significance
Lo Wu (羅湖) is a village and transit point located in the North District of Hong Kong. It sits on the northern bank of the Shenzhen River, directly adjacent to the city of Shenzhen. Its strategic importance as a transit hub grew significantly following the **1898** Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory, which established the border along the Shenzhen River.

## The Railway Connection
The construction of the Kowloon-Canton Railway (KCR) in **1910** linked Tsim Sha Tsui with Guangzhou, passing directly through Lo Wu. For the British colonial administration, the Lo Wu station served as the official customs and immigration checkpoint. During periods of geopolitical tension, such as the **1950s** and the Cold War era, the border at Lo Wu became a symbolic flashpoint, representing the physical and ideological division between the British-governed territory and the People's Republic of China.

## Differing Perspectives
Historical interpretations of the Lo Wu boundary vary. Colonial-era records often frame the site as a strictly regulated barrier designed for administrative and security control. Later scholarship has emphasized how the checkpoint evolved from a military and administrative necessity into the primary artery for the economic integration of Hong Kong and the Pearl River Delta during the late **20th century**.

## Modern Development
The role of Lo Wu was further refined by infrastructure projects such as the **2007** Completion of the Lok Ma Chau Spur Line, which sought to alleviate congestion at the main Lo Wu border crossing. While its primary function remains transit, the site continues to be a focal point for studies on urban development and cross-border mobility in the Greater Bay Area.
