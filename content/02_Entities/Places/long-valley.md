## Historical Significance
Located between Sheung Shui (上水) and Lok Ma Chau (落馬洲), **Long Valley** (塱原) serves as one of the largest remaining contiguous freshwater wetlands in Hong Kong. Historically, the area has been utilized for intensive vegetable farming by local New Territories communities, reflecting traditional land-use patterns that date back decades.

## Infrastructure and Conflict
The area gained national and international attention during the early **2000s** due to the planning of the **Lok Ma Chau Spur Line** (落馬洲支線). The **Kowloon-Canton Railway Corporation** (九廣鐵路公司) originally proposed an elevated railway crossing through the heart of the wetland. This project sparked significant debate between environmental advocates, who argued that the development would irreparably damage the habitat of migratory birds, and government planners, who emphasized the necessity of the rail link to alleviate border congestion.

## Perspectives on Development
Interpretations regarding the impact of the **Lok Ma Chau Spur Line** remain divided. Government and corporate stakeholders emphasized that the project was a vital infrastructural necessity for regional integration with the mainland. Conversely, environmental NGOs and local community groups argued that the proposed alignment failed to prioritize ecological conservation. Following intense public discourse, the government eventually opted to construct the railway through a tunnel to mitigate environmental impact. Later scholarship has frequently cited this episode as a turning point in Hong Kong’s environmental policy, marking an era where public consultation and ecological impact assessments became central to infrastructure planning.

## Current Status
Today, **Long Valley** operates under a management agreement scheme involving the **Agriculture, Fisheries and Conservation Department** (漁農自然護理署) and local farmers. It is widely recognized for its high ecological value, providing a vital stopover for migratory birds, while continuing to serve as a symbol of the ongoing tension between urbanization and rural preservation in Hong Kong.
