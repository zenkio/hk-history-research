## Geography and Early Settlement

**Ma Liu Shui** (馬料水) is located on the shore of Tolo Harbour (吐露港) in the Sha Tin (沙田) district of the New Territories. Historically, the area consisted of small coastal villages and agricultural lands inhabited primarily by local indigenous clans. Traditional accounts and local community memory suggest the area's name historically linked to local geography or early maritime activities, though precise etymological origins remain subject to varying local interpretations.

## Colonial Administration and Development

Following the Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory in **1898**, Ma Liu Shui fell under British colonial administration as part of the New Territories. For decades, the area remained largely rural. The opening of the Kowloon-Canton Railway (British Section) brought a station to the vicinity, gradually integrating the isolated coastal area into the wider territorial transport network.

## The Establishment of the Chinese University

Ma Liu Shui gained profound significance in modern Hong Kong history when it was selected as the site for the **Chinese University of Hong Kong** (CUHK). Established in **1963** by combining existing tertiary institutions such as Chung Chi College, New Asia College, and United College, the university was granted a sprawling hillside campus overlooking Tolo Harbour. 

From the perspective of colonial administration and institutional history, the choice of Ma Liu Shui provided the physical space necessary to foster a distinctive bilingual and bicultural higher education model. Conversely, local New Territories communities witnessed significant landscape transformation as rural terrain gave way to modern academic infrastructure, dormitories, and transport links including the later University Station. Later scholarship often highlights how the establishment of CUHK at Ma Liu Shui reflected broader post-war geopolitical dynamics, balancing British colonial policy with the preservation of traditional Chinese humanist scholarship in Hong Kong.
