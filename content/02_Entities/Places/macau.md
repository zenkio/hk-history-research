## Early Portuguese Contact and Trade
Macau served as the primary base for Portuguese maritime expansion in South China during the early **16th century**. Following exploratory voyages such as that of **Jorge Álvares** landing at Tuen Mun (屯門) in **1513**, and subsequent military confrontations including the Battle of Tunmen in **1521** and the Battle of Xicaowan (西草灣) in **1522**, Portuguese traders shifted their focus from the immediate Pearl River Estuary approaches toward a permanent foothold on the Macau peninsula, which was formally leased from the Ming dynasty in **1557**.

## Evolution of Regional Trade
For centuries, Macau functioned as the dominant European entrepôt in the region, managing trade between China, Japan, and Europe. Later, with the establishment of the **Canton Trade System** (廣州貿易體系), Macau remained a vital administrative and residential base for foreign merchants before the opening of Hong Kong.

## Refuge and Wartime Connections
Macau's role as a neutral territory under Portuguese administration during various regional conflicts frequently intersected with Hong Kong's history. During geopolitical crises, such as the early phases of British involvement and later wartime disruptions, Macau provided a sanctuary. Notably, during periods of extreme danger or displacement, including events connected to the **Evacuation of British Community to Hong Kong Anchorage**, the proximity and neutral status of Macau offered a crucial refuge for displaced populations.

## Modern Integration
In the contemporary era, the historical trade and maritime relationship between the two territories has been physically reinforced by modern infrastructure, notably the opening of the **Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macau Bridge** (港珠澳大橋), linking the two Special Administrative Regions directly.

## Timeline

- **1513**: Jorge Álvares lands at Tuen Mun, initiating direct Portuguese exploration of the Pearl River Delta.
- **1557**: Portuguese reach a formal agreement with Ming authorities to establish a permanent settlement in Macau.
- **1841**: Britain occupies Hong Kong island, shifting the primary regional entrepôt balance away from Macau.
- **2018**: The Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macau Bridge opens, physically connecting Macau, Hong Kong, and Zhuhai.
