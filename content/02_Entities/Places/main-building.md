## History and Construction
Completed in **1912**, the Main Building is an Edwardian-style structure designed by the architectural firm **Leigh & Orange**. It was funded largely by the Parsi philanthropist **Sir Hormusjee Naorojee Mody**. The building was constructed to house the newly established University of Hong Kong, reflecting the British colonial administration's desire to provide higher education for the local elite and strengthen influence in the region.

## Symbolic Significance
For many historians of British colonial governance, the building represents the 'civilizing mission' and the transplantation of British academic traditions into an Asian context. In contrast, scholars examining the interaction between the university and the broader Chinese community often point to the building as a site where local identity began to shift, as students from diverse backgrounds interacted within its halls. 

## The Closure of 1941
During the **Battle of Hong Kong** in December **1941**, the Main Building and the wider university campus faced significant disruption. Following the surrender of the British garrison, the university entered a period of closure. British colonial narratives often emphasize the wartime suffering of the academic staff and students. Conversely, some later scholars and local accounts highlight how the university was utilized or targeted by the invading Japanese forces, viewing the closure as a rupture in colonial institutional life. The building later served as a makeshift infirmary and was subject to looting during the occupation. The facility resumed operations following the **1945** liberation, serving as a focal point for the university's post-war reconstruction efforts.
