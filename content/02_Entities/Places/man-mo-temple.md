## Introduction and Foundation
Located on Hollywood Road in Sheung Wan, the **Man Mo Temple** (文武廟) is dedicated to the God of Literature (Man) and the God of Martial Arts (Mo). Constructed between **1847** and **1862** largely through the donations of wealthy Chinese merchants, the temple complex quickly became the administrative and spiritual centre for the local Chinese population during the early British colonial period.

## Role in the Chinese Merchant Guild System
In the absence of trusted British legal institutions in the decades following the cession of Hong Kong in **1841**, the Chinese merchant elite utilized the temple as a quasi-judicial venue. As explored in the history of the **Formation of the Chinese Merchant Guild System**, community leaders resolved commercial disputes, swore oaths, and settled internal governance matters within the sacred space of the temple. This practice underscored the autonomy and self-regulation of the early Chinese commercial network.

## Historiographical Perspectives
Interpretations of the temple's early functions vary among scholars. British colonial records often viewed these indigenous community-led arbitration practices through the lens of indirect rule, wherein colonial authorities permitted Chinese elites to manage their own populace to maintain social order. Conversely, local and mainland perspectives emphasize the temple as a symbol of cultural preservation and anti-colonial resilience, where the Chinese community maintained traditional governance structures independent of European interference. Later scholarship highlights the complex negotiations between Chinese merchant capital and colonial administration, noting how the temple served as an institutional bridge between two distinct legal cultures.
