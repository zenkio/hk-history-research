## Role in the 2003 SARS Outbreak
The **Metropole Hotel** (京華國際酒店), located in **Waterloo Road**, Kowloon, is primarily recognized in historical accounts for its role in the global spread of **Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome (SARS)**. In **February 2003**, a visiting physician from **Guangzhou**, later identified as a primary carrier, checked into room **911** of the hotel. 

Medical historians and epidemiological studies generally agree that this location served as a 'superspreading' hub. While the physician was symptomatic, he reportedly transmitted the virus to several other guests staying on the same floor. These individuals subsequently traveled to other parts of the world, including **Canada**, **Singapore**, and **Vietnam**, facilitating the international dissemination of the disease. The specific mechanism of transmission—whether via elevator air currents or shared facilities—remains a subject of professional debate among epidemiologists. 

## Historical Significance and Impact
The events at the **Metropole Hotel** prompted a shift in how Hong Kong health authorities addressed infectious diseases. Public health officials and international observers noted that the hotel’s concentration of cases highlighted the challenges of managing global travel hubs during a nascent epidemic. In the aftermath of the crisis, the hotel became a symbolic marker of the city’s vulnerability, leading to significant reforms in local hospital infection control protocols and enhanced collaboration between the **Hong Kong SAR Government** and the **World Health Organization**. While the hotel remains a functional business today, its name remains inextricably linked to the early weeks of the 2003 health emergency in the collective memory of the city.
