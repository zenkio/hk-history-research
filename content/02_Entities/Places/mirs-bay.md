## Geography and Strategic Importance
Mirs Bay (大鵬灣) sits between the Dapeng Peninsula of Guangdong province and the northeastern coast of the Hong Kong New Territories, including Sai Kung (西貢) and Ping Chau (坪洲). Because of its complex coastline, numerous islands, and relative isolation from urban colonial centers, the bay historically served as a haven for fishermen, salt panners, and maritime traders.

## Wartime Operations and the Secret Rescue Network
During the Japanese occupation of Hong Kong (**1941**–**1945**), Mirs Bay gained significant historical prominence as a transit route for the **Secret Rescue of Allied Airmen**. Following the fall of Hong Kong on **Christmas Day 1941**, local guerrilla forces—notably the **Dongjiang Column** (東江縱隊)—utilized the secluded coves and islands of Mirs Bay to smuggle downed American pilots, British soldiers, and Chinese intellectuals out of occupied territory into Free China.

## Historiographical Perspectives
Interpretations of the wartime activities in and around Mirs Bay vary across different political and community traditions. 

* **Mainland Chinese historiography** (associated with Beijing and the descendants of the guerrillas) emphasizes the leadership of the Chinese Communist Party and the **Dongjiang Column** in organizing patriotic local resistance and coordinating intelligence with the British **British Army Aid Group** (BAAG).
* **Local New Territories and fisherman oral histories** often focus on the agency of ordinary boat people and villagers who undertook immense personal risks for humanitarian reasons, sometimes independent of rigid ideological affiliations.
* **British colonial and Western accounts** historically acknowledged the success of these operations in recovering valuable military personnel, though Cold War dynamics sometimes led authorities to downplay the communist-led elements of the wartime rescue networks in official post-war narratives.
