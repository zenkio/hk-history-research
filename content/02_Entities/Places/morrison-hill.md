## Topography and Development
Morrison Hill (摩理臣山) was a significant geographical feature located in the Wan Chai district on Hong Kong Island. Named after the Scottish missionary **Robert Morrison** (馬禮遜), the hill served as a major site for urban development as the British colonial government sought to increase buildable land in the rapidly growing colony. 

## The Morrison Hill Quarry
In the **1920s**, the area became central to the city’s infrastructure projects due to the **Opening of the Morrison Hill Quarry**. This operation played a pivotal role in providing stone and fill materials necessary for the massive **Praya East Reclamation** (海旁東填海計劃). While colonial authorities viewed the levelling of the hill as a pragmatic necessity for modern urban expansion and public health, later historical scholarship often highlights the physical erasure of the natural landscape and the displacement of earlier land uses. The removal of the hill significantly altered the topography of the Wan Chai coastline, turning a once-prominent landmark into the flat terrain that defines the modern district today.

## Conflicting Perspectives
Interpretations of this landscape transformation vary. From a colonial administrative perspective, the quarrying was framed as a triumph of engineering and economic necessity that enabled the expansion of residential and commercial spaces. Conversely, local history records often reflect on the loss of the original geographical identity of Wan Chai. Some historical accounts suggest the presence of early burial grounds or informal settlements on or near the hill slopes prior to large-scale development, though precise details remain sparse and are often contested in academic discourse. Today, the name survives primarily through local street names and the legacy of the **Morrison Hill Swimming Pool** and **Morrison Hill Technical Institute**, preserving the memory of a landmark that no longer exists in its physical form.
