## Introduction and Geography
Mui Wo (**梅窩**), historically also known as Mei Wo, is a rural township located on the eastern coast of Lantau Island (**大嶼山**). While modern development has transformed it into a residential area and ferry hub, its historical significance largely stems from its role as a temporary refuge for the fleeing imperial court of the Southern Song dynasty (**南宋**).

## Song Dynasty Refuge
In **1276**, as Mongol forces advanced, the Song court fled south. According to traditional accounts and local histories, the young Emperor Duanzong (**宋端宗**), along with his brother and remaining officials, sought refuge in the territory of Hong Kong. Mui Wo and the surrounding Silver Mine Bay area are traditionally associated with these movements, specifically linked to the *Establishment of Imperial Residence at Sacred Hill* and the eventual *Death of Emperor Duanzong at Mui Wo*.

## Historiographical Perspectives
Interpretations of this imperial episode vary across different historical traditions. 
* **Local New Territories Communities:** Oral traditions and local lineage histories often emphasize Lantau Island and the coastal areas as sites of royal loyalty, preserving ancestral shrines and place-name legends connected to the Song entourage.
* **Qing and Imperial Historiography:** Qing dynasty officials and court historians recorded these events primarily to document the final desperate resistance of the Song, often framing it through the lens of dynastic legitimacy.
* **Later Scholarship:** Modern historians treat the specific details of the court's movements with caution due to the scarcity of contemporary, verifiable primary sources. While the broad southward flight of the Song loyalists is undisputed, scholars debate the precise locations and duration of the imperial stays, noting that local legends frequently conflate multiple historical events and figures from the era.
