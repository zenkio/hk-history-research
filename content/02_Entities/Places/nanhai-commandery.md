## Establishment and Early Administration

Following the **Qin dynasty**'s military campaigns into southern China, the First Emperor (**Qin Shi Huang**) divided the newly subjugated Lingnan region into several administrative commanderies around **214 BC**. Among these was **Nanhai Commandery** (南海郡), headquartered at Panyu (番禺). Modern historical scholarship generally agrees that the southern boundaries of Nanhai Commandery encompassed the Pearl River Delta, including the islands and coastal territories that constitute modern-day Hong Kong.

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations regarding the nature of early imperial control over the Hong Kong region vary across different historiographical traditions:

* **Traditional Chinese & Mainland Scholarship:** Official histories generally emphasize the rapid integration of Lingnan into the Chinese imperial fold through the implementation of commandery-county systems, infrastructure projects like the Lingqu Canal, and the relocation of northern populations.
* **Local Hong Kong & New Territories Communities:** Local ancestral and clan traditions often focus on subsequent migration waves rather than the initial Qin military presence, viewing the deep historical roots of the region through centuries of localized lineage development.
* **British Colonial & Later Academic Scholarship:** Western and modern archaeological studies often characterize early imperial administration in peripheral regions like Hong Kong as relatively indirect, relying heavily on existing indigenous social structures rather than intensive bureaucratic control.

## Subsequent Dynastic Transitions

As the Qin fell, Nanhai Commandery briefly formed the core of the independent **Nanyue Kingdom** (南越國) founded by **Zhao Tuo** (趙佗) in **203 BC**. After the **Han dynasty** conquered Nanyue in **111 BC**, the administrative structure was reorganized, eventually leading to further subdivision and the later establishment of jurisdictions such as **Dongguan County** (東莞縣).
