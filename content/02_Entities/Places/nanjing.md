## The Treaty of Nanking

Nanjing (南京) occupies a pivotal role in Hong Kong history as the location where the **Treaty of Nanking** (南京條約) was signed on **August 29, 1842**. This agreement, concluded aboard the British warship HMS Cornwallis, ended the First Opium War between the British Empire and the Qing Dynasty (清朝).

## Differing Historical Interpretations

Interpretations of the significance of the treaty signed in Nanjing vary widely across historiographical traditions:

* **British Colonial Perspective:** Traditionally, British scholarship framed the treaty as a necessary instrument to establish formal diplomatic relations and commercial equity, following the perceived obstructionism of Qing officials in Canton (廣州).
* **Qing/Beijing Perspective:** Official mainland historiography often categorizes the Treaty of Nanking as the first of the "Unequal Treaties" (不平等條約), marking the beginning of the "Century of Humiliation." From this viewpoint, the cession of Hong Kong was an act of imperial coercion imposed through gunboat diplomacy.
* **Local Hong Kong Perspective:** Scholarly and community discourse in Hong Kong often reflects a more nuanced view. While the loss of sovereignty is acknowledged, local historians sometimes highlight the treaty as the catalyst for the unique socio-economic development that transformed a sparsely populated coastal area into a global entrepôt. The perspectives of New Territories communities, though more tied to later treaties, are often framed by the impact of these initial colonial structures on land rights and local customs.

## Connection to Hong Kong

Under the terms agreed upon in Nanjing, the Qing government ceded the island of Hong Kong (香港島) to Queen Victoria "in perpetuity." This established the legal foundation for British administration in the region. While the site of the signing was geographically distant from Hong Kong, the diplomatic outcome directly mandated the territorial transition that defined Hong Kong's status for the next 155 years until the 1997 transfer of sovereignty.
