## Historical Significance
**Nantou** (南頭), historically known as the seat of **Xin'an County** (新安縣), played a central role in the administration of the territory that now comprises Hong Kong. Located on the **Nantou Peninsula**, it served as the regional government hub from the establishment of **Xin'an County** in **1573** until the early 20th century.

## Administrative Role
Following the Ming Dynasty's decision to carve out **Xin'an County** from the existing **Dongguan County** (東莞縣), **Nantou** was designated as the county seat. For centuries, the magistrate based in **Nantou** exercised jurisdiction over the local population of the New Territories and the Hong Kong islands. The walled city at **Nantou** functioned as both a civil administrative center and a defensive garrison, tasked with maritime security and anti-piracy operations.

## Differing Historical Interpretations
Historians and scholars present varied perspectives on the relationship between **Nantou** and Hong Kong. Under the **Qing Dynasty** (清朝) and later perspectives from **Beijing**, **Nantou** is viewed as the legitimate sovereign authority over the region. Conversely, **British colonial** accounts often minimize this administrative connection, framing the New Territories as largely autonomous or loosely governed prior to the **1898** Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory. 

Local **New Territories** communities, particularly the indigenous inhabitants or *Punti* (本地), often recall the influence of **Nantou** through the lens of lineage and land rights. Some scholars argue that the distance between **Nantou** and the far reaches of the Hong Kong islands meant that the magistrate's influence was often indirect, relying on local gentry and village councils to maintain order. Other research emphasizes the integration of these areas into the broader imperial tax and examination systems managed directly from the **Nantou** office. As imperial power waned in the late 19th century, the authority of the **Nantou** magistrate over the colonial-controlled areas became largely symbolic, eventually ceasing with the **1898** lease.

## Timeline

- **1573**: Establishment of Xin'an County with its headquarters located in Nantou.
- **1842**: The Treaty of Nanking transfers Hong Kong Island to Britain, creating a divide between local administration and the Nantou seat.
- **1898**: The Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory leads to the British lease of the New Territories, effectively ending Nantou's administrative reach over the area.
