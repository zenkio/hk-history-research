## Historical Context
The **Nanyue Kingdom** (南越國) was established in **204 BC** by **Zhao Tuo** (趙佗), a former Qin dynasty general, following the collapse of the Qin. Centered in modern-day **Guangzhou** (廣州), then known as **Panyu** (番禺), the kingdom integrated various indigenous tribes and Han Chinese settlers. Its significance to Hong Kong lies in the transition of the region from a collection of maritime chiefdoms into the formal administrative structure of the Chinese imperial system.

## Administrative Integration
Archaeological evidence suggests that during the **Nanyue** period, the territory of Hong Kong fell under the jurisdiction of **Panyu County** (番禺縣). Artefacts unearthed at sites such as **Lei Cheng Uk** (李鄭屋) and **Tai Wan** (大灣) on **Lamma Island** (南丫島) indicate that the region was a node in a wider maritime trade network. While historians widely accept that the kingdom exerted influence over the Pearl River Delta, the extent of direct daily administration in the rugged coastal areas of Hong Kong remains a subject of ongoing debate.

## Differing Perspectives
Interpretations of the Nanyue Kingdom's legacy vary significantly across political and historical schools. Beijing-centric scholarship often emphasizes the kingdom as a foundational moment of territorial integration, viewing it as the earliest instance of Chinese sovereignty over the region. In contrast, local Hong Kong and New Territories historical narratives often focus on the indigenous **Yue** (越) populations, framing the period as a complex process of cultural synthesis rather than simple assimilation. British colonial-era historians frequently downplayed the kingdom’s administrative reach, preferring to characterize the region’s early history through its later maritime development. Contemporary scholars generally favor a balanced view, acknowledging that while the **Nanyue** court provided the initial administrative framework, the local inhabitants maintained a distinct cultural identity rooted in maritime trade long after the kingdom fell to the **Han** (漢) dynasty in **111 BC**.

- **111 BC**: The Han dynasty conquers Nanyue, incorporating Hong Kong into the imperial administrative system.
