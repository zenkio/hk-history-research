## Background and Legal Status

**New Kowloon** (新九龍) refers to the portion of the **New Territories** (新界) situated immediately north of **Boundary Street** (界限街) down to the Kowloon hills. Under the **Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory** signed on **9 June 1898**, the British Empire leased the New Territories, including these areas, from the **Qing dynasty** (大清) for **99 years**. 

## Administrative Interpretations

Perspectives on the administrative categorization of New Kowloon varied among different groups:

* **British Colonial Administration:** While legally part of the leased New Territories, the colonial government recognized early on that New Kowloon's proximity to the existing urban core of **Kowloon** (九龍) necessitated different management. Consequently, New Kowloon was gradually integrated into the urban land administration rather than remaining under the rural **New Territories Block Crown Lease System** (新界分區批租契約系統) in the same manner as the outer rural districts.
* **Local Communities:** Indigenous inhabitants and later urban migrants experienced a distinct legal and social landscape. Land tenure in New Kowloon transitioned more rapidly to crown leases and urban development, separating its trajectory from the traditional village structures found deeper in the New Territories.
* **Qing and Successor Chinese Perspectives:** From the perspective of Beijing and Qing officials, the entire leased territory remained under Chinese sovereignty subject to the lease terms, though practical administration was ceded to Britain.

## Subsequent Development

As Hong Kong's population surged in the mid-20th century, New Kowloon became a primary zone for dense urban expansion, public housing, and industrial development, effectively erasing much of its rural past and bridging the urban core with the wider New Territories.
