## Geography and Early Settlement

The New Territories (新界) comprise the area north of Kowloon up to the Shenzhen River, along with outlying islands. Archaeological evidence indicates human activity spanning thousands of years, but the region's distinct historical identity was largely shaped by the arrival of the "Five Great Clans" (五大氏族) during the Song and Yuan dynasties. Lineages such as the **Tang (鄧)**, **Hau (侯)**, **Liu (廖)**, **Pang (彭)**, and **Man (文)** established prominent settlements including **Kam Tin (錦田)**, **Ho Sheung Heung (何上鄉)**, **Sheung Shui (上水)**, **Fanling Wai (粉嶺圍)**, and **San Tin (新田)**.

## Fortified Villages and Local Society

To protect against pirates, bandits, and rival groups, these clans engaged in the **Development of Fortified Walled Villages (圍村發展)**, constructing structures like **Kat Hing Wai Walled Village (吉慶圍)** with thick brick walls, moats, and watchtowers. Local historiography often highlights these defensive measures as evidence of fierce self-reliance and community autonomy in a peripheral frontier zone.

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of New Territories history vary significantly across traditions. 

* **British Colonial Historiography:** Often framed the New Territories through the lens of late-19th-century imperialism, portraying the 1898 Lease (Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory) as a necessary administrative expansion for the security and sanitation of the colony, while occasionally romanticizing traditional village life.
* **Qing and Beijing Perspectives:** Historically viewed the region as part of China's southern frontier, where imperial control was maintained through local gentry and tax administration, and later regarded the 1898 lease as an unequal treaty signed during a period of national weakness.
* **Local Indigenous and Academic Perspectives:** Indigenous New Territories communities emphasize their distinct legal and ancestral traditions, particularly land rights protected under the Qing dynasty and later accommodated under British rule. Modern scholarship frequently re-examines the region's socio-economic networks, showing how these rural lineages actively negotiated their status amidst changing colonial and national authorities.

## Timeline

- **1898**: The Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory leased the New Territories to Britain for 99 years.
- **1899**: The British colonial administration took formal possession, sparking the Six-Day War resistance from local clans.
