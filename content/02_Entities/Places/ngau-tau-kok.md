## Introduction
Ngau Tau Kok (**牛頭角**) is located in the eastern part of Kowloon, historically bordered by Kwun Tong (**觀塘**) and Kowloon Bay (**九龍灣**). Originally a rural coastal area with granite quarries and small villages, the district underwent dramatic transformation in the mid-20th century as the British colonial government sought to house an influx of refugees from mainland China.

## Post-War Development
During the **1950s** and **1960s**, Ngau Tau Kok became a major site for resettlement estates, notably the Ngau Tau Kok Estate (**牛頭角邨**), built to accommodate displaced residents from squatter fires and rapid urbanization. While the British colonial administration viewed these housing projects as necessary infrastructure for economic stabilization and social order, local residents frequently faced cramped living conditions, poverty, and inadequate municipal services, shaping a distinct working-class identity in the New Kowloon region.

## The 2003 SARS Outbreak
The district gained international attention during the **2003** Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome (SARS) epidemic. Block E of **Amoy Gardens** (**淘大花園**) became the epicenter of the most severe cluster of infections in Hong Kong, with **321** cases recorded. Epidemiological investigations by the Hong Kong government and the World Health Organization concluded that faulty U-traps in drainage systems, combined with structural ventilation designs and viral transmission via wastewater aerosols, facilitated the rapid spread of the virus within the housing estate. This event prompted widespread criticism of public housing management and urban hygiene standards, leading to major reforms in building maintenance regulations and infection control protocols across the territory.

## Modern Era
In the decades following the SARS outbreak, urban renewal projects have largely replaced the older resettlement blocks with modern high-rise residential and commercial developments, though the area retains its historical significance in Hong Kong's public health and urban history.
