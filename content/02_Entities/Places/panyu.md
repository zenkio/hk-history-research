## Administrative Origins

During the **Qin Dynasty Conquest of Lingnan** (214 BC), Panyu (番禺) was established as a county and administrative center in the newly conquered southern territories. Following the collapse of the Qin, **Zhao Tuo** (趙佗) founded the **Foundation of the Nanyue Kingdom** (204 BC), making Panyu his capital. Historical scholarship generally agrees that the early commandery system laid the groundwork for integrating the Pearl River Delta, including modern Hong Kong, into broader Chinese imperial networks.

## Connection to Hong Kong

Panyu's direct administrative reach extended across the region that encompasses present-day Hong Kong. During the **Han Conquest of Nanyue** in **111 BC**, Han imperial forces defeated the Nanyue kingdom, absorbing Panyu and the surrounding territories into the Han Empire under **Jiaozhi Province** (交趾刺史部). Archaeological finds in Hong Kong, such as the **Construction of Lei Cheng Uk Tomb**, reveal material culture and coin types (such as *wuzhu* coins) that reflect the southward expansion of Han governance and trade originating from administrative centers like Panyu.

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of Panyu's role diverge between imperial traditions and modern regional studies. The traditional **Qing and Beijing historiography** emphasizes the civilizing mission and the inevitable unification of Lingnan under a centralized state. Conversely, **local Hong Kong and Lingnan scholarship**, alongside modern archaeological analysis, often highlights the region's indigenous Yue identity, maritime trade autonomy, and a prolonged process of cultural synthesis rather than rapid assimilation.
