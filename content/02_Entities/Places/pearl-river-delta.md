## Geographic and Cultural Foundation
The **Pearl River Delta** (珠江三角洲) forms the broader environmental context for the history of Hong Kong and the New Territories. Archaeological evidence demonstrates that the delta was central to the **Integration into the Baiyue Cultural Sphere** (百越文化圈), where indigenous coastal communities developed distinct regional traditions.

## Maritime Adaptations and Early Industries
As settlement patterns evolved, the region saw the **Rise of Specialized Coastal Maritime Culture** (專業沿海海洋文化) and the **Transition to Sedentary Coastal Villages** (定居沿海村落). Local communities engaged in specialized crafts, evidenced by the **Flourishing Quartz Ring and Ornament Workshops** (繁榮的石英環及裝飾品作坊) and the **Development of Kiln-Fired Hard Pottery** (窯燒硬陶的發展). 

## Social Evolution and External Contact
Over time, the delta experienced the **Introduction of Iron Tools** (鐵器的引入), which transformed local agriculture and production, contributing to the **Rise of Hierarchical Tribal Societies** (階級化部落社會的崛起). As inter-regional networks grew, the **Expansion of Pearl River Delta Trade** (珠江三角洲貿易的擴展) linked the area more closely to broader economic systems in the Central Plains.

## Historiographical Perspectives
Interpretations of the delta's early history vary. Traditional **Qing dynasty and Beijing-centric scholarship** often framed the region as a southern frontier that was gradually civilised through imperial integration. In contrast, **local Hong Kong and New Territories indigenous perspectives** emphasize the agency and long-standing autonomy of indigenous inhabitants. Meanwhile, **British colonial and modern international scholarship** frequently analyze the delta through the lens of maritime trade networks, environmental adaptation, and comparative regional development.
