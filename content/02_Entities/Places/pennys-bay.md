## Geographic and Historical Context
Penny's Bay (竹篙灣), located on the northeast coast of **Lantau Island**, was historically a quiet bay known for small-scale agriculture, fishing, and limestone processing. Before the late 20th-century development, the area was populated by local communities whose livelihoods were tied to the maritime landscape. Scholars note that the area reflects the broader transition of Hong Kong from rural hinterlands to high-density industrial and service hubs.

## Development and Disneyland
In the late **1990s**, the Hong Kong government identified Penny's Bay as the site for **Hong Kong Disneyland** (香港迪士尼樂園). This required extensive land reclamation, which permanently altered the original coastline. While the government emphasized the project as a vital pillar for tourism recovery following the **1997** handover, some local activist groups and environmentalists raised concerns regarding the impact on marine ecology and the erasure of existing cultural heritage sites. The park officially opened in **2005**.

## Modern Usage and Crisis Response
Following the **2003** SARS outbreak and later during the **COVID-19** pandemic, the character of Penny's Bay shifted again. The site became a central location for the Penny’s Bay Quarantine Centre (竹篙灣檢疫中心). This phase of the bay's history is viewed differently by various stakeholders: government authorities describe it as an essential logistical solution for public health safety, while some members of the public and commentators view the rapid construction and utilization of the facility as a significant moment in Hong Kong’s social history during the pandemic. The facility's transition from a tourist destination to a state-managed medical site remains a subject of ongoing discussion regarding urban planning and the role of government-owned land in times of emergency.
