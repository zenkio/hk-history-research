## Geographic and Historical Context
Located in the Tai Po (大埔) District, **Plover Cove** (船灣) was historically a rural area characterized by small Hakka villages and coastal farming communities. Before the mid-20th century, the region maintained a traditional agricultural and fishing economy. The area is notable for its proximity to the Plover Cove Country Park (船灣郊野公園), which was established later to protect the surrounding ecological diversity.

## The Reservoir Project
In the 1960s, the Hong Kong colonial government initiated the construction of the **Plover Cove Reservoir** (船灣淡水湖) to address the territory's acute water shortages. Completed in **1968**, the project involved building a massive dam across the mouth of the bay, effectively isolating the sea water and pumping it out to create a freshwater reservoir. This engineering feat was internationally recognized as the first "marine reservoir" of its kind.

## Diverse Perspectives
Interpretations of this development vary significantly. From the perspective of the **British colonial government** and urban planners, the reservoir was an essential infrastructural triumph that secured water stability for a rapidly growing population. Conversely, many **local New Territories communities** and indigenous residents experienced the project as a major disruption to their ancestral livelihoods. The flooding of traditional farmland and the relocation of village families prompted significant displacement. While some later scholars emphasize the humanitarian costs and the loss of cultural heritage, others argue that the project provided the necessary water security that fueled Hong Kong’s industrial and economic expansion during the late 20th century. The transition from a local farming landscape to a primary water supply facility reflects the broader shift in Hong Kong’s identity from a collection of rural enclaves to a modern metropolis.
