## Geography and Settlement
**Po Toi Island** (蒲台島) is the main island of the **Po Toi Islands** group, situated off the southeastern tip of **Hong Kong Island**. The island is primarily composed of coarse-grained granite, which has been sculpted by erosion into distinctive formations such as the 'Tortoise Ascent to the Mountain' and 'Palm Cliff'. Historically, the island served as a base for fishing communities, although the population has significantly declined since the mid-20th century due to modernization and shifting economic patterns in Hong Kong.

## The Rock Carvings
The island is home to significant ancient rock carvings, which are protected as a declared monument. Located on the southern tip of the island, these carvings consist of two groups of lines and spirals. While their exact origin remains a subject of academic debate, they are generally attributed to the **Bronze Age** peoples of the region. 

Interpretations regarding their purpose vary. Some scholars suggest they served as navigational markers or ritualistic sites for coastal communities seeking safety at sea. Local oral traditions in the **New Territories** sometimes associate such carvings with mystical origins or protective spirits, whereas colonial-era archaeological reports focused on stylistic comparisons with other prehistoric sites in the Pearl River Delta. Because there is no written record from the era of their creation, the specific identity of the creators remains unconfirmed by modern historiography.

## Modern Status
Today, **Po Toi** is a popular destination for hikers and those interested in Hong Kong's maritime heritage. The village area retains remnants of traditional architecture, including the **Tin Hau Temple** (天后廟), which continues to be a focal point for local religious life and festivals. While the island is geographically isolated from the urban core of **Hong Kong**, it remains a vital site for understanding the prehistoric and traditional maritime history of the territory.
