## Geographic and Historical Significance
**Pok Fu Lam** (薄扶林) is located in the Southern District of Hong Kong Island. Historically, the area was characterized by a rugged coastline and small, scattered villages engaged in agriculture and fishing. Following the establishment of British colonial rule in the mid-19th century, the area became a focal point for institutional development.

## The Pok Fu Lam Reservoir
A defining moment in the area's history was the **Completion of the Pok Fu Lam Reservoir** (薄扶林水塘) in **1863**. It was the first reservoir built in Hong Kong, commissioned to address the chronic water shortages faced by the growing population in Victoria City. While colonial records emphasize the engineering achievement and the necessity of stable water supply for the burgeoning commercial port, some local historiographical perspectives note the displacement of indigenous village land and the disruption of local water sources. These differing interpretations reflect the broader tension between colonial administrative goals and the traditional land use rights of indigenous inhabitants.

## Modern Evolution
By the early 20th century, **Pok Fu Lam** transformed from a rural outpost into a site of institutional expansion. The establishment of **The University of Hong Kong** (香港大學) in **1911** on the northern boundary of the district shifted the area's identity toward education. Concurrently, the colonial administration utilized the area for large-scale projects, including the construction of the **Queen Mary Hospital** (瑪麗醫院) in **1937**. 

Modern scholarship on the region often highlights the juxtaposition of these colonial-era institutions against the enduring heritage of **Pok Fu Lam Village** (薄扶林村), one of the few remaining traditional villages on the island. The village serves as a symbol of cultural continuity, standing in contrast to the high-density residential developments that define much of the surrounding terrain today. The coexistence of the heritage-listed reservoir, the university, and the traditional village remains a point of academic interest regarding Hong Kong’s evolving urban identity.
