## Introduction
Possession Point (**水坑口**), historically also known as Possession Mount, is a small hill and coastal area located in the Sheung Wan district of Hong Kong Island. It holds a foundational place in the historiography of British Hong Kong as the purported site where Royal Navy forces landed to claim the island.

## The British Landing and Formal Possession
On **January 26, 1841**, Commodore **Sir Gordon Bremer**, Captain **Charles Elliot**, and other British officers and troops landed at Possession Point. They hoisted the British flag and performed a formal ceremony to take possession of Hong Kong Island in the name of Queen Victoria. ## Historiographical Interpretations
Interpretations of the events surrounding Possession Point vary significantly depending on the historical perspective:

*   **British Colonial Narrative:** Traditional British accounts emphasize the landing at Possession Point as the legitimate and orderly birth of a Crown Colony, marking the establishment of free trade, rule of law, and modern administration under the British Empire.
*   **Qing and Beijing Perspective:** From the viewpoint of the Qing Imperial court and modern mainland historiography, the landing and subsequent occupation are viewed as an act of imperialist aggression resulting from the unequal treaties forced upon China during the First Opium War.
*   **Local and Modern Scholarship:** Contemporary local historians and community perspectives often look beyond the colonial narrative to examine the indigenous Tanka and Hakka populations already residing in the coastal areas and surrounding villages before the British arrival, questioning the notion of Hong Kong as a barren rock transformed solely by British enterprise.
