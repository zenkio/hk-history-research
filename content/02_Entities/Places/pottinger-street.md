## Origins and Construction

**Pottinger Street** (砵甸乍街), also commonly known as **Stone Slab Street** (石板街) due to its unique uneven granite paving, was laid out in the **1840s** shortly after the British occupation of Hong Kong Island. Situated in Central, the street was designed to connect the upper levels of the colonial administration—centered around the **First Government House** (香港總督府) constructed on __Government Hill__—with the commercial port areas below. 

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of the street's historical significance vary across different scholarly and political viewpoints. 

From a traditional **British colonial** perspective, the development of Pottinger Street and the surrounding administrative precinct represented the establishment of modern urban planning, rule of law, and institutional infrastructure in a previously sparsely populated area. The granite paving stones were uniquely laid in an interlocking pattern to prevent slippage on the steep incline and to facilitate drainage during heavy monsoons, viewed as a practical engineering solution.

Conversely, **later scholarship** and local heritage advocates often contextualize the street's creation within the broader dynamics of British imperialism and displacement. The rapid reshaping of Central's topography served the administrative and residential needs of the colonial elite while bypassing or restructuring existing local patterns of land use. 

From the perspective of **Qing dynasty and mainland historiography**, early colonial infrastructure projects like Pottinger Street are frequently viewed as physical manifestations of the unequal treaties stemming from the First Opium War, marking the forced cession of Hong Kong territory.

## Preservation and Heritage

Today, Pottinger Street is recognized as a Grade 1 historic building. While the street retains its historic character and distinctive stone steps, it remains a subject of ongoing debate regarding heritage preservation versus urban redevelopment in modern Hong Kong.
