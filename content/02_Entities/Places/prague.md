## Historical Origins of the Lennon Wall
The original **Lennon Wall** in **Prague** emerged in the 1980s following the assassination of **John Lennon**. Located on **Velkopřevorské náměstí**, the wall became a site for young Czechs to express grievances against the communist regime of the time, utilizing lyrics, poems, and graffiti. While authorities repeatedly whitewashed the wall, it became a symbol of resistance and pacifism.

## Connection to Hong Kong
During the **2014 Umbrella Movement**, protesters in **Hong Kong** began creating their own versions of the 'Lennon Wall' by posting handwritten sticky notes on public surfaces, most notably near the **Central Government Complex** in **Admiralty**. This practice evolved during the **2019 anti-extradition bill protests**, spreading across various districts, pedestrian subways, and residential neighborhoods. 

## Interpretations of the Phenomenon
The significance of these walls is subject to differing interpretations. Pro-democracy activists and many local Hong Kong residents view the walls as essential spaces for community expression and digital-physical participation, representing a local adaptation of global protest culture. Conversely, the **Hong Kong government** and **Beijing** officials have frequently characterized the widespread use of these spaces as acts of vandalism or public order offenses, often leading to their systematic removal. Later scholarship notes that while the aesthetic and tactical inspiration is drawn from **Prague**, the Hong Kong manifestations evolved into a distinct digital-physical hybridity, where physical notes were often archived digitally, reflecting the unique technological context of the 2019 protests. Whether these walls represented a legitimate expression of civil society or an illegal obstruction remains a point of intense public debate.
