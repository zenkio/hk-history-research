## Origin of Tin Hau Worship

Located in Fujian province, **Putian** (莆田) is historically significant to Hong Kong's cultural landscape as the birthplace of **Lin Moniang** (**林默娘**), who later became venerated as **Tin Hau** (**天后**), the Queen of Heaven and protector of seafarers. According to tradition, she was born on **Meizhou Island** (**湄洲島**), which falls under Putian's administration. 

## Connection to Hong Kong Maritime Networks

As junk trade and migration routes connected the southeastern coast of China with the Pearl River Delta, coastal communities and fishermen carried the cult of Tin Hau to the waters that would become Hong Kong. While local oral traditions and lineage histories in the New Territories attribute the establishment of early Tin Hau temples to local settling clans or salt panners, the overarching religious tradition traces its roots directly back to the ancestral temples of **Meizhou** in Putian.

## Historiographical Interpretations

Scholarly interpretations regarding the spread of this cult differ in emphasis. Local New Territories communities often emphasize the grassroots, protective function of Tin Hau temples for individual fishing hamlets against pirates and typhoons. Conversely, later maritime and religious scholarship often contextualizes the expansion from Putian as part of broader state-sanctioned and commercial coastal networks. During the **Qing dynasty** (**清朝**), imperial recognition of Tin Hau elevated her status, aligning official state religion with popular maritime beliefs originating from Putian, which further solidified her temples as focal points of coastal society in British colonial Hong Kong and beyond.
