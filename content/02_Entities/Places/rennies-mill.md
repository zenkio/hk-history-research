## Origins and Name
The area was originally known as Tiu Keng Leng (調景嶺), literally "ridge where one adjusts the scenery." In the early 20th century, Canadian businessman **Alfred Herbert Rennie** established a flour mill here, which failed financially, leading to the name Rennie's Mill. After the factory's closure, the site remained largely undeveloped until the aftermath of the **1949** revolution in China.

## The Refugee Crisis
Following the **1950** arrival of thousands of Kuomintang (KMT) soldiers and their families seeking refuge from the incoming communist government, the British colonial authorities allocated the area for a refugee camp. The settlement became a bastion of support for the Republic of China (Taiwan). For decades, the site was distinct for its public displays of the KMT flag and celebrations of the **October 10th** Double Tenth Day, which occasionally led to political tensions within the colony.

## Historical Perspectives
Interpretations of the settlement's significance vary. Pro-Taiwanese perspectives and many residents viewed the camp as a heroic enclave preserving traditional Chinese identity and political loyalty. Conversely, Beijing-aligned groups historically viewed the area as a site of colonial-sponsored anti-communist agitation. British colonial records often prioritized the administrative challenge of managing the settlement's infrastructure and political volatility. Later academic scholarship often examines the site through the lens of the **May Exodus Refugee Crisis**, highlighting the humanitarian complexities of displaced populations residing in a contested Cold War space.

## Redevelopment
Beginning in the **1990s**, the colonial government initiated the clearance of Rennie's Mill to facilitate large-scale urban development. By **1996**, the settlement was demolished to make way for the Tiu Keng Leng MTR station and modern public housing estates. The clearance marked the end of a unique era for the area, transitioning it from a politically charged refugee encampment into a standardized residential district integrated into the broader Hong Kong urban landscape.
