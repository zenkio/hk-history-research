## Ecclesiastical Administration

In the history of Hong Kong, Rome (羅馬) is primarily significant as the center of the Holy See and the Vatican, which directed the expansion of the Roman Catholic Church in East Asia. Following the British occupation of Hong Kong Island in **1841**, the Vatican sought to organize pastoral care for the growing Catholic population and facilitate missionary work into mainland China.

## Establishment of the Prefecture Apostolic

On **April 22, 1841**, Pope Gregory XVI erected the Prefecture Apostolic of Hong Kong, separating it from the Diocese of Macau. This decision reflected Rome's growing direct involvement in the region's religious affairs, operating somewhat independently of Portuguese patronage. The move placed the new territory under the jurisdiction of the Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith (Sacra Congregatio de Propaganda Fide) in Rome.

## Historiographical Perspectives

While British colonial authorities viewed religious institutions largely through the pragmatic lens of social welfare, education, and moral order among European soldiers and local converts, Church historians in Rome and Hong Kong interpreted these developments within the framework of global spiritual expansion. Conversely, Qing (大清) dynasty officials and later mainland historiography often viewed foreign missionary activities emanating from Rome and Western Europe through the complex lens of imperialism and extraterritorial encroachment, though the local Catholic community in Hong Kong integrated these ties into a distinct identity under successive apostolic prefects and vicars.
