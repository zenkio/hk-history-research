## Geography and Early Settlement

Sai Kung District (西貢區) occupies the southeastern part of the New Territories (新界), encompassing the Sai Kung Peninsula and numerous islands in Port Shelter. Historically, the area was populated by scattered fishing and agricultural communities, including Hakka and Cantonese villagers. Under British colonial administration following the **1898** Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory, the region was governed as part of the New Territories.

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of Sai Kung's development vary across perspectives. British colonial histories often emphasized the introduction of infrastructure, policing, and rural administration to a previously isolated area. Conversely, local New Territories communities and oral histories highlight long-standing self-governance, maritime traditions, and the resilience of indigenous lineages. Qing dynasty and mainland Chinese historical frameworks frequently view the coastal geography through the lens of maritime defense and anti-piracy operations, particularly during the Ming and Qing periods.

## Modern Transformation and Higher Education

In the latter half of the twentieth century, Sai Kung underwent significant physical and demographic changes. The establishment of the **Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (香港科技大學)** in Clear Water Bay (清水灣) in **1991** marked a major turning point, transforming part of the district into a prominent academic center. While parts of the district were urbanized—most notably through the development of the **Tseung Kwan O New Town (將軍澳新市鎮)**—other areas were preserved within country parks, balancing urban growth with ecological conservation.
