## Historical Context

**Sai Wan** (西灣), located on the eastern coast of the **Sai Kung Peninsula**, is historically significant as a settlement area for local Hakka and other rural communities. While much of Hong Kong’s history is centered on Victoria Harbour, the eastern coastal bays have long served as essential nodes for traditional fishing, agriculture, and coastal trade. The area is part of the broader **Sai Kung** (西貢) district, which has been inhabited for centuries, though specific records of early settlement patterns remain fragmented.

## Archaeological and Migration Perspectives

## Colonial and Modern Development

Following the British lease of the **New Territories** in **1898**, the region was integrated into the colonial administrative framework. While the British colonial government focused heavily on the urbanization of the Kowloon Peninsula and Hong Kong Island, the rural reaches of the eastern peninsula were largely governed through traditional village structures. In the later 20th century, the designation of the area within the **Sai Kung East Country Park** fundamentally shifted the trajectory of the locale, prioritizing conservation and public recreation over further agricultural or industrial expansion. Today, the area is recognized for its historical rural character and its importance in understanding the environmental history of the region.
