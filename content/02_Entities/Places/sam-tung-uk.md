## Historical Background
Sam Tung Uk (三棟屋), meaning 'Three Beam House', was founded in **1786** by the **Chan** (陳) clan, a Hakka family who migrated from Guangdong to the Tsuen Wan area. The village layout follows a traditional symmetrical design, featuring a central axis with an ancestral hall, flanked by two side rows, enclosed by a defensive perimeter wall. This architectural style was typical of late 18th-century settlements in the region, serving both residential and protective functions.

## Historical Interpretations
The history of Sam Tung Uk is viewed through multiple lenses. From the perspective of local New Territories lineages, the village represents a self-sufficient agrarian community governed by clan customs and ancestral worship. Conversely, colonial-era historical records often emphasize the village as part of the broader administrative transformation following the **1898** lease of the New Territories. Scholars note that while the village functioned as a stable social unit for over two centuries, its later integration into the Tsuen Wan New Town development project in the **1970s** highlights the tension between preserving rural heritage and the requirements of modern urban expansion.

## Preservation and Heritage
In **1981**, the village was declared a gazetted monument under the Antiquities and Monuments Ordinance. The site was subsequently renovated and opened as the Sam Tung Uk Museum in **1987**. While some historians argue that the restoration process may have idealized the village’s original state, the site remains a vital record of the Hakka diaspora's adaptation to the terrain of the New Territories. It serves as a tangible connection to Hong Kong's pre-industrial past, documenting the daily lives, agricultural practices, and lineage hierarchies that defined the region before the transformation into a global financial center.
