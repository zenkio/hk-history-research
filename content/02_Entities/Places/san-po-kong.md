## Development and Industrial Origins
San Po Kong (新蒲崗) is located in the Wong Tai Sin District of Kowloon. Historically, the area was part of the reclaimed land developed in the late **1950s** and early **1960s** to accommodate the rapid expansion of Hong Kong’s manufacturing sector. Characterized by its high-density factory buildings, the area became a vital hub for the textile, plastic, and electronics industries during the territory's economic transition.

## The 1967 Leftist Riots
San Po Kong is most notably remembered as the starting point of the **1967 Leftist Riots**. On **May 6, 1967**, a labor dispute at a plastic flower factory escalated into a physical confrontation between workers and the police. This incident served as the catalyst for widespread civil unrest that lasted for several months.

## Historical Interpretations
Perspectives on the riots and the role of San Po Kong remain diverse. From the British colonial government’s standpoint, the events were viewed as an orchestrated attempt by local pro-Beijing elements, inspired by the Cultural Revolution in mainland China, to destabilize social order. Conversely, contemporary accounts from pro-Beijing groups and participants in the protests often framed the events as an anti-colonial struggle against poor labor conditions and what they perceived as British oppression. Later historical scholarship often emphasizes the interplay between domestic labor grievances and the geopolitical influence of the Cultural Revolution, noting that while labor disputes were a genuine domestic issue, the subsequent protests were significantly shaped by shifting political loyalties and external propaganda. The area remains a focal point for researchers examining the intersection of industrial labor history and the political identity of mid-century Hong Kong.
