## Historical Background
San Tin (新田) is located in the Yuen Long District of the New Territories. It is historically significant as the primary settlement area of the **Man** (文) clan, who moved to the region during the **Yuan Dynasty** (1271–1368). The clan claims descent from **Man Tin-cheung** (文天祥), a famous scholar-official of the **Southern Song Dynasty**. While genealogy records preserved by the clan support this lineage, historians note that such ancestral associations were common among New Territories lineages to enhance social standing and legitimacy.

## Land and Administration
Prior to the **1898** Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory, San Tin functioned under the administrative framework of the **Qing Dynasty** (1644–1912), with local governance managed by village elders and clan leaders. Following the British takeover in **1899**, the area was incorporated into the New Territories. British colonial records from the early 20th century often characterized the San Tin community as part of the 'indigenous' population (the **Punti**), distinct from the later arriving **Hakka** or **Tanka** groups. However, contemporary scholarship emphasizes that interactions between these groups were more complex and porous than colonial-era classifications suggest.

## Cultural Heritage
San Tin is defined by its traditional architecture, including the **Lau Man Shek Tong** (麟峯文公祠) and various walled villages. These structures served both defensive purposes against pirates and local rivals and as centers for ancestral worship. Scholars debate the extent to which these walled villages were built in response to regional insecurity during the **Qing** period or as a reflection of clan prosperity. Today, San Tin remains a focal point for understanding the social structure of the New Territories and the preservation of **Lingnan** cultural heritage amidst the rapid urbanization of the **Northern Metropolis** project.
