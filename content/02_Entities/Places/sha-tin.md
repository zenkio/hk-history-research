## Geography and Early History

Located along the Shing Mun River (城門河) basin, Sha Tin (沙田)—historically known as Lek Yuen (瀝源)—comprised numerous traditional villages inhabited by local clans. The area's economy originally centered on agriculture and incense production, with the **Expansion of the Guanxiang Incense Trade** linking local production routes to wider regional markets during the Qing period.

## Mid-Century Challenges

Like much of the territory, the valley was vulnerable to extreme weather. During the catastrophic **Devastation by Typhoon Wanda** in **1962**, coastal storm surges caused severe flooding and loss of life across the low-lying agricultural areas, highlighting the precarious living conditions in the rural New Territories.

## Urban Transformation and Housing

Sha Tin's landscape changed dramatically under the **Ten-Year Housing Programme Announced** in the **1970s**, which designated the valley as one of the government's primary sites for new town development. Land reclamation in Tolo Harbour and extensive hillside grading created flat land for high-density public housing estates and private developments, absorbing hundreds of thousands of residents.

## Royal Visits and International Events

As the new town grew, it became a prominent backdrop for major civic milestones. **Queen Elizabeth II's Second Royal Visit** in **1975** included stops to inspect the early phases of the ambitious new town project. Decades later, the district gained international attention when the area hosted elite equestrian competitions during the **Hong Kong Hosts Olympic Equestrian Events** and **Hong Kong Hosts Beijing Olympic Equestrian Events** in **2008**, centered around the Sha Tin Racecourse.
