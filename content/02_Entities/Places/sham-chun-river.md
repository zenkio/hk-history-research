## Geographic and Strategic Role

The **Sham Chun River** (深圳河) forms a natural dividing line between the northern New Territories of Hong Kong and Shenzhen in Guangdong Province. Originally a meandering waterway prone to flooding, its course and geopolitical significance shifted dramatically with the expansion of the British colonial sphere of influence.

## Colonial Boundary and Treaties

Following the **Signing of the Second Convention of Peking** on **June 9, 1898**, and the subsequent **Signing of the Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory** on **June 9, 1898**, Great Britain leased the New Territories for **99 years**. While initial British military surveys contemplated a broader frontier, the Sham Chun River was ultimately adopted as the demarcated boundary for the newly acquired jurisdiction.

From the perspective of Qing dynasty officials and later Chinese historiography, these conventions represented unequal treaties imposed under foreign pressure. Conversely, British colonial authorities viewed the river as a pragmatic administrative and military frontier to secure the defense of the deep-water port of Victoria Harbour.

## Wartime and Refugee Crises

During the Pacific War, the river formed part of the contested frontier leading up to the **Japanese Invasion of Hong Kong Begins** on **December 8, 1941**. Imperial Japanese forces crossed the border region to engage British, Indian, and Canadian defenders.

In the post-war era, particularly following the establishment of the People's Republic of China in **1949**, the river became a heavily monitored border. During periods of political upheaval in mainland China, such as **The May Exodus Refugee Crisis** in **1962**, thousands of migrants swam across or waded through the Sham Chun River to enter British jurisdiction.

## Modern Scholarship and Regulation

Later historical scholarship emphasizes how the river transformed from a fluid, interconnected agrarian borderland shared by local villagers into a hard international barrier. In recent decades, joint Hong Kong and Shenzhen river-training projects have significantly straightened and deepened the channel to mitigate seasonal flooding.

## Timeline

- **1898**: Designated as the boundary between British Hong Kong and imperial China under the Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory.
- **1941**: Crossed by invading Japanese forces during the initial assault on Hong Kong.
- **1962**: Served as a primary crossing point for refugees during the May Exodus crisis.
- **1997**: Transitioned from a British-Chinese border to the boundary between the Hong Kong SAR and mainland China.
