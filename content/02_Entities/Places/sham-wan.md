## Introduction

Sham Wan (**深灣**), located on the southeast coast of Lamma Island (**南丫島**), is widely recognized by archaeologists as a pivotal site for understanding the prehistoric settlement of the Pearl River Delta. Archaeological excavations have revealed multiple distinct cultural strata, making it a cornerstone for the chronology of early human activity in the region.

## Neolithic Pioneers and Material Culture

Evidence from Sham Wan indicates that the site was utilized by **Neolithic pioneers** as early as the **4th millennium BCE**. Excavations have uncovered significant layers associated with the **Emergence of Fine Geometric Pottery**, characterized by meticulously impressed patterns that demonstrate advanced ceramic technologies among coastal communities.

Furthermore, the site is renowned for the **Flourishing Quartz Ring and Ornament Workshops**. Archaeologists recovered numerous unfinished quartz rings, hammerstones, and debris, indicating that Sham Wan served as a specialized manufacturing center for ritual or decorative items traded throughout the broader regional network.

## Historiographical Interpretations

Historical and archaeological interpretations of Sham Wan generally divide into two main frameworks:

*   **Colonial-Era and Modern Archaeological Scholarship:** British-era and contemporary archaeologists, beginning systematically in the **1970s**, focused primarily on typo-chronology, stratigraphy, and environmental adaptation. They emphasize Sham Wan's role in illustrating indigenous maritime adaptation, long-distance exchange, and craft specialization within the coastal Lingnan sphere.
*   **Local and Regional Perspectives:** Local New Territories communities and mainland Chinese historians often integrate these findings into the broader narrative of Chinese civilization's southern expansion, viewing the sophisticated material culture of Sham Wan as an indigenous development deeply rooted in the Lingnan ancient peoples (**嶺南古越族**).

Because written records from the Neolithic period do not exist, precise social structures and ethnic affiliations remain matters of ongoing scholarly debate.
