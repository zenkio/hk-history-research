## Geography and Early Settlement

The **Shan Pui River** (山貝河) is located in the northwest of Hong Kong's New Territories, flowing through the Yuen Long plain before emptying into Deep Bay (深圳灣). Historically, the river and its network of tributaries provided essential irrigation for agricultural production and served as a primary transportation route for the indigenous communities of the region.

## Connection to Yuen Long Kau Hui

The development of the **Shan Pui River** is closely linked to the founding of the **Yuen Long Kau Hui** (元朗舊墟) market. Established primarily by the **Tang clan** (鄧氏) of Kam Tin (錦田) during the Qing dynasty, the market's location near the river was strategically chosen to facilitate waterborne trade. Boats carrying agricultural produce, fish, and imported goods could navigate the waterway directly to the market area, allowing Yuen Long to grow into a major commercial hub for the northwestern New Territories.

## Historiography and Perspectives

Interpretations of the river's economic role vary across different historical traditions. Local New Territories lineage narratives emphasize the agency and foresight of indigenous clans in establishing river-adjacent markets to secure regional economic dominance. In contrast, British colonial administrative records, compiled after the **1898** Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory, tended to view these waterways through the lens of rural infrastructure and public health management. Later scholarship often highlights how the river integrated local agrarian networks into broader regional trade patterns spanning the Pearl River Delta, while also noting the ecological transformations the waterway underwent due to subsequent urbanization.
