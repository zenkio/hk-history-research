## Origins and Early Settlement
Historically known as **Lek Yuen** (瀝源), the area comprising Shatin (沙田) was originally a collection of scattered Hakka and Punti farming and fishing villages. Under the **Qing dynasty** (清朝), the valley supported agriculture and local clan networks. Following the British lease of the New Territories in **1898**, the area was gradually integrated into the colonial administration while largely retaining its rural character for the first half of the twentieth century.

## New Town Development
In the **1970s**, the colonial government designated Shatin as one of the first generation of new towns to alleviate severe urban congestion in Kowloon and Hong Kong Island. Massive land reclamation in **Tolo Harbour** (吐露港) and extensive flattening of hills reshaped the geography. Local indigenous communities in the New Territories often experienced these sweeping transformations with mixed reactions, balancing the disruption of traditional village life against the economic opportunities brought by modernization. Later scholarship frequently analyzes this era through the lens of state-led spatial engineering and colonial housing policy.

## The Home Ownership Scheme and Expansion
By the **1980s**, Shatin emerged as a densely populated residential district featuring extensive public housing estates and private developments. The implementation of the **Home Ownership Scheme** (居者有其屋計劃), particularly during its notable expansion phases in the **late 1980s**, played a crucial role in shaping the socioeconomic fabric of Shatin. Phase VII and subsequent developments provided affordable housing that enabled hundreds of families to achieve homeownership, cementing the district's identity as a bustling suburban community within the wider Hong Kong metropolis.
