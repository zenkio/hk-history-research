## Historical Significance
Shek Pai Wan (石排灣), located on the southern coast of Hong Kong Island, holds historical significance as a primary transshipment hub for the incense trade during the **Ming dynasty**. The area is closely linked to the trade of 'incense trees' (*Aquilaria sinensis*), which were harvested in the surrounding hills and transported to the pier at Shek Pai Wan before being shipped to mainland markets, including Guangzhou. 

## Interpretations of the Trade
The role of Shek Pai Wan in the broader history of the region is subject to differing scholarly perspectives. Some historians emphasize the importance of local indigenous communities in the cultivation and management of the incense trade, framing Shek Pai Wan as a vital node in an autonomous maritime network. Conversely, administrative accounts from the Qing (清) period focus on the formalization of trade routes and the integration of these coastal activities into the broader imperial tax and regulatory systems. Later scholarship often highlights the complex transition from this early localized economy to the British colonial era, during which the geographical focus of Hong Kong’s maritime development shifted significantly toward the deep-water harbor on the north side of the island.

## Geographic Evolution
While the name Shek Pai Wan is historically associated with the bay itself, the area’s identity has evolved significantly due to land reclamation and urban development. By the mid-**20th century**, the coastal profile was fundamentally altered. Some accounts from the local Tanka (蜑家) community describe the transition of the bay from a vibrant boat-dwelling settlement to its current status as part of the wider Aberdeen (香港仔) urban district. While the exact scale of the incense trade passing through the bay during the **15th and 16th centuries** remains a subject of ongoing archaeological inquiry, the site remains an essential reference point for understanding Hong Kong’s pre-colonial maritime heritage.
