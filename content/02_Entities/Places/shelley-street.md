## Introduction and Naming

**Shelley Street** (些利街) is located in the Mid-Levels and Central districts of Hong Kong Island. Like many colonial-era streets, its exact naming origins remain a subject of minor ambiguity in early administrative records, though it was named during the early decades of British colonial governance to support urban expansion up the steep slopes of Victoria City.

## Connection to the Jamia Mosque

Shelley Street is most notable in Hong Kong history for housing the **Jamia Mosque** (回教廟), also known as the Shelley Street Mosque, which is the oldest mosque in the territory. 

- **Colonial and Local Perspectives:** British colonial records and local Islamic community histories document that the land for the mosque was acquired in the **1890s** to serve the growing South Asian, Eurasian, and Middle Eastern Muslim communities, many of whom served in the British garrison, police force, or as merchants.
- **Architectural and Urban Context:** The steep topography of Shelley Street and surrounding areas shaped the urban integration of the Muslim community, placing a place of worship within walking distance of the central business district.

## Historiography

While traditional British administrative histories frame the development of Shelley Street primarily through the lens of orderly colonial land sales and infrastructure expansion, contemporary local scholarship often emphasizes the agency of Hong Kong's diverse ethnic minority communities in carving out permanent religious and social spaces within the colonial grid.
