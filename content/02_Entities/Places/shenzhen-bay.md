## Geographic and Historical Context
Shenzhen Bay (深圳灣), historically known as Deep Bay (后海灣), is a body of water separating the **New Territories** of Hong Kong from **Nanshan** district in **Shenzhen**. For the indigenous inhabitants of the New Territories, the bay has traditionally been a site for oyster farming and fishing. Following the 1898 Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory, the bay was partitioned by the border between the British colonial administration and the Qing dynasty.

## British and Mainland Perspectives
Interpretations of the bay's function have shifted over time. During the British colonial era, the bay was largely viewed as a peripheral buffer zone and a geographic barrier. Conversely, post-1997 perspectives from Beijing and the HKSAR government have emphasized the bay’s role as an integration corridor. The construction of the **Shenzhen Bay Bridge** (深圳灣大橋), which opened in **2007**, physically linked the two territories, representing a departure from the bay’s historical status as a maritime boundary.

## Contemporary Significance
The bay is also a significant ecological site, hosting the **Mai Po Nature Reserve** (米埔自然護理區) on the Hong Kong side. The area is a vital wetland for migratory birds, leading to ongoing debates regarding the balance between large-scale infrastructure development and environmental preservation. Scholarship remains divided on whether the bay should primarily serve as an economic integration engine or as a protected conservation area. The **Shenzhen Bay Port** (深圳灣口岸) has become a primary checkpoint, notably serving as a focal point during the phased **Reopening of the Border** following the COVID-19 pandemic, reflecting the bay's transition from a border marker to a nexus of daily cross-boundary activity.
