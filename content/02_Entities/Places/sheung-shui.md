## Early Settlement and the Liu Clan

According to local lineage histories, the **Liu Clan** (廖氏) migrated from northern China and settled in Sheung Shui (上水) during the late Yuan or early Ming dynasty, establishing powerful walled villages such as **Wai Loi Tsuen** (圍內村). Local New Territories historiography emphasizes the ability of these indigenous lineages to build self-sustaining agricultural and defensive networks. From the perspective of Qing (清朝) imperial administration, these communities were managed through indirect rule and local gentry structures, maintaining considerable autonomy until the late 19th century.

## British Colonial Era and Urbanization

Following the Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory in **1898**, Sheung Shui came under British colonial administration. British colonial records often framed the New Territories through the lens of land revenue surveys and public health modernization. While early colonial governance left traditional rural structures largely intact, the mid-20th century brought rapid development. Sheung Shui became a key node in the **Fanling/Sheung Shui New Town** development project initiated in the 1970s, transforming agrarian market plains into high-density urban residential zones.

## Olympic Equestrian Events

In **2008**, Sheung Shui gained international prominence when the **Hong Kong Sports Institute** in neighboring Fo Tan and temporary venues in **Sheung Shui** were selected to co-host the equestrian events of the Beijing Olympic Games. Official accounts from Beijing and the Hong Kong government highlighted this as a symbol of national pride and administrative efficiency. Meanwhile, later sociological and local scholarship noted the sharp contrast between the hyper-modern global event and the ongoing rural-urban tensions concerning land use and heritage preservation in the district.
