## Geography and Early Settlement

Shing Mun (城門) is located in the central New Territories, situated in the valley south of Tai Mo Shan (大帽山). Historically, the area comprised several Hakka village communities engaging in agriculture and forestry. Local lineage ties and land use rights shaped the social fabric of the valley before large-scale colonial intervention.

## The 1929 Water Crisis

During the severe Hong Kong water crisis of **1929**, the Shing Mun area gained critical strategic importance for the colony's survival. As prolonged drought severely depleted existing reservoirs like Tai Tam (大潭), the colonial administration accelerated plans to harness the water resources of the Shing Mun valley. 

## Infrastructure Development and Interpretations

British colonial authorities viewed the development of the Shing Mun catchment and subsequent reservoir projects as essential modernization to support a growing urban population. Conversely, local New Territories communities often experienced these large-scale engineering works as disruptive intrusions that displaced ancestral lands, altered traditional waterways, and dismantled rural economies. Later historical scholarship frequently examines these developments through the lens of colonial resource extraction versus local agrarian displacement.

During the **1930s**, these initial works laid the groundwork for the larger Shing Mun (Jubilee) Reservoir (城門水塘 / 銀禧水塘), completed in **1936**, which permanently transformed the valley's landscape and submerged several original villages.
