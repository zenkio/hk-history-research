## Geography and Border Status
The **Shum Chun River** (深圳河) forms the southeastern boundary between the **New Territories** of Hong Kong and the **Shenzhen** Special Economic Zone. Historically, the river served as a natural demarcation line; following the **1898** Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory, the **Qing** government and the **British** colonial administration formalised the river as the official frontier. While the British viewed the river primarily as a strategic buffer, local villagers in the New Territories often maintained cross-border familial and agricultural ties that predated colonial mapping.

## Water Resource and Droughts
The river's significance expanded beyond its role as a border during the mid-**20th century**. As Hong Kong’s population surged, the river became a vital focal point for water supply infrastructure. During the **1963** severe drought, the river became a symbol of both regional vulnerability and cooperation. British colonial authorities and local municipal officials in **Guangdong** negotiated arrangements to divert water from the **Dongjiang** (East River) system, an initiative that heavily relied on the river's existing hydraulic corridors. 

## Conflicting Interpretations
Perspectives on the river's management vary. British colonial records from the mid-century often frame the river as a frontier to be policed against illegal immigration and smuggling. Conversely, scholarship from mainland China often emphasizes the river as a shared artery of economic integration and historic kinship. Local New Territories communities have historically occupied a middle ground, frequently navigating the rigid border controls that treated the river as a hard barrier between their ancestral lands and mainland counterparts. In recent decades, extensive river training and environmental engineering projects have significantly altered its natural flow, with debates persisting between ecologists, urban planners in Shenzhen, and Hong Kong authorities regarding the balance between flood prevention and environmental conservation.
