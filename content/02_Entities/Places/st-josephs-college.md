## Historical Foundation
**St. Joseph's College** (聖若瑟書院) was established in **1875** by the Brothers of the Christian Schools, a Catholic religious teaching congregation. Originally founded as St. Saviour's College, it was renamed in **1881**. The school has occupied several notable locations, most prominently its long-standing campus on **Kennedy Road**, which was completed in **1920**. The site, which includes buildings categorized as historic monuments, reflects the architectural influence of the colonial period in Hong Kong.

## Education and Influence
Throughout its history, the college has been recognized as a prominent English-medium secondary school catering to the diverse ethnic and social strata of Hong Kong's population. It has produced many influential alumni in fields such as government, law, and medicine. Perspectives on the school's role often vary; while some historians emphasize its contribution to the modernization of education under British colonial governance, others highlight its role in fostering an elite local identity that bridged traditional cultural values with Western pedagogical approaches.

## Connection to the Boy Scouts
**St. Joseph's College** is widely recognized as the site where the **1st Hong Kong Troop** of the Boy Scouts was organized in **1913**. This event is cited as the official beginning of the scouting movement in Hong Kong. While colonial records emphasize the role of British administrators and the school’s staff in adapting the scouting ethos to the territory, local community narratives often highlight the enthusiasm of the students themselves in adopting global youth movements. The establishment of this troop at the college served as a template for the expansion of the movement throughout Hong Kong and the New Territories, playing a key role in the development of extra-curricular youth organizations in the region.
