## Strategic Importance
Stonecutters Island (**昂船洲**) is located in the northwestern part of Victoria Harbour. Originally a rocky outcrop used by stonecutters for quarrying granite, the island became a critical military asset under British colonial rule. Its location provided an ideal vantage point for monitoring shipping traffic entering the harbour from the west.

## Sovereignty and Cession
The status of Stonecutters Island was formalized under the **Convention of Peking** (北京條約) in **1860**. While British colonial accounts characterize this period as the necessary expansion of the colony's defensive perimeter, Qing officials and later nationalist scholarship have emphasized the coerced nature of the unequal treaties signed during this era. Under the treaty, the island was ceded in perpetuity to the British Crown alongside the Kowloon Peninsula.

## Military Development
Following the cession, the British military established a significant presence on the island. By the late **19th century**, the island housed a variety of military installations, including naval bases, ammunition depots, and later, radio stations. These facilities integrated the island into the wider defensive network of the British Empire in East Asia. Throughout the **20th century**, the island remained restricted territory, maintaining a separate administrative identity from the civilian development of Kowloon.

## Land Reclamation and Integration
For much of its history, Stonecutters remained physically separated from the mainland. However, as part of the **West Kowloon Reclamation** project in the **1990s**, the island was connected to the Kowloon Peninsula by land reclamation. This transformation fundamentally altered the local geography, turning the former island into a contiguous part of the mainland. Following the handover of sovereignty in **1997**, the island's military facilities were transitioned to the People’s Liberation Army (PLA) Hong Kong Garrison. Historical interpretations continue to diverge on whether the site represents a relic of colonial militarism or a modern landmark of regional integration.
