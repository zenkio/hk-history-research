## Introduction and Route
**Stubbs Road** (司徒拔道) is a winding arterial road on Hong Kong Island that links Wan Chai and the Mid-Levels to Happy Valley, Wong Nai Chung Gap, and eventually Repulse Bay and Stanley. Named after **Sir Reginald Edward Stubbs**, who served as the Governor of Hong Kong from **1919 to 1925**, the road was constructed to facilitate access to the higher elevations of the island as the colonial government sought to expand residential areas beyond the congested central urban core.

## Architectural and Cultural Heritage
Along its winding path, Stubbs Road features several notable historical structures that reflect the evolving socio-economic landscape of 20th-century Hong Kong. Most prominent among these is the **King Yin Lei Mansion** (景賢里), a historic residence completed in **1937** that blends traditional Chinese architectural styles with Western construction techniques. The opening and subsequent preservation history of King Yin Lei have drawn significant public and scholarly attention to the road's corridor as a repository of heritage architecture.

## Historiographical Perspectives
Interpretations of the development along Stubbs Road often vary. From a British colonial administrative perspective, the expansion of infrastructure into areas like Stubbs Road represented modernization, sanitary reform, and the orderly expansion of elite residential zones away from tropical diseases in lower-lying districts. Conversely, local Hong Kong and heritage preservationist communities have increasingly viewed the surviving structures along the road, such as King Yin Lei, as vital physical links to the colony's affluent merchant history and a testament to vernacular craftsmanship. Later scholarship often analyzes these hillside developments through the lens of spatial segregation, highlighting how geography reinforced social stratification between the colonial elite, wealthy local industrialists, and the broader population living in Victoria City below.
