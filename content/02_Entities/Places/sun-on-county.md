## Administrative Origins
**Sun On County** (**新安縣**) was established in **1573** during the **Ming Dynasty**, carved out of the former Dongguan County (**東莞縣**). The county seat was located at Nantou (**南頭**). Its formation was a strategic move by the imperial government to strengthen coastal administration and maritime defense in the Pearl River Delta. For over two centuries, this district served as the primary governing body for the area now known as Hong Kong.

## The Hakka Resettlement
During the early **Qing Dynasty**, the region underwent significant demographic shifts. Following the **Great Clearance** (**遷界令**) of **1661–1669**, which forced coastal inhabitants to move inland, the government later incentivized repopulation. The **Qing Encouragement of Hakka Resettlement** brought large numbers of Hakka people into the sparsely populated coastal areas of **Sun On County**. While colonial-era British records often framed these movements as the arrival of 'outsiders' to a 'barren' territory, local New Territories lineages and later scholarship emphasize that this migration transformed the agrarian landscape and established the complex social fabric of village life that persists in the New Territories today.

## Differing Perspectives
Interpretations of Sun On County’s legacy vary significantly. British colonial historiography frequently portrayed the region as a neglected backwater prior to the British lease of the **New Territories** in **1898**, emphasizing the modernization brought by colonial administration. Conversely, Beijing-centric and local traditionalist narratives highlight the area’s deep integration into Chinese imperial civilization and the resilience of indigenous *Punti* and Hakka communities. Scholars today often focus on the tension between the peripheral nature of the county relative to the imperial center and its importance as a local nexus of salt production, maritime trade, and military defense. Following the **Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory**, the county was split, leaving the majority of its landmass in mainland China while the southern portion became part of the British crown colony.
