## Geography and Early Settlement
**Tai Mo Shan** (大帽山), located in the New Territories, stands at 957 meters, making it the highest point in Hong Kong. The mountain's slopes have historically been inhabited by indigenous communities, primarily the **Tang** (鄧) clan, who cultivated the surrounding valleys.

## The Tea Industry
One of the most notable historical developments on the mountain was the production of **Tai Mo Shan Tea** (大帽山茶). Local chronicles suggest that tea cultivation emerged in the mid-18th century. Indigenous villagers in the New Territories view this as a vital part of their agrarian heritage, representing a self-sufficient local economy that predated widespread British influence. Conversely, colonial-era historical records often categorize this activity as a minor cottage industry, emphasizing how the terrain's altitude and mist provided unique conditions for high-quality tea, which was reportedly traded as far as Canton.

## Colonial and Strategic Significance
Following the **1898** Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory, the British administration brought the mountain under colonial jurisdiction. During the mid-20th century, the site gained strategic importance. The British military established the **Tai Mo Shan Radar Station**, which remains a defining feature of the summit. Interpretations regarding this period vary: some academic perspectives focus on the geopolitical importance of the site during the Cold War as a monitoring outpost, while local heritage researchers emphasize the displacement of agricultural practices caused by restricted military access.

## Modern Status
Today, the area is largely protected within the **Tai Mo Shan Country Park** (大帽山郊野公園), established in **1979**. While debates continue regarding the preservation of traditional land use versus the needs of modern conservation and public recreation, the mountain remains a focal point for understanding the transition of the New Territories from a rural, agricultural landscape to a core component of the contemporary Hong Kong SAR.
