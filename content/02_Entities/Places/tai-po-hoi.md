## Geography and Early Significance

**Tai Po Hoi** (大埔海), historically also known as Tolo Harbour, is a sheltered inlet situated in the northeastern New Territories of Hong Kong. The body of water held major economic importance due to the presence of freshwater pearl mussels, specifically *Pinctada fucata*, which produced high-quality pearls known in historical texts as southern pearls or *nanpan* (南珠).

## The Pearl Harvesting Industry

Pearl harvesting in the area dates back centuries, with imperial records noting operations as early as the **Han dynasty**, though commercial scale expanded significantly during the **Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period** and the **Song dynasty**. The **Yuan suspension of Tai Po pearl harvesting** reflects the intense economic pressure and social unrest caused by over-harvesting and harsh government quotas imposed on local divers, known as the *danmin* (蛋民) or Tanka people. 

## Historiographical Interpretations

Interpretations of the pearl industry vary across different perspectives:

*   **Imperial and Qing/Beijing perspectives** traditionally viewed Tai Po Hoi as a peripheral resource extraction site, focusing on tribute yields and state revenue.
*   **Local Hong Kong and New Territories communities**, particularly indigenous and boat-dwelling populations, preserved oral and social histories emphasizing the severe physical toll, dangers of diving, and economic exploitation suffered by local laborers under imperial monopolies.
*   **Later scholarship** often frames the history of Tai Po Hoi through the lens of environmental history and maritime trade, analyzing how intensive state demand drastically altered the local ecology and eventually led to the depletion of the pearl oyster beds.

## Timeline

- **960-1279**: Pearl harvesting flourishes under Song dynasty administration.
- **13th-14th century**: Yuan dynasty suspends official pearl harvesting due to depletion and local unrest.
- **1898**: Tai Po Hoi comes under British administration with the lease of the New Territories.
