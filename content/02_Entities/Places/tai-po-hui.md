## Historical Background

**Tai Po Hui** (大埔墟), historically known as Tai Po Market, served as a vital commercial and administrative hub in the northeastern New Territories. Before the British lease of the New Territories in **1899**, the area was dominated by powerful local lineages, most notably the **Tang** clan of Kam Tin and the **Tai Po Seven Kei** (大埔七約), an alliance of local villages.

## The Man Mo Temple and Local Power

Constructed between **1892** and **1893**, the **Man Mo Temple** (文武廟) in Tai Po Hui stands as a focal point of local history. While traditionally viewed by local communities as a sacred space to worship the deities of literature and martial arts, historical interpretations of its construction vary.

* **Local Community Perspective:** Local oral traditions and lineage accounts emphasize the temple's role in unifying the **Seven Kei** villages, serving as a neutral meeting hall and a court for settling disputes and managing market affairs independent of external interference.
* **Colonial and Later Scholarship:** British colonial records and modern historians often analyze the temple and the surrounding market dynamics through the lens of lineage rivalry, particularly the economic competition between the **Tang** clan and the rising alliances of surrounding villages seeking to break monopolistic control over trade in the region.

## British Takeover

Upon the arrival of the British administration in **1899**, Tai Po Hui became a focal point for both resistance and administrative reorganization. The tensions surrounding the takeover culminated in the **Six-Day War of 1899** (新界六日戰), during which local militias clashed with British forces near Tai Po before the colonial government consolidated its control over the New Territories.
