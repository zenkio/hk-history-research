## Early History and Pearl Harvesting

Tai Po's historical prominence began centuries before British colonial rule, centered around the **Tolo Harbour** (大埔海), historically known as the Tai Po Hoi. During the **Southern Han Dynasty** (**南漢**, 917–971 CE), Tolo Harbour was a major center for pearl harvesting. The region's economic significance led to the **Institutionalization of the Pearl Garrison** (官富巡司), as the imperial court sought to protect and control the lucrative pearl trade. Local folklore and historical records indicate that heavy taxation and over-harvesting severely impacted the local divers and communities.

## Colonial Integration and the Railway

The landscape of Tai Po transformed dramatically following the 1898 Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory, which leased the New Territories to the British. Tai Po became an administrative hub for the British colonial government. A pivotal moment in this era was the **Opening of the Kowloon-Canton Railway British Section** (**九廣鐵路英段**) in **1910**, with Tai Po Market station serving as a key stop connecting the rural New Territories to urban Kowloon.

Interpretations of this colonial transition often diverge. British colonial administration accounts typically emphasize modernization, infrastructural development, and the establishment of law and order. In contrast, local New Territories communities and later critical scholarship often highlight the disruption of traditional land tenure, economic shifts, and local resistance to alien rule.

## Wartime Conflict

Tight integration into the broader region also exposed Tai Po to major geopolitical conflicts. During the turbulent events surrounding the **Six-Day War in the New Territories** (**新界六日戰**) in **1899**—when indigenous clans armed themselves to resist the British takeover—Tai Po was a central site of military confrontation and British pacification.

Later, during the Japanese occupation of Hong Kong (1941–1945), Tai Po's geography made it both a site of guerrilla resistance by the **Hong Kong-Kowloon Independent Brigade** (**港九獨立大隊**) and a target of harsh occupation policies. Modern historical consensus views Tai Po as a microcosm of Hong Kong's broader transition from a collection of rural coastal villages to a modern administrative and industrial satellite town.
