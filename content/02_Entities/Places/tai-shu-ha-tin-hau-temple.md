## Introduction
Located in Kung Um Road, Yuen Long, the **Tai Shu Ha Tin Hau Temple** (大樹下天后廟) is dedicated to the sea goddess Tin Hau (天后). The temple derives its name from a massive banyan tree ("tai shu ha" meaning "under the big tree") that once overshadowed the structure. While exact early construction records are scarce, local oral tradition and historical repairs suggest the site gained prominence during the mid-Qing dynasty as the agrarian and commercial settlements of Yuen Long expanded.

## Connection to the Shap Pat Heung Alliance
The history of the temple is deeply intertwined with the **Shap Pat Heung** (十八鄉, Eighteen Villages) alliance. According to local New Territories lineage traditions, the temple served not only as a place of worship but also as the central meeting hall where representatives from the allied villages gathered to resolve disputes, coordinate regional defense, and manage local market economies. Later scholarship notes that religious alliances in the New Territories often doubled as sociopolitical networks used by indigenous clans to negotiate power dynamics, first under Qing imperial administration and later during the transition to British colonial rule after **1898**.
