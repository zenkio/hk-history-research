## The Great Clearance Connection

During the early Qing dynasty, Taiwan played a direct role in the coastal policies that devastated the region encompassing modern-day Hong Kong. To suppress the anti-Qing resistance led by **Koxinga** (鄭成功) operating from Taiwan, the Qing court issued the **Edict of the Great Clearance** (遷界令) in **1661**. This order forced the evacuation of the entire coastal population of Guangdong, including the area of Xin'an County (新安縣) which covers present-day Hong Kong. 

Perspectives on this policy differ significantly. Qing state historiography framed the forced depopulation as a necessary and regrettable military measure to secure the empire against maritime insurgents. Conversely, local New Territories communities and later historians emphasize the immense human suffering, economic ruin, and destruction of local lineage structures caused by the displacement. When the threat from Taiwan receded, the **Rescission of the Great Clearance** (復界令) was enacted in **1669**, allowing displaced residents and settlers from other provinces to slowly return and rebuild.

## Refuge and Modern Migration

In the 20th century, Taiwan emerged as a vital political and geographical counterweight during the upheaval of the Chinese Civil War and the Cold War. Following the establishment of the People's Republic of China in **1949**, the British colonial government in Hong Kong sought to balance relations between Beijing and Taipei while managing massive influxes of refugees. 

While many mainland refugees remained in Hong Kong—leading to the establishment of settlements such as the **Tiu Keng Leng Refugee Camp** (調景嶺難民營)—Taiwan maintained active political, cultural, and intelligence networks within the colony. Pro-Nationalist (Kuomintang) sympathizers in Hong Kong frequently looked to Taiwan for political alignment, leading to periodic tensions and clashes with leftist groups in the colony, most notably during the **1967 riots**. Later scholarship highlights how Hong Kong served as a unique middle ground between Taiwan and mainland China, functioning as a space of transit, ideology, and economic exchange.

## Timeline

- **1661**: Qing court issues the Great Clearance edict to counter Koxinga's forces in Taiwan, evacuating the Hong Kong region.
- **1669**: The Great Clearance is rescinded, allowing inhabitants to return to the coastal areas.
- **1949**: The Nationalist government relocates to Taiwan, shaping decades of Cold War politics and refugee dynamics affecting Hong Kong.
