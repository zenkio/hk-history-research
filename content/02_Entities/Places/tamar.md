## Historical Significance
Historically, the **Tamar** site served as the primary base for the **Royal Navy** in Hong Kong. Named after the **HMS Tamar**, a troopship that served as a receiving ship at the base from **1897** until the **Battle of Hong Kong** in **1941**, the area became synonymous with British naval presence in the region. Following the **Second World War**, the site was redeveloped, maintaining its role as the **HMS Tamar** naval base until it was decommissioned in **1997** prior to the transfer of sovereignty.

## Transition to Government Headquarters
Following the handover, the site remained vacant for years, sparking significant debate regarding its future use. Some stakeholders advocated for preserving its maritime heritage, while others, including the government, prioritized its location as a symbol of administrative authority. Eventually, the **Tamar Development Project** was initiated, resulting in the construction of the new **Central Government Complex**, which opened in **2011**.

## Political Protests
In recent history, the forecourt of the government headquarters at **Tamar** has become a central venue for civic expression. It gained prominence during the **2012** anti-national education movement, where protesters—including students and activists—gathered to challenge the proposed 'Moral and National Education' curriculum. Interpretations of these events remain divided; government officials often viewed the protests as a challenge to administrative stability and the implementation of educational policy, while local activists and various civil society groups framed the site as a space for protecting academic freedom and local identity. The site's physical design, often referred to as the 'Door to the Future,' has subsequently served as a backdrop for various political demonstrations, reflecting the evolving relationship between the public and the HKSAR government.
