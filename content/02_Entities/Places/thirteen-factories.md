## Historical Significance
The **Thirteen Factories** (十三行) were a specialized enclave in Canton (Guangzhou) where foreign traders were permitted to reside and conduct commerce under the **Canton System**. Established by the **Qing Dynasty** (清朝), this district served as the sole gateway for maritime trade between China and Western powers until the **First Opium War** (1839–1842). 

## Connections to Hong Kong
The history of the factories is inextricably linked to Hong Kong's development as an entrepôt. Before the formal cession of Hong Kong Island, British merchants utilized the commercial infrastructure and established trading houses—such as **Jardine Matheson**—within the Thirteen Factories. These firms later migrated their operations to Hong Kong, bringing with them established practices in banking, shipping, and the role of the **comprador** (買辦). Scholars often debate the legacy of this period; while British colonial historiography frames the transition as the birth of a 'free port,' Beijing-centric scholarship frequently characterizes the factories as a site of early semi-colonial exploitation, while local historical perspectives in Hong Kong emphasize the agency of Chinese merchants who navigated these restrictive conditions to build the foundation of the territory's modern economy.

## Economic Dynamics
The factories were central to the 'Silver Outflow' crisis, as the massive purchase of tea and silk by Westerners led to monetary instability in the Qing empire. This trade imbalance became a primary catalyst for the illegal opium trade, which eventually fueled the geopolitical tensions leading to the founding of Hong Kong. The cultural brokerage provided by compradors facilitated daily operations between foreign traders and Qing officials, a dynamic that persisted in the early administrative structure of British Hong Kong. Whether the Canton System was a strictly oppressive regime or a flexible, albeit rigid, commercial framework remains a subject of ongoing historical interpretation.

## Timeline

- **1757**: Qing Emperor Qianlong restricts all Western maritime trade to the port of Canton.
- **1822**: A major fire destroys many of the Thirteen Factories, leading to temporary trading disruption.
- **1841**: Following the outbreak of the First Opium War, trading operations begin shifting toward Hong Kong.
- **1856**: The factories are destroyed by fire during the Second Opium War, effectively ending the Canton System.
