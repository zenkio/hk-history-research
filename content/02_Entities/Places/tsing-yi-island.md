## Historical Settlement and the To Clan

Historically, Tsing Yi Island (青衣島) was home to various fishing and agrarian communities. Among the prominent local lineages, the **To clan** (陶氏) established a significant presence on the island. According to local oral traditions and lineage histories preserved by New Territories communities, the To clan migrated to the area centuries ago, engaging in fishing, salt production, and small-scale agriculture. 

## Perspectives on Land and Governance

Documentation regarding early Tsing Yi is sparse in formal imperial records, leaving much of the island's early administrative history open to interpretation. 

- **Local Communities:** Oral histories and clan genealogies emphasize self-governance, maritime livelihoods, and strong kinship networks that predated intensive British administration.
- **British Colonial Records:** Upon the lease of the New Territories in **1898**, British colonial authorities viewed Tsing Yi primarily as an outlying area of strategic and maritime interest. Colonial land surveys and tax assessments began to formally document land use, sometimes conflicting with traditional customary rights understood by indigenous lineages like the To clan.
- **Later Scholarship:** Modern historians note that Tsing Yi's history reflects the broader pattern of New Territories development, where marginal fishing islands were gradually integrated into regional economies through clan migration and maritime trade.

## Modern Transformation

In the latter half of the **20th century**, Tsing Yi underwent radical transformation. Through extensive land reclamation, the island was connected to the mainland and neighboring islands, eventually hosting major infrastructure including container terminals, power stations, and highway networks such as the Tsing Ma Bridge (青馬大橋). Today, little of the historic rural landscape remains, though the memory of its early settlers persists through local heritage studies.
