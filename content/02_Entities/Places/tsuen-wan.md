## Early History and Village Settlement

Historically known as **Tuen Mun Lok** (屯門澳) in ancient records, the area comprising modern **Tsuen Wan** (荃灣) was long characterized by agricultural and fishing communities. Among the most notable local landmarks is the construction of **Sam Tung Uk** (三棟屋), a walled Hakka village established in **1786** by the **Chan** clan. Local New Territories historiography emphasizes the self-sufficient agrarian nature of these communities, whereas colonial administration records often framed the area merely as a peripheral tax-yielding district prior to 20th-century development.

## Industrialization and Textile Hub

Following the Second World War, Tsuen Wan became the epicenter of **Rise of Hong Kong as a Global Textile Hub**. Benefiting from incoming Shanghai capital, entrepreneurial expertise, and cheap labor, textile mills proliferated across the district. Later economic scholarship highlights how government land policies actively facilitated this transformation, turning Tsuen Wan into Hong Kong's first satellite industrial town.

## Social Unrest and Natural Disasters

Rapid urbanization brought severe social and environmental pressures. During the **The 1956 Double Tenth Riots** in **October 1956**, Tsuen Wan saw intense clashes between Nationalist (KMT) and Communist (CCP) sympathizers, reflecting the broader Cold War fractures within the local population. Just a few years later, in **September 1962**, the area suffered catastrophic **Devastation by Typhoon Wanda**, which caused massive structural damage and loss of life in the shanty towns and industrial quarters.

## Modern Urban Development

To manage the expanding population and industrial workforce, the government integrated Tsuen Wan into the **Ten-Year Housing Programme Announced** in the **1970s**. This initiative shifted the landscape from low-rise factories and villages toward high-density public housing estates and modern transport infrastructure, laying the groundwork for the modern Tsuen Wan New Town.
