## Early Settlement and Maritime Trade

Archaeological findings indicate human activity in Tuen Mun (屯門) stretching back to prehistoric times, notably featuring the **Emergence of Fine Geometric Pottery**. Its strategic geographical position at the estuary of the Pearl River made it a natural gateway for maritime traffic.

## Imperial Expansion and Military Significance

During the **Qin Dynasty's Southern Expansion** in the **3rd century BCE**, the region was brought under imperial Chinese administration. Its importance grew due to the **Establishment of Tuen Mun Military Garrison**, particularly noted during the **Rise of Tang Maritime Silk Road** when the area served as a vital anchorage and naval outpost for vessels traveling between China and Southeast Asia.

## Administrative Integration

Throughout successive dynasties, Tuen Mun underwent various administrative changes. It was influenced by the **Eastern Wu Maritime Development**, the **Establishment of Dongguan County**, and the **Sui Dynasty Administrative Consolidation**. Local traditions and later scholarship often emphasize its role as a salt-producing center, tied to the **Emergence of the Coastal Salt Industry**.

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of Tuen Mun's history diverge. British colonial historiography often framed the New Territories as an isolated, agrarian periphery prior to **1898**. In contrast, local Hong Kong scholars and New Territories lineage communities emphasize a long, continuous history of maritime trade, salt production, and military presence that integrated the area into broader coastal networks long before British administration.
