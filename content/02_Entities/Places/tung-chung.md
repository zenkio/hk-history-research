## Strategic Significance
Located on the northern coast of Lantau Island, **Tung Chung (東涌)** has long served as a vital anchorage. Throughout the **Qing dynasty (清朝)**, its geography made it a focal point for maritime defense against both foreign incursions and local piracy. The area is most noted for the construction of the **Tung Chung Fort (東涌炮台)** in **1817**, which was intended to bolster coastal security following the turbulent pirate era of the early **19th century**.

## The Era of Cheung Po Tsai
**Tung Chung** is prominently featured in historical accounts concerning the surrender of the notorious pirate **Cheung Po Tsai (張保仔)**. Following prolonged pressure from both Qing naval forces and Portuguese support, **Cheung Po Tsai** negotiated his surrender in **1810**. While historical interpretations differ on the degree of his autonomy versus the extent of Qing control in the region, most scholars agree that the waters surrounding Lantau were the primary theater of his maritime operations. Local oral histories often emphasize his role as a Robin Hood-like figure, whereas colonial and imperial records frequently characterize him strictly as a maritime criminal whose surrender was a calculated tactical concession.

## Colonial and Modern Development
With the **British** annexation of the New Territories in **1898**, the strategic military value of **Tung Chung** diminished as the colonial administration shifted its focus toward Victoria Harbour. For much of the **20th century**, the area remained a quiet rural landscape dominated by traditional village communities. The interpretation of this transition is contested; some narratives highlight the resilience of local indigenous populations in maintaining ancestral lands, while others focus on the rapid modernization brought by the construction of the **Chek Lap Kok Airport** in the **1990s**. Today, the site remains a bridge between the maritime history of the Pearl River Delta and the urban expansion of modern Hong Kong.
