## Archaeological Significance
**Tung Wan Tsai South** (東灣仔南) is located on the southern coast of **Ma Wan** (馬灣) island. It is recognized as one of the most important archaeological sites in Hong Kong due to the depth and continuity of its cultural layers. Excavations conducted during the late **1990s**—prior to major infrastructure projects in the area—revealed evidence of human activity spanning from the **Neolithic period** to the **Tang Dynasty** (唐朝).

## Historical Interpretations
Scholars have utilized the findings at **Tung Wan Tsai South** to better understand the maritime culture of the early **Pearl River Delta** (珠江三角洲). The site is particularly noted for its well-preserved burials. While academic consensus acknowledges the site's role in documenting regional prehistoric migrations, interpretations of the societal structures of these early inhabitants remain a subject of active research. Some researchers emphasize the site's role as a seasonal outpost for maritime groups, while others suggest it may have served as a more permanent settlement for communities reliant on coastal resources.

Following the discovery of these artifacts, the connection between **Tung Wan Tsai South** and the broader historical narrative of Hong Kong became a focal point for heritage preservation. The recovered materials offer a rare window into the material culture of the area's earliest inhabitants, contrasting with later documentation that prioritizes the records of the **Qing Dynasty** (清朝) or the administrative history of the **British colonial** period. The lack of extensive written records for this site means that the precise social organization and origins of these early populations remain uncertain, leaving room for ongoing scholarly investigation into their cultural links with neighboring coastal regions.
