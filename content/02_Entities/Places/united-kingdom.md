## Historical Context
The United Kingdom (英國) governed Hong Kong for over 150 years following the First Opium War. The territory was acquired in stages: Hong Kong Island via the **Treaty of Nanking (1842)**, the Kowloon Peninsula via the **Convention of Peking (1860)**, and the New Territories (新界) via the **Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory (1898)** on a 99-year lease.

## Differing Interpretations of Sovereignty
Interpretations regarding the nature of British rule vary significantly. From a British colonial perspective, the administration was often framed as a mission to establish the 'rule of law' and economic prosperity. Conversely, the Qing dynasty and later the People's Republic of China viewed the acquisitions as 'unequal treaties' imposed through force. Local Hong Kong communities have historically held diverse views; some welcomed the stability and civil liberties provided by British law, while others, particularly those in the New Territories, maintained traditional customs and experienced tensions with colonial land policies.

## Nationality and Citizenship
The legal status of Hong Kong residents was fundamentally altered by British domestic legislation. The **British Nationality Act 1981** reclassified many Hong Kong residents as British Dependent Territories Citizens (BDTCs), a change that caused significant concern regarding the right of abode. This eventually led to the **British Nationality (Hong Kong) Act 1990**, which granted full British citizenship to a select group of professionals and civil servants. Scholarship often highlights these nationality shifts as a reflection of the United Kingdom's evolving domestic politics and its shifting responsibilities toward its overseas territories leading up to the 1997 handover.

## Timeline

- **1841**: British forces occupy Hong Kong Island during the First Opium War.
- **1898**: The New Territories are leased to the United Kingdom for 99 years.
- **1981**: The British Nationality Act 1981 removes the right of abode in the UK for most Hong Kong residents.
- **1997**: Sovereignty over Hong Kong is transferred from the United Kingdom to the People's Republic of China.
