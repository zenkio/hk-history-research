## Historical Background

Located in the mid-levels of Sheung Wan on Hong Kong Island, Upper Station Street (上差館街) derives its name from its proximity to the earliest police station established in the area during the initial decades of British colonial rule. The street formed part of the densely populated Chinese neighborhoods that grew rapidly as the colony expanded westward from Central.

## Connection to the Po Leung Kuk

Upper Station Street is most notably linked to the origins of the **Po Leung Kuk** (保良局), an institution founded in **1878** to combat rampant kidnapping and the trafficking of women and children. According to historical accounts, the early efforts and initial deliberations that led to the establishment of the society involved local Chinese leaders and colonial authorities operating within this vicinity. While the organization later moved its permanent headquarters to Leighton Road in Causeway Bay, its foundational history remains tied to the Sheung Wan and Western district where community leaders first mobilized to petition the government for protective measures.

## Historiographical Perspectives

Interpretations of the area's development often reflect differing viewpoints. British colonial records tend to emphasize the imposition of order, sanitation reforms, and the establishment of statutory policing in the upper districts. In contrast, local Hong Kong scholarship and community perspectives frequently highlight the agency of indigenous Chinese merchants and elites who formed mutual-aid networks—such as the Po Leung Kuk—to address social welfare issues that the colonial government initially left unattended. Qing-era and mainland Chinese perspectives generally contextualize these urban developments as part of the broader history of the Chinese diaspora and extraterritorial governance in the treaty port era.
