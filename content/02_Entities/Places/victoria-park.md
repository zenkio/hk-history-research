## Introduction and Origins
Named after **Queen Victoria**, **Victoria Park** (維多利亞公園) was established in **1957** on reclaimed land in **Causeway Bay** (銅鑼灣). The park features sports facilities, a large central lawn, and a prominent statue of Queen Victoria. From a local community perspective, it quickly evolved from a recreational ground into the de facto assembly point for civic expression on Hong Kong Island.

## Political Demonstrations and Commemorations
For decades, the park has been deeply intertwined with Hong Kong's political history. It served as the annual gathering site for the **Mass Rallies Supporting Tiananmen Square Protests** beginning in **1989**, and subsequently hosted the annual candlelight vigils organized by the **Hong Kong Alliance in Support of Patriotic Democratic Movements of China** (香港市民支援愛國民主運動聯合會). 

Beyond the June 4th commemorations, the park's central lawn and surrounding streets have been the starting or ending point for numerous large-scale demonstrations. These include the **Massive Protest Against Article 23 National Security Bill** in **2003**, the **WTO Ministerial Conference Protests** in **2005**, and the **Mass Protest Against the Extradition Bill** in **2019**. 

## Interpretations and Legacy
Interpretations of Victoria Park's role vary significantly. From the perspective of local activists and civil society, the park represents a vital public commons protected by the right to assembly. Conversely, from the perspective of Beijing and the Hong Kong government, especially following the **Dissolution of the Hong Kong Alliance** and the implementation of the **National Security Law**, unauthorized or politically sensitive mass gatherings at the park are viewed through a lens of national security and public order. Later scholarship often analyzes the park as a barometer for the shifting boundaries of political tolerance and civic identity in post-colonial Hong Kong.
