## History and Construction

By the late **19th century**, the population growth in Victoria placed immense pressure on Hong Kong's primary water source, the Pok Fu Lam Reservoir (薄扶林水塘). To secure an additional water supply, colonial authorities approved the construction of the Wong Nai Chung Reservoir (黃泥涌水塘) in the valley of Wong Nai Chung (黃泥涌). Construction commenced and was completed in **1899**. 

## Architectural Significance

While serving a critical utilitarian purpose for the expanding British colony, the reservoir site later gained recognition for its industrial archaeology. Several structures associated with the waterworks, including the dam, intake tower, and servant quarters, reflect the engineering standards of late **19th-century** British colonial infrastructure. 

## Later Use and Conservation

Following its decommissioning as a functional reservoir due to capacity limitations and the development of larger water schemes, the site was integrated into the **Tai Tam Country Park (Quarry Bay Extension)** (大潭郊野公園擴建部份). Today, the former reservoir area is celebrated for recreational use, and its surviving historic structures have been granted statutory monument status, preserving a tangible link to Hong Kong's early public works history.
