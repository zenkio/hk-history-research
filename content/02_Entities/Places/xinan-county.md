## Establishment and Early History

Xin'an County (新安縣) was established in **1573** during the Ming dynasty, carved out from parts of Dongguan County (東莞縣). The **Creation of Xin'an County** reflected the imperial court's desire to strengthen coastal administration and maritime defense in the Pearl River Delta.

## The Qing Dynasty and Coastal Turmoil

During the early Qing dynasty, the region experienced severe disruption due to the **Edict of the Great Clearance** issued in **1661**, which forced coastal populations inland to combat anti-Qing resistance. This was followed by the **Erection of Coastal Watch Posts** to monitor maritime activities. Once the clearance was lifted, the local population gradually returned, and lineage communities rebuilt their settlements, exemplified by the **Construction of Sam Tung Uk Village** (三棟屋村) and other walled villages in the New Territories.

## Documentation and Historiography

In **1688**, the **Compilation of the Xin'an County Gazetteer** (新安縣志) was completed, providing invaluable documentation of the geography, economy, and society of the region. 

## Historiographical Interpretations

Interpretations of Xin'an County's history vary among different perspectives:
- **Qing and mainland historiography** traditionally viewed the area through the lens of imperial integration, pacification, and frontier defense.
- **Local New Territories communities** emphasize indigenous resilience, lineage autonomy, and the adaptation of Hakka and Punti clans to shifting state policies.
- **British colonial and later scholarship** often framed the region as a peripheral borderland whose eventual separation in 1898—when the New Territories were leased to Britain—fractured a cohesive socioeconomic and cultural entity.
