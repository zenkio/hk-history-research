## Administrative History

Xin'an County (新安縣) was established in **1573** during the Ming dynasty, carved out from the eastern part of Bao'an County (寶安縣). The county seat was located at Nantou (南頭). For centuries, the territory covered by modern Hong Kong fell under the jurisdiction of Xin'an magistrates, linking the local population—including Punti clans, Tanka, and Hakka communities—to the imperial bureaucracy of the Chinese empire.

## Perspectives on the Territory

Interpretations of Xin'an's role in local history vary across different historiographical traditions. **Qing imperial records** generally viewed the region through the lens of coastal defense and taxation, notably during the **1661–1669** Great Clearance (遷界), which forcibly depopulated the coast to combat anti-Qing resistance by Zheng Chenggong. 

In contrast, **local New Territories lineage histories**—such as those of the **To Clan** (陶氏) in Tsing Yi (青衣) and other powerful clans—emphasize local autonomy, fortified settlements, and lineage consolidation in the wake of such disruptions. 

During the **British colonial period**, British officials often characterized Xin'an administration as distant or weak, using the 1898 Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory to justify the annexation of the New Territories for security. Modern **Hong Kong scholarship**, however, highlights the deep economic, cultural, and kinship ties that bound the inhabitants of Hong Kong directly to the broader economic networks of Xin'an and the Pearl River Delta, challenging the notion that Hong Kong was a "barren rock" prior to British arrival.
