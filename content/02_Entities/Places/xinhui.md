## Historical Significance
**Xinhui** (新會), located in the modern-day Jiangmen area of Guangdong, is historically significant to Hong Kong as the final destination of the **Southern Song** (南宋) loyalists. Following the intense naval confrontations in the Hong Kong region—most notably at **Lantau Island** (大嶼山) and the subsequent relocation of the imperial fleet—the court moved westward toward the **Yamen** (崖門) waterway in Xinhui.

## The Battle of Yamen
In **1279**, the **Battle of Yamen** (崖門海戰) marked the effective end of the Southern Song dynasty. Scholars generally agree that the Mongol-led **Yuan** (元) forces cornered the remaining Song loyalists at this site. While some traditional narratives focus on the tragic end of the young emperor **Zhao Bing** (趙昺) and his ministers, later historical scholarship often evaluates the logistical and strategic failures that led the court to traverse from the Hong Kong coastline to the Xinhui estuary. 

## Contrasting Perspectives
Interpretations of this migration vary across historical traditions. Local Hong Kong and New Territories genealogical records often emphasize the survival of clans that fled southward during this period, viewing the movement through Xinhui as the catalyst for the settlement of prominent families in the Pearl River Delta. Conversely, official Qing-era historiography often contextualized these events within the framework of dynastic legitimacy and the inevitability of the Mongol transition. While some British colonial historians viewed the period primarily through the lens of maritime geography and defense of the South China coast, contemporary regional historians underscore the cultural shift that occurred as court officials and scholars dispersed into the surrounding districts of Xinhui and Hong Kong following the dynasty’s collapse.
