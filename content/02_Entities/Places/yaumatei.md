## Introduction and Early Settlement

Yaumatei (油麻地) is one of the earliest developed areas on the Kowloon Peninsula. The name historically derives from the local businesses that sold wood oil (油) and hemp (麻) along the tidal flats, which served the maritime needs of local boat people. Long before major British colonial intervention, the area was utilized by fishermen and coastal traders.

## Colonial Development and the Typhoon Shelter

Following the Convention of Beijing in **1860**, the Kowloon Peninsula came under British administration. The colonial government gradually reshaped the area's geography and administration to support expanding trade and population growth. A major milestone in this development was the **Inauguration of the Yaumatei Typhoon Shelter**, which provided vital protection for the burgeoning junk fleet and maritime workers against seasonal typhoons.

Historiographical interpretations of this era often diverge. British colonial records emphasize the introduction of modern urban planning, sanitation, and infrastructural engineering that transformed a loose collection of coastal settlements into an organized port economy. Conversely, local community and critical scholarship often highlight how these infrastructural projects displaced indigenous Tanka boat communities and marginalized traditional maritime livelihoods in favor of colonial commercial interests.

## Urban Heritage

Today, Yaumatei is recognized for its preserved historical landmarks, including the Yaumatei Police Station and the historic fruit market (Yau Ma Tei Fruit Market), which reflect the district's complex socio-economic evolution through the twentieth century.
