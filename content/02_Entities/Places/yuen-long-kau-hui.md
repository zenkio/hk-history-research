## Introduction

Yuen Long Kau Hui (**元朗舊墟**), or the Old Market of Yuen Long, is an early market town whose establishment is central to the history of the New Territories. Located in the northwestern part of Hong Kong, it developed as a regional hub for trade, agriculture, and maritime access.

## Foundation and Clan Dynamics

The founding of the market is traditionally attributed to the **Tang** (鄧) clan of Kam Tin, one of the prominent lineages in the region, alongside the **Man** (文) clan of San Tin. Historical interpretations regarding the exact motivations and timing of its establishment often reflect differing perspectives between local lineages and broader administrative records.

Local oral traditions and clan records emphasize the need for a secure, localized trading center to serve the agrarian communities of the Yuen Long plain. Conversely, Qing dynasty imperial records and later scholarship view such markets as part of a sanctioned network of rural commerce (*xu* or *hui*) designed to facilitate tax collection, local defense, and social stability.

## Shift to the New Market

By the late 19th and early 20th centuries, the commercial focus of the area shifted. Following the British takeover of the New Territories in **1898**, colonial infrastructure projects, particularly the construction of the Castle Peak Road and the Kowloon-Canton Railway, altered regional trade routes. This prompted the development of Yuen Long San Hui (New Market, **元朗新墟**), leading to the gradual decline of the old market as a commercial center.

## Heritage and Historiography

Today, Yuen Long Kau Hui is preserved for its historical architecture, including historic streets like **晉源圍** (Chun Yuen Tsuen) and temples such as **益善堂** (Yik Shing Tong). While British colonial accounts frequently framed the New Territories through the lens of land administration and pacification, contemporary scholarship and local communities emphasize the resilience and autonomy of indigenous economic networks that predated the colonial era.
