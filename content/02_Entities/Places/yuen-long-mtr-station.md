## Overview and Development
**Yuen Long Station** (元朗站) is a station on the **MTR** Tuen Ma line, located in the Yuen Long District of the New Territories. It officially opened in **2003** as part of the West Rail line, connecting the district to the urban core of Kowloon. The station serves as a vital infrastructure link for the surrounding residential and commercial areas, which have evolved from traditional market towns into a modern suburban hub.

## Historical Significance and Perspectives
Historically, the Yuen Long area was a collection of walled villages and rural markets. Following the **1898** Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory, the transition from Qing administration to British colonial rule was met with resistance from the local indigenous population, specifically the **Six-Day War** of **1899**. These historical roots often inform the distinct regional identity of the Yuen Long community compared to the urban districts of Hong Kong Island and Kowloon.

## The July 21st Incident
In contemporary history, the station became the site of a high-profile violent incident on **July 21, 2019**. On that evening, a group of armed individuals in white shirts attacked commuters and protesters inside the station premises amidst the broader 2019 pro-democracy protests. The incident has been interpreted in vastly different ways across the political spectrum. Pro-democracy advocates and many members of the general public view the event as a failure of the **Hong Kong Police Force** to protect citizens, often citing delays in law enforcement response. Conversely, some local New Territories community figures and pro-establishment voices have characterized the unrest as an escalation caused by protesters entering the district, arguing that the social order of the local community was under threat. The event remains a polarizing touchstone that significantly impacted public trust in the SAR government and local law enforcement agencies.
