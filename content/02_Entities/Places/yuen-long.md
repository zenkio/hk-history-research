## Early History and Indigenous Society

Yuen Long (元朗) developed as a major market town and agricultural hub in the northwestern New Territories. Historically, the area was dominated by powerful lineages, most notably the Tang clan, who established numerous settlements including the famous walled village of Kat Hing Wai (吉慶圍). Under British colonial administration following the **1898** Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory, the indigenous inhabitants fiercely resisted the takeover, culminating in the **1899** Six-Day War. 

## Colonial Administration and Modernization

During the British colonial period, Yuen Long retained much of its rural character while gradually integrating into the wider Hong Kong economy. Perspectives on this era diverge: colonial records often emphasize infrastructural development, rural administration, and the establishment of the Yuen Long Kau Hui (元朗舊墟) market town. Conversely, local New Territories communities and later revisionist scholarship frequently emphasize the preservation of traditional land rights, indigenous customs, and the sometimes tense negotiations between rural leaders and the colonial government.

## The 2019 Yuen Long Attack

In recent history, Yuen Long gained international attention due to the **2019** Yuen Long attack. On the evening of **July 21, 2019**, a large group of white-shirted assailants indiscriminately attacked commuters, protesters, and journalists inside the Yuen Long MTR station. This event severely shook public trust in the Hong Kong Police Force amid widespread allegations of delayed response times and collusion with triad elements. While official narratives focused on the complexity of maintaining public order during a period of intense social unrest, public inquiries, journalists, and local citizens widely condemned the incident as a critical watershed that fractured community safety and trust in institutions.
