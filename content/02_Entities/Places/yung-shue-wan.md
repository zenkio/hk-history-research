## Geography and Settlement

Yung Shue Wan (槻樹灣), meaning "Banyan Tree Bay", is located on the northwest coast of Lamma Island (南岬洲) in Hong Kong. Historically, the area consisted of small fishing and farming villages. Over the decades, it transformed into a popular destination for expatriates, artists, and local residents seeking an alternative to urban Hong Kong life, characterized by low-rise housing, pedestrian-only paths, and numerous seafood restaurants.

## Connection to the 2012 Ferry Collision

Yung Shue Wan is centrally linked to the **October 1, 2012** Lamma Island ferry collision (南岬島«海泰號»à撶事故), one of the deadliest maritime disasters in modern Hong Kong history. The collision occurred when the passenger vessel *Lamma IV*, carrying employees and family members of **Hong Kong Electric** (港電) to view the National Day fireworks in Victoria Harbour, was struck by the **Hong Kong & Kowloon Ferry** (*Sea Smooth* [海泰號]) shortly after departing from the Yung Shue Wan pier. 

The disaster resulted in the deaths of **39** people and injured **92** others. Because the ill-fated vessel had just left Yung Shue Wan, the tragedy deeply impacted the local island community, many of whom knew the victims or participated in early informal rescue efforts alongside emergency services.

## Memorial and Legacy

In the aftermath of the disaster, the Yung Shue Wan community, alongside the broader Hong Kong public, engaged in prolonged mourning and scrutiny of maritime safety regulations. Official investigations and subsequent public inquiries highlighted systemic failures in marine oversight, vessel safety equipment, and crew training, leaving a lasting mark on Hong Kong's maritime policy and the collective memory of Lamma Island's residents.
