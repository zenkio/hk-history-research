## Historical Significance
The **Zhoushan Islands** (舟山群島), located off the coast of Zhejiang province, China, hold a somber place in Hong Kong’s history due to their proximity to the site of the **Lisbon Maru** (里斯本丸) incident in **October 1942**. Following the fall of Hong Kong in **December 1941**, the Japanese military utilized the *Lisbon Maru* to transport over 1,800 British Prisoners of War (POWs) from Hong Kong to Japan.

## The Lisbon Maru Incident
On **October 1, 1942**, the vessel was torpedoed by the American submarine **USS Grouper** near the **Zhoushan Islands**. The ensuing tragedy resulted in the deaths of over 800 Allied soldiers, many of whom were trapped in the ship's hold. Local Chinese fishermen from the nearby islands, particularly those from **Dongji Island** (東極島), are credited with rescuing 384 British survivors from the water, an act that has been commemorated by both British veteran associations and the Chinese government.

## Differing Perspectives
Interpretations of this event vary among stakeholders. British colonial accounts and veteran narratives emphasize the heroism of the local fishermen and the brutality of the Japanese guards who fired upon prisoners attempting to escape. Conversely, later scholarship from Beijing and local mainland historians often frames the rescue efforts as a testament to the humanitarian spirit of the Chinese coastal population during the **War of Resistance Against Japanese Aggression** (抗日戰爭). In Hong Kong, the event serves as a focal point for understanding the transition of local soldiers and civilians into the hardships of the Pacific War. While there is a consensus on the factual timeline of the sinking, the degree of coordination between the local fishermen and the broader anti-Japanese resistance remains a subject of ongoing historical discussion.
