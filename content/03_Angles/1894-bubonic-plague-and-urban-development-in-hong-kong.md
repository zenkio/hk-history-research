The **1894 Bubonic Plague** outbreak served as a critical turning point for urban policy and governance in colonial Hong Kong. According to research by **Prof. Cecilia Chu**, the colonial administration faced significant challenges in balancing public health safety concerns with economic interests and housing needs. The epidemic prompted the government to implement various urban renewal projects to address deteriorating living conditions. This period of reform highlighted the complex relationship between land management and the maintenance of public order during the late 19th and early 20th centuries.
