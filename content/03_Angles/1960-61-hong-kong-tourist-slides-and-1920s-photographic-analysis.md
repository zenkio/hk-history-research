According to source materials presented by **David** on the **Gwulo** website, a collection of **20** colour tourist slides depicting **Hong Kong** in **1960**–**61** was discovered by **Pat** and **Chris Lally** in a charity shop in **Seaford**, **East Sussex**. The slides and accompanying video commentary document notable local scenes, including **Kai Tak** Airport's runway and its passenger terminal under construction by **1960** (scheduled to open in **1962**), **Nathan Road** featuring neon signage and the **Broadway Cinema** displaying *The Magnificent Seven* (which premiered in **September 1961**), the **Star Ferry**, **City Hall** under construction around **1961**, **Aberdeen** harbor, the **Tiger Balm Gardens** and **Tiger Pagoda**, and **Lok Ma Chau** overlooking the closed border with **China**. 

In an additional video segment within the source, the presenter analyzes an old photograph purchased on **eBay** showing the **Hong Kong Ferry** and **Kowloon Jetty**. Utilizing historical maps from **1920** and **1924**, newspaper advertisements, and postcards, the speaker dates the image between **1925** and **1933**. This timeframe is established through structural evidence—such as the former **Star Ferry** pier building repurposed in **1914** and the presence of two rickshaw shelters compared to one on the **1920** map—as well as transport and fashion indicators. These include buses bearing the name of the **Kai Tack Motor Bus Co. (1926) Ltd.** (re-incorporated in late **1926**), a **President**-type liner acquired by the **Dollar Line** in **1925**, and passengers wearing sun hats and solar topees.
