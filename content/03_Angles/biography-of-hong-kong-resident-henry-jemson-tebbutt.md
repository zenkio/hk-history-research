According to the blog post by **Philip Cracknell**, **Henry Jemson Tebbutt** (1893–1967) was an architect and military officer who lived in Hong Kong prior to the Second World War. During the **1941** Battle of Hong Kong, **Tebbutt** served in the HKVDC, while his wife, **Elizabeth (Betty) Ada Hindmarsh**, served as a military nurse. Following the Japanese occupation, **Tebbutt** was interned in several prison camps, including North Point, Sham Shui Po, and Argyle Street, until his liberation in **1945**. **Cracknell** notes that the couple's marriage did not survive the war, and **Betty** eventually remarried and settled in Spain. The study also identifies archival evidence of **Tebbutt's** professional life as an architect and his connection to the residence known as 'Tebbutt's House' on Repulse Bay Road.
