During the **1970s**, the youth of **Hong Kong Island** often experienced significant geographical isolation, as the harbour acted as a major social and physical barrier. According to **Professor Ray Yep**, the opening of the **Cross-Harbour Tunnel** on **August 2, 1972**, marked a pivotal moment in connecting the island to the **Kowloon Peninsula**. **Yep** recalls that his own upbringing in the **Ming Wah Estate** in **Shau Kei Wan** reflected a parochial lifestyle characterized by local public housing communities and proximity to **British colonial** infrastructure. The era was also defined by the rapid growth of local manufacturing, such as toy assembly, and shifting perceptions of political affiliations, including the labeling of businesses as 'leftist' following the social unrest of **1967**.
