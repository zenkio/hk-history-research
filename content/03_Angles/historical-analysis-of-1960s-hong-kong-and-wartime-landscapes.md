The video content, presented by **David Bellis** to the **Royal Geographical Society**, offers a dual-focus exploration of **Hong Kong**. The first part analyzes a set of **1960** tourist slides featuring iconic locations such as **Kai Tak** airport, **Nathan Road**, the **Kowloon-Canton Railway**, the **Tiger Balm Gardens**, and **Aberdeen Harbor**. These slides document significant infrastructural transitions, including the expansion of **Kai Tak**'s runway in **1958** and the construction of the passenger terminal between **1960** and **1962**. The slides also provide glimpses into traditional rural life in the New Territories, including farming practices near **Lok Ma Chau** and cultural markers of **Hakka** women.

The second part of the presentation draws upon photographs by **Harrison Forman** captured between **1940** and **1941** to examine the impact of the **Battle of Hong Kong** and the subsequent Japanese occupation. **Bellis** notes that local damage was caused not only by short-term combat but also by American bombing raids targeting Japanese supply lines, alongside widespread looting triggered by resource shortages. The occupation left lasting imprints, such as the construction of a war memorial on **Mount Cameron** (demolished in **1947**) and various military defenses. **Bellis** highlights that vestiges of these preparations, including pillboxes, ventilation shafts, and tunnels near **Queens Road East**, remain visible in the modern landscape. Accompanying web data from **Gwulo** lists ongoing efforts by contributors like **philk**, **moddsey**, and **Jimmy** to verify the locations of historical photographs, including unidentified scenes of **Shatin Valley** and **Victoria Park**.

## Historical context

> [!note] General background (AI, not from the source)

The period between 1940 and 1945 was defined by the transition from British colonial administration to the Japanese military occupation following the fall of Hong Kong in December 1941. By the 1960s, Hong Kong had entered a phase of rapid urbanization, infrastructural modernization, and economic industrialization, which fundamentally reshaped the territory's spatial organization and public landscape.

This recording presents a talk given to the **Royal Geographical Society (RGS)** by **David Bellis** regarding the impact of war on Hong Kong, drawing heavily on the pre-war and wartime photographs taken by American photographer and journalist **Harrison Forman** between **1940** and **1941**. Bellis discusses various architectural and infrastructural changes in Hong Kong during this period, focusing on British preparations for war—such as the construction of pillboxes, air-raid shelter tunnels, and pen shelters—and subsequent alterations during the Japanese occupation. Notable locations examined include the Peak, **Central**, **Wanchai**, **Kowloon**, and **Kowloon Bay**, highlighting the expansion of the **Kai Tak** airfield by the Japanese. Bellis explains that many pre-war buildings, government headquarters, and residential structures suffered severe damage not solely from combat during the **Battle of Hong Kong**, which lasted less than three weeks, but also from American bombing raids aimed at halting Japanese supply lines and widespread looting driven by firewood shortages during the blockade. The talk details how the Japanese occupation left distinct marks, including the establishment of a war memorial on Mount Cameron built upon reinforced concrete platforms that survived until its demolition in **1947**. Finally, Bellis points out remnants of these historical preparations that can still be found in modern-day Hong Kong, such as ventilation shafts in public parks, tunnel entrances along **Queens Road East**, and drainage systems like the **Kallang River**, directing interested viewers to the Gwulo pillbox map and his book series **Old Hong Kong Photos and the Tales They Tell** for further details.
