Based on a collection of color slides taken by a tourist in **Hong Kong** during the **1960s**—rediscovered by contributor **GW**’s mother and published on **Gwulo** on **2026-08-26**—the presentation and accompanying forum post document numerous rapid urban transformations across **Central**, **Kowloon**, **Aberdeen**, and **Causeway Bay**. The speaker highlights panoramic views of **Central**, noting the **St. Joseph's School** buildings, the **Murray Barracks** slated for demolition, and the **Royal Navy** dockyard being filled in for **Harcourt Road** in **1961**. Key structural landmarks included the **Butterfield and Swire** building, demolished in **1960**, and the **Queen's Building**, replaced by the **Mandarin Hotel** with its soft opening on **1963-09-01**. 

In **Kowloon**, aerial perspectives from **Chequerboard Hill** overlooked the **Kai Tak Runway** (with a new passenger terminal opening in **1962**), the **Chinese Christian Cemetery**, and the **Tung Tau** resettlement area, which contained the historic **Hau Wong Temple** dating back to **1730**. Nighttime scenes on **Nathan Road** featured neon signs such as the **Broadway Cinema** advertising ***The Magnificent Seven*** (premiering in **Hong Kong** in **September 1961**), alongside the **Sun Ya Hotel** and **Liang Cha** herbal tea. Additional sights included the **Peninsula Hotel**, the **New City Hall** complex (opening in **1962**), the **Kowloon-Canton Railway** terminus, **Tiger Balm Gardens**, and the **Tiger Pagoda**. 

Other locations featured include **Aberdeen** as a floating town, **Repulse Bay**, a **7-Up** sign at **Ap Lei Chau**, and theaters in **Causeway Bay** including the **Hoover**, **Roxy**, and **New York** theaters. Rural and cultural scenes depicted a man plowing a paddy field with an ox and women in traditional **Hakka** clothing, with belt and tassel colors indicating marital status. The slides were originally discovered in a charity shop in **Seaford**, **East Sussex**, by **Pat and Chris Lally**.

## Historical context

> [!note] General background (AI, not from the source)

The 1960s was a decade of dramatic economic growth and massive urban redevelopment in Hong Kong. The territory experienced a massive influx of refugees, leading to the rapid expansion of public housing estates, extensive land reclamation projects like those at Kai Tak and Harcourt Road, and the steady replacement of colonial-era architecture with modern high-rises.
