During the **1920s**, the colonial administration in Hong Kong faced significant challenges in balancing housing requirements with economic and safety concerns. According to **Prof. Cecilia Chu** of the **Chinese University of Hong Kong**, the land market and urban development were characterized by intense speculation and frequent waves of tenant evictions. These issues were central to the government's efforts to regulate the housing sector in the pre-war period. The period highlighted the tensions between private property interests and the colonial government's need to maintain social stability amidst rapid urban growth.
