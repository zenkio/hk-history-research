During the **Battle for Hong Kong** on **December 19, 1941**, the area around **Stanley Gap** became a site of significant military engagement. According to author **Philip Cracknell**, the location hosted a 3.7-inch anti-aircraft battery and a mess hut that was later utilized by Japanese forces to detain prisoners of war. This site, historically referred to as the 'black hole of Hong Kong' due to the deplorable conditions endured by captives, witnessed numerous war crimes. Surviving structures including splinter-proof shelters, battery headquarters, and a storehouse remain present at the site near the modern-day **Tai Tam Reservoir Road**. The history of these installations is documented through wartime photographs and British war crimes trial files.
