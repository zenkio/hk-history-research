The **Cochrane Street Tragedy** of **1901** served as a critical turning point for urban development and construction oversight in colonial Hong Kong. According to **Prof. Cecilia Chu** of **The Chinese University of Hong Kong**, the incident forced the administration to confront the inherent conflicts between housing needs, public safety, and economic development. The tragedy highlighted the failure of existing housing standards during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Consequently, the government was compelled to evaluate its role in regulating the property market to prevent further catastrophes. This historical analysis examines how the colonial government attempted to navigate these competing interests while managing rapid urban growth.
