According to **Vivian Kong**, Co-Director of the **Hong Kong History Centre** at the **University of Bristol**, the use of visual archives is an effective method for encouraging the Hong Kong diaspora to document their family histories. Writing on September 10, **2026**, for the **Historical Photographs of Hong Kong (HPHK)** platform, **Kong** notes that while family elders are often willing to discuss their past, they frequently require specific triggers to unlock memories that have been obscured by time. 

**Kong** suggests utilizing the **HPHK** platform’s database, which categorizes visual evidence into 42 distinct topics, to prompt conversations with family members. For example, photographs from the **Nigel Spry Collection** featuring the **Mid-Autumn Festival** in **Kowloon Park** during **1983**—such as images of children playing with lanterns and candles—can serve as conversation starters about childhood celebrations and personal experiences in the city. Similarly, the 'Family Life' topic contains numerous images from **1982** to **1984**, including photographs taken in **Shek O** and **Sheung Shui Market**, which depict street vendors and traditional baby carriers. **Kong** posits that asking elders about these specific scenes—such as the nature of the tasks performed by their own caregivers—can reveal domestic histories that might otherwise be dismissed as trivial. By navigating the platform through temporal periods and specific geographic districts, researchers can reconstruct more nuanced narratives of family life in **Hong Kong** during the late **20th century**.

## Historical context

> [!note] General background (AI, not from the source)

The usage of historical photography as a tool for oral history and genealogical research has become increasingly popular in Hong Kong. This approach helps bridge the generational gap within the diaspora, providing a tangible connection to the city's rapidly changing urban and social landscape of the 1980s.
